A. PAIN RELIEF (TENs) (45 mins)   
   
  Tutor to demonstrate the application of TENS for pain relief (15 mins)   
Procedure:   
1. Collect the necessary equipment:  electrical stimulator; electrodes (one pair 
of self-adhesive electrodes) and cable leads; adhesive tape; soap and water 
for cleaning the skin.   
Equipment:   
A battery or mains operated TENS stimulator should be used:  
Biphasic, asymmetrical, square wave with zero net DC component, 
amplitude 0-50mA; pulse frequency 1-150; pulse width 0.05- 02 
msec.  
2. Conduct general safety checks with respect to the equipment.     
 
Safety Checks   
• 
Mechanical safety of equipment (leads, electrodes), service records 
etc.   
• 
Output of the machine.   
• 
Check subject with respect to contraindications listed below:   
 Contraindications:   
• 
Pacemakers or areas where it could cause malfunction of any 
electronic devices   
• 
to anterior neck or carotid sinus    
• 
to areas of impaired peripheral circulation   
• 
to persons with active deep vein thrombosis or thrombophlebitis 
deep vein thrombosis   
• 
areas with impaired sensation   
• 
pelvis, abdomen, trunk and low back during early pregnancy (1-3 
months)   
• 
regions of known or suspected malignancy    
• 
to the chest in persons with cardiac disease, arrhythmias, or heart 
failure   
• 
to the neck or head region of persons known to have seizures   
• 
to recently radiated tissues    
• 
to damaged or at-risk skin areas that would result in uneven 
conduction of current (excluding   
open wounds where the specific intent is to use electrical stimulation for 
tissue healing)   
   
3. Explain the treatment fully to the subject.   
4. Position the subject so that they are comfortable.  Ensure that the position 
allows the electrodes to be placed over the necessary contact points.  The skin 
should be uncovered and examined for any contraindications to treatment.     
5. Wash the skin to ensure good connection between skin and electrode.

6. Placement of electrodes:  This varies in practice according to the cause and 
location of the pain.   
 TENs electrodes can be placed at the following positions:   
• 
above and below or either side of ‘a painful area’   
• 
over trigger points   
• 
over a nerve where it is superficial, e.g. the peroneal nerve   
• 
proximal to the point of pain if part not accessible due to e.g. a cast 
or over relevant   dermatome   
• 
over a nerve trunk   
   
ENSURE THAT THE SUBJECT CAN MOVE ABOUT WITH TREATMENT IN SITU - 
REMEMBER THAT THE EQUIPMENT MAY BE USED FOR UP TO 24 HOURS AT A TIME!   
   
7. Explain what the treatment will feel like (this depends on the pulse frequency)   
• 
slow rate (around 10 Hz) - slow tickling sensation   
• 
high rate (100 Hz +) - continuous buzzing sensation   
   
Warnings:  no specific warnings are necessary but ensure the subject is clear about what 
to expect; they will experience some sensory stimulation which is exacerbated when the 
active electrode is not well placed or when there is poor contact between the skin / 
contact medium / electrode.   
   
8. Always begin with the intensity setting at zero.  Increase the output until the 
subject is aware of a mild buzzing or pulsing. Intensity will be dependent on 
whether convention, low or burst TENS is desired.   
9. Scan through the full range of pulse frequencies.  Note any differences in 
sensation.  (Client will be able to identify which is most effective in relieving their 
pain).   
10. If, after a short time, the subject stops feeling the stimulus the 
intensity/frequency/pulse duration may be tweaked.   
 
   
THE INTENSITY SHOULD NEVER BE STRONG OR PAINFUL 
11. You may need to move the electrode around a little to find the 
optimum position for stimulation.  This is because surface anatomy may 
vary a little between subjects.  12. NEVER LIFT THE ACTIVE ELECTRODE 
FROM THE SKIN OR REPLACE IT WITHOUT TURNING THE INTENSITY TO 
ZERO.   
13. Terminate the treatment and check the skin condition.   
14. Keep a full record of your treatment, noting:   
• 
Area of body treated   
• 
Patient positioning   
• 
Specific stimulation parameters

Hazards:   
• 
local skin irritation at electrode sites due to prolonged use of 
equipment    
• 
allergy to gels or adhesives   
• 
muscle damage or joint injury if the intensity of the current and 
therefore contraction is too strong   
• 
cardiac arrhythmia’s due to autonomic stimulation may occur when

electrical shock   
•

Tutor to demonstrate the use of NMES for muscle strengthening in 
neurology (15 mins)   
 
1.  Collect the necessary equipment:  electrical stimulator; electrodes and 
cable leads; contact gel may be required; adhesive tape; soap and water for 
cleaning the skin.   
 
2.  
Conduct general safety checks with respect to the equipment.     
 
Safety Checks   
 
• 
Mechanical safety of equipment.   
• 
Output of the machine.   
• 
Test the equipment on yourself in front of the subject. Use the 
thenar muscles of the hand and place one electrode at each end of 
the muscle bulk. Demonstrate the treatment and note the 
approximate intensity of current required to produce a contraction.  
The last will vary to some degree depending on the muscle being 
activated.   
3. 
Position the subject so that he/she is comfortable and can remain in 
the given position for the duration of the treatment.  Ensure that 
the position allows the electrode to be placed over the necessary 
motor points and that the limb can be secured to prevent the 
motion through the range of the joint that the muscle crosses. The 
skin should be uncovered and examined for any contraindications 
to treatment.   
4. 
Check subject with respect to contraindications listed below:   
 
Contraindications:   
• 
Pacemakers or areas where it could cause malfunction of any 
electronic devices   
• 
to anterior neck or carotid sinus    
• 
to areas of impaired peripheral circulation   
• 
to persons with active deep vein thrombosis or 
thrombophlebitis deep vein thrombosis   
• 
check skin sensation for information only - lack of sensitivity is 
NOT a contraindication to treatment but you should take 
particular care to watch for any changes in skin colour due to 
chemical irritation of the skin under the electrodes   
• 
pelvis, abdomen, trunk and low back during early pregnancy (1-
3 months)   
• 
regions of known or suspected malignancy    
• 
to the chest in persons with cardiac disease, arrhythmias, or 
heart failure

5. 
Wash the skin over the region of electrode contact.  You may 
reduce skin resistance by soaking the skin for three to four minutes 
either in a bath or with a warm damp pad.  
6. 
Stimulation of muscle via nerve:  Place an electrode more centrally 
on the part to be treated or over the spine and another electrode 
over the motor point.  Apply gentle pressure - poor contact will lead 
to increased sensory irritation and the need for increased current. 
You may need to move the electrode around a little to find the 
optimum position for stimulation.  This is because the position of 
motor points varies a little between subjects.   
7. 
Stimulation of muscle directly: Place the electrode at either end of 
the bulk to be stimulated    
8. 
Restrict the limb to move in order to elicit an isometric contraction.   
 Selecting stimulation parameters: – frequency; pulse duration; on: off time; 
ramp time.    
   
Warnings:  no specific warnings are necessary but ensure the subject is clear about what 
to expect; they will experience some sensory stimulation which is exacerbated when the 
active electrode is not well placed or when there is poor contact between the skin / 
contact medium / electrode.   
  
9. 
Increase the intensity until a muscle contraction is produced.  The 
duration and frequency should mimic a normal, active muscle 
contraction    
   
10. 
NEVER LIFT THE ACTIVE ELECTRODE FROM THE SKIN OR REPLACE IT 
WITHOUT TURNING THE INTENSITY TO ZERO.   You may roll the 
electrode on the skin provided you maintain good contact with the 
tissue.   
11. 
Terminate the treatment; check the skin condition.   
12. 
Keep a full record of your treatment.
