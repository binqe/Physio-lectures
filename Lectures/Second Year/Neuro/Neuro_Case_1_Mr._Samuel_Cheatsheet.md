I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
• 2 PATHOPHYSIOLOGY OF WORKING DIAGNOSIS What Is Happening Neurologically Mr.
• Occlusion of the right MCA results in infarction of the corticospinal tract fibres subserving the contralateral (left) body, producing upper motor neuron
hemiparesis.
• Neuroplasticity — encompassing synaptic unmasking, axonal sprouting, and cortical map reorganisation — begins within hours of the infarct and is
the biological substrate that physiotherapy exploits through high-repetition, task-specific practice to drive functional recovery.
• Typical Signs & Symptoms Explained by Pathophysiology Sign / Symptom Pathophysiological Explanation Left-sided hemiparesis (UL > LL) Right
motor cortex / corticospinal tract infarction.
• UL fibres occupy a more lateral and superior position in MCA territory, hence disproportionately affected by MCA occlusion compared with the more
medially represented LL fibres (ACA territory).
