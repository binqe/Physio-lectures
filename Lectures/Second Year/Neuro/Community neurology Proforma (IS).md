# Community neurology Proforma (IS)

Community Neurology Clinical Simulation for Physiotherapy Students
Name: Mr. G
Age: 59
Gender: Male
Subjective Assessment
Presenting Complaint: Mr. G, a 59-year-old male with a spinal cord injury, presents with restricted movement in both upper and lower limbs. He requires the assistance of two caregivers for transfers and most activities of daily living (ADLs). He reports:
Bilateral shoulder pain
Spasticity
Neuropathic pain
A neurogenic bladder, which may require catheterization
Low mood and suicidal thoughts, though he has not been formally diagnosed with depression.
He relies on an electric wheelchair with essential accessories for mobility indoors and outdoors.
History of Presenting Complaint: Three years ago, Mr. G experienced a traumatic collapse in his garden, with the exact cause remaining unknown. This incident resulted in a spinal cord contusion with myelomalacia at the C3/C4 level. He underwent anterior cervical discectomy and fusion at the C3/C4 level shortly after the injury.
Past Medical History:
History of autonomic dysreflexia episodes post-injury, but no recent occurrences in the past two years.
Likely has degenerative disc disease.
Recent MRI findings indicate small vessel disease with established infarcts.
Drug History:
Gabapentin: 300 mg, taken three times daily for neuropathic pain management.
Baclofen: 5 mg, taken three times daily for spasticity control.
Social History:
Lives in a care home on the first floor, with caregivers assisting in daily activities.
Married, with two adult children who are both employed.
Retired due to disability; previously worked as a construction worker.
Does not smoke or consume alcohol.
Has limited social interactions outside the home due to mobility restrictions.
Uses an electric wheelchair equipped with necessary accessories for daily mobility and independence.
Objective Assessment
General Observation:
Mr. G is seated comfortably in his electric wheelchair, appearing alert and engaged.
His breathing is spontaneous and unlaboured.
Displays effective use of his wheelchair, reflecting good adaptation despite mobility limitations.
Bilateral shoulder pain and spasticity are present, but no signs of acute distress.
Systemic/Segmental Assessments:
Upper and Lower Limb Mobility: Motor function is preserved below the C5 level. However, more than half of the key muscles in both upper and lower limbs have a muscle strength grade of less than 3, resulting in significantly impaired function.
Shoulder Pain: Bilateral shoulder pain noted, especially with movement.
Spasticity: Increased muscle tone observed, more pronounced in the lower extremities.
Sensory Deficits: Sensory impairments present below the C5 level, affecting perception of touch, temperature, and pain.
Special Tests:
ASIA Impairment Scale: AIS Grade C, with the neurological level of injury at C5.
Spinal Cord Independence Measure (SCIM): Score of 17, indicating significant dependency in ADLs.
Visual Analog Scale (VAS) for Pain: Bilateral shoulder pain rated as 6/10.
Questions
In your groups, use the Case study provided and answer the following questions:
Identify any new terminology in the case and research their meaning in your group.
Identify a problem list presented by the patient and classify them into impairment, disability, participation restrictions
Identify contextual factors associated with the patient presented in the case.
Consider some issues that Physiotherapists may focus on with Mr G.
