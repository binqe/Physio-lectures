## Slide 4

- Excitability
- The ability of cells to change their membrane potential in response to stimuli
- Voltage, ligands, light, mechanical stimulation
- Not just neurons
- Muscle cells, endocrine cells, cochlear hair cells, rods and cones in the retina
- Expression of voltage- and/or ligand-gated ion channels
- Change in membrane potential trigger a conformational change which opens a pore
- Permeability to ions (Na+, Ca2+, K+, Cl-)

## Slide 7

- Resting membrane potential (voltage)
- Na+/K+ ATPase keeps high extracellular Na+ and high intracellular K+
- Imbalance of charges along the membrane
- Intracellular side of the membrane has a many negatively charged molecules (proteins, lipids, etc)
- Extracellular side of the membrane has many Na+ cations  Positive charge
- Ions can hardly pass through the membrane

## Slide 8

- Resting membrane potential (voltage)
- Ions will try to move according to the electrochemical gradient
- Chemical concentration of ions
- Imbalance in + and – charges along the membrane (electric potential)
- Ion channels let ions pass according to the membrane potential
- The cell uses the electrochemical gradient to activate rapid mechanisms/signals
- The resting membrane potential is the battery of the cell!

## Slide 9

- Action potential
- At resting membrane potential
- Na+ and K+ channels are closed
- Stimuli positively increase the membrane potential (voltage) (depolarization)
- Upon reaching a voltage threshold, Na+ channels open
- Massive flow of Na+ ions inside the cell depolarizes even more the membrane potential
- Further depolarization opens K+ channels
- Flow of K+ ions outside the cell reduces negatively the membrane potential (repolarization + hyperpolarization)

## Slide 25

- Synaptic Plasticity
- Synaptic strength can change with time
- Frequency and kinetics of synaptic inputs
- Synaptic morphology, pre-synaptic properties and post-synaptic proteins
- Short-term plasticity
- Often pre-synaptic
- Lasts seconds to minutes
- Long-term plasticity
- Often post-synaptic
- Lasts for minutes, hours or even days
- Synaptic strength can change in both directons:
- Potentiation - when synapses become stronger
- Depression - when synapses become weaker
- Nature Reviews Neuroscience volume15, pages 250–263 (2014)

## Slide 29

- Backpropagating Action Potential (bAP)
- AMPAR
- NMDAR
- Starting from the AIS, a bAP can travel back along the dendrites
- Can amplify PSPs, by promoting dendritic depolarization
- Can promote Ca2+ current through NMDAR, by depolarizing the synaptic spines
- bAPs can induce Spike Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP):
- PSP before bAP  LTP
- bAP before PSP  LTD
- The timing between these two events is essential (+/- 50 ms)

## Slide 36

- Network compensatory mechanisms
- Reallocate lost function to alternative connected brain areas after brain injury
- E.g. non-dominant hemisphere compensates for stroke in dominant hemisphere, regaining partial control of the lost limb
- Maladaptive adaptation
- Motor and somatosensory cortices overcompensate missing limb sensory inputs  Phantom limb syndrome
- Synaptic plasticity in overstimulated nociceptive pathways followed by brain-wide network changes  Chronic pain
- Synaptic plasticity in nucleus accumbens and dopamine reward system  Drug addiction

## Slide 37

- Neuroplasticity and aging
- Decline in cognitive performance
- Rapid information processing, decision making, memory, executive cognitive function
- Some functions, such as verbal ability, can remain intact
- Some brain areas are more affected
- Hippocampus  episodic memory
- Decreased or altered synaptic plasticity
- Also decrease in synapse numbers more than neurons
- Increased inflammatory levels in the aging brain

## Slide 38

- Neuroplasticity and aging
- Alzheimer’s Disease (AD) characterized by multiple aspects
- Increased inflammatory presence due to amyloid-b plaques  neuronal death
- Increased synaptic loss and LTD due to oligomeric Ab

## Slide 39

- Intervene on neuroplasticity
- Brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) to stroke or spinal cord injury patients
- Not only to replace but also to stimulate adaptive neural changes
- Trans-cranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) and trans-cranial current stimulation (tDCS)
- Stimulating unused connected brain areas to help compensating affected regions loss
- Pharmacological modulation
- Brain-derived Neurotrophic Factor (BDNF)-enhancing drugs, to induce synapse formation and plasticity
- Ion-channels modulators to rebalance neuronal excitability
- Cognitive and Behavioural interventions
- Skills training (constraint-induced movement therapy in stroke patients)
- Environment enrichment and mental exercise to slow cognitive decline in aging
