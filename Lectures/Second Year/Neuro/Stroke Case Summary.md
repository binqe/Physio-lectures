# Stroke Case Summary

Case Summary MR B: Acute Stroke
A 54-year-old male brought to Accident & Emergency (A&E) with presenting complaints of Sudden onset left-sided weakness and slurred speech
History of Presenting Complaint (HPC)
The patient was brought to A&E by ambulance following a sudden onset of weakness in the left arm and leg, facial droop, and slurred speech about 2 hours prior to arrival. There was no loss of consciousness or seizure activity. He denied chest pain, shortness of breath, or head trauma.
On arrival, he was alert but anxious, with slurred speech (dysarthria) and left-sided hemiparesis. Blood glucose was normal. FAST test was positive.
Past Medical History (PMHx)
Hypertension (10 years)
Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (5 years)
Smoker (20 pack-year history)
No previous stroke or TIA
Social History (SHx)
Lives with wife in a house without banister on the stairs.
Has been independent with all transfers.
Mobilises independently,
works in financial institution.
Medications History: Amlodipine, Metformin, Simvastatin
Allergies: Nil known drug allergy
Red flag assessment: Good bladder and bowel functions. No history of malignancy.
Subjective assessment
Initial Assessment in A&E:
Met in resuscitation bay, oriented, alert and communicated verbally effectively.
Blood Pressure: 184/100 mmHg
Heart Rate: 88 bpm, regular
Respiratory Rtae: 18b/m
Temperature: 36.6 degrees C.
GCS: 15/15
NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS): 8 (moderate stroke)
Investigations:
CT Head (within 1 hour) – No haemorrhage; evidence of right middle cerebral artery (MCA) infarct
ECG – Sinus rhythm, no ischaemia
Bloods – Normal FBC, U&E, elevated glucose (9.8 mmol/L)
Head-
Cognition: Good memory, well oriented in time place and person. Patient presented with good attention.
Visual field: No Hemianopia, nystagmus, or diplopia
Face: facial paralysis noted, deviation of the right face noted.
Speech: No sign of receptive and expressive dysphasia. Patient struggled to swallow (Dysphagia).
RESPIRATORY ASSESSMENT (ABCDE)-
Airway: No artificial airway- oropharyngeal, nasopharyngeal, in situ. Not on oxygen.
Breathing – Abdomen moves with respiration. Normal chest wall, Normal Breath sounds, nil added sounds. Strong cough.
Circulation and cardiovascular- Not pale, Nil limb oedema, No finger clubbing, Pedal pulse rate intact.
Upper and Lower Limbs:
Nil shoulder dislocation.
Painful shoulder on active movement.
AROM were full in all limb joints.
Hyperreflexia,
Astereognosis.
Proprioceptors intact, joint positioning senses.
No spasticity, normal tone.
Trunk:
Good Rolling on bed, Presented with good static and Dynamic balances
Gait: Hemiplegic gait noted
Moving and Handling: Independent with chair and bed transfers. Mobilises with walking stick assistance on one.
Diagnosis: Left hemiparesis secondary to acute stroke.
Problem List:
Poor mobility without walking aids
Facial deviation to right, left facial weakness.
Poor swallowing
Pain in the left shoulder
During his stay in the stroke unit, the patient received MDTs:
•	Neurological monitoring and BP control by medical team
•	Physiotherapy and occupational therapy for left-sided weakness and mobility retraining
•	Speech and language therapy for mild dysarthria
•	Diabetic and hypertension management review by medical team
•	Smoking cessation counselling and lifestyle advice
Physiotherapy Management
Acute stage: (0-72 hours)-
Goals:
To Prevent complications
To Maintain joint range and muscle properties
To encourage early mobilisation
Treatments:
Positioning and repositioning
Passive and active assisted ROM
Chest physio, Deep breathing exercises
Early mobilisation – Sit out of bed
Educate patient more on his case and positioning, smoke cessation etc.
Sub-Acute Phase (3-14 days)
Goals:
To improve posture and balance on sitting and walking
To improve active use of the affected limb and reduced non use
Treatments:
Movement facilitation
Sitting and standing balance
Task oriented therapy
Constraint-induced movement Therapy (CIMT)
Gait re-education
Sensory facilitation
Rehabilitatin stage:
Treatment- Strengthening: Resistance exercises. Endurance retraining. Gait and balance training, intense.
Summary of Stroke Management pathways from hospital to Discharge:
Referred to stroke team from A&E immediately
Hyperacute ward – Stays 2-3 days. Thrombolysis (Alteplase) administered within 3 hours of symptom onset. If Blood pressure, consciousness are stabilised, patient will be moved to ASU
Admitted to Acute Stroke Unit (ASU) for monitoring and rehabilitation – Here patient will stay for 4 – 7 days, then moved to the ward.
Ward – for rehabilitation, if patient still have rehab goals on discharge, then to community inpatient hospital for further rehab. Total time to be here should be 4 weeks maximum.
Community inpatient Rehab – Hospital in the community for stroke rehab.
Repeat CT (day 5) was done: confirmed evolving right MCA infarct, no haemorrhagic transformation.
Discharge Summary
Condition on discharge:
Medically stable, able to mobilise with a stick
Mild residual left arm weakness
Independent in basic activities of daily living with minimal assistance from wife
Medications on discharge:
Aspirin 75 mg once daily
Atorvastatin 40 mg once daily
Amlodipine 5 mg once daily
Metformin 500 mg twice daily
Follow-up:
Community physiotherapy and occupational therapy
Stroke clinic review in 6 weeks
GP follow-up for blood pressure and diabetic control
Education:
Stroke prevention advice: smoking cessation, diet, exercise
Safety-netting for recurrence of stroke symptoms
Outcome
At 6-week follow-up, the patient showed significant improvement in left upper limb strength and was independent in mobility and self-care.
