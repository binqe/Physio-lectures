Development of Dermatomes & 
Myotomes
Functionally, the nervous system can be divided into:
•
Somatic part
•
Innervates structures – skin and most skeletal 
muscle
•
Somatic nerves arise segmentally along with 
developing CNS in association with somites
•
Visceral part
•
Innervates organ systems in the body and other 
visceral elements such as smooth muscle and 
glands, in peripheral regions of the body.

## Page 4

Development of dermatomes and 
myotomes
Neural crest cells differentiate into 
neurons on each side of the neural 
tube and form the sensory neurons
Neurons also develop from within 
the neural tube, and they form the 
motor neuron
Because cells from a specific somite develop into the 
dermis of the skin in a specific location, somatic sensory 
fibres originally associated with that somite carries 
information from that region and become part of one 
spinal nerve
Somatic motor nerves 
that were originally 
associated with a 
specific somite become 
part of one spinal nerve

## Page 5

Consequently…
A dermatome is that area of skin supplied by a single spinal 
cord level or by a single spinal nerve
A myotome is that portion of a skeletal muscle innervated by a 
single spinal cord level or by a single spinal nerve

•
Knowledge of dermatomes and myotomes  
- essential in neurological examination
•
Dermatome: light touch or pin prick 
sensation testing
•
Myotome: testing joint movements or 
specific muscle group

## Page 13

Iliohypogastric 
Nerve
•
Origin: anterior ramus of the first lumbar 
nerve (L1) in the lumbar plexus.
•
Course: it runs inferiorly and laterally, 
passing between the quadratus lumborum 
muscle and the kidney. It then penetrates 
the transversus abdominis muscle and 
runs between the transversus abdominis 
and internal oblique muscles.
•
Branches: it divides into the anterior and 
lateral cutaneous branches:
•
Lateral cutaneous branch: Supplies the 
skin of the superior gluteal region
•
Anterior branch: Supplies sensation to 
the lower abdominal skin above the pubis
•
Sensory distribution: The iliohypogastric 
nerve supplies sensation to the lower 
abdominal skin above the pubis and the 
skin of the superior gluteal region
•
Damage: can occur during abdominal 
procedures, pelvic injuries, sports injuries, 
or rarely during pregnancy. This can 
cause sensory deficits or painful 
paraesthesias.

## Page 14

Ilioinguinal 
Nerve
•
Origin: Anterior ramus of the L1.
•
Course:
•
Abdominal wall: Emerges from the 
psoas major muscle, passes in front of 
the quadratus lumborum, and travels 
obliquely across the iliacus muscle.
•
Transversus abdominis and internal 
oblique: Passes through these muscles. 
•
Inguinal canal: Enters the inguinal canal 
through the superficial inguinal ring.
•
Innervation: In males, the nerve 
innervates the scrotum, while in females, 
it innervates the labium. It also supplies 
sensation to the proximal medial thigh.
•
Damage: can cause chronic pain in the 
groin region, known as post hernia rafi 
pain syndrome or inguinal neuralgia.  
•
Symptoms: burning pain and numbness 
in the lower abdomen, genitalia, and 
inner thigh.

## Page 15

Genitofemoral 
nerve
•
Origin: Arises from spinal root L1 & L2
•
Course: Descend within Psoas major 
muscle, then pierces it to emerge on the 
anterior surface. 
•
Retroperitoneum: It descends further in 
the retroperitoneum, behind the ureter, 
gonadal vessels, and abdominal vessels.
•
Inguinal ligament: It divides into genital 
and femoral branches just above the 
inguinal ligament. 
•
The genital branch passes through the 
deep ring of the inguinal canal. In men: 
cremasteric muscle; in women skin over 
labium majus.
•
The femoral branch runs under the 
inguinal ligament and enters the femoral 
sheath. It then pierces the sheath and 
fascia latae to become superficial in the 
anterior thigh. It supplies the skin over the 
medial surface of upper thigh

## Page 16

Lateral 
cutaneous 
Nerve of thigh
Origin: arises from posterior rami of 
L2 & L3
Course: emerges from the lateral 
border of psoas major muscle and 
passes downwards obliquely, along 
the anterior surface of iliacus. It enters 
the lower limb under the inguinal 
ligament medial to the anterior 
superior iliac spine. It terminates as 
numerous sensory branches 
supplying the anterior and lateral 
surface of the thigh to the level of the 
knee

## Page 17

Obturator Nerve (L2-L4)
Origin: anterior rami of L2 – L4
Course: Descends in the psoas major muscle, emerging 
from its medial side near the pelvic rim. It continues 
posterior to the common iliac vessels, passes across the 
lateral wall of the pelvic cavity and enters the obturator 
canal, through which the obturator nerve gains access to 
the medial compartment of the thigh. It divides into anterior 
and posterior branches in the obturator canal. The two 
branches supply:
Innervations: Articular branches to the hip joint
Muscular branches to the obturator externus, adductor 
longus, gracilis, adductor brevis, adductor magnus 
muscles and pectineus
Cutaneous branches to the medial aspect of thigh, and in 
association with the saphenous nerve, cutaneous 
branches to the medial aspect of the upper part of the leg 
and articular branches to the knee joint
There is also an accessory Obturator N in some individuals

## Page 18

Femoral Nerve (L2-L4)
Origin: Anterior rami of nerve roots L2, l3 & L4
Course: It descends through the substance of psoas major muscle, 
emerging from the lower lateral border of the muscle. It descends 
between psoas major and anterior surface of iliacus, deep to the 
Iliacus fascia and lateral to the femoral artery as it passes posterior 
to the inguinal ligament and enters the anterior compartment of the 
thigh. In the thigh it divides into anterior and posterior branches
Innervations: Cutaneous branches: medial an intermediate 
cutaneous nerves supplying the skin on the anterior surface of thigh 
& saphenous nerve supplying the skin on the medial surface of the 
leg.
Muscular branches innervate the iliacus, pectineus, sartorius, rectus 
femoris, vastus medialis, vastus intermedius, vastus lateralis and 
articular branches to the hip and knee joints.
The terminal cutaneous branch of the femoral nerve is the 
saphenous nerve

## Page 23

Sciatic Nerve (L4-S3)
Origin: arises from L4 to S3 nerve 
roots
Course: forms on the surface of the 
piriformis muscle and leaves the 
pelvic cavity through the greater 
sciatic foramen inferior to the 
piriformis. Passes through the 
gluteal region into the thigh, where 
it divides into 2 major branches –
common fibular (peroneal) nerve 
(L4, L4, S1 & S2) and the Tibial 
nerve (L4, L5, S1, S2 & S3)
Innervations: muscles in the 
posterior compartment of thigh 
(hamstrings), leg and all muscles in 
the sole of the foot
Cutaneous fibres from skin of the 
foot and lateral leg

## Page 28

Pudendal 
Nerve
Origin: Originate from the anterior 
rami of S2 to S4
Course: It leaves the pelvic cavity 
through the greater sciatic foramen 
inferior to piriformis, muscle and 
enters the gluteal region. It courses 
into the perineum through the 
lesser sciatic foramen and traves 
through the pudendal canal. It 
divides into branches : inferior 
rectal nerve, perineal nerve and 
continues and as the dorsal nerve 
of penis or clitoris
Innervations: Innervates skin and 
skeletal muscles in the perineum, 
including the external anal and 
external urethral sphincters
Clinical relevance: 
•
Pudendal block anaesthesia.
•
Pudendal nerve stimulation
•
Pudendal neuralgia
•
Inferior rectal nerve: external 
anal sphincter
•
Perineal nerve: muscles of the 
pelvic floor and perineum
•
bulbospongiosus
•
Ischiocavernosus
•
Levator ani muscles
•
External urethral sphincter
•
Dorsal nerve of penis or clitoris
