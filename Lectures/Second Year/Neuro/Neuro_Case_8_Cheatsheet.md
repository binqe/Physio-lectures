I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
• In FND, this system becomes dysregulated — the brain generates an overpowering, fixed prior that the limb cannot move normally, and this prediction
overrides normal voluntary motor commands.
• Functionally, this manifests as symptoms that are real, involuntary, and neurologically generated, but not driven by structural damage to any specific
pathway.
• This is consistent with the key clinical feature: symptoms fluctuate with attention — they improve when Chloe is distracted and worsen when she
focuses on them or feels observed.
• The precipitating psychological stress likely sensitised this prefrontal-limbic-motor circuit, functioning as a "trigger" event for the disorder.
