Case 1 — Mr. Samuel Okoro
68 M | 5 days post-ischaemic stroke — Right MCA | Acute Stroke Unit, medically stable | Left hemiparesis (UL +
face) Left-sided neglect Pusher syndrome Poor trunk control 2-person assist STS Day 5 acute
Neurological Physiotherapy
I SUBJECTIVE PROBLEM LIST
• Physical Impairments
• Left lower facial weakness (UMN pattern)
• Trunk control deficit — unable to sit unsupported
• Presumed left sensory impairment (proprioception, light touch)
• Unable to bear weight through left LL safely
• Reduced insight / anosognosia:
II RED FLAGS
• New or worsening neurological deficit
• Dysphagia / wet voice / coughing on fluids:
• Immediate aspiration risk — refer urgently to SLT; do not allow
oral intake without SLT clearance.
• Cardiovascular instability
• (BP >180/110 mmHg or <90/60 mmHg, resting HR >100, SpOI
<94%): Physiotherapy is contraindicated — notify the nurse in
charge and await medical review before any active rehabilitation.
• Unilateral calf swelling, warmth, or redness in the hemiplegic leg:
• Suspected DVT — mobilisation risks pulmonary embolism; urgent
Doppler ultrasound and medical review required.
• Severely altered consciousness or acute agitation:
I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• Occlusion of the right MCA results in infarction of the corticospinal tract fibres subserving the contralateral (left) body, producing upper motor neuron
hemiparesis.
• Sign / Symptom Pathophysiological Explanation Left-sided hemiparesis (UL > LL) Right motor cortex / corticospinal tract infarction.
• UL fibres occupy a more lateral and superior position in MCA territory, hence disproportionately affected by MCA occlusion compared with the more
medially represented LL fibres (ACA territory).
• Left lower facial weakness UMN lesion — contralateral lower face only, because the forehead (frontalis) has bilateral cortical representation.
• Classic distinction from LMN facial palsy which affects all ipsilateral facial muscles.
• Left hemispatial neglect Right hemisphere parietal / fronto-parietal network damage.

I OBJECTIVE ASSESSMENTS & HOW TO PERFORM
Key Neurological Observations Before Testing
Tone:
Passive movement of UL and LL — expect flaccid / hypotonic acutely. Use Modified Ashworth Scale. Note any early spasticity emerging
in wrist flexors or ankle plantar flexors.
Posture: In supported sitting — observe direction and magnitude of trunk lean, active pushing against the plinth toward the left, and head position. Does he
perceive himself as upright? Neglect: Observe spontaneous attention — does he orient only to the right? Does he fail to notice or reach for items on the left?
Does he leave the left arm in unsafe positions? Coordination:
Test 1 — Scale for Contraversive Pushing (SCP)
What it measures: The severity of pusher syndrome — specifically, the degree to which the patient spontaneously pushes toward the hemiparetic side and
actively resists passive correction back to midline. Why I choose it:
I TREATMENT PLAN
• "Do you have any pain — particularly in your left shoulder or arm?"
• "Have you had any difficulty swallowing food or drinks, or any changes to your speech?"
• "Before all of this happened, how were you getting around — were you independent at home?"
• Observe the patient in sitting (and standing if tolerated) without manual correction from the therapist.
• Posture subscale (0–1):
• Is there spontaneous lateral lean toward the affected side? Score 0 = no lean; 0.25 = slight lean; 0.75 = moderate; 1 = extreme lean.
• Extension subscale (0–2):
• Does the patient use the non-paretic arm or leg to actively push (extend and abduct)? Score 0 = no; 1 = in one position; 2 = in both sitting and standing.
• Resistance subscale (0–1):
• When the therapist attempts to passively correct the patient toward true midline, does the patient resist? Score 0 = no resistance; 1 = resistance
present.
• Sum all subscale scores. Total range 0–4; score ≥1 = pusher syndrome present.
