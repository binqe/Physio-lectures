## Slide 3

- Excitability
- The ability of cells to change their membrane potential in response to stimuli
- Voltage, ligands, light, mechanical stimulation
- Not just neurons
- Muscle cells, endocrine cells, cochlear hair cells, rods and cones in the retina
- Expression of voltage- and/or ligand-gated ion channels
- Change in membrane potential trigger a conformational change which opens a pore
- Permeability to ions (Na+, Ca2+, K+, Cl-)

## Slide 6

- Resting membrane potential (voltage)
- Na+/K+ ATPase keeps high extracellular Na+ and high intracellular K+
- Imbalance of charges along the membrane
- Intracellular side of the membrane has a many negatively charged molecules (proteins, lipids, etc)
- Extracellular side of the membrane has many Na+ cations
- Ions can hardly pass through the membrane

## Slide 7

- The cell membrane as a circuit
- The cell membrane is a capacitor
- Insulation between intracellular and extracellular spaces
- Membrane potential (voltage source)  Electric field
- Molecules with opposite charges accumulate on either side
- The membrane capacitance is proportional to the membrane area
- The cell membrane is a resistor
- Ions are blocked by a barrier with high resistance
- The number of open channels dictates the membrane resistance (Rm) and the amount of flow of ions (current)

## Slide 11

- Action potential
- At resting membrane potential
- Na+ and K+ channels are closed
- Stimuli positively increase the membrane potential (voltage) (depolarization)
- Upon reaching a voltage threshold, Na+ channels open
- Massive flow of Na+ ions inside the cell depolarizes even more the membrane potential
- Further depolarization opens K+ channels
- Flow of K+ ions outside the cell reduces negatively the membrane potential (repolarization + hyperpolarization)

## Slide 13

- The refractory period
- Na+ channels inactivate (absolute refractory period)
- No following action potential can be initiated
- K+ channels are open (relative refractory period)
- Very strong stimuli can initiate an action potential

## Slide 19

- Neuronal output: the bouton
- Majority of neuronal connections (synapses) are chemical
- The signal is released by the bouton in small messenger molecules  Neurotransmitters
- Neurotransmitters are contained in synaptic vesicles
- The vesicles fuse with the membrane, releasing the neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft
- The release machinery fuses the vesicles and needs Ca2+ channels to work
- The identity (neuronal type) of the neuron is defined by the neurotransmitter

## Slide 24

- Neuronal signal integration
- Each neuron receives numerous inputs
- Axo-dendritic
- Axo-somatic
- Axo-axonic (AIS and terminals)
- When a synapse is activated, it will either generate:
- Excitatory Post-Synaptic Potential (EPSP)
- Inhibitory Post-Synaptic Potential (IPSP)
- The summation of EPSPs and IPSPs will determine the level of depolarization reaching the soma

- Excitatory neurons - principal cells
- Inhibitory neurons  - interneurons
- In cortex and hippocampus
- Principal cells are the pyramidal cells (glutamatergic)
- Interneuron (GABAergic) types can be many (parvalbumin, somatostatin, etc)

## Slide 26

- The neuromuscular junction
- Motor neurons axonal terminals highly branch onto the muscle to form neuromuscular junctions (NMJ)
- NMJ are rich in nicotinic receptors, which recognize acetylcholine
- Nicotinic receptors opening allows Na+ and Ca+ to and depolarization of the muscle cell
