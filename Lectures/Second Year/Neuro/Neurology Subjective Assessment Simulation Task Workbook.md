| Scenario Overview: Stroke |
| Learning Objectives for the Role Play:
Effective Communication:
Students should practice building rapport with the patient, using clear and empathetic communication to explore the patient's condition and concerns.
Ensure that the patient feels heard and supported during the assessment.
Comprehensive Subjective Assessment:
Students must gather all relevant information, including the patient's current condition, functional limitations, exercise tolerance, and social support system.
Students should focus on understanding the impact of SAH on the patient's daily activities and overall quality of life.
Psychosocial Support:
Students should demonstrate their ability to address the patient’s psychological and emotional concerns, especially anxiety about recovery and independence. |

| Instructions for the Patient Role:
Background Information:
You are a 55-year-old male who suffered a Subarachnoid Haemorrhage (SAH) three weeks ago after a ruptured berry aneurysm in the anterior cerebral artery. You were completing DIY tasks at home when you experienced a sudden, severe headache and collapsed.
You were transferred to A&E, underwent an angiogram, and had a coiling procedure to treat the aneurysm. Now, you are recovering in a neurological rehabilitation unit.
Current Condition:
You feel fatigued, weak, and anxious about your recovery, especially the risk of re-bleeding. You have noticed difficulty with balance and slight weakness on one side of your body but are unsure if this is due to the surgery or general fatigue.
You experience occasional headaches and find it difficult to concentrate for long periods.
Mobility and Daily Living:
You can walk short distances with assistance but feel wobbly and unsteady, especially when getting out of bed or walking upstairs.
You need help with some activities of daily living (ADLs), such as dressing or bathing, and you feel uncertain about when you will regain full independence.
Concerns and Expectations:
You are very anxious about your recovery, especially the risk of re-bleeding, and are unsure when you will feel normal again.
Your primary goal is to regain mobility and return to your normal activities at home, but you worry that the process will take a long time.
You hope to get back to work eventually but are unsure of your physical capacity. |
