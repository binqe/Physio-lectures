## Page 5

Why Assess?
• “assessments provide information on which clinical reasoning will be based and upon 
which decision making is reliant.”  (Jones, 2011)
• “Treatment efficacy is only as good as the assessment it is based on, and we only tend to 
treat what it occurs to us to assess.” (Keshner, 1991)
• Assessment is an ongoing & continuous process

Subjective 
assessment – 
the why?
• To identify the main problems as perceived by the

• To assist the setting of short- and long-term goals (in

treatment/management plan for the patient

## Page 13

Planning the 
objective 
assessment
• Analysis of subjective information obtained
• Determine:
• clinical relevance of the provided information
• Which functions are difficult for the patient and whether 
there are common factors influencing them
• Break down functions to analyse the likely joint 
movement, muscle actions and motor control aspects
• Reason the potential causes of the patient’s difficulties 
based on the presenting pathology

(body structure and function)
• Uses observation and handling skills
• Valid outcome measures

## Page 25

SMART patient centred-goals
• What is a goal?
• Discussed and agreed with the patient
• SMART
• Specific – focused and clear
• Measurable – descriptive, quality and time element
• Achievable – based on something the patient wants to do
• Realistic – within the confines of available resources
• Timed – by when?
• Include short- and long-term goals
• Short term: Mr A will be able to sit in an upright symmetrical posture, 
with stand-by assistance, for 30 seconds in 1 week
• Long term: Mr A will be able to sit-to-stand in an arm-chair, weight 
bearing evenly through lower limbs, independently, in 3 weeks
