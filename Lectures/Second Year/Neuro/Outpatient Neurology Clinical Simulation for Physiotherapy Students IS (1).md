# Outpatient Neurology Clinical Simulation for Physiotherapy Students IS

Outpatient Neurology Clinical Simulation for Physiotherapy Students
Case Introduction
You are working in an outpatient clinic, and Mr. A has been referred for physiotherapy due to worsening tremors, freezing episodes, and functional decline. Your goal is to assess Mr. A's current condition, identify impairments, and create a tailored rehabilitation plan.
Patient Information
Name: Mr. A
Age: 83 years
Diagnosis: Advanced Parkinson’s Disease with freezing gait
Subjective Assessment
Mr. A reported a worsening of his tremors, stiffness, and slowness of movement over the past few weeks following a fall outside his home, where he lost consciousness and couldn’t recall the event. He experiences increased difficulty with walking, talking, frequent freezing episodes, and near falls. His speech has declined significantly, impacting his communication with his ex-wife.
History of Presenting Complaint
Diagnosed with Parkinson’s 7 years ago (2017).
Recent worsening of tremors in the right hand.
Increased rigidity and slowness of movement.
Significant decline in speech clarity affecting relationships.
Past Medical History (PMH)
Atrial fibrillation
Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) – 4 years
Pulmonary embolism
Type 2 diabetes
Orthostatic hypotension
Lumbar spondylosis
Parkinson’s Disease (since 2017)
Drug History (DH)
Levodopa 100 mg, 4 times daily
Lisinopril 20 mg, daily
Pramipexole 0.5 mg, 3 times daily
Sertraline 50 mg, daily
Quetiapine 25 mg, daily
Social History (SH)
Lives alone in a single-story house.
Receives occasional help from his ex-wife.
Retired office administrator.
Limited social activities due to Parkinson’s progression.
Balanced diet but reports weight loss due to reduced appetite and difficulty preparing meals.
Objective Assessment
General Observation
Slow, shuffling gait with flexed knees and stooped posture.
Limited arm swing observed, indicating fatigue.
Masked facial expression with reduced movements.
Resting tremor in the right hand, slightly reduced with movement.
Mobility
Gait is slow with short, shuffling steps and minimal foot clearance.
Freezing episodes requiring verbal cues to unfreeze.
Narrow base of support.
Needs assistance of one person and verbal cues for sit-to-stand using a walking frame.
Range of Motion (ROM)
Upper Limbs: Mild restriction in shoulder flexion and abduction, with increased stiffness in the shoulder girdle. Rigidity in elbow and wrist flexion; normal ranges in other movements.
Lower Limbs: Hip flexion is within normal limits, but abduction is restricted with hamstring tightness. Mild restriction in ankle dorsiflexion and plantar flexion.
Manual Muscle Testing (MMT)
Upper Limbs: Mild weakness (4/5) in shoulder flexors, particularly in the deltoid and rotator cuff muscles. Grip strength reduced on the right side (3/5); left side normal (5/5).
Lower Limbs: Mild weakness (4/5) in knee extensors, hip abductors, dorsiflexors, and plantar flexors with increased muscle tone in the quadriceps and hamstrings.
