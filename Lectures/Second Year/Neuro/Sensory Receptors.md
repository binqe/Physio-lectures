## Slide 7

- Excitability
- The ability of cells to change their membrane potential in response to stimuli
- Voltage, ligands, light, mechanical stimulation
- Not just neurons
- Muscle cells, endocrine cells, cochlear hair cells, rods and cones in the retina
- Expression of voltage- and/or ligand-gated ion channels
- Change in membrane potential trigger a conformational change which opens a pore
- Permeability to ions (Na+, Ca2+, K+, Cl-)

## Slide 8

- Action potential
- At resting membrane potential
- Na+ and K+ channels are closed
- Stimuli positively increase the membrane potential (voltage) (depolarization)
- Upon reaching a voltage threshold, Na+ channels open
- Massive flow of Na+ ions inside the cell depolarizes even more the membrane potential
- Further depolarization opens K+ channels
- Flow of K+ ions outside the cell reduces negatively the membrane potential (repolarization + hyperpolarization)
- https://courses.lumenlearning.com/wm-biology2/chapter/resting-membrane-potential/

## Slide 49

- Taste bud cells
- Type I cells - 50%
- Glia-like functions – elimination of neurotransmitters and spatial buffering of K+
- Type II Cells - 30% (sweet/bitter/umami)
- mostly organic chemicals (sugars, glycols, alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, etc)
- mostly organic chemicals (long-chain organic substances and alkaloids)
- L-glutamate
- Type III cells - 2-10%
- Sour taste
- Intercellular communication
- Type II and type III cells - ATP, serotonine and GABA
- Type III to nerve fibres via serotonine and glutamate

**Notes:** Sour Taste: H+ concentration
Salty taste: cations, mainly Na+, but also some anions
Sweet taste: various compounds (mostly organic chemicals) including sugars, glycols, alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, amides, 
esters, some aa, some small proteins, sulfonic acids, halogenated acids and inorganic salts like lead and beryllium
Bitter taste: various compounds (mostly organic chemicals). Main two types are (1) long-chain organic substances
 that contain H+ or (2) alkaloids (many poisonous compounds from plants), like caffeine quinine strychnine and nicotine
Umami taste: mainly L-glutamate, mechanism still unclear.

## Slide 50

- Taste receptors
- Type I receptors (T1R) - GPCRs
- Venus flytrap module (VFM)
- T1R2/ T1R3 heterodimers recognize sweet taste
- T1R1/ T1R3 heterodimers recognize umami taste

- Function as monomers
- 20 or more genes encoding for T2Rs
- GLUT and SGLT1 - transporters
- Alternative sensing of glucose
- SGLT1  Na+/Glucose transporter - possible explanation for salt enhancing sweet taste!
