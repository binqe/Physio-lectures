## Slide 5

- Visualizing the cytoarchitecture of the cortex
- Different staining techniques for different features (Golgi, Nissl, Weigert)
- Layered structure of the cortex (neocortex)
- I - Molecular layer
- II - Outer granular layer
- III - Outer pyramidal layer
- IV - Inner granular layer
- V - Inner pyramidal layer
- VI - fusiform layer
- Formation of neuronal columns
- Major cell types in the neocortex
- Pyramidal cells (excitatory)
- Stellate cells (excitatory, if spiny)
- Interneurons (inhibitory, various types also smooth stellate cells)

## Slide 6

- Visualizing the cytoarchitecture of the cortex
- Granular vs agranular areas
- Granular cortex has more stellate cells in layer IV (mostly sensory and associative areas)
- Agranular cortex has fewer stellate cells in layer IV and more pyramidal cells in layer III and V (mostly motor areas)
- Different cortical mapping works:
- Brodmann (1909)
- Vogt and Vogt (1919)
- Von Economo and Koskinas (1925)
- Bailey and von Bonin (1951)
- Sarkissov et al. (1955)
- Most used mapping is Brodmann’s
- Multimodal maps of the cortex

## Slide 10

- Visual cortex (BA 17-18-19)
- Primary visual cortex BA 17
- Afferents from the optic radiation (geniculocalcarine tract) coming from lateral geniculate bodies and retinas  Striate cortex
- Alternating neuronal columns between left and right eye (ocular dominance columns)
- Association visual cortex BA 18-19 (peristriate or extrastriate cortex)
- Afferents mostly from BA 17 but also from pulvinar

## Slide 42

- Lesions of cortical motor areas: Upper motor neuron syndrome
- “UMN lesions are designated as any damage to the motor neurons that reside above nuclei of cranial nerves or the anterior horn cells of the spinal cord”
- Causes:
- Stroke
- Brain injury (trauma or other causes, e.g. hypoxia, infections)
- Motorneuron diseases (MND)
- Infections
- Malignancy
- Neuro-immunological disorders
- Degenerative disorders

## Slide 43

## Slide 46

- Upper motor neuron syndrome: symptoms
- Tendency to affect specific muscle groups
- Weakness/fatigability/decreased muscle control
- Positive symptoms 
| Symptom | Definition |
| Spasticity | Velocity-dependent increase in a muscle’s resistance to a passive stretch |
| Clonus | Sequence of rhythmic, involuntary muscle contractions to stretch stimuli |
| Hyperreflexia | Abnormal deep tendon reflex to stretch stimuli (eg patellar tendon reflex) |
| Hyporeflexia | (Specific) Reduced or abolished superficial abdominal reflex and cremasteric reflex |
| Synkinesia | Voluntary movement of one muscle provokes the involuntary movement of another |

## Slide 49

- Upper vs Lower motor neuron lesions
|  | Upper Motor Neuron | Lower Motor Neuron |
| Site of Lesion | Cortex and descending tracts up to the spinal cord | Anterior horn neurons, spinal cord roots, nerves |
| Muscle weakness | Present | Present |
| Muscle tone | Spasticity, clonus | Hypotonia |
| Fasciculations | Absent | Present |
| Tendon Reflexes | Hyperreflexia | Hyporeflexia |
| Abdominal reflexes | Absent or reduced | Present |

## Slide 50

- Case 1
- Left face/arm/leg numbness and/or weakness
- Left hemispatial neglect
- Gaphaesthesia (inability to recognize symbols on the skin) and astereognosis (inability to recognize shape and size of objects by touch)
- Gaze preference to the right
- Right Middle Cerebral artery occlusion

## Slide 51

- Case 2
- Contralateral motor weakness
- Sensory loss
- Urinary incontinence for some days
- Abulia (lack of initiative)
- Mutism
- Ideomotor apraxia
- Left Anterior
- Cerebral artery branch occlusion - callosomarginal
- Blood supply to the SMA and lower limb and perineal areas of sensorimotor cortex and supplementary sensory area

## Slide 52

- Case 3
- Cortical blindness (Anton’s syndrome) with complete visual loss
- Anosognosia (lack of awareness of the disease) about the deficit
- Prosopagnosia (inability to recognise people or their faces)
- Achromatopsia (inability to match colours with their names when presented)
- Bilateral Posterior Cerebral
- Artery occlusion
