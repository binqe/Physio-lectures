# Neurological differential diagnosis, case study assessments, and treatment

Neurological Differential Diagnosis
The diagnosis of a neurological condition is made by a doctor, such as a neurologist, who will conduct a neurological examination that typically assesses mental/cognitive status, cranial nerves, motor function, sensory function, reflexes, coordination and balance
A doctor will often request diagnostic tests to confirm a neurological diagnosis, such as imaging (CT, MRI, ultrasound), electrodiagnostic testing (EEG, EMG), nerve conduction studies, blood tests, lumbar puncture, and angiography
Whilst physiotherapists do not make a neurological diagnosis, you may see patients presenting with neurological symptoms that require further investigation. Therefore, it is important to understand the diagnosis of neurological conditions to guide further management.
Key considerations in the neurological examination to differentiate neurological conditions include:
History of symptoms e.g. sudden onset vs progressive over time, constant vs intermittent or relapsing, previous episodes
Location of symptoms e.g. unilateral vs bilateral, upper limb vs lower limb involvement, truncal/central vs peripheral/distal
Type of symptoms e.g. mixed sensory and motor signs vs motor signs only, presence or absence of higher cortical functions
