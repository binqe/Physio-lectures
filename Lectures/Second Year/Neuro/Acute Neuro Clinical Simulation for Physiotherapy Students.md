# Acute Neuro Clinical Simulation for Physiotherapy Students

Clinical Simulation for Physiotherapy Students: Acute Neurology

| Case Report
Patient Details:
Name: Mr. A
Age: 58 years
Gender: Male
Background: Recently repatriated from Hospital B after admission for a dense right-sided weakness and other symptoms.
Complaints on Admission:
Complete right-sided weakness (muscle strength graded 0/5)
Right facial droop
Moderate to severe dysarthria (difficulty articulating speech)
Headache and nausea |
| History of Presenting Complaints:
During his stay, a CT scan revealed a large intracerebral hematoma centred on the left internal capsule, leading to a diagnosis of haemorrhagic stroke. His neurological assessment revealed significant impairment with a score of 13 on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS), while his Modified Rankin Scale (MRS) score of 0 indicated no prior disability. As a result of the stroke, Mr. A experiences constant right shoulder pain rated at 7/10 and has an inability to fully adduct his right eye. Due to swallowing difficulties, he currently has a nasogastric (NG) tube in place, and he is doubly incontinent, presenting with both urinary and faecal incontinence.
Mr. A also reported pleuritic chest pain and episodes of tachycardia, which led to a CT Pulmonary Angiogram (CTPA) to rule out pulmonary embolism. While the scan did not show any evidence of pulmonary embolism to the segmental level, it did reveal stable internal debris and consolidation at the right lung apex. This finding is consistent with Mr. A’s known aspergilloma, a fungal mass, which likely contributes to his respiratory symptoms.
In addition to these complications, Mr. A received an infusion of Posaconazole to manage his aspergilloma but unfortunately experienced a reaction. Following the infusion, he developed swelling in his right upper limb and experienced a significant temperature spike to 39.4°C, suggesting an inflammatory or allergic response to the medication.
Physiotherapy and Occupational Therapy (PT/OT) Findings:
Upon further evaluation by the PT/OT team, Mr. A was found to have an independent sitting balance but currently requires a hoist transfer for mobility, indicating limited independence in movement. He has dense weakness in his right upper limb, with some flickers of movement in his right lower limb. Although he can communicate verbally, his speech coherence is reduced, and he continues to experience moderate to severe oropharyngeal dysphagia, which impacts his swallowing.

Past Medical History (PMH)
Mr. A has a history of Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD), aspergillosis, and longstanding alcohol use. He is a current smoker and was diagnosed with hypertension one year ago, though no antihypertensive treatment has been initiated.
Social History (SH)
Mr. A lives with his partner and two children and receives frequent visits from his extended family. He and his partner are both retired. His daily routine includes smoking 20 cigarettes and consuming approximately 13 cans of high-strength cider. Previously, he was independent and able to mobilize short distances outdoors, despite experiencing shortness of breath, and used an e-bike for longer distances. |

| Instructions for Treatment Planning in Clinical Simulation
Objective:
To equip students with a systematic approach to treatment planning by integrating clinical reasoning, patient-centred goals, and evidence-based interventions.

Step 1: Patient Assessment and Information Gathering
Review Case History:
Understand the patient's primary condition(s) and relevant medical history.
Identify key presenting complaints, risk factors, and any co-morbidities affecting the treatment plan.
Note any restrictions or precautions, such as weight-bearing limitations, surgical considerations, or ongoing treatments (e.g., medications, NG tubes).
Subjective and Objective Data:
Gather subjective data from the patient (or simulated patient) on their symptoms, functional limitations, and personal goals.
Conduct a comprehensive physical assessment focusing on:
Range of Motion (ROM): Active and Passive
Muscle Strength and Control
Sensation and Proprioception
Balance, Coordination, and Gait (if relevant)
Pain Assessment (location, intensity, quality, and duration)
Document findings systematically for clear reference.
Prioritize Issues and Impairments:
Identify and rank the primary impairments affecting the patient’s function and well-being (e.g., weakness, poor balance, dysphagia).
Consider psychosocial factors, such as the patient’s social support and motivation.

Step 2: Establishing SMART Goals
Define Functional Goals:
Establish goals that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound (SMART).
Goals should reflect improvements in functional independence, mobility, and quality of life.
Examples:
Short-term Goal: "Within 2 weeks, the patient will independently transfer from bed to chair with minimal assistance."
Long-term Goal: "In 3 months, the patient will be able to walk 50 meters with a walker without assistance."
Patient-Centered Goals:
Align goals with the patient’s own aspirations and values.
Incorporate patient input to increase engagement and adherence to the plan.

Step 3: Formulating the Treatment Plan
Select Appropriate Interventions:
Choose interventions based on patient assessment and goals. Ensure treatments are evidence-based and suited to the patient’s current ability and tolerance.
Treatment Plan: (Write down your treatment for this patient)


Consider Progression and Adaptation:
Plan for progression based on the patient’s response and functional gains.
Adjust exercise difficulty, intensity, and repetitions to gradually increase patient’s capacity.
Anticipate barriers and strategies to adapt interventions (e.g., modifying exercises if the patient experiences pain or fatigue).
Precautions and Safety Measures:
Adhere to all clinical and patient-specific safety precautions.
Monitor for signs of discomfort, fatigue, or adverse responses.
Educate the patient on safe movements, use of assistive devices, and injury prevention strategies.

Step 4: Monitoring and Documentation
Regularly Monitor Progress:
Evaluate the patient’s progress toward each goal at every session.
Modify the treatment plan as necessary based on observed improvements or setbacks.
Effective Documentation:
Document each session with details on interventions used, patient’s response, and any adjustments made.
Include objective measures (e.g., ROM, strength scores) and patient-reported outcomes (e.g., pain level, ease of movement).
Review and Reflect:
After each session, reflect on the treatment's effectiveness and what could be improved.
Seek feedback from the patient to gauge satisfaction and areas for improvement.
