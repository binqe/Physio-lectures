I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• M Neurological Mechanism — SPMS Secondary Progressive MS represents a transition from the predominantly inflammatory, relapse-driven
pathology of RRMS to a phase dominated by diffuse neurodegeneration and irreversible axonal loss .
• While focal inflammatory plaques still occur, the CNS repair mechanisms that enabled partial remission — remyelination by oligodendrocyte precursors,
cortical plasticity, and functional reorganisation — become progressively overwhelmed.
• Chronically demyelinated axons, deprived of the trophic support normally provided by myelin, undergo Wallerian degeneration at an accelerating rate.
• Grey matter atrophy, reduction in total brain volume, and spinal cord atrophy all accumulate, explaining why symptoms become fixed and progressive
rather than episodic.
• The bilateral adductor spasticity reflects upper motor neuron (UMN) pathway disruption in the bilateral corticospinal tracts at thoracic or lumbar spinal
cord level.
• Loss of supraspinal inhibitory input to spinal interneurons leads to hyperexcitability of alpha-motor neurons supplying the hip adductors and hip flexors.

I OBJECTIVE ASSESSMENTS & HOW TO PERFORM
Key Neurological Observations
Tone: Observe bilateral lower limb tone at rest — scissoring posture of legs (sustained adductor pull), clonus at ankle on rapid dorsiflexion. Perform
Modified Ashworth assessment of adductors in supine. Avoid sustained neck flexion throughout assessment (Lhermitte's precaution). Coordination:
Heel-shin test from supine or seated — look for cerebellar ataxia component overlying the spasticity. Finger-nose if upper limb involvement present. Posture
(seated):
Modified Ashworth Scale (MAS) — Bilateral Hip Adductors
Spasticity What it measures: Resistance to passive movement — grades spasticity on a 0–4 scale (0 = no increase; 1 = slight catch; 1+ = catch with
minimal resistance; 2 = marked increase but passive movement possible; 3 = considerable increase, passive movement difficult; 4 = rigid). The standard
clinical tool for quantifying and tracking spasticity. How to perform: Position Catherine supine on the plinth. If the transfer is too difficult, assess in her bed —
document this adaptation.
Passive Range of Motion — Hip Abduction (Goniometry)
Contracture Screen What it measures: Available hip abduction range bilaterally, distinguishing flexible (dynamic) spasticity from fixed musculotendinous
contracture. This distinction determines whether stretching is likely to be effective or whether surgical/orthopaedic input is needed. How to perform:
Catherine supine; pelvis stabilised. Use a standard goniometer with the axis at the anterior superior iliac spine, stationary arm along the midline, and moving
arm along the femur.
