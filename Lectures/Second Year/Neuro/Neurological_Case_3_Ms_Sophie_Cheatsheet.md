• Refer back to neurologist urgently. Suspend formal exercise
programme until relapse confirmed resolved (typically ≥4 weeks

• I Lhermitte's sign (new onset)
• An electric shock sensation down the spine on neck flexion —
indicates active cervical cord demyelination. New onset suggests an
evolving lesion or early relapse.
I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• Myelin forms the insulating sheath enabling rapid saltatory conduction between nodes of Ranvier; its destruction dramatically slows or completely
blocks action potential propagation.
• Clinically, this produces a discrete neurological deficit corresponding to the anatomical location of the active plaque — Sophie's optic neuritis reflects
inflammation at the retrobulbar optic nerve, causing painful visual loss.
• During remission, inflammation partially resolves and oligodendrocyte precursor cells attempt remyelination — which accounts for partial recovery of
function.
• However, remyelinated axons have shorter, thinner internodes and conduct more slowly; they are also more metabolically vulnerable.
• Sophie's bilateral foot paraesthesia reflects dorsal column demyelination at spinal cord level, disrupting proprioceptive and fine-touch afferent input to
the brain.
• Her central fatigue arises because the brain must work at significantly higher cortical effort to drive signals across the inefficient demyelinated network
— this is metabolically exhausting and cannot be reversed by willpower alone.

I OBJECTIVE ASSESSMENTS & HOW TO PERFORM
Key Neurological Observations
Tone: Assess bilateral lower limb tone at rest and through passive range. In post-relapse RRMS, mild spasticity (UMN pattern) may be present. Check for
ankle clonus on rapid dorsiflexion. Coordination: Heel-shin test (cerebellar/proprioceptive pathway integrity); finger-nose test if upper limb symptoms
present. Look for intention tremor, dysmetria, or dysdiadochokinesia. Posture:
Berg Balance Scale (BBS)
Functional Balance What it measures: Static and dynamic balance across 14 functional tasks, scored 0–4 each (total 0–56). A score below 45 indicates
significant fall risk in the community-dwelling MS population. How to perform: Set up a standard chair, a small step, and a 5-metre clear space.
Key Neurological Observations
Tone: Observe bilateral lower limb tone at rest — scissoring posture of legs (sustained adductor pull), clonus at ankle on rapid dorsiflexion. Perform

Heel-shin test from supine or seated — look for cerebellar ataxia component overlying the spasticity. Finger-nose if upper limb involvement present. Posture

Modified Ashworth Scale (MAS) — Bilateral Hip Adductors
Spasticity What it measures: Resistance to passive movement — grades spasticity on a 0–4 scale (0 = no increase; 1 = slight catch; 1+ = catch with
minimal resistance; 2 = marked increase but passive movement possible; 3 = considerable increase, passive movement difficult; 4 = rigid). The standard

• Test 2: Romberg Test and Tandem Romberg
• Sensory Integration
• What it measures:
• Patient stands with feet together, arms by sides. Observe sway with eyes open for 30 seconds — note amplitude and direction.
• Ask patient to close eyes; maintain the same position for 30 seconds. Remain in close standby to catch.
• Positive standard Romberg = marked increase in sway or loss of balance with eyes closed.
• Advance to tandem Romberg: one foot directly in front of the other (heel-toe), arms folded. Eyes open, then eyes closed.
