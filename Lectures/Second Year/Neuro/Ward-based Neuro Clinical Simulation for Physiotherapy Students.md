| Instructions for Treatment Planning in Clinical Simulation
Objective:
To equip students with a systematic approach to treatment planning by integrating clinical reasoning, patient-centred goals, and evidence-based interventions.

Step 1: Patient Assessment and Information Gathering
Review Case History:
Understand the patient's primary condition(s) and relevant medical history.
Identify key presenting complaints, risk factors, and any co-morbidities affecting the treatment plan.
Note any restrictions or precautions, such as weight-bearing limitations, surgical considerations, or ongoing treatments (e.g., medications, NG tubes).
Subjective and Objective Data:
Gather subjective data from the patient (or simulated patient) on their symptoms, functional limitations, and personal goals.
Conduct a comprehensive physical assessment focusing on:
Range of Motion (ROM): Active and Passive
Muscle Strength and Control
Sensation and Proprioception
Balance, Coordination, and Gait (if relevant)
Pain Assessment (location, intensity, quality, and duration)
Document findings systematically for clear reference.
Prioritize Issues and Impairments:
Identify and rank the primary impairments affecting the patient’s function and well-being.
Consider psychosocial factors, such as the patient’s social support and motivation.

Step 2: Establishing SMART Goals
Define Functional Goals:
Establish goals that are Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound (SMART).
Goals should reflect functional independence, mobility, and quality of life improvements.
Examples:
Short-term Goal: "Within 2 weeks, the patient will independently transfer from bed to chair with minimal assistance."
Long-term Goal: "In 3 months, the patient will be able to walk 50 meters with a walker without assistance."
Patient-Centered Goals:
Align goals with the patient’s own aspirations and values.
Incorporate patient input to increase engagement and adherence to the plan.

Step 3: Formulating the Treatment Plan
Select Appropriate Interventions:
Choose interventions based on patient assessment and goals. Ensure treatments are evidence-based and suited to the patient’s current ability and tolerance.
Treatment Plan: (Write down your treatment for this patient)


Consider Progression and Adaptation:
Plan for progression based on the patient’s response and functional gains.
Adjust exercise difficulty, intensity, and repetitions to gradually increase patient’s capacity.
Anticipate barriers and strategies to adapt interventions (e.g., modifying exercises if the patient experiences pain or fatigue).
Precautions and Safety Measures:
Adhere to all clinical and patient-specific safety precautions.
Monitor for signs of discomfort, fatigue, or adverse responses.
Educate the patient on safe movements, use of assistive devices, and injury prevention strategies.

Step 4: Monitoring and Documentation
Regularly Monitor Progress:
Evaluate the patient’s progress toward each goal at every session.
Modify the treatment plan as necessary based on observed improvements or setbacks.
Effective Documentation:
Document each session with details on interventions used, patient’s response, and any adjustments made.
Include objective measures (e.g., ROM, strength scores) and patient-reported outcomes (e.g., pain level, ease of movement).
Review and Reflect:
After each session, reflect on the treatment's effectiveness and what could be improved.
Seek feedback from the patient to gauge satisfaction and areas for improvement.
