I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
• 2 Pathophysiology of Working Diagnosis What Is Happening Neurologically Parkinson's Disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder
characterised by the selective loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta (SNc).
• This thalamic suppression dampens motor cortex excitation, producing the cardinal features of hypokinesia, bradykinesia, and akinesia.
• Vance's diagnosis, the disease process was likely well established for years before his resting tremor became the presenting complaint.
• Typical Signs and Symptoms — Explained by Pathophysiology Resting tremor (4–6 Hz, "pill-rolling"): Arises from pathological oscillations in the
thalamo-cortical and basal ganglia-thalamic circuits when normal dampening by the dopaminergic system is lost.
• Suppressed during voluntary movement because cortical volitional drive temporarily overrides the oscillation — this is why the tremor disappears
when he reaches for a cup.
