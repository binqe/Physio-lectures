# Initial Neuro Assessment

*The WHO ICF (International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health) framework encourages a wholistic overview of the patient so when assessing a patient don’t forget to get collateral history from the notes and other MDT members.
*Read the notes, imaging reports, check NEWS scores before seeing the patient so you can have an idea what to expect
*The 12 Cranial nerves: (On On On They Travelled And Found Voldemort Guarding Very Ancient Horcruxes). The doctors would usually complete this. But a knowledge of the function and assessment of the cranial nerves would also be beneficial to understanding the patient’s overall presentation. Besides, some of your assessments would already tell you how certain cranial nerves such as the optic, trochlear, abducens, facial and vestibulocochlear nerves are working.
*Don’t forget to liaise with other members of the MDTs for further assessments such as a cognitive screening by an OT.

| HEAD | Cognition: 
Memory: Can you remember what has happened to you? Do you remember where you live?
Orientation: do you know where you are? Do you know what time of the year it is? 
Attention: Are they very distracted? Do you have to constantly prompt them back to the question being asked? 


Visual field: Check for hemianopia. Use the 2-person confrontation test. Also assess for visuospatial neglect. Determine if it’s a neglect or hemianopia. Are they reporting a loss of peripheral vision?
Assess for nystagmus: check if eyes are moving quickly from side to side.  Use the smooth pursuit test. 
Diplopia- Ask patient if experiencing any double vision since the stroke?

Face: Facial paralysis/movement: Has patient got a drooped face or mouth? Is there a loss of nasolabial fold on one side of their face?  When patient takes a sip of water does this come out of the mouth, when patient talks is this on one side of the mouth? Assess their smile/frown. 

Speech: Note if there is some receptive and expressive dysphasia while speaking to patient. 
*Are there some word-finding difficulties? 
*Dysarthria- Note for slurred speech while also speaking to patient | Intervention |
| Respiratory Assessment
(A B C D E) | Airway – 
.Is the airway patent and protected?
.Are they on an artificial airway – oropharyngeal, nasopharyngeal? Are they tubed? 
.Have they got a trache – size, type – is it fenestrated or non-fenestrated? Is the cuff inflated? 
.Check O2 saturation. Are they on supplementary O2?
Breathing – Look feel, listen! 
Look for – use of accessory muscles, tracheal tug, respiratory rate and pattern

Feel for – decreased chest wall movement, symmetry of chest wall movement, tactile fremitus

Listen for – difficulty or inability to speak, cough (is cough strong/weak, wet/dry, productive/non-productive?), breath sounds (are breath sounds normal or adventitious?)
Circulation and Cardiovascular –
Look for – skin colour (flushed/pale), haemorrhage, urine colour, limb oedema, heart rate/rhythm, finger clubbing 
Feel for – pulse rate, temperature, capillary refill time, pitting oedema
Disability – are they alert verbal, in pain or unresponsive (APVU)? What’s their level of consciousness (use the Glasgow Coma Scale)?
Exposure - are there injuries, surgical wounds, lines, drains, tubes? |  |
| Upper and Lower Limb | Remember “Look, feel, move!”

Look at the shoulder joint : is there any sign of deformity? Is arm held in abducted+internally rotated position (for anterior dislocations), is there is a loss of the normal deltoid and acromion contour? Is humeral head palpable anteriorly? Are they reporting pain?
*Do a quick look at the rest of the upper limb: Is there any sign of deformity, swelling, redness/discolouration?  Can you notice during functional activities?

Feel for differential warmth, do some sensation test as well (touch, pain, temperature). 
Is there a body schema deficit e.g. are they having difficulties naming body parts during a sensation assessment? 

Move:
*ROM- active, passive and active assisted 
*Tone- check when completing ROM. Use the Modified Ashworth scale
*Power/strength. Assess the grip strength in upper limbs. Assess gross muscle power in different muscle groups. Use the Oxford grading scale. 
*Proprioception- hold affected side, move limb in different positions  to see if this can be replicated on the other side. 
* Stereognosis- texture (does patient recognise what 
object they are holding i.e. water bottle, comb (with eyes 
closed) 
* Barognosis - weight (does patient recognise the weight 
of object).
*Coordination - finger to nose test, heel to shin test. Any ataxia (lack of coordination)?
Any dysdiadochokinesia?- flip hand up and down. Can they do this accurately.
*Assess both gross and fine motor skills – observed this functionally 
*Reflexes:  Deep tendon: Biceps, triceps, brachioradialis, patella achilles. |  |
| Trunk | *Rolling: Is the patient able to roll by themselves? How much assistance do they need e.g minimum AO1 or moderate AO2? 
Was an equipment such as a bed lever used to complete this task? 
*Assess static balance sitting balance on edge of bed
Dynamic balance: sit/stand, forward reaching, assess patient during functional activities |  |
| Gait | Assess gait. What kind of gait are they displaying? Complete gait analysis. |  |
| Moving and Handling Assessment | How would you recommend that the patient is transferred/mobilized. 
What level of assistance did they need for their lie-sit, sit/stand transfer and during mobility? 
Think about the overall assessment and the patient’s capabilities. Remember to always think safety! |  |
