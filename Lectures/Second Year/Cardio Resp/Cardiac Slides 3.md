## Slide 2

- Step 1: Pre-Assessment Review (Chart and Handover)
- Confirm: surgical procedure, date and time, CONFIRM POST OP PROTOCOLCS, any intra-operative complications (prolonged bypass time, transfusion requirement, unexpected findings).
- Review: current vital signs trend, fluid balance, drain output over last 4- 8 hours, relevant blood results (Hb, K+, CRP, WCC, coagulation), CXR (most recent), and medications (anticoagulants, vasopressors, sedation, analgesia).
- Note: consultant physiotherapy guidelines for this patient, any specific precautions (e.g., sternal precautions, weight-bearing restrictions, anastomotic precautions in lung transplant).
- Things to look out for: Where this information can be found?

## Slide 3

- Step 2: Environment and Safety Scan
- Identify and document all attachments before approaching the patient: IV lines (peripheral and central), arterial lines, pulmonary artery catheter, urinary catheter, nasogastric tube, chest drains (and their drainage systems - e.g., underwater seal vs. digital drainage), pacing wires (temporary epicardial pacing - critical to be aware of), and any wound drains.
- Confirm drain patency and that chest drain tubing is not kinked or clamped during assessment.
- Ensure monitoring (continuous cardiac monitoring, SpO2, NIBP) is active and alarmed appropriately.
- Have suction available at the bedside, particularly if planning any airway clearance techniques.
- Have I liaised with the appropriate staff members? PT, OT, NS, Doctors

## Slide 6

- 3.2  Clinical Reasoning for Early Mobilisation - Preventing Post-Operative Pulmonary Complications (PPCs)Post-Operative Pulmonary Complications (PPCs) represent one of the most significant causes of post-surgical morbidity, ICU readmission, and mortality. They include atelectasis, pneumonia, acute respiratory failure, bronchospasm, prolonged mechanical ventilation, and pulmonary embolism.
- Evidence Base for Early Mobilisation
- Evidence base consisitently demonstrate that early mobilisation (commencing within 24–48 hours post-operatively) reduces rates of PPCs compared to delayed mobilisation.
- Upright positioning alone significantly increases FRC (by re-expanding basal lung zones), reduces V/Q mismatch, and improves mucociliary clearance through gravitational assistance.
- Active exercise increases tidal volume, promotes deep breathing, enhances lymphatic drainage, reduces venous stasis (DVT/PE prevention), and counteracts the deconditioning that begins within 24 hours of bed rest.
- For cardiac surgical patients specifically, early mobilisation has been shown to reduce ICU length of stay, reduce AF incidence, and improve patient-reported recovery quality at 6 weeks.

## Slide 7

- Physiological Rationale by System
- Respiratory: Upright posture shifts the diaphragm downward, increases thoracic volume, reopens collapsed lung units, and improves cough mechanics. Active breathing during exercise increases minute ventilation and promotes collateral ventilation.
- Cardiovascular: Orthostatic stress during sit-to-stand activates baroreceptor reflexes, promotes venous return training, and reduces resting heart rate over time. Reduces risk of venous thromboembolism.
- Musculoskeletal: Prevents critical illness myopathy (CIM) - a devastating complication of prolonged ICU immobility. ICU-acquired weakness begins within 24 - 48 hours of immobility.
- Neurocognitive: Physical activity reduces delirium duration and severity. Providing proprioceptive and vestibular stimulation through upright positioning and movement supports cognitive reorientation.
- Digestion: Facilitates bowel movement and constipation

## Slide 8

- 3.3  Safety Criteria for Progressing from Sitting-on-Edge-of-Bed (SOEOB) to Independent Ambulation
| Stage | Safety Criteria and Clinical Reasoning |
| SOEOB (Sitting on Edge of Bed) | HR: Resting <100 bpm; SpO2: ≥94% on current O2 therapy; BP: Systolic 90–180 mmHg; Patient: Alert and able to follow commands; No new arrhythmia; No drain changes indicating haemorrhage; Adequate analgesia in place; Nurse available to assist and monitor |
| Static Stand (at bedside, supported) | All SOEOB criteria PLUS: No significant postural hypotension (SBP drop >20 mmHg) on sitting; No dizziness or pre-syncope; Adequate lower limb strength to weight-bear; Lines and drains managed safely (not taut, not kinked) |
| Assisted Ambulation (2–3 metres, with 2 staff) | All standing criteria PLUS: HR increase <30 bpm above resting with standing; SpO2 maintained ≥94%; Patient motivated and able to cooperate; Walking aid selected appropriately for patient's needs and line management |
| Progressive Ambulation (increasing distances) | Tolerance of short ambulation without: (1) SpO2 drop >4% below resting; (2) HR >120 bpm; (3) Systolic BP drop >20 mmHg or rise >40 mmHg; (4) Borg RPE >14; (5) New chest pain, diaphoresis, palpitations, or pallor |
| Independent Ambulation (supervised at distance) | Consistent haemodynamic stability across multiple ambulation sessions; Ability to self-monitor symptoms; Adequate drain and line management taught to patient; Physiotherapist or nursing staff within visual range |
