## Page 4

Let’s get the terminologies right

SO2

The percentage of haemoglobin in the blood that is bound with 
oxygen

Representing oxygen-carrying capacity and oxygen delivery 
efficiency

Can only be measured via blood sample

Much more accurate but difficult to access

SpO2

Same as SO2 but measured peripherally and non-invasively via a 
pulse oximetry

Can be slightly less accurate due to factors such as blood flow, nail 
polish and movements

PO2

Partial pressure of dissolved oxygen free flowing in blood, not binded
to haemoglobin

Represents oxygenation of blood from lungs, and amount of oxygen 
available to be utilized

FiO2

Fraction of inspired oxygen

Percentage of oxygen in inhaled gas

Accurate way of measure exact amount of 
oxygen delivered into body

Oxygen flow

Usually use to describe the speed of 100% oxygen 
delivered through a flow metre from source

Measured in L/min

Hypoxia

Deficiency of oxygen in tissues

Hypoxaemia

Deficiency of oxygen in blood

Normal values
SpO2 / SO2
94% or above in an uncompromised 
individual
PaO2
10-13.3kpa
Oxy-haemoglobin dissociation curve
Cooperative binding
High PO2 supports high affinity of O2 
binding
Low PO2 supports faster O2 offloading
Bohr and Haldane effect

BE: -2 - +2 mmol/L

Respiratory failure 
Type 1 
Low PaO2 
Normal PaCO2 
“Hypoxia”
Pneumonia 
Fluid Overload 
when the respiratory system cannot 
adequately provide oxygen to the 
body 
Type 2 
Low or normal PaO2 
High PaCO2
“Hypercapnia” +/- Hypoxia 
Neuromuscular compromise 
Decreased drive to breath 
Chest wall disorders 
Chronic COPD patients
Poor ventilation of the alveoli 
leading to retention of Co2

## Page 9

Humidification

How : 

Air flows through nasal turbinates

Air is humidified and heated almost 
80% via the nose and sinus 

Why : 

To condition the air in preparation 
of gas exchange, which happens 
most ideally at 37 degrees and 
100% relative humidity

Maintains the mucociliary transport 
system 
Prevents drying of secretions 
Aids secretion clearance

Reading a flow meter
Read the value that runs through the middle of the black ball
Make sure it is connected to oxygen port NOT medical air
Use low flow metre for <1 L/min
Wall outlet always delivers pure FiO2 100% oxygen

## Page 14

Fixed Vs variable devices 
Variable devices 
Provide a flow of oxygen but no guaranteed FiO2 
Measured in L/min
FiO2 received depends on 
Change in RR 
Nose/mouth breathing 
Seal/leak
Fixed devices
Provide definite FiO2 based on device chosen
Does not vary with RR or even flow (after reaching minimum threshold)

## Page 15

Low flow : Variable devices 
Nasal cannula
Delivers 1 - 6 L/min
NOT to be used above 6L/min as it can dry up nasal mucosa
Pros: 
Used in hospital and in the community 
Comfortable 
Easy to mobilise 
Eating, drinking and talking are easy
Cons 
Dry oxygen
Variable FiO2 –4% per L/min increase roughly

## Page 16

Low Flow : Variable devices 
Simple face mask 
Can deliver 5-10 L/min 
Pros:
Good for mouth breathers 
Good if nose not appropriate
Cons
Affects communication, eating and drinking and 
mouth care 
Dry oxygen 
Inaccurate FiO2 delivery
Lower than 5L/min will lead to CO2 re-breathing

## Page 17

Low flow devices : Variable 
devices
Non Re-breath mask : 
Delivers 60-80% FiO2, needs 15L/min of flow 
Need bag to be completely inflated before

usage
Pros:
Delivers high amount of O2 
Great for emergencies : fast, simple set up
Cons
Dry oxygen 
Variable fiO2 
Affects communication, eating etc

## Page 18

Low flow devices :  Fixed devices 

Venturi mask

Attach onto a face-mask 

Provide a fixed FiO2 of oxygen as long as it 
reaches minimum flow requirement
Has recommended ideal flow rate for each FiO2 
option, but this can be increased in certain 
circumstances

Pros :
Good for mobility as will deliver a fixed FiO2 
even with a change in respiratory rate 
Provide high FiO2 with simple set up 
Can easily monitor oxygen demand

Cons
Dry Oxygen 
Affects communication, eating etc

Fixed Fio2 from : 28%-60%
Bubbled through sterile water
Pros:
Humidification
Fixed Fio2 
Cons: 
Not portable
Very noisy

## Page 20

High flow devices

High flow nasal cannula :

Give a fixed Fio2 but also a high flow of Oxygen 

Can give from 21%-100% FiO2 and up to 60L flow 

Can be given through Airvo/Optiflow device or ventilator

Pros
Heated and humidified 
Relieves work of breathing 
Provides minimal PEEP – barely beneficial
Evidence on supporting central and upper airway secretion clearance only

Cons 
Not good for mouth breathers 
Can cause pressure areas on face 
Can be uncomfortable

Target oxygenations
Normal:
SpO2 94 – 98%
Asleep:
Average SpO2 90%
CO2 retention:
SpO2 88– 92%
PO2 >7.3Kpa
Pulmonary hypertension / Cor pulmonale / Polycythaemia:
PO2>8kpa
SpO2 >90%
Paediatric cyanotic congenital heart conditions
SpO2 75 - 85%

## Page 25

Oxygen and Hypercapnia
When we give chronic hypercapnic patients too much oxygen …
Some conditions such as COPD leads to chronic CO2 retention
Desensitisation of peripheral chemoreceptors
Central receptors only able to detect an increase in oxygen levels. This 
leads to 3 effects and 1 outcome:
Haldene effect : Hb carrying CO2, dumps into the blood in favour of O2
Reduced drive to breath
Reversal of hypoxic vasoconstriction : increased shunt
All of these lead to an increase in PaCO2 which worsen the T2RF

## Page 26

Oxygen toxicity
High level of oxygen leads to production of oxygen derived free 
radicals
Cell damage
Acute lung injury secondary to increased permeability
Reduced cilia action
Impaired macrophage function
Reduced surfactant production

## Page 27

Absorption atelectasis
Created nitrogen washout
Nitrogen contributes to majority of the composition of inhaled gas
Oxygen is highly soluble to blood in comparison to nitrogen
Reduced gas in alveoli to maintain patency
