## Page 13

Features of 
deconditioning
Deconditioning is the decline in physical function of the body as a result of physical inactivity.
Key features =
- Atrophy of skeletal muscle bulk  
    (quads mean CSA 83.4±16.4 cm2 vs 109±15.6 cm2 p<0.0001 = Bernard et al, 1998)
   reduction in actin and myosin in the myofibrils 
   = decline in muscle strength
- Change in fibre type (wasting of type I slow oxidative =  
                                         proportionately more type II fast twitch) (Whittam et al, 1998)
- Reduced capillary density (Jobin et al, 1998)
- Reduction in cardiovascular fitness (esp endurance) e.g. stroke volume, reduced peripheral 
vascular tone = othostatic hypotension
- Stiffer more effortful movement 
- Increased risk of injury (tendons/ligaments/cartilage)
- Reduced bone density
Casaburi, 2006

## Page 72

Patient A: you are leading a community based Pulmonary rehabilitation service that runs twice weekly 
sessions on a rolling basis from a community hall.  A 64 year old female has been referred to your programme. 
She complains of severe and worsening breathlessness on climbing stairs and general household chores. She 
also reports a history of CHF, type 2 diabetes and difficulty sleeping which she puts down to night worrying. 
She hopes to get back to independently doing her grocery shopping (she does not drive) and also lose some 
weight.
Patient B: you are running a hospital based programme that delivers PR out of the hospital gym An 81 year 
old man has been referred to your service post discharge from hospital. This gentleman lives alone  and has 
been in hospital twice more this year with severe exacerbations of his COPD, for which he required NIV during 
his last visit. He is well know to his Gp practice and also the ambulance service. He is currently hesitant about 
whether he wants to join a PR programme but the gp feels she has exhausted all other options.
