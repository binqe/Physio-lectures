## Slide 4

- Epidemiology of COPD
- Globally around 3 million deaths annually due to COPD
- Expected increase to 5.4 million by 2060
- Huge financial burden on the NHS
- Respiratory disease accounts for approximately 6% of the total healthcare budget
- COPD accounts for 56% of this!
- (Gold, 2023)

**Notes:** Relationship between severity of COPD and cost – cost mainly due to exacerbations, hospitalisations and oxygen

## Slide 5

- Chronic Bronchitis
- Defined as a persistent cough with sputum production for at least 3 months of the year for 2 consecutive years
- Disease of the medium and large sized airways – the bronchioles
- Insidious onset with a morning cough – smoker’s cough

## Slide 6

- Chronic Bronchitis
- Repeated inhalation of pollutants leads to irritation of the airway mucosa
- Hyperplasia and hypertrophy of mucus glands in the large airways
- Excessive amounts of mucus in the airways
- Hyperplasia of goblet cells occurs at the expense epithelial cells
- Reduced ciliary function
- Small airways become obstructed by mucus
- Bacterial colonisation and repeated infection

## Slide 7

- Chronic Bronchitis
- Small airways become narrowed due to inflammatory changes – airway remodelling
- Mucosal oedema
- Smooth muscle hypertrophy
- Peribronchial fibrosis

- Impaired gas exchange

## Slide 10

- Emphysema
- Primarily a disease of the alveoli
- Protein breakdown
- Erosion of alveola septa
- Destruction of elastic fibres
- Destruction of capillary bed
- Airways become floppy
- Formation of bullae
- Permanent enlargement of the airspaces distal to the terminal bronchioles

**Notes:** How many alveoli are there in the lungs? Tennis court surface area

## Slide 11

- Secondary effects of emphysematous changes
- Airflow obstruction
- Increased resistance to airflow
- Reduced surface area for gas exchange
- V/Q mismatch
- Increased work of breathing

## Slide 12

- Aetiology of Emphysema
- Alpha 1 antitrypsin deficiency
- Genetic cause
- Alpha 1 anti-trypsin inhibits the action of neutrophil elastase which destroys elastin
- Develops before the age of 40
- Equal distribution between sexes
- SMOKING is the major cause

## Slide 15

- Clinical features of COPD
- Subjective examination
- Productive cough
- Wheeze

## Slide 16

- Clinical features of COPD
- Objective examination
- Auscultation: wheeze, decreased breath sounds, crackles
- Hyperinflated chest: Increased AP diameter - barrel chest
- Productive cough – purulent sputum

## Slide 18

- Signs of Respiratory Distress in COPD
- Pursed lip breathing
- Prolonged expiration
- Increased RR
- Cyanosis
- Active expiration
- Fixing of upper limbs
- Use and hypertrophy of accessory muscles
- Soft tissue recession
- Hoover’s sign

## Slide 19

- Respiratory Failure
- The inability to maintain partial pressures in arterial blood of carbon dioxide (PaCO2) and oxygen (PaO2) within normal physiological limits
- PaO2 < 8kPa
- PaCO2 > 6.5 kPa

## Slide 20

- Type 1 Respiratory Failure
- Decreased PaO2
- Normal or low PaCO2
- Treated with supplementary oxygen

## Slide 21

- Type 2 Respiratory Failure
- Decreased PaO2
- Increased PaCO2
- Alveola hypoventilation
- Can be acute or chronic

## Slide 22

- Acute Type 2 Respiratory Failure
- Decreased PaO2
- Increased PaCO2
- Normal HCO3-
- Kidneys take 2-3 days to compensate for raised PaCO2
- Decreased pH – respiratory acidosis

## Slide 23

- Chronic Type 2 Respiratory Failure
- Decreased PaO2
- Increased PaCO2
- Raised HCO3-
- Kidneys have now compensated
- Normal pH
- Example - COPD

## Slide 24

- Acute on Chronic Type 2 Respiratory Failure
- Decreased PaO2
- Increased PaCO2
- Raised HCO3-
- Decreased pH – acidotic
- PaCO2 normally high but due to sudden deterioration has risen further so kidneys no longer able to compensate
- E.g. acute exacerbation of COPD
