## Slide 5

- Stimulus → Nociceptor → Aδ/C fibres → Dorsal horn → Spinothalamic tract → Brain
- Key regions: Thalamus, Somatosensory cortex, Limbic system
- Pain Physiology (Nociceptive Pathway)
- ACP – PP – PA – CE – MS

**Notes:** Aδ (A-delta) fibers and C-fibers are types of nerve fibers responsible for transmitting sensory information, particularly pain, temperature, and itch, to the brain and spinal cord.

## Slide 6

- Mechanical
- Stimulus: Respond to intense mechanical stimuli, such as 	pinching, cutting, or stretching.
- Examples: Respond to pressure or incisions that break the 	skin.
- Thermal
- Stimulus: Respond to extreme temperatures, either very hot or 	very cold.
- Examples: Detect harmful heat or cold.
- Chemical
- Stimulus: Respond to chemical substances.
- Examples: Substances that can act on body tissues, such as 	inflammatory mediators.
- Polymodal
- Stimulus: Respond to high-intensity mechanical, thermal, 	and/or chemical stimuli.

## Slide 8

- Gate Control Theory
- Pain signals modulated at spinal cord “gate”.
- Non-painful input can inhibit pain.
- Basis for TENS (Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation) and manual therapy.
- Gate Closing: This activation of the inhibitory interneurons by Aβ fibers effectively "closes the gate," reducing the perception of pain.
- ACP – PP – PA – CE – MS

**Notes:** A diagram of the spinal cord's pain-gating mechanism shows large-diameter A-beta (Aβ) fibers (carrying touch/pressure) activating inhibitory interneurons, which then block pain signals from small-diameter A-delta (Aδ) and C fibers from reaching the brain's transmission neurons. Conversely, strong pain signals from Aδ and C fibers can inhibit these interneurons, allowing more pain signals to pass through the gate. This process, known as gate control theory, is why rubbing a painful spot can provide relief by activating Aβ fibers to "close the gate" to pain.

## Slide 16

- Clinical Examples (Fibromyalgia)
- Chronic widespread pain
- Central sensitisation
- Fatigue, sleep disturbance, cognitive symptoms
- ACP – PP – PA – CE – MS

**Notes:** Fibromyalgia is a chronic condition causing widespread, long-term pain throughout the body, accompanied by fatigue, non-refreshing sleep, and problems with memory and concentration. While there's no known cure, treatments focus on managing symptoms through medication, exercise, stress reduction, and self-care strategies to improve the quality of life. 

Tender points are specific, small areas of the body that are highly sensitive to pressure, causing pain when a doctor presses on them during a physical exam. These points, located on both sides of the body in predictable patterns, are more common with conditions like fibromyalgia and can be a symptom of the widespread muscle pain associated with it. While previously used to diagnose fibromyalgia, other criteria are now preferred, though tender points remain a recognizable symptom.
