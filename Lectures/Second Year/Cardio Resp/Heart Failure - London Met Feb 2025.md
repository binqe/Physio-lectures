## Slide 5

- What is Heart Failure (HF)?
- NICE, 2018:
- ‘A clinical syndrome with typical symptoms (breathlessness, ankle swelling, and fatigue) and signs (elevated jugular venous pressure, basal crepitations, and peripheral oedema).
- Caused by a structural and/or functional abnormality that produces raised intracardiac pressures and/or inadequate cardiac output at rest and/or at exercise.’

## Slide 7

- Important terminology
- End systolic volume (ESV) – the amount of blood in ventricles at the end of systole
- End diastolic volume (EDV) – the amount of blood in the ventricles at the end of diastole
- Pre-load – the degree of ventricular stretch during diastole
- Afterload – the force the heart needs to overcome to pump blood into the aorta
- Cardiac output = Stroke volume x Heart Rate
- Stroke volume = EDV – ESV

## Slide 12

- Diagnosing Heart Failure – NICE, 2018
- Detailed subjective history – signs and symptoms
- Objective tests:
- Blood tests – N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP)
- Transthoracic Echocardiogram (TTE) – assess valve function, systolic and diastolic function, detect intracardiac shunts
- Cardiac Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) – if poor quality image on TTE
- Right Heart Catheter
- Assess potential for differential diagnoses:
- Chest X-ray
- Electrocardiogram (ECG)
- Blood tests – renal, thyroid, liver, lipids, blood glucose (HbA1C), full blood count
- Urinanalysis
- Spirometry or Peak Flow

**Notes:** NT-proBNP – high levels = poor outcomes – specialist assessment and TTE within 2/52, moderate levels = 6/52
Intracardiac shunts – abnormal pathways of blood flow, common in congenital heart disease

## Slide 14

- Heart Failure Classification1 ) Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction
- Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction
- ‘The amount of blood your heart pumps (or ejects) with each heartbeat and is a useful way of measuring Left Ventricular Systolic Dysfunction’. (British Heart Foundation, 2021)
- Given as a percentage of total left ventricular output
- Normal EF = 55-65%
- Used to classify HF with reduced, mildly reduced or preserved EF

**Notes:** Important to note that normal is NOT 100%

## Slide 26

- Non-medical management of Heart Failure – Cardiac RehabilitationACPICR, 2023
- Cardiac Rehabilitation – Exercise-specific:
- Risk stratification – High risk if EF <35%, Moderate if 35-49%
- Sufficient warm up and cool down
- Aerobic training – ‘start low and go slow’
- Aerobic exercise intensity – low to moderate (40-70% Heart Rate Reserve (HRR) or Borg RPE 10-14)
- Resistance training!!!
- Other considerations:
- Energy conservation – aids/equipment, start position
- Flexible programme – accounts for ‘good’ and ‘bad’ days
- Postural training and core strength
- Avoid abrupt postural changes
- Maintain venous return – keep the feet moving!

**Notes:** Warm up and cool down – reducing risk of ischaemia, arrythmias, post-exercise hypotension
Adapting periods of CV depending on physical fitness – deconditioned = short periods of CV then active recovery, resistance training or seated programme
RT – if very deconditioned, consider RT only programme

HRR – Max HR-Rest HR – use Keteyian regression equation in HF

- Summary points:
- Heart Failure is a multi-organ condition that can cause a variety of symptoms
- There are a vast number of causes of Heart Failure
- Heart Failure diagnosis requires extensive assessment – both subjectively and objectively
- Heart Failure is classified in three ways – LVEF, disease time-course and symptomatic severity
