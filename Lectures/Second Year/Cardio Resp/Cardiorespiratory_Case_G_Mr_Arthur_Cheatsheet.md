I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• IPF is a chronic, progressive interstitial lung disease characterised by abnormal wound-healing responses in the alveolar epithelium.
• Repeated micro-injuries to type II pneumocytes trigger aberrant fibroblast and myofibroblast activation, leading to excessive collagen deposition and
parenchymal remodelling.
• This fibrosis thickens and stiffens the alveolar walls, dramatically reducing lung compliance — the lungs require far greater muscular effort to inflate for
the same volume of air.
• The thickened alveolar-capillary membrane increases the diffusion distance for oxygen, impairing gas exchange and causing hypoxaemia that worsens
substantially on exertion.
• The restrictive spirometric pattern (reduced TLC, FVC, and DLCO with preserved or elevated FEV1/FVC ratio) directly reflects this loss of functional
parenchyma.
• Over time, hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction drives pulmonary hypertension, which can lead to right ventricular failure (cor pulmonale).

1. Typically more steroid-responsive and carries a better prognosis.
