I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
• Fletcher's 40 pack-year history is the dominant aetiological driver.
• Emphysematous destruction causes loss of elastic recoil .
• Without the elastic tethering that normally holds airways open, dynamic airway collapse occurs during expiration — gas is trapped distally.
• Repeated air trapping leads to chronic hyperinflation — the barrel chest.
• The chronically hyperinflated, flattened diaphragm sits at a mechanically disadvantaged position (shorter muscle fibre length, suboptimal force-length
relationship on its length-tension curve).
I DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES
1. Bronchiectasis
• Chronic productive cough with large volumes of purulent sputum
• Recurrent chest infections / repeated antibiotic courses
• Coarse crackles in affected lobes on auscultation
2. Left Ventricular Failure
• Dyspnoea on exertion and at rest in a 74-year-old
• Peripheral oedema (ankle swelling)
• Fine bibasal crackles on auscultation

I TREATMENT PLAN
I Initial Phase — Weeks 1–2
• Energy conservation and pacing: Plan activities across the day — prioritise and delegate. Stop at 70% capacity rather than waiting until exhausted
and requiring long recovery.
• Smoking cessation: If still smoking — the single most effective intervention; refer to cessation service. Lung function decline slows markedly after

• Energy conservation and pacing: Plan activities across the day — prioritise and delegate. Stop at 70% capacity rather than waiting until exhausted
and requiring long recovery.
• Smoking cessation: If still smoking — the single most effective intervention; refer to cessation service. Lung function decline slows markedly after

I Long-Term — Weeks 4–12: Pulmonary Rehab and Self-Management
• Formal pulmonary rehabilitation programme: Twice-weekly supervised sessions (exercise + education) for 6–8 weeks. Strong evidence for ↑ 6MWT
distance, ↓ dyspnoea, ↓ hospital admissions, and ↑ quality of life.
• Resistance training: Lower limb focus (quads, glutes) — provides greatest functional gains in COPD. Unsupported upper limb exercise (arms above
shoulder height) trains ventilatory muscles — progress gradually.
• Self-management: Written COPD action plan; home walking diary; buddy walking scheme or community group for social support and accountability.
• Discharge criteria: SpOI stable during ADLs; 6MWT improvement ≥30m from baseline (MCID); able to self-manage acute exacerbations per action
plan; independent home exercise programme established.
