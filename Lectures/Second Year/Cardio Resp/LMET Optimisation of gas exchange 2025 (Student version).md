## Slide 6

- Lung Volumes
- TLC (Total Lung Capacity): The total amount of air in the lungs after a full inspiration
- TV (Tidal Volume): The volume of air moving into or out of the lungs during a normal breath
- RV (Residual Volume): The amount of air left in the lungs when expiration is complete
- VC (Vital Capacity): The maximal amount of gas which can be expired from TLC to RV gently (or inspired)
- FRC (Functional Residual Capacity): The amount of air remaining in the lungs after a normal passive exhalation
- ERV (Expiratory Reserve Volume): The extra volume of air that can be expired with maximum effort beyond the level reached at the end of a normal tidal volume expiration
- IRV (Inspiratory Reserve Volume): The volume of air that can be inhaled forcefully after normal tidal volume inspiration
- CV (Closing Volume/Capacity): The volume of air at which small basal airways start to close on expiration

## Slide 12

- Autoregulatory Mechanisms
- Hypoxic Pulmonary Vasoconstriction
- Occurs in regions of poorly ventilated alveoli
- Matching of poor Q to poor V
- Diverts blood to better ventilated alveoli
- Can become chronic and widespread leading to increased resistance to blood flow and right heart failure
- Cor Pulmonale
- Pulmonary Vasodilation
- Occurs in areas where ventilation is good
- Arterioles dilate
- Matching good V to good Q
- The Bronchial Response
- Sensitive to CO2
- Bronchodilation in the presence of high CO2
- Bronchoconstriction in the presence of low CO2
- Normalising CO2 levels

**Notes:** In addition to positioning, the body will try and compensate for a V/Q mismatch

- (West, 2012)

**Notes:** In the absence of pathology, V and Q vary throughout the upright lung. Ventilation increases slowly from the top to the bottom of the lungs whereas blood flow increases more rapidly

## Slide 14

- Causes of regional differences in ventilation:
- Intra-pleural pressure
- This is the pressure within the pleural cavity
- Intra-pleural pressure is negative to intra-alveolar pressure allowing alveoli to expand
- Spontaneous ventilation = negative pressure ventilation
- Intra-pleural pressure higher (less negative) at the bases due to the weight of the lungs in an erect posture
- The lungs are squashed under their own weight and contained inferiorly by the diaphragm and underlying abdominal organs

**Notes:** Anything that is supported requires a larger pressure below it than above it to balance the downward acting weight forces

## Slide 16

- Dependent Lung
- Dependent region of lung = the lowest areas of the lung, in relation to gravity
- Where there is the most alveolar compliance
- In upright position this is the middle to lower parts of the lung
- In side lying (lateral decubitus position) this is the lower lung, because the hemidiaphragm is at a mechanical advantage due to the pressure of the abdominal contents
