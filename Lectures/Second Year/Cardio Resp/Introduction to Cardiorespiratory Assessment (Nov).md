## Slide 5

- Assessment Components
- Data from alterative sources:: Medical notes, handovers,
- Subjective Assessment
- Interview style assessment
- Information re symptoms, feeling, perceptions and concerns
- Objective Assessment
- Observable and measurable data and signs.
- obtained through observation, physical examination and diagnostic testing

- Palpation
- Imaging
- Lung function testing

**Notes:** A symptom is a manifestation of disease apparent to the patient himself, while a sign is a manifestation of disease that the physician perceives. The sign is objective evidence of disease; a symptom, subjective. Symptoms represent the complaints of the patient, and if severe, they drive him to the doctor's office. If not severe, they may come to light only after suitable questions. The patient perceives, for example, subjective pains and discomforts [Doctor, I have a bad headache], or disturbances of function [Doctor, I can't move my arm the way I used to], or some simple appearance [Doctor, I have had this rash for the past ten days and I'm worried about it].

## Slide 13

- Changes can indicate new infection
- Hemoptysis – Presence of blood in sputum
- - Frank blood VS Streaking

- Do you bring up sputum?
- How effective is it to clear?
- How normal is that for you?
- What is the Colour? Consistency? Quantity per day? Smell?

**Notes:** If possible, several sputum samples should be collected at baseline followed by 3 to 6 monthly samples.
It is desirable for children and adults to learn how to expectorate sputum from their lungs to:
document microbiology
direct antibiotic treatment
assist with the monitoring of the condition

Patients with chronic infectiove lung disease often recurrent haemoptyses

## Slide 25

- Breathing Assessment: Palpation
- Trachea Position
- Chest expansion
- Palpation > Observation
- Reduced movement
- Paradoxal movement
- Integrity of chest was is disrupted ie Rib fractures / flail
- Diaphragm paralysis
- Feeling for expansion
- Hands placed on either side of chest
- Feel through normal and deep breath
- Consider
- How much expansion
- Do both sides feel similar?

**Notes:** Following a thorough inspection, palpate the thorax to assess for chest wall tenderness or masses, pleural friction or rubs, bronchial and tactile fremitus.17 Tactile fremitus is the vibration felt by resting the palmar surface of each hand on the chest simultaneously and symmetrically and asking the patient to repeat the words ‘ninety-nine’.8, 17, 19
Decreased or absent vibration over an area suggests the presence of fluid or air outside the lung, for example a pneumothorax or a pleural effusion. Increased vibration, however, indicates increased tissue density due to consolidation, lobar collapse, or a tumour.19 Finally, check for chest expansion by bringing thumbs together at the patient’s midline and asking the patient to breathe in

## Slide 28

- Listen to each lobe with a systematically
- Stepwise approach
- Start from top to bottom - Compare right and left side
- Repeat posteriorly
- Listen throughout inspiration and expiration
- Instruct patient to take deep breaths through mouth
- (Nasal turbulence interferes with breath sounds)
- Position patient for comfort and access to anterior and posterior chest
- Re-arrange patient clothing to allow access ** with consent
- Place against chest using gentle pressure
- Considerations
- Are there any additional sounds?
- Are the sounds normal, increased? Or diminished
