## Slide 5

- Pulmonary Ventilation: The Physics of Breathing
- Pressure Gradients Drive Air Flow:
- Pₐₗᵥ < Pₐₜₘ → Inspiration (air in)
- Pₐₗᵥ > Pₐₜₘ → Expiration (air out)
- Muscle Actions:
- Inspiration: Diaphragm contracts (75%), external intercostals
- Expiration: Passive (quiet breathing), Active (exercise) - internal intercostals, abdominals
- Key Pressures:
- Atmospheric: 760 mmHg
- Intrapleural: 756 mmHg (negative pressure)
- Alveolar: Varies with breathing cycle

## Slide 6

- Quantifying Ventilation
- Volumes (Non-overlapping):
- Tidal Volume (TV): 500 mL
- Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV): 3000 mL
- Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV): 1100 mL
- Residual Volume (RV): 1200 mL
- Capacities (Combinations):
- Total Lung Capacity (TLC): 6.0 L = RV+ERV+TV+IRV
- Vital Capacity (VC): 4.6 L = ERV+TV+IRV
- Functional Residual Capacity (FRC):
- 2.3 L = RV+ERV
- Inspiratory Capacity (IC):
- 3.5 L = TV+IRV

## Slide 7

- Effective vs. Wasted Breathing
- Alveolar Ventilation (Vₐ): Air reaching gas exchange areas
- Vₐ = (TV - Dead Space) × Respiratory Rate
- Normal: ~4.2 L/min (at rest)
- Dead Space:
- Anatomical: Conducting airways (~150 mL)
- Alveolar: Ventilated but non-perfused alveoli
- Physiological: Anatomical + Alveolar
- Clinical Correlation: Increased dead space in pulmonary embolism, COPD,

## Slide 8

- The Alveolar-Capillary Membrane
- Fick's Law of Diffusion:
- Rate ∝ (Surface Area × ΔPressure × Solubility) / (Thickness × √MW)
- Partial Pressures (Resting):
- Alveolar: PₐO₂ = 100 mmHg, PₐCO₂ = 40 mmHg
- Arterial: PaO₂ = 95 mmHg, PaCO₂ = 40 mmHg
- Mixed Venous: Pv̄O₂ = 40 mmHg, Pv̄CO₂ = 46 mmHg
- Diffusion Capacity:
- O₂: ~21 mL/min/mmHg
- CO₂: 20× faster than O₂ (high solubility)

## Slide 9

- How Oxygen Travels
- Dissolved O₂: Minimal (0.3 mL/dL at PaO₂ 100 mmHg)
- Hemoglobin-bound O₂: Primary transport (20.1 mL/dL)
- 1 g Hb binds 1.34 mL O₂
- Normal Hb: 15 g/dL × 1.34 = 20.1 mL O₂/dL
- Oxygen Content Equation:
- CaO₂ = (1.34 × Hb × SaO₂) + (0.003 × PaO₂)
- Oxyhemoglobin Dissociation Curve:
- Sigmoidal shape (cooperative binding)
- P₅₀: 26.7 mmHg (normal)

## Slide 10

- CO₂ Transport in Blood – Mechanisms
- Three Forms of CO₂ Transport:
- 1. Bicarbonate (70%)
- - CO₂ enters RBCs and is converted by carbonic anhydrase
- - CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
- - HCO₃⁻ enters plasma, H⁺ is buffered by Hb
- 2. Carbamino Compounds (23%)
- - CO₂ binds directly to haemoglobin
- - Binding strongest when Hb is deoxygenated
- 3. Dissolved CO₂ (7%)
- - Transported in plasma
- - Measured as PaCO₂ on blood gas (4.7–6.0 kPa)

## Slide 11

- Key Concepts – Chloride Shift, Haldane Effect, CO₂ Curve
- Chloride Shift (Hamburger Phenomenon):
- - HCO₃⁻ leaves RBC
- - Cl⁻ enters to maintain electroneutrality
- - Reverses in lungs
- Haldane Effect:
- - Deoxygenated Hb carries more CO₂
- - Oxygenation in lungs releases CO₂
- CO₂ Dissociation Curve:
- - Linear relationship
- - No saturation
- - Reflects dissolved + bicarbonate transport
- Haldane Effect
- Deoxygenated haemoglobin carries more CO₂
- When Hb releases oxygen in the tissues:
- It becomes a better buffer for H⁺
- It binds CO₂ more readily
- Promotes formation of bicarbonate
- In the lungs:
- Oxygen binds Hb
- Hb releases CO₂
- CO₂ is exhaled
- ✅ The Haldane effect makes CO₂ unloading in the lungs more efficient✅ It complements the Bohr effect (which works in reverse for O₂)

## Slide 12

- The Perfect Balance
- Ideal Ratio: V̇/Q̇ = 0.8 (Alveolar ventilation 4.2 L/min, perfusion 5 L/min)
- Regional Variations:
- Apex: V̇/Q̇ > 1 (High ventilation, lower perfusion)
- Base: V̇/Q̇ < 1 (High perfusion, moderate ventilation)
- Pathophysiology:
- Shunt (V̇/Q̇ = 0): Perfused but not ventilated (pneumonia)
- Dead Space (V̇/Q̇ = ∞): Ventilated but not perfused (PE)

## Slide 14

- The Heart's Electrical Symphony
- Action Potential Phases:
- Fast Response (Ventricular):
- 0: Rapid depolarization (Na⁺ in)
- 1: Early repolarization (K⁺ out)
- 2: Plateau (Ca²⁺ in, K⁺ out)
- 3: Repolarization (K⁺ out)
- 4: Resting (-90 mV)
- Pacemaker Cells (SA/AV): Slow depolarization (Phase 4)
- Conduction Pathway:
- SA node → Atria → AV node → Bundle of His → Bundle branches → Purkinje fibers

## Slide 15

- Reading the Heart's Electrical Language
- Waves and Intervals:
- P wave: Atrial depolarization
- PR interval: AV conduction delay (0.12-0.20 s)
- QRS complex: Ventricular depolarization (<0.12 s)
- ST segment: Ventricular plateau
- T wave: Ventricular repolarization
- QT interval: Total ventricular activity (HR-corrected)
- Mean Electrical Axis: Normal = -30° to +90°

## Slide 16

- One Heartbeat: From Diastole to Systole
- Systole (0.27 s):
- Isovolumetric Contraction: Valves closed, pressure rises
- Rapid Ejection: Aortic valve opens, blood ejected
- Reduced Ejection: Slowing outflow
- Diastole (0.53 s):
- Isovolumetric Relaxation: Valves closed, pressure falls
- Rapid Filling: AV valves open, passive filling
- Diastasis: Slow filling
- Atrial Systole: "Atrial kick" (25% of filling)

## Slide 17

- The Heart as a Pump
- Cardiac Output (CO) = HR × SV
- Normal: 5-6 L/min
- Maximum: 20-25 L/min (athletes)
- Stroke Volume (SV) Determinants:
- Preload: End-diastolic volume (Frank-Starling Law)
- Afterload: Aortic pressure (inversely related to SV)
- Contractility: Inotropy (Ca²⁺ availability)
- Regulation:
- Intrinsic: Frank-Starling mechanism
- Extrinsic: Autonomic nervous system, hormones

## Slide 18

- The Physics of Circulation
- Ohm's Law for Circulation:
- ΔP = Q × R (Pressure = Flow × Resistance)
- MAP = CO × TPR
- Resistance Determinants (Poiseuille's Law):
- R = (8ηL)/(πr⁴) - Radius is dominant factor!
- Pressure Distribution:
- Arteries: High pressure (100 mmHg)
- Capillaries: Exchange pressure (25 mmHg)
- Veins: Low pressure reservoir (10 mmHg)

## Slide 19

- Maintaining the Vital Force
- Short-term Regulation (Seconds to Minutes):
- Baroreceptor Reflex: Carotid sinus/aortic arch
- Chemoreceptors: Hypoxia/hypercapnia
- CNS Ischemic Response: Last resort
- Intermediate Regulation (Minutes to Hours):
- Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)
- Capillary Fluid Shift
- Long-term Regulation (Days):
- Renal-body Fluid System: Pressure diuresis/natriuresis

## Slide 20

- Where the Action Happens
- Starling's Forces:
- Net Filtration = (Pc + πi) - (Pi + πc)
- Pc: Capillary hydrostatic pressure (32 mmHg arterial end)
- πc: Plasma colloid osmotic pressure (25 mmHg)
- Pi: Interstitial hydrostatic pressure (-3 mmHg)
- πi: Interstitial colloid osmotic pressure (8 mmHg)
- Net: +10 mmHg (arterial end, filtration), -8 mmHg (venous end, absorption)

## Slide 22

- Who's Driving Your Breathing?
- Medullary Centers:
- Dorsal Respiratory Group (DRG): Basic rhythm, inspiration
- Ventral Respiratory Group (VRG): Active expiration, forced breathing
- Pontine Centers:
- Pneumotaxic Center: Limits inspiration, regulates rate
- Apneustic Center: Prolongs inspiration (inhibited by pneumotaxic)
- Central Chemoreceptors: Ventral medulla, sensitive to [H⁺] (CO₂)
- Peripheral Chemoreceptors: Carotid/aortic bodies, sensitive to PaO₂ (<60 mmHg), PaCO₂, [H⁺]

## Slide 23

- The Perfect Partnership
- During Exercise:
- Cardiac: ↑HR, ↑SV, ↑CO (up to 5×), redistribution of flow (muscles ↑, splanchnic ↓)
- Respiratory: ↑Ventilation, ↑V̇/Q̇ matching, ↑O₂ extraction
- Coupled Responses:
- Chemoreflex: ↑CO₂ → ↑Ventilation + ↑Sympathetic tone → ↑BP
- Baroreflex: ↓BP → ↑Sympathetic → ↑HR + Vasoconstriction
- O₂ Delivery Optimization:
- Fick Principle: VO₂ = CO × (CaO₂ - CvO₂)

## Slide 24

- Stress Response Systems
- Hypoxia:
- Acute: Hyperventilation, tachycardia, peripheral vasoconstriction
- Chronic: ↑Hb, ↑2,3-DPG, angiogenesis
- Hemorrhage:
- Stage 1: ↑Sympathetic, vasoconstriction, ↑HR
- Stage 2: RAAS activation, thirst
- Stage 3: Shock, organ failure
- Heart Failure:
- Compensatory: ↑Sympathetic, ↑RAAS, myocardial hypertrophy
- Decompensated: ↓CO, pulmonary edema, organ hypoperfusion

## Slide 25

- Physiology in Practice
- COPD Patient: ↑Airway resistance, ↓Elastic recoil, V̇/Q̇ mismatch, Pulmonary hypertension, Cor pulmonale
- Myocardial Infarction: ↓Contractility, ↓CO, Compensatory mechanisms, Potential cardiogenic shock
- Pulmonary Embolism: ↑Dead space, ↑Pulmonary resistance, ↓CO, Hypoxemia
- Heart Failure: ↓Ejection fraction, ↑Filling pressures, Pulmonary edema, Exercise intolerance

- Essential Takeaways
- Ventilation-perfusion matching is critical for efficient gas exchange
