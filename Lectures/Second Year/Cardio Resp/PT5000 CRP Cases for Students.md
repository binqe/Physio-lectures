# CRP Cases for Students

Cardiorespiratory Cases

### Case A:
Case study: Mrs Margaret Fletcher is a 74-year-old woman with a long-standing diagnosis of COPD who presents with increased shortness of breath and reduced exercise tolerance over the last 6 months. She has a 40 pack-year smoking history and demonstrates a barrel-chest appearance with use of accessory muscles at rest. She reports a chronic productive cough that is worse in the morning. She lives alone and is finding it increasingly difficult to manage household chores like vacuuming or carrying laundry. You are asked to assess her for suitability for a community-based pulmonary rehabilitation program.

### Case B:
Case study: Ms Elena Rodriguez is a 24-year-old postgraduate student who has presented to the emergency department with an acute exacerbation of her asthma. She reports that her symptoms were triggered by a recent viral upper respiratory tract infection and high pollen counts. She is currently breathless at rest, speaking in short phrases, and using her accessory muscles of ventilation. She has an audible expiratory wheeze and reports that her "blue" reliever inhaler is not providing the usual relief. You are asked to assess her breathing pattern and provide education on inhaler technique and rescue management once she is medically stabilized.

### Case C
Case study: Ms Sarah Collins is a 58-year-old woman admitted under the respiratory team with increasing cough and sputum production over the past two weeks. She has a known diagnosis of non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis and reports a history of recurrent chest infections requiring antibiotics. Over recent days, her sputum volume has increased and she describes it as thick and difficult to clear. She reports feeling more breathless on exertion and more fatigued than usual, with reduced ability to complete her daily activities. She usually manages her condition at home but has found her usual airway clearance techniques less effective during this exacerbation. You are asked to assess Ms Collins to guide her physiotherapy management.

### Case D:
Case study: Mr John Harris is a 72-year-old man admitted to the acute medical ward with worsening shortness of breath over the past week. He has a known history of heart failure with reduced ejection fraction secondary to ischaemic heart disease. Over the last few days, he has noticed increasing fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance, and swelling of both ankles. He reports difficulty breathing at rest and particularly at night. A chest X-ray has demonstrated features consistent with pulmonary oedema. He is currently receiving medical management including diuretics and supplemental oxygen. You are asked to assess Mr Harris as part of his physiotherapy management.

### Case E:
Case study: Mr Liam O’Connor is a 19-year-old male with Cystic Fibrosis who has been admitted for a "pulmonary tune-up" due to a decline in his lung function (FEV1). He reports a significant increase in the viscosity and volume of his purulent sputum, which has become tinged with blood (minor haemoptysis). He is finding his current home regimen of PEP therapy and exercise exhausting and has been struggling with adherence due to his new university schedule. You are asked to assess his airway clearance needs and work with him to adapt his treatment plan to improve his exercise tolerance and secretion clearance.

### Case F:
Case study: Mr Robert Miller is a 64-year-old man who is two days post-operative following an elective coronary artery bypass graft (CABG) via a median sternotomy. He has a 30 pack-year smoking history and a known baseline of mild COPD. He is currently reluctant to move, take deep breaths, or cough due to significant sternal pain and "clicking" sensations, despite having prescribed analgesia. On observation, he demonstrates a shallow, apical breathing pattern with reduced chest expansion at the bases and a weak, guarded cough. He is currently attached to a chest drain and a portable telemetry monitor. You have been asked to assess him to prevent post-operative pulmonary complications (PPCs), provide sternal support education, and facilitate early mobilization.

### Case G:
Case study: Mr Arthur Chen is a 69-year-old retired stonemason who has been referred to the outpatient clinic with a diagnosis of Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis (IPD). He complains of a persistent, dry, non-productive cough and progressive breathlessness that now occurs even during low-level activities like dressing. On examination, he demonstrates digital clubbing and fine "velcro" crackles on auscultation at both lung bases. His recent lung function tests show a restrictive pattern with reduced lung volumes. You are asked to assess his functional capacity and provide strategies for breathlessness management and energy conservation.

### Case H:
Case study: Mr David Thompson is a 55-year-old bus driver who reports a persistent cough that has produced sputum for at least three months out of the year for the last two years. He describes himself as a "blue bloater," noting a bluish tint to his lips and frequent episodes of peripheral oedema in his lower legs. He has a high BMI and continues to smoke 15 cigarettes a day. He is currently feeling "congested" and is seeking advice on how to clear his chest more effectively so he doesn't have to take as much sick leave from work. You are asked to assess his airway clearance and provide smoking cessation advice.
CRP OSCE Questions:
Perform a focused subjective assessment by identifying 3–5 key red flag symptoms and explaining their clinical significance for this patient. Develop a prioritized problem list that distinguishes between the patient's physical impairments and their functional activity limitations.
State your most likely clinical diagnosis and briefly describe the pathophysiology of this illness and the typical signs and symptoms or risk factors for developing a PPC (post top pulmonary complication).
Demonstrate two to three objective assessment techniques to evaluate a primary problem and explain the clinical meaning of your findings
Discuss your proposed initial and long-term treatment and management plan including patient education, treatment or management techniques and objective criteria you would use to progress the treatment.
