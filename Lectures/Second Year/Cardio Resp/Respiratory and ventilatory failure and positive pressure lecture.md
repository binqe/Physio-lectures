## Slide 8

- Type 1 respiratory failure
- PaO2 <8kPa with a normal or Low PaCO2 ( <4 )
- Hypoxia (Low O2 in tissue)
- Associated with disease process that directly affects the alveoli:
- Pneumonia
- Pulmonary Odeama
- Adult respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)  etc ….

## Slide 9

- Type 2 respiratory failure
- PaCO2 >6kPa +/- PaO2 <8kpa
- Hypercapnic (high blood CO2)
- Failure of alveolar ventilation
- Commonly associated with COPD
- Can also be associated with :
- Reduced respiratory drive
- Airway impairment
- Neuromuscular conditions
- Chest wall deformity
- Exhaustion associated with prolonged hypoxia

- Restlessness
- Hypercapnia :
- Signs of respiratory distress
- Confusion
- Pounding headache
- Tachycardia
- Breathlessness and Tachypnoea
- Drowsiness or reduced GCS
- Flushed face and extremities

## Slide 12

- Management of respiratory failure
- Try to treat the underlying condition eg:
- Wheeze : bronchodilation
- Retained secretions : airway clearance to clear secretions and allow re-expansion
- Antibiotics for pneumonia
- Diuretics to increase kidney function and reduce Odeama
- Change delivery or titrate up the fio2 oxygen being delivered to resolve hypoxia
- Hypoxia should ALWAYS be a primary concern and should be corrected
- If standard interventions do not work we need to consider using positive pressure to improve oxygenation and/ or ventilation

## Slide 14

- Aids Airway Clearance by :
- Improves VT
- Reduced WOB
- Increases use of collateral channels and interdepence of the alveoli
- Strengthens and augments the expiratory flow during a cough (cough assist)
- Great for patients who :
- Are in pain
- Are struggling to take a deep breath
- Have thick secretions that aren't cleared with other technqiues
- Patients with a weak cough : after surgery or due to neuro complications

## Slide 15

- Positive pressure ventilation : the effects
- Decreases work of breathing
- Off loads the accessory mm
- Improves lung mechanics
- Improves FRC
- Recruits collapsed lung
- Increases alveolar ventilation
- Improves V/Q mismatching
- Increases SA for gas exchange
- Reduced intrapulmonary shunt
- Enhances pulmonary compliance

## Slide 16

- Absolute contraindications :
- Undrained acute pneumothorax
- Severe facial deformity
- Facial burns / trauma
- Fixed upper airway surgery /trauma
- Frank haemoptysis
- Ruptured Oesophagus
- Frequent Vomiting
- Relative contraindications:
- Bronchopleural fistula
- Recent bronchial or oesophageal surgery
- Patient intolerance
- Raised ICP
- Bullous or cystic / cavitating lung disease
- Contraindications

## Slide 17

- CPAP
- What is it ?
- Continuous positive airway pressure
- CPAP delivers PEEP and maintains the set pressure throughout the respiratory cycle through both inspiration and expiration
- Provides the patient with positive end expiratory pressure (PEEP):
- Measured in cmH2O
- Breathing out against resistance creates back pressure and splints open the airways

## Slide 18

- Indications for CPAP
- Acute Type 1 respiratory failure
- Left ventricular failure with pulmonary odeama
- Increased work of breathing
- Decreased FRC/ Atelectasis
- Weaning from mechanical ventilation
- Obstructive sleep apnoea ( OSA)

## Slide 19

- Advantages to PEEP:
- Splints open the airways
- Increases FRC
- Recruits alveoli and prevents collapse on expiration
- Improves V/Q mismatch
- Greater surface area for gas exchange
- Improves collateral ventilation
- Disadvantages to PEEP:
- Reduced venous return to heart
- Reduced Cardiac output and therefore Blood pressure
- DOES NOT IMPROVE VENTILATION
- Can be used in T1RF to improve hypoxia associated with alveolar collapse and congestion
- CPAP effects

## Slide 20

- NIV or BIpap
- What is it ?
- Known as both non-invasive ventilation and biphasic positive airway pressure
- Has an inspiratory phase and an expiratory phase
- IPAP and EPAP or Pressure support and PEEP
- How does it work ?
- A high flow of gas is delivered into the airway via a tight-fitting mask creating positive pressure
- The device alternates between high inspiratory pressure and a lower expiratory pressure
- The higher inspiratory pressure augments the patient’s own inspiratory effort, the lower expiratory pressure creates PEEP.

## Slide 21

- Indications for NIV
- Any patient with un-resolving respiratory acidosis pH<7.35
- To prevent intubation in acute respiratory failure
- Exacerbation of COPD with persistent acidosis pH <7.35

## Slide 22

- NIV / BIPAP
- Aids alveolar ventilation
- Increases tidal volume
- Increases the clearance of CO2
- Improved gas exchange
- Reduced work of breathing
- Prevents respiratory muscle fatigue
- Decreases Resp rate
- PEEP/ EPAP splints open the airways and helps to prevent collapse on expiration

## Slide 23

- Adverse effects of positive pressure therapy
- Pressure injuries from the mask :
- Prolonged use
- Poor fitting mask
- Abdominal distension :
- Poor synchronisation with mask
- Patients not tolerating settings
- Barotrauma
- Always a risk with positive pressure

## Slide 24

- Relative contra-indications to NIV
- Ph < 7.15 + additional signs of deterioration
- Haemodynamic instability
- GCS <8
- Confusion / Agitation
- Small bowel obstruction or marked abdominal distention
- Copius secretions
- Severe co-morbidities
- Need for ongoing sedation

## Slide 25

- Commencement of NIV
- Each trust will have a unique set of guidelines to support safe commencement of NIV
- In an acute scenario you will likely be setting patients up using a ventilator or an acute NIV machine for a longer term patient who has their own machine you will use a different macchine.
- Essentially the main difference:
- Acute machines : Pressure support and PEEP
- Long term machines : IPAP and EPAP

## Slide 30

- Chronic use of Positive pressure
- Pathologies that affect  the respiratory mechanics or compliance of the lungs :
- Degenerative neuro-muscular disease
- Obstructive sleep apneoa
- End stage lung disease

## Slide 31

- Neuro-muscular disease
- Neuro-muscular disease :
- "Progressive muscular impariment leading to loss of ambulation, being wheel-chair bound, swallowing difficulties, respiratory mm weakness "
- Eg motor neuron disease, myesthentia gravis, myotonic dystrophy etc
- Affects the respiratory pump muscles
- Lose ability to clear Co2 due to reduced ability to generate appropriate Vt
- Lose bulbar and glottal function and therefore ability to generate cough
- NIV in this pateint group has been shown to increase quality of life and survival
- Cough assist someevidence to support MI:E, further evidence needed to specifically guide clinical practice

## Slide 32

- Obstructive sleep apneoa
- Obstructive sleep apneoa :
- During sleep due to neurological defect or obesity the airway becomes obstructed
- Diagnosed through sleep studies conducted as an inpatient
- Look at saturations, heart rate and blood pressure overnight
- Complete early morning blood gas
- CPAP and NIV has been shown to be an effective way to manage OSA, reduce the number of wakeful periods during the night and stabilisation of ABGs

## Slide 33

- COPD end stage and acute
- End stage COPD  and acute exacerbations:
- Emphesematous or inflammatory changes to the lungs result in reduced surface area for gas exchange
- Reduced elasticity of the lungs
- Reduced elasticity of airways
- NIV needed to blow off Co2 and splint open the airways
- Shown to improve chronic hypercapnic COPD patients' mortality
