Ventilation in the 
healthy lung:
• Unevenly distributed 
• Gravity causes a distribution of intrapleural 
pressures 
• At normal lung volumes the base of the lung is 
better ventilated than the apex 
• Also influenced by differences in regional 
mechanics and airflow resistance

## Page 6

Perfusion in the 
healthy lung: 
• Unevenly distributed 
• Explained by hydrostatic pressures within blood 
vessels 
• Also influenced by the arrangement of blood 
vessels and capillaries at any given level in the lung

Mechanics of Ventilation

## Page 15

BOYLES LAW: 
• Changes in intrapulmonary pressure occur 
as result of changes in lung volume
• Pressure of gas is inversely 
proportional to volume
• Increase in lung volume = decreased 
intrapulmonary pressure
• Air goes in
• Decrease in lung volume raises 
intrapulmonary pressure above 
atmosphere
• Air goes out

## Page 16

Mechanics of Breathing: 
Inhalation
• Diaphragm contracts and flattens 
• External intercostal muscles draw the ribs 
upward and outward
• Increase in thoracic volume
• Decrease in intrapulmonary pressure 
• Active process

## Page 17

Mechanics of Breathing: 
Exhalation
• Diaphragm and external intercostal muscles 
relax 
• Thoracic cavity volume decreases
• Increased intrapulmonary pressure -
exceeds the atmospheric pressure 
• Air is expelled from the lungs
• Passive process

PHYSICAL FACTORS affecting ventilation –
COMPLIANCE: 
• Willingness of the lungs to distend
• Elastance – willingness of the lungs to return 
to resting state
• Compliance is inversely proportional to 
elastance  
• Relationship between volume & pressure 
• eg: how much pressure (WOB) is required to 
expand the lungs? 
• Compliance = 
• change in volume / change in pressure
