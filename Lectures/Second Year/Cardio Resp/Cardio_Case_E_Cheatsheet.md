Case E
Mr. Liam O'Connor, 19M — Cystic Fibrosis (Pulmonary Tune-up) | FEVI decline · Increased purulent sputum
Target: 70%+ | Cardiorespiratory Physiotherapy
I PROBLEM LIST
• Impaired mucociliary clearance → retained secretions
• Airflow obstruction (↓ FEVI)
• Increased mucus viscosity (CFTR dysfunction)
• Reduced exercise capacity / deconditioning
• Minor haemoptysis (fragile inflamed bronchial mucosa)
• Unable to complete home PEP without exhaustion
• Exercise tolerance reduced (dyspnoea-limited)
• Difficulty maintaining therapy around university schedule
• Risk of further FEVI decline if adherence not addressed
I RED FLAGS
None identified
I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
• Core defect: Mutations in the CFTR gene produce dysfunctional or absent chloride channels on airway epithelium.
• This leads to dehydrated, hyperviscous airway surface liquid — mucus that cannot be cleared by normal mucociliary action.
• Infection-inflammation cycle: Retained mucus plugs obstruct small and medium airways, creating a nidus for chronic infection — predominantly
Pseudomonas aeruginosa .
• Neutrophil-dominated inflammation releases elastase and proteases that destroy airway walls, driving bronchiectasis, air trapping, and progressive
obstructive lung disease.
• FEVI decline mechanism: Each exacerbation accelerates bronchiectatic remodelling, reducing elastic recoil and increasing resistance.
I DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES
1. Allergic Bronchopulmonary Aspergillosis (ABPA)
• Common CF complication (prevalence 2–15%)
• Presents with worsening airflow obstruction, increased mucus, and constitutional symptoms
• Streaky haemoptysis and elevated total IgE may occur
2. Non-CF Bronchiectasis (e.g., post-infective)
• Shares features: chronic productive cough, purulent sputum, recurrent chest infections
• Similar airflow obstruction pattern and exercise intolerance
• Haemoptysis can occur in bronchiectasis of any aetiology

I TREATMENT PLAN
I Initial Phase — Weeks 1–2 (Inpatient Tune-up)
• Sequencing matters: Always use mucolytics (DNase, hypertonic saline) 30–60 min before airway clearance — this dramatically improves sputum
mobilisation and makes every session more effective.
• Adherence strategy: Two sessions daily is the evidence-based minimum; we will map sessions to your lecture timetable — a morning and evening
20-minute routine is realistic even around a full university day.
• Exercise is treatment, not extra: Aerobic exercise augments mucociliary clearance and slows FEVI decline — it is a core part of CF management, not
optional.
• Haemoptysis protocol: Minor streaking during coughing is common in CF. If blood volume increases significantly, you feel unwell, or it occurs outside
coughing, stop all airway clearance and contact the team immediately.
I Long-term — Weeks 4–12 (Outpatient / Self-Management)
• CF Pulmonary Rehabilitation: Supervised aerobic + resistance programme 2–3× per week; target ≥150 min moderate-intensity exercise per week
