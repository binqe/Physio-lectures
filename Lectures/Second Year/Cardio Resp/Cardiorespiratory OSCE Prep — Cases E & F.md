PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Core defect: Mutations in the CFTR gene produce dysfunctional or absent chloride channels on
airway epithelium. This leads to dehydrated, hyperviscous airway surface liquid — mucus that
cannot be cleared by normal mucociliary action.
Infection-inflammation cycle: Retained mucus plugs obstruct small and medium airways,
creating a nidus for chronic infection — predominantly Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Neutrophil-
dominated inflammation releases elastase and proteases that destroy airway walls, driving
bronchiectasis, air trapping, and progressive obstructive lung disease.
FEV₁ decline mechanism: Each exacerbation accelerates bronchiectatic remodelling, reducing
elastic recoil and increasing resistance. The cycle of infection → inflammation → structural
damage compounds over time.
Exercise limitation: Increased dead space ventilation, dynamic hyperinflation, and impaired
gas exchange create ventilatory limitation well below peak cardiac capacity. Deconditioning
from reduced activity further reduces peak VO₂.
Haemoptysis mechanism: Chronic inflammation drives neovascularisation of bronchial
arteries, which are fragile and prone to rupture with forceful coughing during exacerbations.
Minor haemoptysis indicates mucosal inflammation, not necessarily structural erosion.
Why Liam fits: Purulent sputum (active bacterial infection), exhaustion with PEP (ventilatory
limitation from ↓ FEV₁ and dynamic hyperinflation), and haemoptysis (inflamed neovascularised
mucosa) all map directly onto this cycle of infection → inflammation → structural decline.

PATIENT EDUCATION — 4 KEY MESSAGES
1. Sequencing matters: Always use mucolytics (DNase, hypertonic saline) 30–60 min before
airway clearance — this dramatically improves sputum mobilisation and makes every session
more effective.
2. Adherence strategy: Two sessions daily is the evidence-based minimum; we will map sessions
to your lecture timetable — a morning and evening 20-minute routine is realistic even around a
full university day.
3. Exercise is treatment, not extra: Aerobic exercise augments mucociliary clearance and
slows FEV₁ decline — it is a core part of CF management, not optional.
4. Haemoptysis protocol: Minor streaking during coughing is common in CF. If blood volume
increases significantly, you feel unwell, or it occurs outside coughing, stop all airway clearance
and contact the team immediately.

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Surgical mechanism of PPC: Median sternotomy divides the sternum and retracts the chest
wall, causing direct thoracic cage trauma. Combined with general anaesthesia, cardiopulmonary
bypass, supine positioning, and post-operative pain, this produces a predictable pattern of
reduced tidal volume, loss of functional residual capacity (FRC), and progressive alveolar
collapse — predominantly in dependent basal zones.
Atelectasis cascade: Surfactant dysfunction from bypass, diaphragmatic splinting from pain,
and impaired cough reflex allow distal airways to collapse. Gravitational forces compound basal
zone collapse in supine positioning. The result is ventilation/perfusion (V/Q) mismatch,
hypoxaemia, and a fertile environment for post-operative pneumonia.
COPD additive risk: Robert's mild COPD means he has reduced mucociliary clearance,
increased mucus production, and already-compromised small airway function at baseline.

## Page 11

General anaesthesia and surgical stress further impair these defences, placing him at
significantly greater risk of PPCs than a non-COPD patient.
Pain–breathing feedback loop: Pain with inspiration → splinting → shallow breathing →
atelectasis → hypoxaemia → anxiety → more splinting. Breaking this cycle with adequate
analgesia and supported deep breathing is the primary physiotherapy goal in the early post-
operative period.
Haemodynamic context: Post-CABG patients are at risk for arrhythmias (particularly AF in the
first 48–72 hours), fluid overload (pulmonary oedema), and early graft complications.
Physiotherapy must be timed and dosed to avoid excessive myocardial demand.
Why Robert fits: His shallow apical breathing, guarded cough, reluctance to move, and
reduced basal breath sounds all map directly to the atelectasis-pain-splinting cycle. His 30 pack-
year history and COPD compound the baseline risk for secretion retention and PPC
development.
