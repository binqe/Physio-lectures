# Heart Failure Teaching New

## Slide 1

- Heart Failure
- Dr Kola Akinlabi

## Slide 2

- Heart Failure – Definition & Overview
- Inability of the heart to pump sufficient blood to meet metabolic demands
- Reduced cardiac output and/or increased filling pressures
- Leads to pulmonary and/or systemic congestion
- Acute (sudden onset) or chronic (progressive condition)
- Common causes: IHD, hypertension, valvular disease, cardiomyopathy

## Slide 3

- Pathophysiology
- ↓ Cardiac Output → SNS + RAAS Activation
- 👉 When the heart cannot pump effectively:
- ↓ Blood flow to vital organs (brain, kidneys)
- The body compensates:
- 🔸 Sympathetic Nervous System (SNS)
- ↑ Heart rate (tachycardia)
- ↑ Contractility
- Vasoconstriction
- 👉 Short term: helps maintain BP👉 Long term: increases cardiac workload → worsens HF
- RAAS (Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System)
- Triggered by ↓ renal perfusion:
- Renin → Angiotensin II → Aldosterone
- 👉 Effects:
- Vasoconstriction → ↑ afterload
- Sodium + water retention → ↑ blood volume
- 👉 Result:
- More strain on already failing heart

## Slide 4

- Pathophysiology cont’d
- 2. Fluid Retention → Pulmonary Oedema
- 👉 Because of RAAS activation:
- ↑ circulating volume
- ↑ pressure in pulmonary circulation
- Leads to:
- Fluid leaking into alveoli
- 🔬 What happens in lungs:
- Alveoli fill with fluid instead of air
- Gas exchange becomes impaired
- 🫁 Clinical signs:
- Crackles
- Dyspnoea
- Orthopnoea
- Pink frothy sputum (severe)
- 🔹 3. V/Q Mismatch → Dyspnoea
- 👉 Normal:
- Ventilation (V) matches Perfusion (Q)
- 👉 In HF:
- Alveoli filled with fluid → poor ventilation
- Blood still flowing → good perfusion

## Slide 5

- Clinical Significance & Types
- HFrEF vs HFpEF
- Left HF: pulmonary oedema, dyspnoea
- Right HF: peripheral oedema, ascites
- Symptoms: fatigue, exercise intolerance
- High morbidity and hospital admission rates

## Slide 6

- Clinical Features
- Dyspnoea, orthopnoea, PND
- Peripheral oedema
- Crackles, fatigue

## Slide 7

- Investigations
- CXR: pulmonary oedema
- “Bat-wing” / Perihilar shadowing
- Bilateral central opacities radiating from the hilum
- Classic for alveolar pulmonary oedema
- 2. Cardiomegaly
- Enlarged cardiac silhouette (CTR > 50%)
- Suggests cardiogenic cause (heart failure)
- Kerley B lines
- Short horizontal lines at lung bases
- Indicate interstitial oedema
- Alveolar shadowing
- Patchy or diffuse white opacities
- Represents fluid-filled alveoli
- Pleural effusions
- Blunting of costophrenic angles
- Often bilateral
- Pathophysiology Link (teach this clearly)
- ↑ Left ventricular pressure→ ↑ pulmonary venous pressure→ Fluid leaks into:
- Interstitium (early → Kerley B lines)
- Alveoli (late → consolidation)
- BNP elevated
- Echo: EF assessment
