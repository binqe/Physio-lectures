## Page 3

The cardiovascular 
system
Circulatory system is made up of 3 
components:  
-
Heart – establishes a pressure 
gradient by putting pressure on 
the blood, allowing it to flow to 
the tissues 
-
Blood vessels – Passageways 
through which blood is 
distributed around the body 
-
Blood – Transport medium –
which the vital materials are 
suspended in. Including: O2, CO2, 
nutrients, electrolytes, hormones 
and waste products

## Page 8

Sulci of 
the Heart
• Heart interior is divided into four chambers 
demarcated by grooves on the external 
surface (Sulci [plural]).
• Coronary / atrioventricular sulcus is a 
transverse band around the heart separating 
atria & ventricles (contains the right coronary 
artery).
• Anterior and Posterior interventricular sulci, 
vertically aligned bands on front and rear, 
separating the ventricles.

## Page 12

Right Atrium: 
• Thin muscle wall
• Deoxygenated blood from 
SVC/IVC and coronary sinus 
(drains blood from myocardium)
• Most blood flow in occurs during 
inspiration (when RAP drops 
below SVC/IVC) (high-low)
• No valves in SVC/IVC therefore 
increased RAP results in systemic 
congestion

## Page 13

Left Atrium: 
• Receives oxygenated blood from 
the lungs through pulmonary 
veins (PVs)
• Wall of LA is slightly thicker than 
RA and breathing does not affect 
its filling.

## Page 15

Right Ventricle:
•
Body of the RV 
•
Inflow region consisting of - 
tricuspid valve, the chordae 
tendineae, the papillary muscle, 
and a heavily trabeculated 
myocardium
•
Infundibulum
•
a smooth outflow region
•
‘Separated’ by four muscular bands
•
Little pressure is needed to pump blood 
to the lungs.
•
The RV generates less than ¼ stroke work 
of the left ventricle.

## Page 16

Left Ventricle:
• Thick muscular wall
• Receives blood from the LA 
through MV and ejects through 
AV to the systemic circulation via 
aorta.
• Pressure in LV is high.
• The ventricular septum - houses 
electrical conduction tissue and 
provides stability for the 
ventricles during contraction.

## Page 18

Valves: 
• Blood flows through the heart in one fixed 
direction
• 4 x 1 way valves ensure no backwash
• Valves open and close passively due to pressure 
differences – ‘one way door’

## Page 19

Atrioventricular 
valves: 
• Between atria & ventricles
• Atrial pressure > ventricle 
pressure 
• Ventricular filling 
• Prevents backflow during 
ventricular emptying 
• Right AV valve = tricuspid 
• Left AV valve = bicuspid/mitral

## Page 20

Semilunar 
Valves: 
• Between ventricles and major 
arteries 
• Semilunar – 3 cusps, like half 
moons 
• Forced open when ventricular 
pressure > pressure in 
aorta/pulmonary artery 
• Ventricular contraction & 
emptying 
• Close when ventricles relax

## Page 21

Pathology link – 
Valve disease:
•
Regurgitation: blood flows backwards 
through leaky valve leads to 
progressive overload and then heart 
failure
•
Stenosis: narrowing of the valve, 
caused by scarring, calcification or 
congenital.
•
Causes: congenital heart disease, 
rheumatic fever, cardiomyopathy, 
damage to the heart muscle from an 
MI, getting older, a previous infection 
with endocarditis
•
Treatment : TAVI ( transcatheter aortic 
valve implantation), valve repair or 
replacement.

## Page 25

Pericardium:
The pericardium has several 
protective functions:
• Is fused to the diaphragm 
so downward movement 
during inspiration pulls 
heart more vertically
• Keeps the heart 
contained within the 
chest cavity,
• Prevents the heart from 
over-expanding when 
blood volume increases,
• Limits heart motion,
• Reduces friction between 
the heart and 
surrounding tissues, and.
• Protects the heart 
against infection.

## Page 26

Pathology link – 
Pericarditis:
• Inflammation of the 
pericardial sac 
• Potential leak of blood 
or fluid between layers
• Painful friction 
• Often due to 
viral/bacterial infection

## Page 27

Blood supply of 
the heart – 
Coronary Arteries
• Heart has its own blood supply from the 
Coronary arteries (crown the apex)
• Coronary arteries wrap around outside of 
heart with small branches penetrating into 
heart muscle for blood
• 2 major coronary arteries branch off from 
the aorta near where aorta and left 
ventricle meet (Left and Right Main 
coronary arteries)
• LMCA supplies left side of heart (LA and LV) 
and has L anterior descending artery (front 
of left side of heart) and circumflex artery 
(outer side and back of heart)
• R Coronary artery supplies blood to the 
right ventricle, the right atrium, and the SA 
(sinoatrial) and AV (atrioventricular) nodes. 
Also supplies middle (septum)
• Coronary veins take blood that has been 
used by the muscles of the heart and 
return it to Right atrium

## Page 29

Flow of blood through 
the heart: 
•
Deoxygenated blood from body > 
Right atrium via superior & inferior 
vena cava
•
Right atrium > Right ventricle (via 
tricuspid valve) 
•
Right ventricle > Pulmonary artery 
(via pulmonary valve)
•
Pulmonary artery > L & R branches 
> Lungs 
•
Oxygenated blood from lungs > Left 
atrium via R & L branches of 
pulmonary veins
 
•
Left atrium > Left ventricle (via 
mitral valve)
•
Left ventricle > Aorta (via aortic 
valve)
•
Aorta > Rest of body

## Page 33

Mediastinum 
- Borders, Organs & 
Nerves 
Superior: 
Bordered by the thoracic outlet superiorly
Transverse thoracic plane or sternal angle 
inferiorly 
Medial border of the pleural sacs laterally
Dorsal surface of the sternum anteriorly
Ventral surface of the first four thoracic vertebral 
bodies posteriorly.
Organs: thymus, trachea, oesophagus
Nerves: left and right vagus, recurrent laryngeal, 
cardiac, left and right phrenic

## Page 34

Mediastinum 
- Borders, Organs 
& Nerves 
Anterior: 
Bordered by the pericardium 
posteriorly
Medial border of the pleural sacs 
laterally 
Sternum, transversus thoracis 
muscles, and 5th, 6th and 7th left 
costal cartilages anteriorly.
Organs: thymus
Nerves: none

## Page 35

Mediastinum 
- Borders, Organs 
& Nerves 
Middle: 
Formed by the borders of the pericardial sac 
anteriorly and posteriorly
Reflected to the medial borders of the pleural sacs 
bilaterally
Transverse thoracic plane superiorly
Thoracic surface of the diaphragm inferiorly
Organs: the heart and its great vessel roots, 
trachea and main bronchi
Nerves: phrenic, vagus, sympathetics
