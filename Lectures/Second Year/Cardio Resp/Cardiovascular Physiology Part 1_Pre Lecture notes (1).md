## Slide 18

- Capillary blood flow is slow and intermittent reflecting the need for an erythrocyte transit time of >1 second for complete O2 diffusion from the capillary to surrounding tissue.
- The erythrocyte has a diameter of 7.2μm, slowing its passage through the capillary due to their diameter exceeding the size of the capillary lumen (erythrocyte diameter = 3-20μm). The erythrocyte deforms enabling passage through the capillary loop, slowing the transit time and encouraging complete O2 conductance.
- Types: nutritional [unresponsive to thermal stress and do not increase blood flow in response to heat]capillaries & thermoregulatory capillaries
- Under resting conditions skin may be over-perfused and available figures suggest that 0.8 ml of blood/min/100 ml would be adequate to supply the skin oxygen requirements at rest. However the mean and maximum skin blood flow is higher than this value. A considerable reserve capacity therefore exists which would enable flow to increase by a magnitude of 100 times the resting value during hyperaemia.
- The number of capillaries per unit area is considered a determinant of tissue oxygenation

- 5. Myocardial action potential and contraction coupling

## Slide 48

- Cardiac Rhythm
- Key words!
- Depolarisation- Contraction, blood leaving, systole
- Repolarisation- Relaxation, blood filling, diastole
- There are two types of cells within the cardiac muscle:
- Cardiomyocytes- Cells which make up the atria and the ventricles.
- Autorhythmic cells - Specialised cardiac muscle cells, which are self-excitable (intrinsic).
- Action potential – Spread from the autorhythmic cells and kick start the intrinsic conduction system

**Notes:** Cardiomyocytes- Cells which make up the atria and the ventricles. A myocardial cell contains myofibrils, which are specialized organelles consisting of long chains of sarcomeres, the fundamental contractile units of muscle cells. 

Autorhythmic cells - Specialised cardiac muscle cells, which are self-excitable (intrinsic). Therefore Autorhythmic cells repeatedly generate action potentials that trigger heart contractions, through the conduction nodes and pathways.

Action potentials – Spread from the autorhythmic cells within the intrinsic conduction system to the contractile cells are electrical events. Contracting the Cardiomyocytes causing a heartbeat.
