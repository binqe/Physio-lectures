Partial Pressure: 
• Partial pressure : The pressure exerted by 
an individual gas in a mixture 
• Daltons law expresses the fact that the 
total pressure of a mixture of ideal gases is 
equal to the sum of the partial pressures of 
the individual gases in a mixture

## Page 11

3. Thickness of membrane
• 0.5 - 1 thick
• Diffusion efficiency is highly dependent on distances involved 
• Alveolar membrane is ultra thin, resulting in rapid and efficient gas exchange 
Clinical significance: 
• If membrane is thickened there is a greater distance between alveoli and capillary 
• Diffusion slow and/or impaired
• Leading to hypoxia 
• Thickening may occur due to inflammation, infection or fibrosis

## Page 12

4. Surface Area
• Adult lung = approx. 300million alveoli 
• If opened and spread out, would cover an entire tennis court 
• Gas exchange SA  = 70-80m2 
Clinical relevance: 
• If SA ↓, despite adequate diffusion, sufficient gas exchange may not 
be possible resulting in:  ↑ PCO2 + ↓PO2 + ↓pH 
• Temp. loss of SA : Bronchial obstruction ( mucus plug, tumor), 
actelectasis, consolidation
• Permanent loss of SA – emphysematous bullae (breakdown of alveolar 
walls)

## Page 13

5. Ventilation – Perfusion Coupling 
• V  = ventilation = air which reaches the lungs
• Q = perfusion = blood which reaches the lungs 
Vital to maintain V/Q ratio to achieve adequate gas 
exchange 
If off-balance = V/Q mismatch 
Alveolar dead space = ventilation but no perfusion 
Eg: PE, blood clot, emphysema 
Shunt = perfusion but no ventilation 
Eg: Atelectasis, airway obstruction, pulmonary oedema

OXYGEN TRANSPORT:
O2 does not easily dissolve in water so needs a 
carrier haemoglobin (Hb) to be transported in 
blood
§ 98.5% of blood O2 is bound to haemoglobin in 
RBC
• Small diameter of the capillaries 
surrounding the alveoli - only one red 
blood cell can pass through at a time.
• Slows down the blood cells - gases have 
more time to diffuse through 
• It also forces the red blood cells close to 
the walls of the capillaries, decreasing the 
diffusion distance.

## Page 16

Haemoglobin
§ Haem portion contains 4 atoms of iron, each capable of binding to one O2 
(oxyhaemoglobin HbO2) 
§ When O2 binds - Hb alters shape > from T shape to R shape and is cooperative 
§ Normally Hb is almost completely saturated in room air 96% (FiO2 = 21%)
§ As blood flows through capillaries, haem - oxygen reaction reverses and 
Haemoglobin releases oxygen, which diffuses into interstitial fluid and then 
into cells

## Page 18

O2 - Haemoglobin 
Dissociation Curve:
§ At the lung, oxygen diffuses in to blood 
stream and binds to Hb (High SaO2 and 
PaO2)
§ At the tissue oxygen dissociates from Hb to 
enter the tissue (Low SaO2 and PaO2)
§ Steep section of curve is dissociation - 
results in large change in % saturation 
which allows for large quantities of O2 to be 
dumped in the tissues
§ CO and NO have a higher affinity for Hb 
than O2 so they would shift curve to left - 
causes deoxygenation of tissues with 
normal PO2.
