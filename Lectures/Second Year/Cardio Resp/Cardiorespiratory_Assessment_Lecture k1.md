# Cardiorespiratory Assessment Lecture k1

## Slide 1

- Cardiorespiratory Assessment in Clinical Conditions
- Bronchiectasis, Emphysema, Chronic Bronchitis, Cystic Fibrosis, Surgical Conditions, and Heart Failure
- BSc & MSc Physiotherapy Lecture

## Slide 2

- Learning Outcomes
- By the end of this session, students will be able to:
- • Understand the principles of cardiorespiratory assessment.
- • Apply assessment techniques to specific clinical conditions.
- • Interpret physiological findings and relate to pathology.
- • Identify physiotherapy priorities in complex multimorbidity cases.
- • Use evidence-based frameworks for respiratory assessment.

## Slide 3

- Overview of Cardiorespiratory Assessment
- • Importance of assessment in physiotherapy practice.
- • Integration of subjective and objective findings.
- • Use of frameworks: SOAP, ICF, or hypothesis-oriented reasoning.
- • Link between pathophysiology, symptoms, and function.

## Slide 4

- Subjective Assessment
- • Presenting complaint (breathlessness, cough, sputum).
- • History of presenting condition and past medical history.
- • Functional impact (ADL, exercise tolerance).
- • Medications, smoking history, and psychosocial factors.

## Slide 5

- Objective Assessment
- • Observation: posture, breathing pattern, cyanosis, accessory muscles.
- • Palpation: chest expansion, tactile fremitus, tracheal position.
- • Percussion: resonance, dullness, hyper-resonance.
- • Auscultation: vesicular, crackles, wheeze, diminished breath sounds.

## Slide 6

- Pathophysiology of Bronchiectasis
- Definition: Chronic irreversible dilation of bronchi due to wall destruction.
- Key Mechanism: Infection → Inflammation → Structural Damage → Impaired Clearance → Recurrent Infection.
- Neutrophilic inflammation leads to protease release and mucus hypersecretion.
- Airway remodeling causes thickening, fibrosis, and airflow obstruction.

- Pictorial Depiction of Pathophysiology
- Include diagrams:
- - Normal vs. bronchiectatic airway (mucus plugs, ciliary damage).
- - Vicious cycle: infection–inflammation–damage loop.
- - Cross-section showing airway wall thickening and mucus pooling.

## Slide 8

- Radiological Features (CT Imaging)
- Gold Standard: HRCT.
- Key Signs:
- - Signet ring sign (dilated bronchus > artery).
- - Tram-track sign (parallel lines).
- - Tree-in-bud (small airway impaction).
- Types: Cylindrical, varicose, cystic bronchiectasis.
- Distribution: Upper (CF), lower (aspiration), central (ABPA).

## Slide 9

- Respiratory Assessment
- History: Frequency of infections, sputum, dyspnoea, exercise tolerance.
- Observation: Posture, accessory muscle use, clubbing, cyanosis.
- Inspection: Chest wall movement, scars, hyperinflation.
- Palpation & Percussion: Chest expansion, resonance changes.
- Auscultation: Coarse crackles, rhonchi, wheeze.
- Functional Tests: 6MWT, MRC Dyspnoea, CAT score.

## Slide 10

- Bronchiectasis – Physiotherapy Assessment
- • Assess sputum load, cough effectiveness, infection status.
- • Observe posture, chest expansion, and exercise tolerance.
- • Outcome measures: sputum volume, 6MWT, MRC dyspnoea scale.
- • Prioritise airway clearance and energy conservation education.

## Slide 11

- Emphysema – Pathophysiology & Assessment
- • Alveolar wall destruction → reduced elastic recoil, hyperinflation.
- • Clinical: barrel chest, reduced breath sounds, pursed-lip breathing.
- • Spirometry: ↓FEV₁, ↑TLC, ↓DLCO.
- • Assess dyspnoea, exercise capacity, and secretion management needs.

## Slide 13

- Chronic Bronchitis – Assessment Focus
- • Productive cough for ≥3 months in 2 consecutive years.
- • Clinical: wheeze, cyanosis, peripheral oedema (blue bloater).
- • Objective: coarse crackles, sputum assessment, auscultation findings.
- • Priorities: airway clearance, breathing control, exercise tolerance.

## Slide 14

- Cystic Fibrosis – Overview
- • Autosomal recessive disorder causing thick, sticky mucus production.
- • Multisystem disease: respiratory, GI, reproductive.
- • Assess sputum viscosity, infection frequency, nutritional status.
- • Physiotherapy: airway clearance, exercise prescription, inhalation therapy.

## Slide 15

- Surgical Conditions – Assessment & Risks
- • Post-op risks: atelectasis, infection, secretion retention, pain.
- • Pre-op assessment: smoking status, baseline lung function.
- • Post-op assessment: pain score, cough effectiveness, SpO₂, mobility.
- • Priorities: breathing exercises, early mobilisation, airway clearance.

## Slide 16

- Heart Failure – Pathophysiology & Assessment
- • Pump failure → pulmonary congestion and systemic hypoperfusion.
- • Symptoms: orthopnoea, PND, fatigue, ankle oedema.
- • Signs: crackles, JVP, hepatomegaly, weight gain.
- • Physiotherapy: low-intensity exercise, pacing, education, monitoring.

## Slide 17

- Multimorbidity – Physiotherapy Implications
- • Common overlap: COPD + heart failure, diabetes, obesity.
- • Requires integrated assessment and prioritisation of treatment goals.
- • Red flag awareness: unstable vitals, acute decompensation.
- • Holistic management and MDT collaboration essential.

## Slide 18

- Summary & Integration
- • Systematic cardiorespiratory assessment underpins clinical reasoning.
- • Identify pathophysiological patterns across conditions.
- • Link findings to prioritised physiotherapy goals.
- • Promote patient-centred, evidence-based care.
