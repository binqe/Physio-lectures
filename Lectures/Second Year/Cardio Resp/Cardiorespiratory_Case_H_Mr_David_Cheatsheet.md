I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• Chronic bronchitis results from sustained inhalation of irritants — primarily cigarette smoke — triggering a chronic inflammatory response in the large
central airways.
• This causes hypertrophy and hyperplasia of the submucosal mucous glands, quantified by the Reid Index (ratio of gland layer thickness to total
bronchial wall thickness — normal <0.4; chronic bronchitis >0.4).
• Goblet cell metaplasia also extends distally into smaller airways that do not normally secrete mucus.
• Together, these changes produce dramatically increased volumes of thick, viscid mucus that overwhelm the mucociliary escalator, leading to mucus
pooling, bacterial colonisation, and recurrent lower respiratory tract infections.
• Chronic inflammation, airway wall oedema, and mucus plugging narrow the lumen, producing the obstructive spirometric pattern (reduced FEV1/FVC)
characteristic of COPD.
