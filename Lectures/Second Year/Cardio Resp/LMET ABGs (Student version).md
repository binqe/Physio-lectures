## Slide 3

- Acid-base balance refers to the degree of acidity or alkalinity in the body which is measured by the pH scale
- Measure of H+ concentration
- Normal pH: 7.35-7.45
- Acid-base Relationship

## Slide 4

- Acid-base is controlled by the lungs, the kidneys and their associated buffer systems
- Optimum cell function requires maintenance of well controlled H+ concentration – homeostasis
- Normal metabolism results in the production of acids
- An acid is a proton or hydrogen ion (H+) donor
- A base is a proton or hydrogen ion acceptor

## Slide 5

- Acid-base Relationship
- Bicarbonate buffer system
- Involving the balance between carbonic acid, bicarbonate ion & carbon dioxide to maintain pH in the blood to support metabolic function
- Failure of this buffer system to work correctly will alter the pH of the blood causing acidosis or alkalosis
- What could effect this equation?

## Slide 6

- Normal Values
- pH 7.35-7.45
- Mean 7.4
- pCO2 4.7-6kPa
- pO2 11-14kPa
- HCO3- 22-26mmol
- Mean 25
- BE -2 - +2

## Slide 7

- Interpretation
- Step 1
- Look at the pH
- Is it acidic or is it alkaline?
- pH > 7.45 = alkalotic
- pH < 7.35 = acidotic

## Slide 8

- Interpretation
- Step 2
- Look at the pCO2
- A high pCO2 will cause the pH to become more acidotic whereas a low pCO2 will cause the pH to become more alkalotic
- Is it within normal range?
- If no, does the abnormality match that of the pH i.e. is the pCO2  high and the pH low or is the pH high and the pCO2  low? (Seesaw)
- If this is the case then a primary respiratory problem has been identified
- (4.7-6.0kPa)

## Slide 9

- Interpretation
- Step 3
- Look at HCO3-
- A high HCO3- will cause the pH to become more alkalotic whereas a low HCO3- will cause the pH to become more acidotic
- Is it within normal range?
- If no, does the abnormality match that of  the pH i.e. is the HCO3- high and the pH High or is the pH low and the HCO3-  low? (Elevator)
- If this is the case then a primary Metabolic problem has been identified
- (22-26mmol)

## Slide 10

- Interpretation
- Compensation
- Look at compensating variable
- Is it within its normal range?
- If yes, then it is uncompensated
- If no, it has started to compensate
- Refer back to the pH
- Is it within normal range?
- Yes – full compensation has occurred
- No – partial compensation has occurred

## Slide 11

- Interpretation
- Base excess
- Acidotic:  BE < -2
- Normal: BE -2 to +2
- Alkalotic: BE >+2

## Slide 20

- Respiratory Failure
- The inability to maintain partial pressures in arterial blood of carbon dioxide (pCO2) and oxygen (pO2) within normal physiological limits
- PaO2 < 8kPa
- PaCO2 > 6.5 kPa

## Slide 21

- Type 1 Respiratory Failure
- Decreased pO2
- Normal or low pCO2
- Treated with supplementary oxygen

## Slide 22

- Type 2 Respiratory Failure
- Decreased pO2
- Increased pCO2
- Alveolar hypoventilation
- Can be acute or chronic

## Slide 23

- Acute Type 2 Respiratory Failure
- Decreased pO2
- Increased pCO2
- Normal HCO3-
- Kidneys take 2-3 days to compensate
- Decreased pH –  uncompensated respiratory acidosis

## Slide 24

- Chronic Type 2 Respiratory Failure
- Decreased pO2
- Increased pCO2
- Increased HCO3-
- Kidneys have had time to compensate
- Normal pH –  fully compensated respiratory acidosis

## Slide 25

- Acute on Chronic Type 2 Respiratory Failure
- Decreased pO2
- Increased pCO2
- Increased HCO3-
- pH high
- pCO2 normally high but due to sudden deterioration has risen further so kidneys no longer able to compensate
