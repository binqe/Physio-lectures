## Page 6

Nasal 
Turbinates
• Curved shelves that protrude 
from the lateral nasal walls
• Increase the surface area 
• Responsible for warming and 
humidifying the air as it passes 
through the nasal cavity and 
into the nasopharynx and on 
to the lungs 
• Also play a role in immune 
surveillance and olfaction

## Page 7

Paranasal 
Sinuses 
• Decrease the relative weight of 
the bones of the face 
• Increase the resonance of the 
voice 
• Provide a buffer against blows to 
the face 
• Insulate sensitive structures like 
dental roots and eyes from rapid 
temperature fluctuations in the 
nasal cavity 
• Humidification and heating of 
inhaled air

## Page 9

The Larynx:  
Larynx – cartilaginous structure which helps regulate 
volume of air entering and leaving lungs
• Continuous with trachea inferiorly and pharynx 
superiorly
• 3 large cartilage pieces (thyroid, epiglottis, cricoid)
• ‘Adams apple’ - part of thyroid cartilage
• Epiglottis – flexible piece of elastic cartilage covers 
opening of trachea, to prevent aspiration

## Page 10

The Larynx 
•
Cartilaginous skeleton with ligaments and muscles that move and stabilise the 
surrounding mucus membrane.
•
Formed of 9 cartilages: 
•
Thyroid : protective shield. Largest cartilage and forms the adam’s apple in 
men
•
Cricoid: encircles the trachea completely, made up of the arch (ant) and the 
lamina (post)
•
Epiglottis : leaf-shaped flap, attached to the internal surface of the thyroid 
cartilage 
•
Arytenoid : pair of small, hard but flexible pyramid-shaped cartilages that sit 
over posterior cricoid.
•
Corniculate : pair of small elastic cone shaped cartilages that articulate with 
arytenoid 
•
Cuneiform : two elongated fibrous pieces of yellow cartilage. Support the 
vocal folds and the lateral aspects of the epiglottis

Immune 
response in the 
airways 
• The airway epithelium senses 
bacterial exposure and responds 
• Phagocytes, dendritic cells and

## Page 17

Coughing 
• Important component of lung health 
• Dependent on airway characteristics and integrity of 
neurophysiological pathway 
• 3 phases : 
• Inspiration : Generates the volume necessary for an 
effective cough 
• Compressive : closure of the larynx combined with 
contraction of muscles of chest wall, diaphragm and 
abdominal wall. Rapid rise in intrathoracic pressure  
• Expiratory : The glottis opens, resulting in high 
expiratory airflow. Airway compression occurs. The 
high flow dislodges mucus from the airways
• With rapid coughing intrathoracic pressure could rise as 
high as 300mmHg and expulsion could be as fast as 
800km/hr

## Page 18

Coughing 
• Cough occurs through the stimulation of a reflex 
arc:
• Afferent pathway : nerve fibres located in the 
epithelium of the upper airways travel via 
vagus nerve to the medulla 
• Central pathway ( cough centre) : 
coordinating region for coughing is located in
the upper brain stem and pons 
• Efferent pathway : impulses travel from the 
cough centre via the vagus, phrenic and spinal 
motor nerves to the diaphragm, abdominal 
wall, mm and the larynx.
