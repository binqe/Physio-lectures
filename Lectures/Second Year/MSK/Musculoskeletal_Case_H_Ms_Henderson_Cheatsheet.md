Case H — Ms. Henderson
19F · Competitive Gymnast · 8-month sharp LBP at L5, extension-provoked
Musculoskeletal Physiotherapy
I SUBJECTIVE PROBLEM LIST
• Physical Impairments
• Painful and restricted lumbar extension
• Paraspinal muscle guarding/spasm secondary to pain
• Unable to perform gymnastics skills requiring extension
(walkovers, back handsprings, beam skills)
• Reduced training capacity and skill progression
II RED FLAGS
• Nutritional habits and energy:
• "How is your nutrition — do you feel you have enough fuel for
training?"
• Radicular symptoms:
• "Have you had any pain, numbness, or tingling travelling down
your legs? Any weakness?"
• I RED-S / Female Athlete Triad
• Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport
• Significance:
• Screen with LEAF-Q questionnaire; refer to sports dietitian and
sports physician/endocrinologist; DEXA scan for BMD; notify team
physician. Do not dismiss as simply a training injury.
I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• Spondylolysis is a stress fracture of the pars interarticularis — the isthmus of bone connecting the superior and inferior articular processes of a
vertebra.
• The injury follows a stress fracture continuum: from bone oedema and early cortical stress reaction, through incomplete fracture, to complete bilateral
pars defect.
• If bilateral pars fractures occur, the structural connection between the posterior elements and the vertebral body is severed, which — under continued
loading — can allow the vertebral body to translate anteriorly, producing spondylolisthesis.
• The L5/S1 level is most commonly affected because L5 is the most lordotic segment and bears the greatest extension moment.
• In young female athletes, hypoestrogenism from RED-S impairs osteoblastic bone repair, dramatically increasing susceptibility to and slowing recovery
from stress fractures at this and other sites.
• Sharp pain at L5 on extension: Hyperextension closes the posterior elements, directly compressing and shearing the fractured pars — reproducing
sharp, localised pain at the stress fracture site.

1. Normal lower limb reflexes argue against L5/S1 radiculopathy
