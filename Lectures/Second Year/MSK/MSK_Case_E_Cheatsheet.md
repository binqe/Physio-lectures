I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
• 2 Pathophysiology of Working Diagnosis Working Diagnosis: Thoracic Outlet Syndrome — Neurogenic/Vascular Mixed Type The thoracic outlet is a
narrow triangular space bounded by the anterior and middle scalene muscles, the first rib, and the clavicle.
• The brachial plexus (C5–T1), subclavian artery, and subclavian vein all pass through this space, making any reduction in its dimensions clinically
significant.
• Lee's case, hypertrophy and hypertonicity of the anterior scalene muscle — consistent with her repetitive overhead work — narrows the scalene
triangle, compressing the lower trunk of the brachial plexus (C8/T1) and intermittently compressing the subclavian artery.
• This produces the mixed neurogenic-vascular presentation we see clinically.
• Tightness of pectoralis minor, confirmed on examination, creates a secondary compression point in the subcoracoid space.

I TREATMENT PLAN
I Initial Phase — Weeks 1–4: Decompression, Neural Mobility & Postural Correction
• "Your symptoms are caused by compression of nerves and blood vessels in your shoulder region — this is very manageable with physiotherapy and
postural changes." — Provide a clear, non-threatening explanation to prevent catastrophising and empower self-management.

• "We're going to work on your posture and the tight muscles around your shoulder to take pressure off those structures — you can actively contribute
to your own recovery." — Linking treatment to mechanism builds adherence and buy-in.
• "Please rest your arm immediately if your hand turns blue or pale and does not recover within a few minutes — this is a safety sign that needs medical
attention." — Critical safety education specific to vascular TOS; empowers appropriate help-seeking.
• "At work, try to take regular microbreaks, vary your posture, and avoid sustained overhead positions for more than 2–3 minutes without rest." —
Modifiable occupational exposure is the primary driver — load management is central to recovery.
• VAS reduced to ≤3/10 at rest and during usual work tasks
I Long-Term Plan — Weeks 4–12: Strength, Endurance & Work Rehabilitation
• Serratus anterior and lower trapezius strengthening — upward rotation of the scapula widens the thoracic outlet during overhead activity; these
muscles are essential for dynamic stability and shoulder girdle positioning during Ms. Lee's warehouse tasks.
• Thoracic mobility and postural endurance training — address thoracic kyphosis contributing to chronic outlet compromise; progress to sustained
posture challenge in work-simulated positions.
• Graduated return-to-work planning — collaborate with Ms. Lee and employer on workstation modification (height-adjustable surfaces, task rotation),
progressive exposure to overhead tasks with monitoring of symptoms using the DASH and Roos re-test.
• Cardiovascular fitness and weight management — low-impact aerobic exercise to improve peripheral circulation and reduce adipose tissue load on
the outlet. Coordinate with her endocrinologist to ensure exercise safety with Type 1 Diabetes (glucose monitoring, hypoglycaemia awareness).
• Ongoing glycaemic management education — encourage tight blood glucose control as this directly affects peripheral nerve health and vascular
compliance. Refer to diabetes nurse/dietitian as needed.
