# Community Msk Clinical Simulation for Physiotherapy Students IS

Community MSK Clinical Simulation for Physiotherapy Students
Case Introduction
You are working in a community-based musculoskeletal (MSK) physiotherapy setting. Mr. O, a 51-year-old patient, has been referred for physiotherapy due to worsening lower back pain with recent onset of radiating pain down her right leg. Your objective is to assess Mr. O’s condition, identify impairments, and develop a rehabilitation plan to alleviate his symptoms, restore function, and enhance his quality of life.
Patient Information
Name: Mr O
Age: 51 years
Subjective Assessment
Presenting Complaint: Pain on the right side of the lower back.
History of Presenting Complaint (HPC):
Pain has persisted for over six months.
In the last two weeks, pain has radiated down the right leg; no reports of paraesthesia.
Works as a cleaner, previously full-time (Monday to Friday), but reduced to two houses per week due to pain.
Pain is triggered by lumbar flexion, lifting heavy objects, and prolonged standing.
Has stopped participating in sports, including running, due to pain.
Red Flags: None reported.
Past Medical History (PMH)
High cholesterol
High blood pressure
Drug History (DH)
Statins (for cholesterol management)
Social History (SH)
Lives with a partner and teenage daughter.
Previously active (ran 10km regularly).
Enjoys yoga, but currently limited due to back pain.
Objective Assessment
Observations
No redness, swelling, or oedema noted.
Palpation
Normal temperature.
Tenderness on palpation (TOP) around lumbar spine and sacrum.
Spine Range of Motion (ROM)
Flexion: Reaches mid-thigh level with pain.
Extension: Limited to the top of gluteals.
Side Flexion: Limited to lateral mid-thigh on both sides.
Rotation: Limited.
Special Tests
Slump Test: Positive (indicative of neural tension).
Problem List
Difficulty with bending.
Radiating pain down the right leg.
Sleep disturbances due to discomfort.
Impression/Diagnosis
Likely Diagnosis: Degenerative Disc Disease (DDD) in the lumbar spine, potentially contributing to sciatica, given the positive slump test and pain radiating down the leg.
Notes: No indications of serious red flags like Cauda Equina Syndrome or spinal fractures. Osteoarthritis (OA) unlikely, as it would have been evident on imaging.
Simulation Structure
1. Learning Objectives
Conduct a detailed subjective and objective assessment for lower back pain with suspected sciatica.
Develop an evidence-based treatment plan for pain management and functional restoration.
Educate the patient on ergonomic adjustments and self-management strategies.
Encourage safe return to daily activities and hobbies.
2. Subjective Assessment Task
Instructions: Interview Mr. O to gather relevant information on his condition:
Pain Characteristics: Onset, intensity, aggravating and relieving factors.
Functional Impact: Explore how pain affects his cleaning job, sleep, and physical activities.
Social Context: Understand his family dynamics and any support available.
Sample Questions:
Can you describe how the pain has progressed over the past six months?
What activities are most affected by your back pain?
Are there any particular movements or positions that worsen or relieve your symptoms?
3. Objective Assessment Task
Instructions: Conduct a thorough physical examination:
General Observation: Assess posture and functional movements.
Palpation: Check for tenderness around the lumbar and sacral regions.
Spine Range of Motion (ROM):
Measure lumbar flexion, extension, side flexion, and rotation.
Document any pain or limitations during movement.
Special Tests: Perform a Slump Test to assess for neural tension.
