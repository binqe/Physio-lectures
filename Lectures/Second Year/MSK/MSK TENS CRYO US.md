Acute management of pain
General physiotherapy aims of early phase management (Phase I & II)
PHASE 1: BLEEDING & PHASE 2: INFLAMMATION
The aims are: 
• to reduce pain; 
• to limit and reduce inflammatory exudates; 
• to reduce metabolic demands of tissue; 
• to protect newly damaged tissue from further injury; 
• to protect the newly-forming tissue from disruption; 
• to promote new tissue growth and fibre realignment; 
• to maintain general levels of cardiovascular and musculoskeletal fitness/activity.

What does cryotherapy do?
• Cryotherapy uses cold and cooling agents for therapeutic management. 
• There are various forms, crushed ice, cryocuffs, gel packs, ice 
submersion, cryosprays, whole body cryotherapy (etc). 
• Theoretically cooling the tissues results in vasoconstriction and reduces 
the metabolic rate of tissues. 
• Whilst it is often suggested that there may be faster recovery times from 
acute injury, delayed onset muscle soreness – evidence is inconclusive.
• Cryotherapy has an analgesic effect by reducing the nerve conduction 
velocity (Chesterton 2002; Algafly 2007). 
17

## Page 19

Cryotherapy for recovery?
• Cryotherapy has been considered to reduce the metabolic rate / stress by 
reducing nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate oxidase (NOX) 
and reducing mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) activity). 
• There is no conclusive evidence to support the use of ice post-injury in 
managing inflammation. 
• The primary goal of cryotherapy should be to maintain a reduction in 
intramuscular temperature for as long as possible in the immediate stages 
following both injury and exercise to hinder the proliferation of secondary 
damage.

degrees C, or compression of superficial nerves where an ice pack may be 
placed. 
• The general evidence basis for cryotherapy is mixed.

## Page 33

Introduction
• Ultrasound is a type of sound wave that transmits energy by alternately
compressing and rarefying material.
• Ultrasound is sound with a frequency greater than 20,000 cycles per
second[Hz].
• Humans can hear sound with a frequency of 16
to 20,000Hz, sound
greater than this is known as ultrasound.
• Therapeutic ultrasound has a frequency between 0.5 and 5.0 megahertz 
(MHz) (1-3 MHz) to maximize energy absorption at a depth of 3 to 5 cm of
soft tissue.

Attenuation of Ultrasound
Attenuation is a gradual reduction 
in the intensity of ultrasonic 
beams once it has left the 
treatment head.
Factors that contribute to 
attenuation –
• Absorption – ultrasound is 
absorbed and converted to 
heat within tissues as a 
thermal effect. 
• Scatter – the cylindrical 
ultrasonic beam is deflected 
from its path by reflection at 
interfaces, bubbles, or 
particles in its path.
Therefore the US beam intensity 
decreases with depth.

Dosage in Chronic Conditions
• Chronic condition may be treated with pulsed  or 
continuous mode.
• The maximum intensity of ultrasound which  should 
be used is that which produces mildly  perceptible 
warmth.
• This usually occurs around 2 W/cm2. Initially  low 
dose is tried. Intensity 0.8 W/cm2, time 4  minutes.
• Maximum dose of ultrasound: 2 Watts/cm2  for 8
minutes.

Non – Thermal Effects
• CAVITATION –isThe vibrational effect on gas bubbles by an US beam.
The oscillatory activity of highly compressible bodies within the tissues such as
gas or vapour-filled voids.
-
Stable Cavitation - Stable cavitation occurs when bubbles oscillate to and
fro within the ultrasonic pressure waves but remain intact.
-
Microstreaming is the unidirectional movement of fluids along the
boundaries of the cell. Due to microstreaming, the permeability of the cell 
membrane and the direction of movement of molecules into the cells is
influenced.
-
Unstable/Transient Cavitation - This occurs when the volume of the
bubbles changes rapidly and then collapses.

## Page 48

Evidence for US
More evidence is required to support the use of ultrasound, there 
are some studies which suggest a potential benefit – but lack 
strength when compared to placebo effects. 
If Ultrasound is to be used, it should be as an adjunct to other 
therapy, and relevance of tissue depth should also be considered. 
Future research may be required with improvements in US 
technology.
