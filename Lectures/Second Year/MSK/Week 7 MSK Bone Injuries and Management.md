## Page 5

What is a fracture
A fracture is a broken bone. It can range from a thin crack to a 
complete break. Bone can fracture crosswise, lengthwise, in several 
places, or into many pieces. Most fractures happen when a bone is 
impacted by more force or pressure than it can support.
A break in the continuity of the bone, when the capacity of the bone to 
absorb force is exceeded.

salts.
6. Blood formation. Red bone marrow is the major producer of blood 
cells, including cells of the immune system.

## Page 22

Direct healing does not commonly occur in the natural process of 
fracture healing. 
Requires anatomical reduction of the fracture ends, without any gap 
formation, and a stable fixation. 
This is the primary goal to achieve after open reduction and internal 
fixation surgery. 
When these requirements are achieved, direct bone healing can occur 
by direct remodelling of lamellar bone, the Haversian canals and blood 
vessels. 
Usually takes from a few months to a few years, before complete 
healing is achieved.

## Page 23

Primary healing of fractures can either occur through contact healing 
or gap healing. 
Both attempt to directly re-establish an anatomically correct and 
biomechanically competent lamellar bone structure. 
Direct bone healing can only occur when an anatomic restoration of 
the fracture fragments is achieved with rigid fixation. 
Bone on one side of the cortex must unite with bone on the other side 
of the cortex to re-establish mechanical continuity.

## Page 24

Cutting cones are formed at the ends of the osteons closest to the 
fracture site. The cones consist of osteoclasts which cross the fracture 
line, generating longitudinal cavities at a rate of 50–100 μm/day. 
These cavities are later filled by bone produced by osteoblasts residing 
at the rear of the cutting cone. 
This results in the simultaneous generation of a bony union and the 
restoration of Haversian systems formed in an axial direction. 
Re-established Haversian systems allow for penetration of blood vessels 
carrying osteoblastic precursors.15, 21
The bridging osteons later mature by direct remodelling into lamellar 
bone resulting in fracture healing without the formation of periosteal 
callus.
https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3105171/
