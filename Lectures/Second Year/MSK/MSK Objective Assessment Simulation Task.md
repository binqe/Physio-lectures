# MSK Objective Assessment Simulation Task

MSK Objective Assessment Simulation Task

| Objective Assessment
1. Observation:
Posture: Check for any abnormal postures, asymmetry, or muscle atrophy.
Guarding: Observe if the patient is protecting the affected shoulder or limiting movement.
Gait: Assess the patient’s walking pattern for any abnormal movements or compensation due to shoulder pain.
2. Palpation:
Palpate the shoulder region for tenderness, swelling, or abnormalities around:
Bony Landmarks (e.g., clavicle, acromion, scapula)
Soft Tissues (e.g., rotator cuff muscles, deltoid, biceps tendon)
Note any pain or discomfort during palpation.
3. Range of Motion (ROM):
Active ROM: Ask the patient to perform the following movements while observing the quality and range of motion:
Flexion (lifting the arm forward)
Abduction (lifting the arm sideways)
Internal and External Rotation (rotating the arm inward and outward)
Extension (moving the arm backward)
Passive ROM: Perform the same movements for the patient to assess if the range of motion improves or pain persists with passive motion.
4. Strength Testing:
Test muscle strength using the Oxford grading scale (0-5) for the following shoulder movements:
Flexion
Abduction
External Rotation
Internal Rotation
Extension
Test the patient's grip strength to assess overall upper limb strength and detect any discrepancies.
5. Special Tests: Perform relevant special tests to identify potential pathologies:
Neer’s Test: For shoulder impingement
Hawkins-Kennedy Test: For shoulder impingement
Empty Can Test: To assess the integrity of the supraspinatus tendon (part of the rotator cuff)
Speed’s Test: For biceps tendon pathology
Upper Limb Tension Test (ULTT): To assess for nerve involvement, especially if paraesthesia is reported.
Spurling's Test: To rule out cervical radiculopathy (given the "strange feeling" in the middle two fingers).
6. Neurological Testing:
Sensory Testing: Assess light touch and pinprick sensation in the affected upper limb, especially focusing on the dermatomes of C5, C6, and C7.
Reflex Testing: Test biceps and triceps reflexes to identify any abnormalities.
Upper Limb Proprioception: Assess the patient’s sense of joint position and movement to check for potential proprioceptive deficits.
7. Functional Testing:
Overhead Reach: Ask the patient to raise their arms above their head to recreate the painful movement.
Hand-to-Neck and Hand-to-Back Test: To assess functional limitations in the shoulder joint. |
| Post-Assessment Questions for Reflection:
Based on the patient’s ROM and strength testing, which specific muscle group or structure do you suspect to be involved?
Did any special tests indicate a clear pathology, such as impingement, rotator cuff tear, or nerve involvement?
How would you differentiate between a musculoskeletal shoulder problem and a potential neurological cause based on your findings? |
