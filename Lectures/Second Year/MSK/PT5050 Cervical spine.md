## Slide 5

- Cervical vertebrae
- C3-7 classic vertebrae
- Body is small, oval shaped and short in height
- Large triangular vertebral foramen
- Short pedicle project laterally
- Long narrow laminae

## Slide 6

- Cervical vertebrae
- Short bifid spinous process, muscles attach to nuchal ligament
- C6-7 long SP
- C7 rare to be bifid
- Short transverse processes which house foramen transversarium

## Slide 7

- Cervical facet joints
- Facet joints lie at approx. 45° angle
- Superior facet faces superiorly and posteriorly
- Inferior facet faces inferiorly and anteriorly
- Allows for all movements

## Slide 8

- Atlas (C1)
- Articulates with Occiput (atlanto-occipital joint) and with the Axis (atlantoaxial joint)
- No body or spinous process
- Large, wide transverse processes
- Superior facets articulate with occiput
- Anterior arch which articulates with the odontoid process (dens) of C2
- Anterior tubercle origin of anterior longitudinal ligament

## Slide 9

- Axis (C2)
- Odontoid process (dens) protrudes superiorly and articulates with C1
- Bifid spinous process
- Superior facets support the weight of head allowing dens to articulate freely with Atlas
- Strongest cervical vertebra
- Superior facets face up and slightly laterally

## Slide 10

- Atlanto-occipital joint
- Articulation of superior facets of C1 and occipital condyle
- Flexion and extension (nodding)
- Lateral flexion
- Very minor rotation from occiput ‘slipping’

## Slide 11

- Atlanto-axial joint
- Lateral and median joints
- Lateral atlanto-axial joint
- Articulation of inferior facets of C1 and superior facets of C2
- Median atlanto-axial joint
- Articulation of anterior surface of dens with the posterior surface of the anterior arch of C1
- Posterior surface of dens with the transverse ligament of C1

## Slide 12

- Atlanto-axial joint
- Complex movements due to lateral and medial joints moving together
- Main movement rotation (shaking head to say “no”)
- Atlas spins on its axis
- The head and C1 move as a unit on C2

## Slide 14

- Sternocleidomastoid
- Origin – Manubrium of sternum (sternal head), medial 1/3 of clavicle (clavicular head)
- Insertion – Mastoid process, superior nuchal line of occiput
- Action – Lateral flexion and (contralateral) rotation (unilateral), flexion of neck and extension of head (bilateral)
- Innervation – Spinal accessory nerve C1-5.

## Slide 15

- Splenius Capitis
- Origin – Lower ligamentum nuchae, C7-T3 spinous processes
- Insertion – Mastoid process, superior nuchal line of occiput
- Action – Head extension (bilateral), rotation and lateral flexion (ipsilateral)
- Innervation – Posterior rami of C2-3

## Slide 31

- Axillary Nerve
- Roots C5-6
- Innervates Teres minor and Deltoid
- 3% to 6% of all brachial plexus pathologies
- Usually injured from compressive forces with anterior should dislocation

## Slide 32

- Musculocutaneous Nerve
- Roots C5 – 7
- Innervates muscles at front of arm and skin of lateral forearm
- Innervates Biceps Brachii, Brachialis and Corocobrachialis

## Slide 33

- Vertebrobasilar System
- Vertebrobasilar artery mainly supplies the hindbrain which is responsible for balance, communication, co-ordination
- Can be restricted due to too much movement (rotation) – vertebrobasilar insufficiency

## Slide 34

- Vertebrobasilar System
- Composed of Subclavian Artery, Vertebral Artery and Basilar Artery
- Vertebral artery runs through foreman transversarium of Cx and then meets the contralateral VA to form the Basilar Artery
- VA kinks between C2 and C1

## Slide 40

- Whiplash - Pathophysiology
- Sudden hyperextension and hyperflexion of the neck
- Overstretching and damage to cervical muscles and ligaments, joint capsule strain, annular tears, compression/traction of nerve roots and nociceptor irritation.
- Release of cytokines during inflammatory response resulting in pain sensitisation.
- Increased muscle tone/activity of superficial muscles and inhibition of deep neck flexors
- Central sensitisation

## Slide 41

- Whiplash – Clinical presentation
- Symptoms often appear within 24-48 hours
- Neck pain and stiffness
- Reduced ROM
- Headaches
- Dizziness or light-headedness
- Parathesia in upper limbs
- Muscle spasm
- Visual disturbance – rare
- Psychological effects

## Slide 42

- Whiplash - Grading
- Quebec Task Force Classifications
- Grade 0 – No complaint and no physical signs
- Grade 1 – Complaint of neck (stiffness or tenderness) and no physical signs
- Grade 2 – Neck complaint and MSK signs (ROM)
- Grade 3 – Neck complaint and neurological signs
- Grade 4 – Neck complaint and # or dislocation

## Slide 43

- Whiplash – Physiotherapeutic intervention
- Early mobilisation and education
- Avoid prolonged immobilisation
- Address psychological factors – fear avoidance
- Strengthening of deep flexors

## Slide 44

- Cervical Radiculopathy
- Cervical nerve root compression or irritation
- Disc herniation
- Spinal stenosis
- Other degenerative changes
- Tumours, infection and trauma
- Direct pressure causing impaired conduction
- Chemical irritation from inflammatory mediators (cytokines, prostaglandins)

## Slide 45

- Cervical Radiculopathy
- Unilateral neck pain
- Pain radiating down arm – “sharp, shooting, lancing”
- Often accompanied by paresthesia, numbness or weakness in the corresponding dermatome/myotome.
- Reflex changes may be present
- Biceps C5
- Brachioradialis C6
- Triceps C7
- Pain aggravated by neck movements (especially extension and rotation/lateral flexion to the affected side)

## Slide 46

- Degenerative Disc Disease - Pathophysiology
- Dehydration – loss of proteoglycans causing reduced height
- Decreased function and osteophyte formations
- Tears in annulus fibrosis leading to disc bulges and herniations
- Increased load on facet joints
- Ligament thickening – ligamentum flavum
- Spinal stenosis
- Degenerative tissues release chemical
- mediators causing sensitisation of nociceptors

## Slide 47

- Degenerative Disc Disease – Clinical Presentation
- Pain and stiffness with a gradual, insidious onset
- Aggravated from prolonged activity/posture
- Reduced ROM, often extension and rotation
- Radicular symptoms with nerve root involvement
- Muscle spasms, headaches

## Slide 48

- Summary
- Cervical vertebrae are smaller than Tx and Lx due to being less weightbearing
- C1-occipital, Atlanto-occipital joint (flexion/extension)
- C2-C1, Atlantoaxial joint (rotation)
- Cervical nerve roots
- Vertebrobasilar system passes through the cervical spine
- Be aware of VBI
