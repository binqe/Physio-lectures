## Slide 7

- Humero-ulnar joint and Humero-radial joint
- Classification: synovial hinge joint - uni-axial
- Movements available: flexion / extension
- Plane of movement: sagittal
- Axis of movement : medial-lateral
- Humero-ulnar joint articulation:
- trochlea of humerus & trochlea notch of ulna
- Humero-radial joint articulation:
- capitulum of humerus and head of radius
- Radial head

## Slide 8

- Superior (proximal) radio-ulnar joint
- Classification: synovial pivot joint - uni-axial
- Movements available: pronation / supination
- Plane of movement: transverse
- Axis of movement : superior-inferior
- formed by the head of the radius and the radial notch (trochlea notch) of the ulna
- -    enclosed within the elbow joint capsule
- Annular ligament encircles head of radius.
- Strong fibrous ring that surrounds the
- outside of radial head to prevent dislocation

## Slide 9

- Stability: Ligaments
- Lateral (radial) Collateral Ligament
- - on lateral side
- - strong band from lateral epicondyle to annular ligament
- Role: resists varus force applied to joint
- (forced adduction of forearm)
- Medial (ulnar) Collateral Ligament
- - on medial side
- - 3 bands: anterior, posterior, intermediate (transverse)
- - fan / triangular shaped arrangement
- Role: resists valgus force applied to joint
- (forced abduction of forearm)

## Slide 15

- Function: The Carrying Angle
- Carrying angle is an acute angle formed between the arm and forearm when in the anatomical position
- Joint between humerus & radius/ulna form an angle usually between  5-15°
- Male / female difference - why?
- - same angle before puberty
- Evolutionary reasons:
- - arms clear the hips when walking
- - carrying a child

## Slide 16

- Common Elbow Injuries
- Lateral Epicondylitis (Tennis Elbow)
- inflammation of the origin of the extensor carpi radialis brevis on the lateral epicondyle
- overuse injury from wrist extension
- Medial Epicondylitis (Golfer’s Elbow)
- tendinosis of the medial common flexor tendons at the common attachment site (enthesis)
- overuse or repetitive injury from flexion and pronation

## Slide 17

- Innervation - Brachial Plexus
- (relates also to shoulder)
- The brachial plexus is a bundle of nerves that stems from nerve roots in the cervical and upper thoracic sections of the spinal cord (C5-T1), creating a network that connects to the nerves in the arm.
- Responsible for cutaneous and muscular innervation of the entire upper limb.
- Medial cord, lateral cord, posterior cord.

## Slide 18

- Median Nerve
- Roots: C6 - T1
- (from the medial and lateral cords of the brachial plexus)
- Motor Functions: innervates most of the muscles in the anterior forearm, and some intrinsic hand muscles
- Sensory Functions: comes off the palmar cutaneous branch, which innervates the lateral aspect of the palm, and the digital cutaneous branch, which innervates the lateral three and a half fingers on the anterior (palmar) surface of the hand

## Slide 19

- Ulnar Nerve
- Roots: C8 - T1
- (from the medial cord of the brachial plexus)
- Motor Functions: innervates the muscles of the hand, flexor carpi ulnaris and medial half of flexor digitorum profundus
- Sensory Functions: innervates the anterior and posterior surfaces of the medial one and half fingers, and associated palm area

## Slide 20

- Radial Nerve
- Roots: C5 - T1
- (from the posterior cord of the brachial plexus)
- Motor Functions: innervates the triceps brachii, and the extensor muscles in the posterior compartment of the forearm
- Sensory Functions: innervates the posterior aspect of the arm and forearm, and the posterior, lateral aspect of the hand
