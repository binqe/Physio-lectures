## Slide 11

- Nucleus pulposus
- Central core of the intervertebral disc mainly type II collagen – accepts compressive forces
- Gelatinous in nature
- Avascular
- Tends to be situated more posteriorly
- Act as a fulcrum during movement
- Transmits compressive loads from one vertebrae to another
- Herniation can occur when there is degeneration of the annulus

## Slide 12

- Annulus fibrosis
- Consists of concentric rings of predominantly type I collagen tissue (strong!)
- Bound together by proteoglycan gel and elastic fibres. Proteoglycans attract water which give discs their cushion effect (shock absorbers)
- Composed of layers (lamella)
- Outer 1/3 highly innervated with receptors

## Slide 13

- Vertebral end plate
- Continuation of annulus fibrosis to transition to vertebral body
- Serve as the interface between rigid vertebral bodies and pliant intervertebral disc
- Discs don’t have a dedicated blood supply - end plates must balance conflicting requirements of being strong to prevent vertebral fracture and porous to facilitate transport between disc cells
- Horizontal collagen fibre orientation

## Slide 15

- Lumbar discs
- Approximately cylindrical in shape
- Each lamella thicker anteriorly than posteriorly therefore disc thinner posteriorly
- Collagen in posterior fibres are more closely packed therefore stronger
- Wedge shape of the disc contributes to the formation of lordosis
- Able to withstand flexion pressure but fatigue predisposes – Rowing up to 53% of injuries are disc related (Thornton et al., 2016)

## Slide 25

- Pelvic girdle anatomy
- A ring of bones that attach the lower limbs to the axial skeleton i.e. the vertebral column, via the sacrum
- The pelvis  made up of two innominate bones which are large, flattened and irregularly shaped fusions of three bones; ilium, ischium, pubis
- At birth, these bones are connected by cartilage at the acetabulum and only fuse into one bone around the age of 16-18 years
- Anteriorly, the pubic bones articulate with one another
- Posteriorly, the ilium articulates with the sacrum

## Slide 30

- Pelvic girdle functions
- Supports Lx
- Connects spine and pelvis
- Transmits forces from spine into lower limbs (and vice versa)
- Serves as attachment sites for muscles of the trunk and lower limb
- Contains and protects reproductive organs
- In females, it provides bony support for the birth canal

## Slide 32

- Functional anatomy
- Made up of Sacrum and Illium
- Capsule and anterior sacroiliac ligament situated anteriorly
- Posterior is largely fibrous ligaments (posterior sacroiliac ligament and interosseous ligament)
- Very stable so small ROM – 2-3mm (Goode et al., 2008)
- Sacrum not mobilised by muscles - All surrounding muscles stabilise
