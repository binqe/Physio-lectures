# Outpatient MSK Workbook

Outpatient MSK Clinical Simulation Workbook for Physiotherapy Students
Learning Objectives
Assess and Analyse Post-Surgical Complications
Develop Individualized Rehabilitation Plans
Demonstrate Clinical Reasoning in Gait Analysis
Apply Evidence-Based Strategies for Neuro-Musculoskeletal Rehabilitation
Clinical Reasoning Questions
In your groups, use the Case study provided and answer the following questions:
Identify any new terminology in the case and research their meaning in your group.
Create a problem list presented by the patient and classify them into impairment, disability, participation restrictions
Identify contextual factors associated with the patient presented in the case.
Consider some issues that Physiotherapists may focus on with the patient.
Provide a clinical reasoning for the interventions mapped out in the treatment plan of the case.
Match the interventions to the problems highlighted in your problem list.
The patient has been provided with 2 gutter crutches. What are the potential benefits and drawbacks of using these, and how would you assess whether they are the best option for her mobility?

| Patient Profile
Name: Anonymised
Age: 66
Sex: Female
Referral: Left Posterior Tibialis Tendon Transfer to Tibialis Anterior, Extensor Digitorum Longus (EDL), and Extensor Hallucis Longus (EHL).
Relevant Medical History: Left total hip replacement (THR) resulting in sciatic nerve injury and foot drop 1 year ago.

Presenting Complaint:
The patient is currently 6 weeks post-op following a tendon transfer procedure. She arrived in a wheelchair, with the Aircast removed on the day of assessment.
Reports coping well overall and attempts to move her toes while in the cast.
Complains of heel pain upon weight-bearing (WB).
Unable to use elbow crutches due to weak wrists, so she uses a scooter for mobility at home.
Reports persistent numbness along the common peroneal nerve (CPN) pathway from the fibula head to the anterolateral leg and dorsum of the foot.
Experiencing post-operative pain in the affected area.

Objective Examination:
Atrophy noted in the Left Tibialis Anterior (TA).
Tinel’s sign positive at the fibula head.
Left Ankle Active Range of Motion (AROM):
Dorsiflexion (DF): To neutral; an additional 5° possible with passive range (PROM), but stiff at end of range (EOR).
Plantarflexion (PF): Full available range of motion (FAROM).
Eversion (EV): FAROM.
Inversion (INV): 1/4 range; increased to 1/2 range with slight shooting pain along the lateral leg.
Left Ankle Muscle Strength:
Dorsiflexion (DF): 3+/5 within available range.
Extensor Digitorum Longus (EDL): 4-/5, functionally adequate due to tendon transfer.
Extensor Hallucis Longus (EHL): 3-/5, functionally adequate due to tendon transfer.
Plantarflexion (PF): 4/5.
Eversion (EV): 4/5.
Inversion (INV): 3+/5 within available range.
Gait Analysis:
Antalgic gait pattern observed between parallel bars:
Reduced weight-bearing on the left side.
Reduced heel strike and push-off on the left side.

Treatment Plan:
Education:
Explanation of surgical procedure and expected recovery timeline.
Instructions on scar massage to prevent stiffness and adhesions.
Precautions to avoid overstretching into plantarflexion (e.g., avoid kneeling or praying positions).
Home Exercise Program (HEP):
Active Assisted Range of Motion (AAROM): Dorsiflexion exercises using a belt to enhance mind-to-muscle connection.
Standing calf stretch: 3 sets of 30 seconds.
Standing weight shifts to improve balance and proprioception.
Gait re-education: Heel-toe walking practice between parallel bars.
Mobility Aid:
Provided with 2 gutter crutches for daily ambulation.

Assessment (A)
The patient is a 66-year-old female, currently 6 weeks post tendon transfer of the posterior tibialis to the Tibialis Anterior, EDL, and EHL, following complications from a previous left hip replacement that resulted in a sciatic nerve injury and foot drop.
Current Status:
Can actively dorsiflex the ankle to neutral.
Able to walk comfortably with 2 gutter crutches.
Rehabilitation Goals:
Encourage mind-to-muscle connection to regain active dorsiflexion.
Gradually improve strength and function of the Tibialis Anterior.

Plan (P)
Patient advised to self-refer for follow-up appointments as needed and was given contact details to book future sessions.
Considerations for Next Session:
Assess the need for Boxia (ankle-foot orthosis) to support dorsiflexion if required. |
