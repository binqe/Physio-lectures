## Page 7

Atlas (C1)
• Articulates with Occiput (atlanto-occipital 
joint) and with the Axis (atlantoaxial joint)
• No body or spinous process
• Large, wide transverse processes
• Superior facets articulate with occiput
• Anterior arch which articulates with the 
odontoid process (dens) of C2
• Anterior tubercle origin of anterior 
longitudinal ligament

## Page 8

Axis (C2)
• Odontoid process (dens) protrudes 
superiorly and articulates with C1
• Bifid spinous process
• Superior facets support the weight of head 
allowing dens to articulate freely with Atlas
• Strongest cervical vertebra
• Superior facets face up and slightly laterally

## Page 9

Atlanto-occipital joint
• Articulation of superior facets of C1 and 
occipital condyle
• Flexion and extension (nodding)
• Lateral flexion
• Very minor rotation from occiput ‘slipping’

## Page 10

Atlanto-axial joint
• Lateral and median joints
• Lateral atlanto-axial joint
• Articulation of inferior facets of C1 and 
superior facets of C2
• Median atlanto-axial joint
• Articulation of anterior surface of dens 
with the posterior surface of the 
anterior arch of C1
• Posterior surface of dens with the 
transverse ligament of C1

## Page 11

Atlanto-axial joint
• Complex movements due to lateral and 
medial joints moving together
• Main movement rotation (shaking 
head to say “no”)
• Atlas spins on it’s axis
• The head and C1 move as a unit on C2

## Page 13

Nuchal Ligament
Fibroelastic tissue extending
from:
• External Occipital Protuberance to
• Posterior Border of Foramen Magnum to
• Spinous Processes of Cervical Vertebrae
Function:
• Restricts Hyperflexion: It acts as a powerful 
mechanical restraint that limits forward bending of 
the neck protecting the cervical spine from excessive 
range of motion.
• Passive Postural Support: It supports the weight of 
the head and provides a vital attachment point for 
major muscles, allowing for upright stability with 
minimal active muscular effort.
Image from: Moore 8th Ed Fig 2.18

Trapezius
Superficial Posterior Thoracoappendicular Muscle
Innervation: Spinal Accessory Nerve (CN XI)
Action: 
• Upper Fibers: Scapula Elevation & Upward Rotation
• Middle Fibers: Scapula Retraction
• Lower Fibers: Scapula Depression & Upward

roles that drive the postural imbalance: Upper Trapezius Hyperactivity 
& Lower/Middle Trapezius Inhibition

## Page 16

Levator Scapulae
Origin: Transverse processes of C1-C2 vertebrae, 
posterior tubercles of transverse processes of C3-
C4 vertebrae.
Insertion: Superior angle of scapula, medial 
border of scapula superior to spine of scapula.
Action: Elevates and downwardly rotates pectoral 
(shoulder) girdle at acromioclavicular and 
sternoclavicular joints; extends and laterally 
flexes neck at cervical vertebral joints.
** Clinical Pearl**
In the context of Upper Crossed Syndrome, the 
levator scapulae is a primary culprit in neck and 
shoulder dysfunction
Image from: Complete Anatomy

## Page 22

Sternocleidomastoid
Broad, strap-like muscle that has two heads, 
Sternal and Clavicular.
Origin – Manubrium of sternum (sternal head), 
medial 1/3 of clavicle (clavicular head)
Insertion – Mastoid process, superior nuchal line 
of occiput
Action –
• Unilateral: Head Lateral Flexion with Contralateral 
Rotation
• Bilateral: Head Extension, Neck Flexion
**Clinical Pearl**
Trigger points in the SCM can cause tinnitus, 
dizziness, and blurred vision without any primary ear 
or eye pathology, as its tension disrupts 
proprioceptive input to the vestibular system.

• Vertebrobasilar artery mainly supplies

• Composed of Subclavian Artery, 
Vertebral Artery and Basilar Artery
• Vertebral artery runs through foreman 
transversarium of Cx and then meets 
the contralateral VA to form the Basilar 
Artery
• VA kinks between C2 and C1

## Page 35

Nerve roots
• A dermatome is a specific area of skin 
supplied by the sensory fibers of a single 
spinal nerve root, whereas a myotome is a 
group of muscles innervated by the motor 
fibers of that same spinal nerve root.
• Testing these allows a clinician to localize the 
level of a nerve root lesion; for example, 
diminished sensation at the lateral thumb 
(C6 dermatome) paired with weak wrist 
extension (C6 myotome) strongly suggests 
a compromise at the C6 spinal level.

## Page 50

Summary
• Cervical vertebrae are smaller than Tx and Lx due to being less 
weightbearing
• C1-occipital, Atlanto-occipital joint (flexion/extension)
• C2-C1, Atlantoaxial joint (rotation)
• Cervical nerve roots
• Vertebrobasilar system passes through the cervical spine
• Be aware of VBI
