# MSK Cases For Students

Musculoskeletal Cases
Case A:
Mrs. Gupta is a 50-year-old graphic designer presenting with a 1-year history of persistent, dull neck pain and stiffness that worsens after long hours at her workstation. She describes the pain as a "heavy" sensation in the base of her skull and tops of her shoulders. She denies any "electric shocks," numbness, or clumsiness in her hands. Symptoms typically improve with movement or when she takes a break from her computer.
Medical History: Osteoporosis diagnosed 2 years ago.
Examination: Visible protracted head posture and "rounded" shoulders. Palpable tenderness and "tightness" in the upper trapezius and levator scapulae muscles. Full cervical range of motion, though end-range extension is stiff. Negative Spurling’s test; normal upper and lower limb reflexes; negative Hoffmann’s sign.
Case B:
Mr. O’Shea is a 72-year-old retired librarian with a 10-month history of mid-back pain and an increasing "stoop" in his posture. Pain is a localized ache (VAS 4/10) over the mid-thoracic region, worse with prolonged standing. He feels he is "shrinking" and getting shorter.
Medical History: Long-term corticosteroid use for asthma; Osteoporosis.
Examination: Increased thoracic kyphosis; localized tenderness at T7-T8. Pain on percussion over the spinous processes; negative neurological screen for lower limbs.
Case C:
Ms. Taylor is a 32-year-old yoga instructor, 6 months postpartum, with persistent right-sided low back and buttock pain. Pain (VAS 6/10) occurs when rolling in bed, getting out of a car, or during single-leg yoga poses.
Medical History: Unremarkable.
Examination: Tenderness over the PSIS. Normal lumbar range of motion. Positive Thigh Thrust and Sacral Thrust test. Negative SLR and negative hip FADDIR.
Case D:
Mr. Rossi is a 45-year-old amateur marathon runner complaining of deep buttock pain and a "tight hamstrings" feeling on the left side for 3 months after a recent increase in mileage. The pain is localized to the buttock but occasionally feels like a "cramp" traveling to the knee. Worse with sitting on hard chairs and during the initial phase of running.
Medical History: No significant.
Findings: Tenderness deep to the gluteus maximus; positive FADIR test; pain on resisted hip abduction in a seated position. Negative lumbar spine clearance.
Case E:
Ms. Lee is a 29-year-old heavy-set warehouse worker who reports heaviness, numbness, and "pins and needles" in her entire right arm, especially when lifting boxes above shoulder height. Symptoms (VAS 5/10) are worse at the end of a shift. She sometimes notices her hand looks slightly blue or pale when the pain is at its peak.
Medical History: Type 1 Diabetes since age 8
Findings: Positive Roos test (East test) within 60 seconds; positive Adson’s maneuver with diminished radial pulse. Tightness in pectoralis minor and scalene muscles.
Case F:
Mr. Walters is a 65-year-old retired truck driver with a 15-year history of intermittent low back pain that has become constant over the last 2 years. Pain is a dull, heavy ache (VAS 4/10) that feels "stiff" all the time. He has stopped walking his dog because he is terrified his back will "give out." He expresses fear that his spine is "crumbling" and avoids all lifting.
Medical History: Obesity (BMI 34).
Examination: High levels of kinesiophobia (fear of movement). Poor activation of core muscle. Generalized lumbar stiffness. Negative Straight leg raise. No specific radiographic findings provided.

### Case G:
Mr. Khan is a 28-year-old software engineer presenting with a 2-year history of worsening low back and buttock pain that feels "deep and throbbing."  Pain is worst in the early morning, causing him to wake at 4:00 AM due to stiffness. Stiffness lasts over an hour but improves significantly after he moves or showers.
Medical History: History of uveitis (eye inflammation).
Findings: Decreased lumbar forward flexion range; bilateral tenderness over the SI joints; Chest expansion measured at 2 cm.

### Case H:
Ms. Henderson is a 19-year-old competitive gymnast presenting with an 8-month history of localized low back pain. The pain is sharp and localized to the L5 level. It is significantly aggravated by movements involving extension, such as her walkover routines. Resting generally eases the discomfort.
Medical History: Unremarkable.
Examination: Painful and limited lumbar extension range; tenderness on palpation of the L5 spinous process; normal lower limb reflexes; negative straight leg raise
MSK OSCE Questions:
Perform a focused subjective assessment by identifying 3–5 key red flag symptoms and explaining their clinical significance for this patient. Develop a prioritized problem list that distinguishes between the patient's physical impairments and their functional activity limitations.
State your most likely clinical diagnosis and provide two plausible differential diagnoses based on the case history. Briefly explain the specific clinical reasoning or findings you would use to differentiate between these three possibilities.
Demonstrate two to three objective assessment techniques to evaluate a primary problem and explain the clinical meaning of a positive finding.
Discuss your proposed initial and long-term treatment and management plan including patient education, intervention (manual therapy or therapeutic exercise) and objective criteria you would use to progress the treatment. Your examiner will ask you to demonstrate part of your intervention.
