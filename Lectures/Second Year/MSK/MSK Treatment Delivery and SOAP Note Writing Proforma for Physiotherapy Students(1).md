| A 43-year-old man who works as a police officer presented in the clinic
with a two-week history of right shoulder pain. The patient explained that his pain started when he woke up one morning. He had also noticed, “a strange feeling” in his middle two fingers that came and went with no pattern. He did not recall straining his shoulder and had not undertaken any strenuous lifting. He had no other health complaints and was a keen runner. His pain was worse at night.
His pain was brought on when he raised his arms above his head and eased as soon as he stopped the painful movement. Daily pattern – No pattern, the problem was dependent on the activity. Past medical history – Nothing significant. Medication – Not taking any at present Social History – Lives at home with his wife and two children.
On examination, he shows forward shoulder posture with protraction of the right scapula. Active and passive shoulder abduction is reduced to 120 degrees (normal 180 degrees), with pain between 60 and 120 degrees (positive painful arc). External rotation is limited to 45 degrees (normal 90 degrees) with pain at the end range, while internal rotation is full and pain-free. Muscle strength testing reveals mild weakness (4/5) in the right shoulder’s external rotators and supraspinatus. Other shoulder girdle muscles, including the deltoid and biceps, are normal in strength. 
Special testing revealed  negative Spurling test bilateral, positive Hawkins-Kennedy test, along with tenderness over the supraspinatus tendon and biceps long head. Neurological examination, including reflexes, sensation, and grip strength, is normal in both arms.
You are seeing them in your FU 3 weeks after you initial consultation, you have provided the patient with active and active assisted range of motion exercise for the neck and shoulder, the patient reported he is able to do them well. Upon review you noticed some movements were faulty with multiple compensation. 
You also provided isometric exercises for shoulder abduction at 30 degrees abduction, patient has performed them well and feel are becoming two easy to perform.

SOAP Note for ongoing Treatment
Provide standardized documentation of the patient’s ongoing progress and treatment in a clinical setting. Below is a breakdown of what to include in each section based on the patient’s case.

S – Subjective:
Include any new information or changes reported by the patient or family regarding their condition or progress.


O – Objective:
Document the measurable data gathered during the session, such as strength tests, range of motion, or observations about the patient’s functional performance.


A – Assessment:
Provide a professional assessment of the patient's progress, functional status, and any changes in their condition.


P – Plan:
Outline the next steps in treatment based on the assessment and any changes in goals. |
