I OBJECTIVE ASSESSMENTS & HOW TO PERFORM
Key Observation Findings
Posture: Protracted head posture (ear in front of acromion in sagittal plane), rounded/elevated shoulders, increased cervicothoracic junction kyphosis,
reduced thoracic extension. Movement: Cervical rotation appears within normal limits; end-range extension reproduces the suboccipital heaviness and

TEST 1
Cranio-Cervical Flexion Test (CCFT) What it Measures Endurance and segmental activation of the deep cervical flexors (DCF) — longus colli and longus
capitis — which are the key stabilisers inhibited in Upper Crossed Syndrome. How to Perform 1. Patient lies supine with a small pillow to maintain neutral
cervical position. 2. A pressure biofeedback unit (or palpation) is placed behind the neck, inflated to 20 mmHg baseline. 3. Patient is asked to perform a
gentle nodding action ("tuck your chin toward your chest without lifting your head"), progressively increasing through 22, 24, 26, 28, 30 mmHg pressure
targets. 4. Each level is held for 10 seconds; observe for substitution (SCM/scalene activation, jaw clenching, breath-holding).
TEST 2
Spurling's Test (Foraminal Compression Test) What it Measures Cervical nerve root compression — narrows the intervertebral foramen to reproduce
radicular symptoms. How to Perform 1. Patient seated. 2. Passively laterally flex and rotate the cervical spine toward the symptomatic side. 3. Apply gentle
axial compression (approximately 7 kg) through the top of the head. 4. Maintain for 10 seconds or until symptoms reproduced. 5. Test both sides and
compare.

• Exercise 1: Deep Cervical Flexor (DCF) Activation — Chin Tucks (Craniocervical Flexion)
• Supine lying, small towel roll under neck for neutral. Gently retract the chin ("make a double chin") without lifting the head. Hold 10 sec, 10 reps, twice
daily.
• Perform standing against a wall — wall provides tactile feedback, reduces gravitational demand. Start with 5-second holds.
• Add 2.5 cm head lift (endurance challenge). Then progress to sitting, standing without support. Add resistance band at forehead for advanced loading.
• Exercise 2: Scapular Retraction and Depression (Lower Trapezius Activation)
• Seated or standing. Arms by sides. Gently squeeze shoulder blades together and DOWN (not up). Hold 5–10 sec, 10 reps, avoid shoulder shrugging.
Three sets, twice daily.
• Prone lying ("Y" exercise): arms overhead in Y shape, gently lift arms off table squeezing shoulder blades together. Gravity assists initially.
• Seated cable row or resistance band row emphasising scapular retraction. Progress to single-arm dumbbell row at low load. Monitor neck pain — stop
if upper trapezius substitutes.
