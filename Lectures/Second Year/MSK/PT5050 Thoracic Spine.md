## Slide 7

- Thoracic vertebrae
- Described as ‘typical’ and ‘atypical’
- Typical = T2-9
- Atypical = T1,10,11,12
- T5-8 most typical of Tx
- Heart shaped body (smaller than Lx)
- Smaller circular vertebral canal
- Transverse process extend posterolaterally (length decreases T1-12)
- Facet joints lie anterior to TP

## Slide 8

- Spinous Process
- Long spinous processes that slope in a posteroinferior direction
- Can extend to the vertebrae below
- Palpation of the transverse processes in the thoracic region is notoriously difficult due to anatomical variation

## Slide 9

- In 1979, Mitchell proposed a so-called ‘rule of three’
- 1st group of 3 spinous processes (T 1-3) are level with vertebral body of the same level
- 2nd group of 3 spinous processes (T 4-6) are level with the disc of the inferior level (HALF WAY DOWN)
- 3rd group of 3 spinous processes (T 7-9) are level with the vertebral body of the level below (SO ONE DOWN)
- T10  one down (as T7-9)
- T11  half way down (as T4-6)
- T12  same level (as T1-3)

## Slide 11

- However
- Geelhoed et al. (2006) dissected 15 cadavers and measured the vertical distance between the transverse plane of the transverse processes of 1 vertebra and the spinous processes for all levels of the thoracic spine
- They concluded that the “transverse processes of each thoracic vertebra are generally at the level of the spinous processes of the vertebra 1 level above, throughout the thoracic spine”

- Facet joints project almost vertically

- Restrict flexion and extension

## Slide 15

- Thoracic vertebrae movement
- Angle of facet joints restrict flexion/extension and anterior translation of vertebral segment
- 1-2° of extension is available at each thoracic segment, giving an overall average of 15-20° of thoracic extension for the entire thoracic spine
- Tight to protect organs but loose enough to allow respiratory movement
- Narrow disc compared to vertebrae body
- Presence of thoracic cage (ribs, sternum, costal cartilage, ligaments)

## Slide 21

- 1st rib - atypical
- Wide and short with no rib angle
- Only articulates with one vertebrae (T1)
- Insertion point for scalenes
- Makes up the ‘thoracic outlet’
- Anterior scalene
- Middle scalene

## Slide 22

- Other atypical ribs
- 2nd rib – Thinner and longer than 1st rib, upper surface is origin for serratus anterior. Two articular facets on head as normal.
- 10th rib – only one facet, only articulates with T10.
- 11th and 12th rib – no neck and only one facet. Short ribs that do not attach to sternum (floating ribs).

## Slide 28

- Neural Pathways
- Thoracic Spinal Nerves
- After exiting the intervertebral foramina, each nerve splits into:
- Dorsal ramus → innervates deep back muscles and skin over thoracic spine.
- Ventral ramus → forms intercostal nerves (T1–T11) and subcostal nerve (T12).
- These supply intercostal muscles, abdominal wall, and thoracic skin.
- Dermatomes
- Thoracic dermatomes are clinically important for sensory mapping:
- T1: Medial arm
- T4: Nipple line.
- T6: Xiphoid process.
- T10: Umbilicus.
- Useful for assessing thoracic radiculopathy or spinal cord injury.

## Slide 30

- Intervertebral Discs
- Narrow discs compared to vertebral body
- Cervical   ̴40%
- Thoracic   ̴20%
- Lumbar   ̴33%
- Disc pathology is rare in thoracic spine and often found incidentally
- 0.25-0.75 of all symptomatic disc herniation
- (Fogwe, Zulfiqar & Mesfin, 2025)
- Can cause radicular pain often following dermatome pattern.
- Myelopathy – compression of spinal cord

## Slide 31

- Thoracic Spine
- Kyphotic curve rather than lordotic
- Due to shape of vertebrae and disc
- Curve can vary but ‘normal’ is considered between 20-45 degrees
- Hyperkyphosis
- Postural kyphosis
- Scheuermann's kyphosis
- Congenital kyphosis

## Slide 32

- Postural kyphosis
- ‘Poor’ posture
- Can correct by ‘standing up straight’
- More common in teenage years
- Females > males
- Usually painfree but can be a sign of Ankylosing Spondylitis
- Poor posture does not always cause pain!

## Slide 33

- Swain CT, Pan F, Owen PJ, Schmidt H, Belavy DL. No consensus on causality of spine postures or physical exposure and low back pain: A systematic review of systematic reviews. Journal of biomechanics. 2020 Mar 26;102:109312.

## Slide 34

- Postural degeneration
- Degenerative changes to
- Discs
- Vertebrae
- Facet joints
- Muscles
- Causes increase load on anterior vertebrae and spine extensors
- Decreases mobility of thoracic spine further increasing kyphosis
- Early intervention is important
- Extensor strengthening
- Manual therapy and therapeutic exercise

## Slide 35

- Scheuermann’s (disease) kyphosis
- “Juvenile kyphosis” - diagnosed in adolescents between 12-17 years old
- Developed large wedge shaped vertebrae and discs
- Believed to be hereditary
- ̴50% cases cause back pain

## Slide 36

- Congenital kyphosis
- Failure of formation or segmentation of vertebrae
- Occurs in first 6-8 weeks of embryonic life
- Unknown cause – genetic ‘mistake’

## Slide 38

- Idiopathic scoliosis
- >80% scoliosis – cause is unknown
- Usually occurs between 10-14 years old
- Growth can worsen scoliosis
- Congenital scoliosis
- Misshaped vertebrae
- Identified from early infancy
- Watch and wait approach
- Neuromuscular scoliosis
- Weak and imbalanced muscles as a results of a neurological condition
- Cerebral palsy
- Will usually continue to get worse
- Surgery recommended at 50°, breathing affected at 80°

## Slide 39

- Thoracic spine injuries
- Thoracic spine injuries are less common than Lx and Cx
- Due to rigidity and stability
- Higher force needed to sustain injury
- Due to high force needed injury can be more severe!
- Narrow vertebral canal                      spinal cord damage
- Due to low incidence rate, Tx injuries are missed!

## Slide 42

- Psychological Factors
- Body image concerns
- Hunched back, tall stature, breast size (macromastia)
- Fear avoidance behavours
- Anxiety over movements or posture worsening pain
- Stress
- Increase in pain perception and muscle tension
- Breathing related anxiety
