# cervical case study part 1

Cervical Spine Case Study – Part 1
David M., a 48-year-old accountant, presents to physiotherapy with a three-month history of progressive neck pain and right arm discomfort. The pain started gradually with no clear mechanism of injury and has worsened over time, now interfering with his work and sleep.
He describes the pain as a deep ache in the neck area (VAS 6/10) with intermittent sharp sensations running down his right arm to the thumb and index finger (8/10) and some tingling on the right hand (3/10). He also has a focal point of severe pain in the left upper shoulder (upper trapezius) (9/10) that occasionally flares up for several days which prevents him from carrying out his daily activities.
He reports morning stiffness, difficulty maintaining his posture at work, and occasional headaches. He reported that prolonged sitting, looking down at screens, head rotation worsens his pain. He tried NSAIDs heat application, gentle movement, avoiding prolonged positions to relief his pain.
No reported history of cancer, previous surgeries, or systemic conditions. No recent fever, night sweats, or unexplained weight loss.

Q2: How would you plan your objective assessment based on the information provided above?
