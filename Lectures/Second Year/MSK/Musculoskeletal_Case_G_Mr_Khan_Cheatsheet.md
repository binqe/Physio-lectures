# Musculoskeletal Case G Mr Khan Cheatsheet

## Page 1

Case G — Mr. Khan
28M · Software Engineer · 2-year progressive low back & buttock pain
Musculoskeletal Physiotherapy
I SUBJECTIVE PROBLEM LIST
• Physical Impairments
• Reduced lumbar flexion (Schober's test <5cm)
• Restricted chest expansion (2cm — severely reduced)
• Inflammatory pain at rest and at night
• Fatigue (common in active inflammatory disease)
• Difficulty sustaining static postures at desk (prolonged sitting as
software engineer)
• Reduced walking tolerance during flares
• Difficulty with bending/dressing secondary to spinal stiffness
• Sedentary software engineering role with prolonged static
postures may perpetuate stiffness and deconditioning.
• 2-year history may have caused anxiety, fear-avoidance, or loss of
confidence in movement — worth screening with a validated tool
(e.g. STarT Back).
II RED FLAGS
• I Saddle Anaesthesia / Bowel-Bladder Change
• Significance:
• Cauda equina syndrome — surgical emergency
• Immediate emergency referral to A&E; cease assessment
• I Unexplained Weight Loss / Fever / Night Sweats
• Potential malignancy or systemic infection (e.g. spinal TB)
• Urgent same-day GP/oncology referral; imaging required
• I History of Malignancy
I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• Inflammatory cytokines, particularly TNF-α and IL-17, promote synovitis, bone marrow oedema, and ultimately osteoproliferation: the pathological
formation of new bone (syndesmophytes) at the vertebral endplates.
• The SIJ is typically affected first bilaterally, distinguishing it from mechanical back pain, and costovertebral involvement restricts chest expansion below
the normal threshold of ≥5cm.
• Inflammatory nocturnal pain (4am waking): Pro-inflammatory cytokines peak in the early hours of the morning, correlating with symptom worsening at
rest when the anti-inflammatory effect of cortisol is lowest.
• Morning stiffness >1 hour, improved by movement: Inflammatory exudate within SIJ/spinal joints accumulates during rest; movement promotes synovial
fluid circulation and reduces joint stiffness.
• Bilateral buttock pain: Bilateral SIJ enthesitis and synovitis refers pain to the gluteal region — often described as alternating sides in early disease.
• Reduced lumbar flexion (Schober's test): Spinal and paraspinal enthesitis with early syndesmophyte formation restricts segmental mobility.
I DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES
1. No history of uveitis or systemic features
2. Another seronegative spondyloarthropathy
3. Classic anterior uveitis is more typical of AS than PsA

Posture: Observe for flattening of lumbar lordosis, increased thoracic kyphosis, and forward head posture — early signs of spinal ankylosis in AS Gait:
Rigid, stiff lumbar gait with reduced spinal rotation — distinguishes from antalgic gait of discogenic pain Chest expansion:
I TREATMENT PLAN
• I CONTRAINDICATION — HVLA Spinal Manipulation
I Goal:
• Hydrotherapy / Pool-Based Exercise:
• Warm water reduces muscle guarding and pain, enabling active movement in an anti-gravity environment — ideal for active inflammatory disease.
• — I choose this because the warmth and buoyancy allow exercise that would be painful or impossible on land during flares.
• Breathing Exercises:
• Diaphragmatic and thoracic expansion exercises to maintain chest wall mobility given 2cm chest expansion.
• — I prioritise this because costovertebral ankylosis is progressive and irreversible; early intervention preserves respiratory function.
• Gentle Sustained Stretching (not ballistic):
• Hip flexor and thoracic rotation stretches held 30–60 seconds.
• — Sustained low-load stretching is preferred over aggressive mobilisation given tissue inflammation; it gently lengthens without provocative loading.
• Exercise 1: Thoracic Extension Over Foam Roller
