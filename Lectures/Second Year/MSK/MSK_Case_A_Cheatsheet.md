Case A
Mrs. Gupta — 50-Year-Old Graphic Designer | Working Diagnosis: Cervical Postural Dysfunction / Upper Crossed Syndrome
Target: 70%+ | Musculoskeletal (MSK) Physiotherapy
I PROBLEM LIST
• Dull, persistent cervical and suboccipital pain (1 year)
• Reduced end-range cervical extension
• Protracted head posture + rounded shoulders
• Tightness of upper trapezius and levator scapulae (bilateral)
• Weakness of deep cervical flexors and lower scapular stabilisers
(presumed)
• Known osteoporosis — bone fragility risk
• Difficulty sustaining prolonged screen-based work
• Heaviness at skull base limiting concentration/productivity
• Likely sleep disruption secondary to discomfort
• Potential limitation in driving (cervical rotation for shoulder-checking)
I RED FLAGS
• Bilateral upper limb paraesthesia / hand clumsiness / gait
disturbance
• Unexplained weight loss, night sweats, night pain unrelieved by
position change
• Sudden onset of severe headache ("worst headache of my life")
• History of significant fall or trauma in context of osteoporosis
• Dysphagia, hoarseness, or cranial nerve signs
I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
• This creates a "crossed" pattern of tightness and weakness across the shoulder girdle and cervical spine.
• The protracted head posture shifts the centre of gravity of the head (approx.
• 5 kg) anterior to the cervical spine, increasing compressive forces and erector muscle demand by approximately 4–5x per inch of forward
displacement.
I DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES
1. 1. Cervical Spondylosis (Degenerative Disc/Facet Joint Disease)
• Age-appropriate degenerative change at 50 years
• Neck stiffness, particularly end-range extension/rotation
• Deep aching pain — can mimic postural pain
2. 2. Cervical Radiculopathy (most likely C5–C6)
• Dermatomal radiation into upper limb
• Positive Spurling's test (positive in this presentation — ABSENT here)
• Myotomal weakness, reflex changes

I TREATMENT PLAN
I Initial Phase — Weeks 1–4 (Pain Relief, Activation, Posture Correction)
• Postural correction at the workstation: "Monitor should be at eye level. Your ears should be directly above your shoulders. Set a timer to stand,
stretch, and reset your posture every 30–40 minutes — micro-breaks are highly effective."
• Pain is mechanical, not structural damage: "Your scan (if done) or symptoms suggest this is a postural and muscular issue, not damage to your discs
or nerves. Movement and activity are safe and will help — resting too much will make it worse."
• Osteoporosis and exercise safety: "Because of your osteoporosis, we will avoid any heavy loading or manipulation of the neck. All exercises I give you
are safe and will actually help protect your bones through gentle loading."
• Sleep hygiene and pillow advice: "Use a contoured cervical pillow that supports the natural neck curve. Avoid sleeping in a twisted position or on your
front. Side-lying with one pillow is ideal."
I Progression Criteria (Before Advancing to Phase 2)
• NPRS ≤3/10 at rest and during workstation activities (from baseline)
• CCFT sustained at ≥24 mmHg without SCM substitution for 10 seconds

• Deep neck flexor endurance hold ≥30 seconds
• Full pain-free cervical extension ROM (re-measured with goniometer)
• Patient reports improved ability to sustain 2+ hours at workstation (PSFS)
I Long-Term Plan — Weeks 4–12 (Strength, Function & Self-Management)
• Progressive strengthening: Advance to resistance band rows, prone cobra, wall angels (shoulder mobility), and DCF loaded holds. Gradual
progression of sets/reps/resistance aligned with bone loading guidelines for osteoporosis.
• Postural retraining: Introduce dynamic postural correction during task-specific activities (e.g., practising sustained computer work in upright posture,
gradually increasing tolerance from 30 min to 2 hours).
• Self-management programme: Written home exercise programme with clear instructions and photo guides. Discuss return-to-activity milestones and
signs to watch for (neurological symptoms = return to physiotherapist immediately).
• Workstation ergonomic assessment: Provide written ergonomic checklist or refer for formal ergonomic assessment of her graphic design workstation.
• Discharge criteria: NPRS ≤2/10 at rest and during sustained work; NDI score <10% (minimal disability); independent with full home exercise
programme; full cervical ROM in all planes.
