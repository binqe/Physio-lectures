# Ward-Based MSK Clin Sim Workbook

Ward-Based Orthopaedic Clinical Simulation Workbook for Physiotherapy Students
Learning Objectives
By the end of the simulation, students will be able to:
Perform a comprehensive initial assessment of a post-operative MSK patient.
Develop and Implement Effective Rehabilitation Plans
Create and justify a physiotherapy treatment plan for a patient with a hip hemiarthroplasty.
Make clinical judgements in ward based MSK conditions based on EBP.

| Case Report: Left hip hemiarthroplasty 

1. Patient Profile
Name: Mrs. S 
Age: 75 years
Gender: Female
Medical History: Osteoporosis, hypertension, type 2 diabetes (well-controlled)
Social History: Lives alone in a one-story house; uses a walking stick for mobility. Previously independent in all activities of daily living (ADLs).
Reason for Admission: Fall at home resulting in a left hip fracture.

2. Surgical Intervention
Procedure: Left hip hemiarthroplasty performed due to a displaced femoral neck fracture.
Post-Operative Status: Surgery was uneventful; patient transferred to the orthopaedic ward for post-operative care and rehabilitation.

3. Initial Assessment (Day 1 Post-Op)
Vital Signs: Stable
Pain Level: Reports pain at 7/10 on the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) during movement.
Mobility Status: Non-weight-bearing on the left leg as per surgeon’s orders.
Range of Motion (ROM): Limited in left hip flexion and abduction due to pain and surgical restrictions.
Muscle Strength:
Left lower limb: Reduced strength (2/5) due to pain inhibition.
Right lower limb: 4/5 strength.
Functional Status:
Bed mobility: Requires moderate assistance.
Transfers: Unable to perform independently.
Walking: Unable to walk due to pain and non-weight-bearing status.
Goals of Physiotherapy:
Pain management.
Early mobilization within surgical restrictions.
Improve bed mobility and transfer ability.
Prevent post-operative complications like deep vein thrombosis (DVT) and muscle atrophy.

4. Physiotherapy Treatment Plan
Day 1-3: Early Post-Operative Phase
Pain Management:
Education on deep breathing and relaxation techniques.
Application of ice packs for pain relief.
Bed Exercises:
Ankle pumps and circles to promote circulation.
Isometric quadriceps and gluteal exercises to maintain muscle activation.
Bed Mobility Training:
Assisted bed rolling and sitting up at the edge of the bed.
Education on using the bed rails for support.
Chest Physiotherapy:
Incentive spirometry and deep breathing exercises to prevent respiratory complications.
Day 4-7: Progressive Mobilization
Pain Assessment: Reduced to 4/10 on VAS with movement.
Transfers and Mobility:
Assisted sit-to-stand exercises using a frame.
Non-weight-bearing hopping with a walker to promote safe transfers.
Strength and ROM Exercises:
Gentle hip and knee range of motion exercises within pain-free limits.
Strengthening exercises for the unaffected leg and upper body to aid in transfers.
Balance and Functional Training:
Seated balance exercises and reaching tasks to improve stability.
Education on maintaining balance and avoiding falls.
Patient Education:
Post-operative hip precautions (e.g., avoiding hip flexion beyond 90°, no crossing legs).
Safe mobility strategies and energy conservation techniques.

5. Progress and Reassessment (End of Week 1)
Pain Level: Improved to 3/10 on VAS.
Mobility Status:
Transfers: Independent with a walker and minimal supervision.
Ambulation: Partial weight-bearing allowed on the left leg as per updated surgeon's orders.
Distance: Able to walk 10 meters with supervision.
ROM and Strength:
Left hip flexion improved to 70° (previously 40°).
Left lower limb strength increased to 3/5.
Functional Status: Improved independence in bed mobility and transfers.

6. Discharge Planning
Discharge Criteria:
Safe transfers and ambulation with a walker.
Independence in self-care tasks like toileting and dressing.
Home Exercise Program:
Strengthening exercises for lower limbs.
Walking program with progressive weight-bearing as tolerated.
Hip precaution education to prevent dislocation.
Referrals:
Outpatient physiotherapy for continued rehabilitation.
Occupational therapy for home environment assessment and modifications.
Follow-Up: Scheduled outpatient physiotherapy appointment in 2 weeks. |
