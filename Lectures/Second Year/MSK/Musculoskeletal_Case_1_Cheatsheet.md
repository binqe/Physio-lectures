I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• Working Diagnosis: Postpartum SIJ Dysfunction / Pelvic Girdle Pain During pregnancy, the hormone relaxin is secreted to increase ligamentous laxity
across the pelvis, facilitating delivery.
• Postpartum, circulating relaxin decreases but remains elevated during breastfeeding; concurrently, the postural and neuromuscular demands of
pregnancy alter motor recruitment patterns, leaving the force-closure system temporarily deficient.
• The result is excessive translational and rotational shear at the SIJ, particularly during unilateral loading.
• The posterior SIJ ligaments and the posterior interosseous ligament become the primary stabilisers under load — their repeated stress-straining
generates localised nociception, perceived as deep buttock/PSIS pain.
• Activities that increase torsional forces (rolling in bed, stepping out of a car, single-leg stance) dramatically increase SIJ shear forces, explaining the
classic provocation pattern.
• Unilateral PSIS tenderness: Posterior SIJ ligament and joint capsule irritation from excessive shear.

I OBJECTIVE ASSESSMENTS & HOW TO PERFORM
Key Observation Findings
Observe gait: antalgic gait, Trendelenburg sign (gluteus medius weakness), reduced step length on right Standing posture: posterior pelvic tilt, asymmetric
PSIS height (palpate bilaterally), increased lumbar lordosis Active movement: observe for lateral trunk shift during single-leg stance, hip hitching pattern
Thigh Thrust Test (Posterior Shear Test)
What it measures: Posterior SIJ ligament integrity and joint stability under posterior shear force. High sensitivity (88%) for SIJ pain. How to perform: Patient
lies supine on the plinth. Flex the test hip to 90° and slightly adduct (approximately 10°).
Sacral Thrust Test
What it measures: Anterior shear stress across both SIJs simultaneously — tests the integrity of the anterior SIJ ligaments and evaluates bilateral posterior
ligament stress. How to perform: Patient lies prone on the plinth, arms by sides. Stand to the side. Place both hands stacked over the sacrum (at S2 level —
midway between the posterior superior and posterior inferior iliac spines).
Active Straight Leg Raise (ASLR)
What it measures: Force-closure capacity of the lumbopelvic region; specifically assesses whether the patient can transfer load through the pelvis
efficiently. A key functional test in postpartum PGP research (Mens et al.). How to perform: Patient lies supine, legs straight, arms by sides. Ask the patient
to lift one leg (keeping it straight) approximately 20cm off the plinth without bending the knee.
I TREATMENT PLAN
• Pelvic Girdle Questionnaire (PGQ)
• Exercise 1: Transversus Abdominis / Deep Core Activation ("Drawing In" with Pelvic Floor Co-contraction)
• Reduce hold time to 5 seconds; use ultrasound biofeedback if difficulty with isolation; perform in supported sitting if crook-lying is uncomfortable.
• Add gentle heel slide while maintaining activation → progress to 4-point kneeling with arm/leg reach → progress to sitting/standing functional contexts.
• Exercise 2: Clamshell (Hip External Rotation, Gluteus Medius Activation)
• Reduce range of motion; perform against a wall to cue neutral pelvis; reduce sets to 2×10.
• Add resistance band above knees → side-lying hip abduction (straight leg) → standing hip abduction with band → single-leg mini-squat with pelvic
control cue.

• Technique: Sacroiliac Joint Mobilisation (Grade I–II Posterior-Anterior Glide) + Soft Tissue Release to Posterior SIJ Ligaments
• Soft tissue release (sustained pressure × 60–90 seconds) to the posterior SIJ ligament and gluteal trigger points can reduce guarding and facilitate
subsequent exercise.
• Patient Education — 4 Key Messages
• "Your pelvis is currently hypermobile, not damaged."
• The ligaments softened during pregnancy to allow birth — they are healing, not torn. This reframes pain as a temporary instability rather than injury,
reducing fear-avoidance.
