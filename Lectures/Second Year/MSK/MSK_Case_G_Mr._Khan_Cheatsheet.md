Case G — Mr. Khan
28M · Software Engineer · 2-year progressive low back & buttock pain | Working Dx: Ankylosing Spondylitis / Axial SpA
Target: 70%+ | Musculoskeletal (MSK) Physiotherapy
I PROBLEM LIST
• Reduced lumbar flexion (Schober's test <5cm)
• Bilateral SIJ tenderness / inflammation
• Restricted chest expansion (2cm — severely reduced)
• Inflammatory pain at rest and at night
• Fatigue (common in active inflammatory disease)
• Difficulty sustaining static postures at desk (prolonged sitting as
software engineer)
• Impaired sleep quality (waking 4am)
• Reduced walking tolerance during flares
• Difficulty with bending/dressing secondary to spinal stiffness
I RED FLAGS
• Saddle Anaesthesia / Bowel-Bladder Change Significance: Cauda
equina syndrome — surgical emergency Action: Immediate
emergency referral to A&E; cease assessment
• Unexplained Weight Loss / Fever / Night Sweats Significance:
Potential malignancy or systemic infection (e.g. spinal TB) Action:
Urgent same-day GP/oncology referral; imaging required
• History of Malignancy Significance: Spinal metastases — fracture
and cord compression risk Action: Urgent rheumatology/oncology
referral; plain X-ray and MRI
• Acute Uveitis Flare (current) Significance: Risk of vision loss if
untreated; indicates active disease flare Action: Refer to
ophthalmology same day; liaise with rheumatologist regarding
disease activity
• Progressive Neurological Deficit Significance: May indicate
atlantoaxial instability or cord compression from fused segment
fracture Action: Cease physiotherapy; urgent neurosurgical/spinal
team referral
