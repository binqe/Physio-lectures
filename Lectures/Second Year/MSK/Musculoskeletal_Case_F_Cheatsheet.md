Case F
Mr. Walters — Chronic Non-Specific Low Back Pain | Working Dx: CNSLBP with Central Sensitisation &
Fear-Avoidance Behaviour
Musculoskeletal Physiotherapy
I SUBJECTIVE PROBLEM LIST
• Physical Impairments
• Generalised lumbar stiffness across all planes
• Poor transversus abdominis and multifidus activation
• Reduced cardiovascular fitness and physical deconditioning
• Central sensitisation — amplified, disproportionate pain response
• Reduced social participation and daily activity
• Loss of retirement role and identity
II RED FLAGS
• "You mentioned you believe your spine is 'crumbling' — can you
tell me more about what you think is happening inside your back?"
• "You've stopped walking your dog — can you describe what you're
worried will happen if you do walk, or lift, or exercise?"
• "How is your sleep — do you wake because of pain, and how is
your mood and energy generally?"
• Cauda Equina Syndrome (Bowel / Bladder Dysfunction, Saddle
Anaesthesia)
• Action: Immediate emergency department referral. Do not proceed
with assessment if confirmed.
• Unexplained Weight Loss / Night Pain / Systemic Symptoms
(Malignancy or Infection)
• Action: If weight loss, night sweats, night pain, fever, or
age-appropriate cancer screening is overdue — urgent GP referral
with bloods (FBC, CRP, PSA, calcium, protein electrophoresis).
• History of Osteoporosis / Significant Trauma (Vertebral Fracture
Risk)
I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• In this state, the dorsal horn neurones of the spinal cord become sensitised such that non-noxious stimuli (normal movement, light touch, routine
posture changes) are amplified and perceived as painful.
• This explains why Mr.
• Walters experiences constant pain despite no structural findings on imaging.
• The result is a pain system that is "turned up" independently of tissue state.
• Fear-avoidance behaviour operates as a powerful amplifier of this central sensitisation.
• Walters' belief that his spine is crumbling activates the limbic system, increasing amygdala-mediated threat appraisal.
I DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSES
1. Chronic Non-Specific LBP with Central Sensitisation & Fear-Avoidance
2. Lumbar Spondylosis / Facet Joint Osteoarthritis
3. Lumbar Canal Stenosis (Neurogenic Claudication)
I OBJECTIVE ASSESSMENTS & HOW TO PERFORM
Key Observation Findings
Posture: Observe for loss of lumbar lordosis, increased thoracic kyphosis (from years of sustained driving posture), or lateral shift Movement quality:
Observe for pain behaviours (guarding, grimacing, bracing), hesitation before movement, catastrophic movement patterns — often more revealing than the
ROM itself Gait:
Test 1 — Tampa Scale of Kinesiophobia (TSK-17)
What it measures A validated 17-item self-report questionnaire measuring pain-related fear of movement and (re)injury. Scores range from 17 to 68 —
higher scores indicate greater kinesiophobia. How to perform
1. Provide the patient with the Tampa Scale questionnaire — it can be completed in the waiting room or at the start of the session.
2. Patient rates each of 17 statements on a 4-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree, 4 = strongly agree). 3. Score the total and note subscale patterns:
"harm" (fear of pain/injury) and "activity avoidance" subscales identify dominant themes. 4. Interpret in context: scores >37 indicate clinically significant
kinesiophobia. Positive finding TSK score >37 (confirmed "high" in Mr. Walters' case)
Test 3 — Repeated Movement Assessment (McKenzie Method — Directional Preference)
What it measures Whether repeated movements in a specific direction (flexion or extension) centralise, peripheralise, or have no effect on symptoms —
identifies if a mechanical or directional component exists within this chronic presentation. How to perform 1. Perform 10 repeated end-range lumbar
extensions in standing (or prone press-ups if tolerated) while monitoring symptom response after each set. 2. Then perform 10 repeated lumbar flexions in
seated or standing and monitor again.
I TREATMENT PLAN
• "What has your GP or specialist told you about your back? Have you been told what's wrong, and have your scans been explained to you?"
