# MSK Subjective Assessment Clinical Simulation Workbook

MSK Subjective Assessment Clinical Simulation Task

| MSK Case

A 43-year-old man who works as a police officer presented in the clinic with a two-week history of right shoulder pain.
 
The patient explained that his pain started when he woke up one morning.  He had also noticed, “a strange feeling” in his middle two fingers that came and went with no pattern.   He did not recall straining his shoulder and had not undertaken any strenuous lifting. He had no other health complaints and was a keen runner.  His pain was worse at night.
 
His pain was brought on when he raised his arms above his head and eased as soon as he stopped the painful movement.
Daily pattern – No pattern, the problem was dependent on the activity.
 
Past medical history – Nothing significant
Medication – Not taking any at present
Social History – Lives at home with his wife and two children |
| Role Play :
Patient role :
Think about: Background information, Presenting symptoms, Communication style, Concerns to raise

Physiotherapist role :
Think about: Initial approach, history taking, asking detailed questions

Learning Objectives for the Role Play:
Effective Communication:
Students should demonstrate how to build rapport and trust with the patient.
They must practice active listening and empathetic responses to the patient’s concerns.
Comprehensive History Taking:
Students should elicit a detailed history of the patient’s condition, symptoms, lifestyle, and how these relate to the patient’s work and hobbies. |
| PROFORMA:

Instruction:
In your groups, take turns conducting a subjective assessment based on the provided case study. Use the MSK Assessment Sheet on the module as a guide to help structure and focus your assessment. |
| During the Role-Play:
Presenting the Pain:
When asked, describe that the shoulder pain started without any clear cause. Be specific that the pain intensifies with overhead arm movements and subsides when you lower your arms.
Mention that there is no consistent daily pattern to the pain, but it seems activity-related.
Describing Other Symptoms:
Refer to the “strange feeling” you’ve noticed in your middle two fingers. When prompted, explain that it comes and goes without any clear pattern and isn’t directly related to your shoulder movements.
Clarifying Medical and Social History:
When asked about your past medical history, state there’s nothing significant to report, and you are not on any medication.
Share that you live at home with your wife and two children and enjoy running regularly, though your shoulder pain has recently affected this.
Responding to Questions:
If asked about specific activities, explain that you haven't done anything out of the ordinary, like heavy lifting or strenuous exercise, that might have triggered the pain.
Mention that your pain is worse at night, making it harder to sleep comfortably.
Emotional and Behavioural Aspects:
Play the role with mild frustration or concern about how the shoulder pain is affecting your daily life and activities like running.
Express hope that the physiotherapy will help you return to your usual activities without pain. |
