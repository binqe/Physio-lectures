## Page 9

Principles of Range of Motion
(AROM) Assessment
• Assess the environment before the patient starts the movement 
to ensure their safety.
• Ensure the patient understands what you will do and what to 
expect.
• Explain any expectations of pain and to inform you when it 
occurs and location.
• Ensure the patient is supported and not at risk of falling or 
losing balance or at a risk of a limb drop.
• The patient should be comfortable, and respected as it may 
influence the ROM achieved. Also, the patient needs to be 
wearing comfortable close to not restrict the movement.
• Understand the factors that influence the measurement of ROM 
and include in your clinical reasoning.

In ROM Assessment, the therapist observes:
• Rhythm and quality of movement
• Symmetry between the RT and LT 
sides.

location
• Ease of movement
• Willingness of the patient to move
• Movement of associated joints or 
any substitutions: Example: 
Shoulder elevation or trunk side 
bending with flexion or abduction.

2- Principles of Passive ROM
Assessment
The limb is carried safely and passively moved 
by the therapist.
The joint is allowed to move freely through the 
range.
The tested part must be supported throughout 
the motion then placed back to the resting 
position.
The patient’s face is also observed and 
communicate with the patient about their 
symptoms.
At the end of the movement, note end feeling 
and limiting factors (Pain/ Stiffness, etc).
The ROM is then measured.
Record the findings.

## Page 20

1.Check for contraindications or precautions. 
2.Organise the testing sequence by body position to minimise changes in 
positioning.
3.Communication and Consent to expose part and touch.
4.Positioning: Ensure the patient is comfortable and well-supported. The 
joint can move through its full ROM without obstruction.
5.Avoid active and passive insufficiency
6.Stabilisation: proximal joint is stabilised to minimise any substitute 
movements 
7.Assess End Feel
8.Alignment of the Measurement Tool: 
•
Fulcrum or Axis - Positioned over a point near the joint's axis of rotation.
•
Stationary Arm - Usually aligned with the midline of the stationary segment of the joint.
•
Moving Arm - Usually aligned with the midline of the moving segment of the joint.

End-feel
Description
Example
Soft
•Soft Tissue ApproximationOccurs when two soft tissue 
masses meet one another, limiting further movement.
•The quality of resistance is soft, and this gradually 
increases as the soft tissue is compressed between body 
parts.
Knee Flexion: Contact between the 
soft tissue of the posterior leg and 
posterior thigh
Firm
•Muscular End FeelOccurs when muscular tension limits 
the ROM.
•Quality of resistance is firm, although not as firm as with 
a capsular end-feel, and somewhat springy.
•Feels like stretching a tyre inner tube
Hip Flexion with Knee Straight 
(SLR):Passive elastic tension of the 
hamstring muscles
•Ligamentous End FeelOccurs when tension in the 
ligaments surrounding the joint limits the ROM.
•Quality of resistance felt is firm, although not as firm as 
with capsular end-feel, and somewhat springy.
•Feels like stretching a leather belt.
Forearm Supination: Tension in the 
palmar radioulnar ligament of the 
inferior radioulnar joint, interosseous 
membrane and oblique cord
•Capsular End FeelOccurs when the joint capsule and 
surrounding non-contractile tissues limit the ROM.
•Quality of resistance felt is firm but not hard. There is a 
slight "give" to the movement.
•Feels like stretching a leather belt, with more resistance 
than a ligament.
Extension of Metacarpophalangeal

•Bone on Bone: Occurs when the approximation of two
