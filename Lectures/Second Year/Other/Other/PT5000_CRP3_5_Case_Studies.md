## Slide 3

- How we will work through cases (method)
- Tue 3 Feb · 16:00–17:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Step-by-step
- 1) Identify red flags + immediate risks (ABC, sepsis, rapid O2 escalation).
- 2) Build a problem list (symptoms, impairments, activity limitations).
- 3) Link problems to likely mechanisms (restriction vs infection vs mixed).
- 4) Choose assessment priorities (what will change your plan today?).
- 5) Set goals and plan interventions with monitoring + progression.
- Output expected
- A one-paragraph assessment summary.
- A short-term plan (today/next 24h) and a rehab plan (next week).
- Outcome measures you will use to judge response.
- Education/safety-netting: what you will tell the patient.

## Slide 4

- Case 1: Progressive ILD (restrictive phenotype)
- Tue 3 Feb · 16:00–17:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Scenario
- 62-year-old with diagnosed fibrotic ILD; increasing exertional dyspnoea over 6 months.
- SpO2 94% at rest on room air; drops to 86% after 2 minutes walking.
- Dry cough; fine bibasal crackles; CXR/HRCT consistent with fibrosis.
- Goal: maintain independence and walking tolerance; anxious about breathlessness.
- Tasks for your group
- What additional assessment is most useful today? (pick 3)
- How will you structure an exercise session safely?
- What education will you prioritise (pacing/oxygen/safety)?
- How will you measure progress over 4–6 weeks?

## Slide 5

- Case 2: Community-acquired pneumonia (acute ward)
- Tue 3 Feb · 16:00–17:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Scenario
- 48-year-old admitted yesterday with CAP; CURB-65 = 2.
- On 3 L/min O2 via nasal cannula; SpO2 93% at rest; RR 28, HR 112.
- Productive cough; pleuritic pain limits deep breathing; CXR shows RLL consolidation.
- Today: wants to get to bathroom independently but feels “wiped out”.
- Tasks for your group
- What are the priorities for physio input in the next 24 hours?
- How will you dose mobilisation and monitor response?
- Is ACT indicated? If yes, which technique and why?
- What would trigger escalation to medical review?

## Slide 6

- Case 3: Bronchiectasis exacerbation (infective + secretion phenotype)
- Tue 3 Feb · 16:00–17:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Scenario
- 35-year-old with non-CF bronchiectasis; ↑ sputum volume and purulence for 5 days.
- Daily productive cough; reports difficulty clearing sputum; wheeze on exertion.
- SpO2 96% at rest; coarse crackles and rhonchi; fatigued and less active than usual.
- Has previously used ACBT but adherence is inconsistent.
- Tasks for your group
- Select an ACT plan (technique + dose + progression) and justify it.
- What outcome measures will you use to judge ACT effectiveness?
- How will you address adherence and long-term self-management?
- When would you stop/modify ACTs (safety)?

## Slide 7

- Model answer structure (what “good” looks like)
- Tue 3 Feb · 16:00–17:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Assessment summary (example)
- “Primary problems: exertional desaturation + high symptom burden limiting mobility; secondary problem: ineffective secretion clearance.”
- “Mechanism hypothesis: restrictive mechanics (↓volumes/↓compliance) or infective consolidation causing V/Q mismatch.”
- “Risk: deterioration if oxygenation worsens or mobilisation intolerance increases.”
- Plan (example)
- Short-term: mobilise little-and-often with SpO2 monitoring; teach pacing/breathing control; ACT if secretions retained.
- Rehab: structured exercise programme (interval if needed); progress using outcome measures.
- Education: self-management + red flags; coordinate with MDT for analgesia/oxygen/meds.
