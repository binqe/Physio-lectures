# CASP-checklist-randomised-controlled-trials-RCT

CASP Checklist:
For Randomised Controlled Trials (RCTs)
During critical appraisal, never make assumptions about what the researchers have done. If it is not possible to tell, use the “Can’t tell” response box. If you can’t tell, at best it means the researchers have not been explicit or transparent, but at worst it could mean the researchers have not undertaken a particular task or process. Once you’ve finished the critical appraisal, if there are a large number of “Can’t tell” responses, consider whether the findings of the study are trustworthy and interpret the results with caution.
Referencing recommendation:
CASP recommends using the Harvard style referencing, which is an author/date method. Sources are cited within the body of your assignment by giving the name of the author(s) followed by the date of publication. All other details about the publication are given in the list of references or bibliography at the end.

| Can the results be applied to your local population/in your context?

YES – in physiotherapy – if patients who are struggling with FAI fail conservative therapy – it would be a good idea to send them for an orthopaedic review (if able) as we know that there was a statistically significant improvement in symptoms with the iHOTT-33 at 12 months post intervention. 

FAI is a common, MSK condition presenting to physiotherapy, the control group was individualised best practiced physiotherapy which is what we provide as autonomous professionals. 

The study baseline characteristics represented a similar characteristic to those in the NHS / private population

It would have been useful to know how many people received physiotherapy + injection as this could be a useful adjunct if surgery if not available. 

The iHOTT-33 would be a useful outcome measure to use in this population when they present to physiotherapy
