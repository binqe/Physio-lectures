# Evidence base case review of a case in 2

Evidence base case review of a case Managed in ED – Mrs A.
Scenario 1.
Case Summary
Patient: 66-year-old female presented in ED
Presenting Complaint: Low back pain with right buttock pain
History of Presenting Complaint (HPC):
Onset: 2 weeks ago, after usual brisk walking and daily activities.
Had about 5 recorded low back pain episodes in the last 3 years, was not associated with increased pain after prolonged walking or standing.
Character: Low back pain, intermittent shooting pain in posterior right hip.
Relieving factors: Sitting/resting, analgesics, heat application, lumbar corset.
Aggravating factors: Bending backward, prolonged walking, prolonged standing, some lying positions.
Associated features:
Intermittent pins and needles in right lower limb.
Denies numbness, weakness, saddle anaesthesia, bowel/bladder/sexual dysfunction.
No fever or systemic symptoms.
Functional impact: Unable to walk without stick, stopped work, reduced hobbies.
Past Medical History:
Breast cancer (treated, 2015).
Hypertension.
Obesity (BMI 35.4 kg/m²).
Ex-smoker (quit 24 years ago).
Medications:
Amitriptyline.
Paracetamol.
Social History:
Lives alone, independent with stairs.
Part-time nurse.
Previously active: brisk walking, gardening, gym exercises, volunteering.
Currently off work (self-certified sick leave).
Reports low mood/depressive symptoms.

SUBJECTIVE ASSESSMENT
Consent gained verbally, Chaperon present.
PC: Low Back Pain, right buttock pain
HPC: A 66-yr-old female, presented with a history of ongoing low back pain 2/52 ago, worse after her normal brisk walking exercises and daily activities. Sitting relieved her pain. Reported intermittent shooting pain in the posterior R hip. Denied numbness but reported pins and more needle sensation in the right. Had about 5 recorded low back pain episodes in the last 3 years but was not associated with increased pain after prolonged walking or standing. All episodes responded positively with pain medications and self-directed exercises. Now, unable to walk without using a walking stick and have stopped going to work. Using a lumber corset relieves her pain during walking. Had good bowel, bladder or sexual functioning. No saddle anaesthesia and progressive LL muscle weakness suggestive of CES.  No history of fever prior to presentation. Had a history of treated breast cancer in 2015. Off duty and has self-certified sick leave. No history of overseas travel in the last 3 years outside Europe.
Aggravating factors:  bending backward during exercises, prolonged walking, prolonged standing, and some lying positions.
Relieving factors: Sitting, pain medications and heat,
Sleep: changing position reduces pain was not disturbing her night sleep.
Meds Hx: Amitriptyline, Paracetamol, Amlodipine.
PMHx: Breast Ca, HTN, Ex-smoker (stopped 24 years ago).
SHx: Lives alone in a house with no stair lift but has been independent of stair use. Part-time worker with an agency as a nurse. Hobbies: Brisk walking, gardening, volunteering in church. Said she attends group exercises in a gym. Overweight – BMI 35.4 kg/m squared, now feeling depressed.
Patient’s Expectations:
To learn more on how to self-manage and individualise her care
Able to Manage her emotions due to pain
Education on LSS and its prognosis
Perception of symptom – Chronic Osteoarthritis
Worries:
Decline in functions due to worsening Low Back Pain
Getting back to work to continue paying her mortgage.
Red flag assessment: Good bowel and bladder functions. No progressive muscle weakness. No foot drop. No fever. There was history of radiating pain to right lower limb. There was history of treated breast cancer. No history of weight loss.
Investigations-
20/12/2024 Lumbar Xray reported obvious bilateral marginal osteophytes on L4-L5, L5-S1. There is remarkable joint space reduction in L4-L5.
20/12/2024 Lumber MRI Report: lumbar spinal stenosis with multilevel spondylosis L4-L5, L5-S1.
Blood result – CRP, ESR, FBC, all were normal.
National Early Warning Score (NEWS) - 1.
Reasons for further Investigations (MRI):
Because of high VAS 8-9/10, about 5 episodes of low back pain managed in the hospital and the objective examinations is more tending to be lumbar spinal stenosis. MRI was recommended to confirm the diagnosis by medical team.
The confirmatory imaging may change the treatment plan.
Imagine findings with symptoms, gives more guide towards treatment and informed shared decision on either nonsurgical or surgical approach.
Early diagnosis and management help slow down progression of LSS.
Note: McKenzie method MDT supports treating patient based on their assessment outcome than using the radiological findings.

Conclusion:
In this case study, the evidenced-based reviews showed that comprehensive assessment by MSK clinicians can lead to an accurate diagnosis, timely planned individualised and conservative treatment plans that would improve patient outcome. The outcome can be achieved through patient education, shared decision-making, individualised care focused on a self-management approach which reduces pain, functional limitations and reduces the negative emotions associated with chronic pain in LSS and the overall disease process.
It is not certain but about 20% of patients with LSS recorded improvement in their symptoms, 60% staying the same and 20% have worsening outcomes.
