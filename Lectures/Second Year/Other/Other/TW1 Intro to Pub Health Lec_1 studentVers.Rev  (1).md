## Slide 20

- What is Public Health Nutrition (PHN)?
- Public Health Nutrition is concerned with:
- the promotion, protection and maintenance of nutrition‑related health and wellbeing at population level,
- through policy, systems, environmental and behavioural interventions,
- informed by epidemiological evidence and social determinants of health.

**Notes:** Relatively new and evolving area of practice

Focused on solving nutrition problems & developing interventions for populations

## Slide 21

- Key characteristics of PHN practice:
- Operates at population and sub‑population level, rather than individual care
- Addresses structural, social, economic and environmental determinants of diet and health
- Integrates nutrition science, public health, behavioural science and policy
- Focuses on prevention, equity and sustainability
- Requires intersectoral action (health, education, food systems, local government)
- PHN draws on principles of health promotion:“Enabling people and communities to increase control over the determinants of health and improve health outcomes.”

## Slide 22

- Prevention in Public Health Nutrition
- In public health nutrition, prevention refers to interventions implemented before disease onset, targeting upstream determinants of diet and health rather than downstream treatment.
- Defining features:
- Implemented across whole populations or defined population groups
- Focused on risk reduction, resilience and health‑enabling environments
- Uses multiple, coordinated strategies (policy, education, regulation, environmental change)
- Prioritises equity and proportionate universalism
- Evaluated over medium to long timeframes

## Slide 23

- Levels of Prevention
- The term ‘prevention’ is reserved for interventions that occur before the diagnosis of a condition/ disease is made
- Levels of prevention – conventional classification

## Slide 24

- Levels of Prevention in Public Health Nutrition
- Key distinction:Public Health Nutrition primarily operates at primary and secondary prevention, while clinical dietetics is predominantly tertiary.
| Level | Focus | Nutrition Examples |
| Primary | Prevent disease before it occurs | Food reformulation (salt/sugar), fiscal policies, mandatory fortification, school food standards |
| Secondary | Early detection & risk identification | NHS Health Checks, gestational diabetes screening, childhood obesity surveillance |
| Tertiary | Reduce complications of established disease | Dietetic management of diabetes, CVD rehabilitation programmes |

## Slide 25

- Different Modes of Nutrition Practice
- Clinical dietetics utilises scientific principles and methods in the treatment of illness, by optimising the nutritional status of individuals in an institutional environment
- Community dietetics utilises scientific principles and methods in the treatment of illness, by optimising the nutritional status of individuals in a community setting
- Community and Public Health Nutrition utilises scientific principles and practice in the promotion and prevention of nutrition related illness, by optimising the nutritional status of communities, sub-populations & whole populations

## Slide 26

- Modes of Nutrition & Dietetic Practice
- Clinical Dietetics
- Individual‑level therapeutic nutrition care
- Predominantly tertiary prevention
- Biomedical and clinical outcomes
- Typically delivered in healthcare settings
- Community Dietetics
- Individuals and small groups in community settings
- Mix of secondary and tertiary prevention
- Focus on access, behaviour change and service delivery
- Community & Public Health Nutrition
- Whole populations and sub‑populations
- Primarily primary prevention
- Focus on policy, environments, systems and inequalities
- Delivered through multi‑sectoral partnerships

## Slide 27

| Dimension | Clinical Dietetics | Community Dietetics | Public Health Nutrition |
| Unit of action | Individual | Individual / group | Population |
| Primary prevention role | Limited | Moderate | Central |
| Dominant paradigm | Illness | Risk management | Health & equity |
| Key drivers | Referral & diagnosis | Community need | Policy, evidence, population need |
| Outcome timeframe | Short–medium | Medium | Medium–long |
| Workforce | Dietitians | Dietitians | Multi‑disciplinary |

## Slide 31

- Policy & Strategy development & implementation
- Policy: Statement of what the government is trying to achieve and why. Specific principles adopted by organisations, teams etc often based on evidence but strongly influenced by politics, public opinion, and the media
- Strategy:  Steps that need to be taken for achieving the changes needed over long term.  Needs to be supported by evidence. The strategy sets out actions for various partners in public health system to support and develop policies, it is a formally planned set of actions to address a particular issue.

## Slide 32

- Policy
- In England, 63% of adults and 1 in 3 children leaving primary school are living with overweight or obesity
- Higher risk of type 2 diabetes, heart disease & certain cancers.
- Excess weight can make it difficult to find and keep work, affect self-esteem and mental health…
- Health problems associated with being overweight or obese cost the NHS an estimated £6.1 billion every year.
- By 2030, government wants to see:
- a downward trend in the level of excess weight in adults
- Halve the number of children living with excess weight

## Slide 37

- Summary
- Public Health Nutrition is a distinct, advanced area of practice addressing nutrition‑related health at population level
- It requires integration of nutrition science, epidemiology, policy, and social determinants of health
- Effective PHN practice moves upstream, focusing on prevention, equity and systems change
- Interventions must be critically evaluated in terms of impact, unintended consequences and who benefits
- Nutrition and dietetics professionals have a role beyond clinical care: influencing policy, environments and population health
