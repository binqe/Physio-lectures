# Test your knowledge - Lecture 1

Test your knowledge
1. Treatment adherence refers to:
A. Obeying the therapist’s instructions exactly
B. Following a treatment plan that has been agreed upon collaboratively
C. Performing treatment only when convenient
D. Attending appointments but not doing exercises
2. According to the WHO, approximately what percentage of patients with chronic illness adhere to their treatments?
A. 25%
B. 40%
C. 50%
D. 75%
3. Which statement best distinguishes adherence from compliance?
A. Adherence is therapist-led; compliance is patient-led
B. Adherence is collaborative; compliance is passive
C. Adherence focuses on attendance; compliance focuses on effort
D. They mean exactly the same thing
4. Which factor is not part of the Health Belief Model (HBM)?
A. Perceived severity
B. Perceived barriers
C. Self-efficacy
D. Reinforcement
5. In the Health Belief Model, “perceived susceptibility” refers to:
A. Belief about the seriousness of the condition
B. Belief about personal risk of developing the condition
C. Confidence in ability to perform an action
D. Awareness of treatment side effects
6. Which component of the Self-Determination Theory (SDT) relates to feeling capable and effective in performing a behaviour?
A. Autonomy
B. Competence
C. Relatedness
D. Self-control
7. According to SDT, which type of motivation is more sustainable?
A. Extrinsic motivation
B. Intrinsic motivation
C. Reactive motivation
D. Passive motivation
8. In the Transtheoretical Model, a patient who is planning to start an exercise program next week is in which stage?
A. Precontemplation
B. Contemplation
C. Preparation
D. Maintenance
9. Motivational Interviewing (MI) primarily aims to:
A. Convince patients to change through argument
B. Directly instruct patients to follow recommendations
C. Help patients resolve ambivalence and strengthen motivation
D. Provide cognitive restructuring for negative beliefs
10. Which of the following demonstrates rolling with resistance?
A. Arguing when the patient disagrees
B. Ignoring the patient’s concerns
C. Reflecting and acknowledging the patient’s point of view
D. Repeating instructions more forcefully
11. The “O” in the OARS technique stands for:
A. Observation
B. Open questions
C. Outcome planning
D. Objective assessment
12. Which statement about affirmations in MI is correct?
A. They are compliments about appearance
B. They reinforce patient strengths and efforts
C. They summarise the patient’s statements
D. They challenge unhelpful beliefs directly
Answer
1. Treatment adherence refers to:
A. Obeying the therapist’s instructions exactly
B. Following a treatment plan that has been agreed upon collaboratively
C. Performing treatment only when convenient
D. Attending appointments but not doing exercises
Answer: B
Rationale: Adherence means the patient’s behaviour matches agreed recommendations, emphasizing collaboration rather than obedience.
2. According to the WHO, approximately what percentage of patients with chronic illness adhere to their treatments?
A. 25%
B. 40%
C. 50%
D. 75%
Answer: C
Rationale: The WHO reports that only around 50% of patients with chronic illness adhere to long-term treatments.
3. Which statement best distinguishes adherence from compliance?
A. Adherence is therapist-led; compliance is patient-led
B. Adherence is collaborative; compliance is passive
C. Adherence focuses on attendance; compliance focuses on effort
D. They mean exactly the same thing
Answer: B
Rationale: Adherence is based on shared decision-making, whereas compliance implies passive obedience.
4. Which factor is not part of the Health Belief Model (HBM)?
A. Perceived severity
B. Perceived barriers
C. Self-efficacy
D. Reinforcement
Answer: D
Rationale: Reinforcement belongs to behavioural learning theory, not the HBM.
5. In the Health Belief Model, “perceived susceptibility” refers to:
A. Belief about the seriousness of the condition
B. Belief about personal risk of developing the condition
C. Confidence in ability to perform an action
D. Awareness of treatment side effects
Answer: B
Rationale: Perceived susceptibility is the person’s belief about how likely they are to experience illness or complications.
6. Which component of the Self-Determination Theory (SDT) relates to feeling capable and effective in performing a behaviour?
A. Autonomy
B. Competence
C. Relatedness
D. Self-control
Answer: B
Rationale: Competence reflects the person’s sense of skill and confidence in performing an activity.
7. According to SDT, which type of motivation is more sustainable?
A. Extrinsic motivation
B. Intrinsic motivation
C. Reactive motivation
D. Passive motivation
Answer: B
Rationale: Intrinsic motivation (driven by enjoyment or personal value) leads to long-term adherence and engagement.
8. In the Transtheoretical Model, a patient who is planning to start an exercise program next week is in which stage?
A. Precontemplation
B. Contemplation
C. Preparation
D. Maintenance
Answer: C
Rationale: The preparation stage involves making plans and taking small steps toward action.
9. Motivational Interviewing (MI) primarily aims to:
A. Convince patients to change through argument
B. Directly instruct patients to follow recommendations
C. Help patients resolve ambivalence and strengthen motivation
D. Provide cognitive restructuring for negative beliefs
Answer: C
Rationale: MI focuses on exploring and resolving ambivalence to enhance intrinsic motivation for change.
10. Which of the following demonstrates rolling with resistance?
A. Arguing when the patient disagrees
B. Ignoring the patient’s concerns
C. Reflecting and acknowledging the patient’s point of view
D. Repeating instructions more forcefully
Answer: C
Rationale: Rolling with resistance involves empathy and reflection, not confrontation.
11. The “O” in the OARS technique stands for:
A. Observation
B. Open questions
C. Outcome planning
D. Objective assessment
Answer: B
Rationale: OARS = Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, and Summaries.
12. Which statement about affirmations in MI is correct?
A. They are compliments about appearance
B. They reinforce patient strengths and efforts
C. They summarise the patient’s statements
D. They challenge unhelpful beliefs directly
Answer: B
Rationale: Affirmations are used to recognise and reinforce patient strengths, building self-efficacy and confidence.
