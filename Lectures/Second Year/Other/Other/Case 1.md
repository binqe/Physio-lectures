# Case 1

Clinical Case Study
Patient: PB
Age: 98
Subjective Assessment
Presenting Complaint (PC):
Patient sustained a fall at home while showering. The incident was discovered when the neighbour raised concern after not seeing the patient leave the house, prompting police attendance. Injuries sustained included a right lower rib fracture and nasal bruising.
Currently presenting with retained secretions and a productive wet cough secondary to rib fracture, associated with mild shortness of breath (SOB).
History of Presenting Complaint (HPC) / Past Medical History (PMH):
Chronic kidney disease (CKD) with end-stage renal failure; on haemodialysis (Mon/Wed/Fri)
Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM)
Hypertension (HTN)
No previous falls reported
Drug History:
Furosemide
Amlodipine
Diazepam
Ibuprofen
Paracetamol
Vitamins
Social History (SH):
Resides in a ground-floor flat (single step entry, all rooms on one level).
Owns the property.
Lives with wife, who provides assistance with activities of daily living (ADLs), cooking, and shopping. Couple are considering employing a cleaner.
Retired; enjoys watching television.
Functional baseline: Independent mobility indoors and in the community using a walking stick; occasionally rides a bicycle.
Objective Assessment
Observations: Stable as charted.
On initial assessment, patient had audible secretions during coughing and required chest physiotherapy/airway clearance prior to functional assessment. He was awake, alert, and resting in bed.
A to E Assessment:
Airway/Breathing: Own, patent; SpO₂ 93% on room air
Circulation: BP 130/90 mmHg, HR 82 bpm
Disability: GCS 15/15
Exposure: Dialysis catheter and line in situ (arm). Patient positioned comfortably in hospital bed.
Airway Clearance:
Patient attempted active cycle of breathing techniques (ACBT) with moderate success; however, huffing was ineffective. Despite this, coughing produced secretion clearance, with sputum becoming clearer as session progressed.
Functional Assessment & Mobilisation
Bed mobility: Independent; able to move from lying to sitting on the edge of the bed (SOEOB).
Transfers: Mobilised to standing with rotunda frame (RF) under close supervision (CSO1).
Mobility:
Walked 10 metres with RF before requiring rest in a following wheelchair.
Stood with RF and mobilised a further 10 metres back to bed, CSO1.
Transfers post-mobility: Independently transferred from standing to SOEOB, and SOEOB to lying.
Patient left lying comfortably in bed with call bell within reach.
Impression / Diagnosis/ Review
Patient is currently functioning below baseline. Previously mobilising independently with a walking stick, he now requires a rotunda frame but demonstrates good potential to return to stick use in the near future. Bed transfers remain independent.
Secretions are manageable with basic airway clearance strategies and expected to improve as the rib fracture heals. With continued physiotherapy input, the patient is likely to achieve safe discharge in approximately one week.
