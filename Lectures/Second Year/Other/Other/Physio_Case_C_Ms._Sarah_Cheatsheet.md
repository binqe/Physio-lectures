CASE C — Ms. Sarah Collins
58-year-old Female · Non-CF | Bronchiectasis Exacerbation · Admitted with increased cough
Target: 70%+ | Physiotherapy
I PROBLEM LIST
• Excess retained sputum in bronchiectatic airways
• Increased sputum viscosity and purulence
• Airflow obstruction secondary to mucus plugging
• Impaired mucociliary clearance
• Reduced respiratory muscle endurance
• Generalised deconditioning from infection/inflammation
• Exertional dyspnoea — reduced exercise tolerance
• Impaired ability to self-manage ACT effectively
• Fatigue limiting ADL performance
• Disrupted sleep from nocturnal cough
• Reduced participation in community activities
I RED FLAGS
• I Haemoptysis (frank blood in sputum)
• Indicates possible erosion of bronchial artery — a known
life-threatening complication of bronchiectasis. Volume matters:
streaking vs. massive haemoptysis carry different urgency.
• Action: Stop ACT immediately. Do NOT perform percussion or
vigorous expiratory techniques. Notify medical team immediately.
• I SpOI <92% at rest / Acute severe dyspnoea
• Suggests acute respiratory failure — possible from mucus plugging,
pneumothorax, or pneumonia superimposed on bronchiectasis.
Physiotherapy cannot proceed safely.
• Action: Sit patient upright. Apply supplemental OI as per
prescription. Call medical emergency.
• I Systemic sepsis (fever >38.5°C, confusion, hypotension,
tachycardia >120 bpm)
• Severe infective exacerbation requiring immediate medical
management. The patient is not safe for active physiotherapy in this
state.
