Qualitative Research Methods
• Qualitative research is a type of research that explores and provides deeper 
insights into real-world problems.
• Instead of collecting numerical data points or intervening or introducing 
treatments, just like in quantitative research, qualitative research helps 
generate hypotheses to further investigate and understand quantitative data.
• Qualitative research gathers participants' experiences, perceptions, and 
behaviour. It answers the how's and whys instead of how many or how much.

Qualitative Research Methods
• Qualitative research involves collecting and analysing non numerical data (e.g. 
text, video or audio) to understand concepts, opinions or experiences
• It can be used to gather in depth insights into a problem or generate current 
ideas for research
• Qualitative research is commonly used in the humanities and social sciences

Research Question – PEO Framework
• Qualitative research, at its core, asks open-ended questions whose answers 
are not easily put into numbers, such as "how" and "why.
• Due to the open-ended nature of the research questions, qualitative research 
design is often not linear like quantitative design.
• The PEO framework is useful for building research questions that have a 
qualitative focus.

## Page 27

Phenomenology
- Definition:
- Study of the meaning of phenomena or the study of the particular
- Investigate experiences from the individual's perspective
- Phenomenology is looking into the LIVED EXPERIENCE of the participants and aims to examine 
how and why participants behave in a certain way from their perspective
Understanding lived experiences and how individuals perceive a phenomenon
- Key Features:
- In-depth interviews to explore subjective experiences
- Bracketing to reduce researcher bias
- Open ended questionnaires

## Page 28

Ground Theory
- Definition: 
- Generation of a theoretical model through the experience of observing a study population and 
developing a comparative analysis of their speech and behaviour 
- Developing theories from systematically collected data
- Goal: explain how and why an event occurs or how and why people might behave a certain way
- Key Features:
Inductive approach – theories emerge from data (bottom up)
Constant comparison – data is analysed throughout. 
Collecting data from interviews or observations
Analysing to identify patterns and themes

Ethnography (understanding through experiences)
• Ethnography as a research design originates in social and cultural anthropology and involves the 
researcher being immersed in the participants environment.
• Through immersion, the ethnographer can use a variety of data collection techniques to produce an 
account of the social phenomena
• Researches aim immerse themselves into the research population and come out of it with accounts 
of actions, behaviours, events etc
• Direct involvement of the research with the target population is one benefit of ethnographic 
research
• Involves observing participants in their natural environment
• Can use in depth interviews to understanding

## Page 31

Case Study
• Definition: 
In-depth analysis of a single case or small group within a real-life context. 
Investigate a single individual or group of individuals to gain an in depth understanding of their 
experiences, behaviours, or outcomes
• Key Features: 
Uses multiple data sources (e.g., interviews, documents). 
Can be exploratory, explanatory, or descriptive.

Narrative Research
• Definition: 
Collecting and analysing life stories and personal experiences. 
Reporting on qualitative research involves including details and descriptions of the setting 
involved and quotes from participants
• Key Features: 
Chronological or thematic storytelling analysis

Action Research
• Definition: 
A collaborative approach where researchers and participants work together to solve a problem. 
• Key Features: 
• Cyclical process: Planning →Action →Observation →Reflection. 
• Stakeholder involvement for real-world impact.

## Page 35

In Depth Interviews
• Guided conversation
• Qualitative interviews are one of the most widely used methods in qualitative research
• They involve direct interaction between a researcher and a participants to gather detailed 
information about individual experiences, beliefs and perspectives
• Interviews can take place in person, phone, video
• Depending on the studys aims, they may follow a structured format with pre determined questions, 
a semi structured format with flexible prompts or unstructured  that allows open conversation
• Interviews are effective for exploring personal narratives and understanding emotional topics

## Page 36

Sampling for in depth interviews
• Aim is to identify 'key informants'
• Goal is breadth not representativeness
• Size varies depending on the complexity of the inquiry
• Determined by theoretical saturation (point at which no new concepts emerge from the data)

## Page 39

Focus Groups
• Focus groups are most useful when the goal is to understand how opinions 
form and evolve through discussion.
• They are effective for exploring collective experiences, testing concepts or 
materials, and identifying themes that reflect group consensus or 
disagreement.
• Group of people with certain  characteristics generate narrative data in a 
focused discussion

## Page 40

Observations
• Observations involve systematically watching and recording behaviours, interactions, or 
events as they occur in their natural context.
• This method helps researchers understand how people act and interact in real situations 
rather than relying only on self-reported accounts.
• Observations can be structured, where the researcher follows a specific checklist or 
framework, or unstructured, allowing events to unfold naturally.
• They are valuable for studying patterns of behaviour, social dynamics, and environmental 
influences that participants themselves may not articulate in interviews or surveys.

## Page 41

Case Studies
• Case studies involve an in-depth examination of a single case or a small number of cases 
within their real-life context.
• A “case” can refer to an individual, group, organization, community, or event.
• This method provides a holistic understanding of complex issues by combining multiple 
sources of qualitative data, such as interviews, observations, and document analysis.
• Case studies are particularly useful when researchers aim to describe, explain, or interpret 
phenomena that cannot be separated from their surrounding circumstances.

## Page 42

Document and Content Analysis
• Document and content analysis is best used when researchers aim to understand how 
meaning is constructed in texts or other communicative materials.
• It is appropriate for studies focused on discourse, representation, or cultural values as 
reflected in language and imagery.
• This method is also suitable when time, access, or ethical considerations make direct data 
collection from participants impractical.
•
Researchers often use it to complement interviews or observations, providing background 
context or evidence to support other findings.

Section A: Research Aims
