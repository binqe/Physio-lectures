## Slide 3

- What do we mean by “restrictive” vs “infective”?
- Tue 3 Feb · 10:00–11:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Restrictive patterns (mechanics)
- Reduced total lung capacity (TLC) → reduced vital capacity (VC) and smaller operating volumes.
- Often preserved or ↑ FEV1/FVC ratio; confirm restriction with lung volumes.
- Causes may be parenchymal (ILD/fibrosis) or extra-pulmonary (chest wall, obesity, neuromuscular, pleural).
- Clinical consequences and physio impact:  ↑ work of breathing, exertional desaturation, exercise intolerance.
- Infective lung disease (gas exchange + inflammation)
- Alveolar/airway inflammation + exudate → impaired ventilation, diffusion and V/Q matching.
- Examples: community-acquired pneumonia, aspiration pneumonia, bronchiectasis exacerbation, tuberculosis.
- Systemic response: fever, tachycardia, raised inflammatory markers; severe cases may progress to sepsis/respiratory failure.
- Clinical consequences and Physio impact: mobilisation tolerance, secretion load, oxygen needs, fatigue.

## Slide 5

- Interstitial lung disease (ILD)
- refers to a group of over 100 chronic lung disorders characterized by inflammation and irreversible scarring (fibrosis) of the lung tissue, specifically the alveoli, which impairs oxygen transfer to the bloodstream.
- Common types include idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), sarcoidosis, and hypersensitivity pneumonitis. Key symptoms include progressive, exertional shortness of breath, a dry, non-productive cough, and severe fatigue.
- Causes and Risk Factors
- Environmental/Occupational Exposure: Long-term exposure to silica, asbestos, coal dust, or mold can lead to ILD.
- Autoimmune Diseases: Conditions like rheumatoid arthritis, scleroderma, and lupus.
- Medications and Radiation: Certain drugs (e.g., chemotherapy, heart medications) and chest radiation.
- Idiopathic: Often, no clear cause is identified (e.g., in IPF).
- Risk Factors: Primarily affects adults, with risk increasing with age.
- Symptoms and Diagnosis
- Symptoms: Shortness of breath (especially with activity), a dry, hacking cough, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, and sometimes clubbing (widening) of fingers or toes.
- Diagnosis: Often involves high-resolution CT scans (HRCT),
- lung function tests (spirometry)
- blood tests to rule out autoimmune causes, and occasionally a lung biopsy.

## Slide 6

- Stages and Prognosis
- ILD is not typically staged in a simple, linear manner, but rather categorized by its progression, which can be rapid or gradual.
- Some forms are progressive and lead to respiratory failure, with IPF having a median survival of 2.5 to 3.5 years if left untreated, according to a report in JAMA.
- Treatment and ManagementWhile most damage is irreversible, treatment focuses on reducing inflammation, slowing progression, and managing symptoms.
- Medication:
- Immunosuppressants
- anti-inflammatory agents
- and antifibrotic medications (specifically for IPF).
- Supportive Therapy ( mostly Physio): Supplemental oxygen, pulmonary rehabilitation, and smoking cessation.
- Advanced Cases: In severe, progressive cases, a lung transplant may be considered.

## Slide 9

- Clinical clues: restrictive vs infective (at the bedside)
- Tue 3 Feb · 10:00–11:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Key contrasts (high-yield)
- Domain
- Typical features / examples
- Onset
- Restrictive: often gradual (ILD) or chronic; Infective: acute–subacute (days) or exacerbation pattern.
- Cough/sputum
- Restrictive ILD: usually dry; Infective: productive sputum common (but not universal).
- Auscultation
- Restrictive ILD: fine end-inspiratory crackles; Infective: coarse crackles, bronchial breathing, wheeze if airway involvement.
- Systemic features
- Restrictive: weight loss/fatigue possible; Infective: fever/rigors, raised CRP/WCC.
- Oxygenation
- Both may desaturate; infective disease may show rapidly changing O2 requirement.
- Imaging
- Restrictive: reticulation/ground-glass on HRCT; Infective: consolidation on CXR/CT.

## Slide 10

- Wrap-up: what should you remember?
- Tue 3 Feb · 10:00–11:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Mechanisms
- Restriction = volume problem (↓TLC) often driven by ↓ compliance or reduced chest expansion.
- Infection = gas exchange and systemic inflammation problem; can coexist with chronic disease.
- Clinical pattern recognition is useful, but objective tests and imaging confirm diagnosis.
- Clinical priorities for physiotherapists
- Identify red flags and escalate early (deteriorating oxygenation, RR/HR, confusion).
- Optimise oxygenation and mobilisation tolerance; manage secretions when present.
- Use structured assessment and document response to interventions.
