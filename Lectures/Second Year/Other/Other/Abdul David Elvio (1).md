## Slide 2

- Summary of the podcasts
- BBC World Service “Health Talks” podcast (2000-2001)
- This podcast aims to explain how culture, environment, and government policy shape the health challenges faced by different countries. Its goal is to educate listeners and raise awareness about significant global health events from the recent past.
- The target audience is professionals and specialists interested in worldwide health issues, such as public health workers, researchers, policy makers, and aid workers.
- ZOE Science and Nutrition podcast
- podcast aims to translate complex health science into simple, practical advice for daily life. It focuses heavily on giving listeners actionable tips they can use to improve their diet and lifestyle immediately.
- The target audience is the general public anyone interested in building healthier habits and willing to make real changes, attracted by the podcast's clear and accessible explanation of otherwise complicated topics.

## Slide 3

- Scientific evidence base – podcast 1
- This 2000 episode provided a credible journalistic report on Cuba's healthcare at the time, mixing official data with personal stories. It used verified statistics, like Cuba's high number of doctors, alongside powerful real-life accounts to show both the system's successes and its struggles with shortages.
- While engaging and historically useful, the report is not scientifically rigorous by today's standards. Its evidence is largely anecdotal and its data is now 25 years old. This makes it a valuable historical snapshot, but not a reliable source for understanding Cuba's current healthcare situation.

## Slide 4

- Scientific evidence base - podcast 2
- This podcast aims to empower listeners by translating the latest nutritional science into practical advice for improving personal health and weight management. It establishes strong credibility by exclusively featuring leading experts and doctors as its authoritative voices.
- The information presented is backed by high-quality scientific research. This includes randomized controlled trials (considered the gold standard of evidence), large-scale population studies involving hundreds of thousands of people, and in-depth expert interviews. The focus is on providing listeners with reliable, actionable knowledge they can trust and use.

- Podcast 2
- The podcast's core purpose is to make complex scientific research on nutrition and weight loss easy to understand for the general public. It successfully achieves this by breaking down advanced topics into simple, clear language.
- This approach effectively builds a strong foundation of crucial health knowledge for listeners who are not experts or regular readers of scientific literature. The success of this simplified format is noted by users and professional reviewers, confirming that the information is genuinely easier to grasp.

## Slide 6

- Key take home messages
- Podcast 1:
- Healthcare is a relatable topic of discussion, problems such as disease and pollution can affect us all to an extent.
- Wealth is not the sole determinant of health, there are many cases of “wealthier” countries having problems with managing the rising rates of obesity while other lesser developed countries can have a great standard for healthcare yet have a shortage of imported medicine.
- Location of residence heavily impact your health and the quality of healthcare you may receive
- The way in which a countries healthcare system is set up reflects upon the societies  priorities and politics.
- Podcast 2:
- Nutrition is a universal aspect of our lives, and the choices we make about food impact everyone's well-being.
- There is no single perfect diet for everyone, as individual needs and responses to food can vary significantly.
- The quality of your diet is more important than any single nutrient, with a focus on whole, diverse foods being a common foundation for good health.
- The way we approach our diet reflects our personal priorities and lifestyle, showing that health is built through consistent daily habits.
