## Slide 3

- Assessment framework: what do we need to know?
- Tue 3 Feb · 11:00–12:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Subjective assessment (history)
- Presenting symptoms: dyspnoea, cough (dry vs productive), chest pain, haemoptysis, fever.
- Timeline and triggers: gradual vs acute; recent infection contacts; aspiration risk.
- Exposures: smoking/vaping, occupational (dusts), drugs (e.g., amiodarone), birds/moulds.
- Past history: ILD, bronchiectasis, immunosuppression, cardiac disease, neuromuscular weakness.
- Function: ADLs, stairs, sleep, fatigue; baseline vs current mobility.
- Objective assessment (bedside)
- Observation: posture, accessory muscle use, breathing pattern, ability to speak, cough effectiveness.
- Vitals: RR, HR, BP, temperature; oxygen delivery device and FiO2/flow.
- Oxygenation/ventilation: SpO2 trends; consider ABG if hypercapnia risk.
- Chest assessment: expansion, palpation, percussion (where trained), auscultation (crackles, bronchial sounds).
- Mobility screen: sit-to-stand, short walk test, exertional desaturation response.

## Slide 6

- Investigations: what you might see (and why it matters)
- Tue 3 Feb · 11:00–12:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Respiratory investigations
- Spirometry + lung volumes + DLCO: confirm restriction and suggest parenchymal vs extra-pulmonary patterns.
- Imaging: CXR for consolidation; HRCT for ILD patterns and extent; ultrasound as locally available.
- ABG/VBG: oxygenation and ventilation; identify hypercapnia and acid–base issues.
- Microbiology: sputum culture, viral swabs where indicated (esp. moderate/high severity).
- Differential diagnosis “watch-outs”
- Cardiac causes (HF, ACS) can mimic breathlessness + crackles.
- Pulmonary embolism: pleuritic pain, tachycardia, disproportionate hypoxia.
- Anaemia/deconditioning: dyspnoea with normal lung exam in some cases.
- Upper airway dysfunction (VCD), anxiety/hyperventilation in select presentations.

## Slide 7

- Community-acquired pneumonia: severity stratification
- Tue 3 Feb · 11:00–12:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- CURB-65 (hospital) – use with clinical judgement
- NICE NG250 (2025) retains CURB-65 as a validated tool for adult CAP in hospital
- C
- Confusion (AMTS ≤8)
- U
- Urea >7 mmol/L
- R
- Respiratory rate ≥30/min
- B
- BP <90 systolic or ≤60 diastolic
- 65
- Age ≥65 years
- Typical severity bands (NG250 visual summary):
- Score 0–1: usually low severity → consider home management + safety-netting (if suitable).
- Score 2: usually moderate severity → consider virtual ward / hospital-at-home / inpatient (shared decision, system-dependent).
- Score ≥3: usually high severity → hospital admission; consider critical care assessment as appropriate.
- Physio implication: prioritise early mobilisation, oxygenation monitoring, and escalation if deteriorating.

## Slide 8

- Differential diagnosis: dyspnoea + cough (quick map)
- Tue 3 Feb · 11:00–12:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Key contrasts (high-yield)
- Domain
- Typical features / examples
- Dry cough + fine crackles
- Consider ILD/fibrosis; check exposure history, HRCT, DLCO.
- Fever + productive cough
- Consider pneumonia/exacerbation; check vitals, CXR, microbiology; assess severity.
- Copious sputum + recurrent infections
- Consider bronchiectasis; consider ACT needs and culture history.
- Orthopnoea + oedema
- Consider heart failure; BNP/echo if indicated.
- Sudden pleuritic pain + tachycardia
- Consider PE; urgent medical review.
- Hypercapnia risk features
- Consider neuromuscular/chest wall/obesity hypoventilation; ABG/VBG; cough augmentation.
