## Slide 4

- Module Overview
- Aim: to develop your ability to understand, interpret, and critically appraise research evidence used in physiotherapy practice.
- In this module you will:
- Develop an understanding of scientific enquiry and how research evidence is generated
- Explore quantitative and qualitative research designs commonly used in healthcare
- Learn how data are collected, analysed and interpreted in different types of research
- Practice using critical appraisal frameworks to evaluate research quality and trustworthiness
- Develop skills to judge whether research findings are appropriate, valid and relevant to practice
- Explore how clinical audit and quality assurance contribute to service evaluation
- Consider ethical and governance frameworks that underpin clinical research and evaluation

## Slide 19

- What is Research?
- Research is what we do when we have a question or a problem we want to resolve
- We may already think we know the answer to our question already
- We may think the answer is obvious, common sense even
- But until we have subjected our problem to rigorous scientific scurrility, our ‘knowledge’ remains little more than guesswork or at best, intuition.

## Slide 20

- What is Research?
- The first priority is to formulate your research question
- Then figure out how you are going to answer it
- How have others answered it?
- How does your proposal fit in with what others have done?
- How will you know when you have answered it?
- Then you can present your answer

## Slide 21

- Clinical Research
- The ultimate goal of clinical research is to maximise the effectiveness of practice
- Clinical research is essential to inform clinical judgement
- We MUST exercise a commitment to scientific discovery that will lead to improvements in standards of care and patient outcomes
- The importance of engaging in collaborative and interprofessional efforts cannot be over emphasised
- (Gross Portney, 2020)

## Slide 22

- Clinical Research – Three Parts
- Patient oriented research: studies conducted with human subjects to improve our understanding of the mechanisms of diseases and disorders, and of what therapeutic intervention  will be most effective in treating them
- Epidemiological and behavioural studies: Observational studies focused on describing patterns of disease and disability
- Outcome research and health service research: studies to determine the impact of research on a population health and utilisation of evidence-based therapeutic interventions
- (Gross Portney, 2020)

## Slide 24

- Quantitative Research – Assignment 1
- Quantifies relationships between two or more variables
- Tests a hypothesis
- Measures outcomes using numerical data under standardised conditions
- Quantitative information may be obtained using formal instruments that address physical, behavioural or physiological parameters or putting subjective information into an objective numerical scale
- To measure, count and quantity a problem

## Slide 25

- Qualitative Research – Assignment 2
- Explores issues, underlying reasons, motivations
- Identified new concepts and theories
- To gain a detailed understanding or explanation of underlying reasons, beliefs and motivations
- E.g. why, how, who, when?
- In qualitative methodology ‘measurement’ is based on subjective, narrative information which can be observed using
- Focus groups
- Interviews
- Observation
- Analysis of narrative data is based on thick description to identify themes
- Qualitative research is considered descriptive in that it involves collection of data through interviews and observations

## Slide 27

- Types of Research
- Research can be viewed along a continuum that reflects the type of question the research is intending to answer
- The research continuum is not hierarchical – each type of research fulfils a particular purpose and need
- Systematic Reviews:
- Present a comprehensive analysis of the full range of literature on a particular topic. These studies look at methodological quality of studies to frame the state of knowledge on a topic
- Meta-analysis:
- Goes a step beyond systematic review, applying a process of statistically combing the findings from several studies to obtain a summary analysis
- Scoping Review:
- Incorporates a broader range of study designs and data gathering methods to comprehensively synthesise evidence often with the aims of informing policy, practice, programming and providing directions to future research

## Slide 34

- Evidence- Based Practice
- EBP requires that decisions about health care are based on the best available, current, valid and relevant evidence.
- These decisions should be made by those receiving care, informed by the explicit knowledge of those providing care, within the context of available resources
- Integration of best research evidence with clinical expertise and patient values
- As physiotherapists we have a commitment to use the best available evidence to inform decision making about the care of individuals that involves integrating physiotherapy practices and individual professional judgement with evidence gained through systematic research (Veras, Kairy and Paquet, 2016)
- Clinical reasoning and decision making is based on the best available evidence and the integration of professional practice expertise with patient values.

## Slide 35

- Why is EBP so important?
- To underpin and shape how the profession delivers patient care
- To promote  the value and impact of UK physiotherapy contribution to meeting population and patient needs in a clinically / cost effective way
- To inform the development of physiotherapy practice, service delivery and education
- To progress the professions approach to developing, using and promoting research and its contribution to generating new evidence, knowledge and service improvement

## Slide 36

- Chartered Society Physiotherapy Expectations
- Members need to critically engage with available and emerging evidence that relates to professional activity and service delivery
- Members need to critically appraise their practice in light of available and emerging evidence
- Members need to contribute to identifying evidence gaps and priorities for new research

- Research Process

## Slide 39

- Identify the Research Question
- Select a topic of interest:
- Identify the problem: Clinical experience, Clinical Theory, Professional Literature
- Identify the rationale: Research Rationale, Theoretical Framework, Purpose
- Identify the type of research: Qualitative OR Quantitative
- Define the Research Question: PICO
- P: Population: What is the target population or problem of interest?
- I: What intervention is being studied?
- C: Will you study any comparison variables?
- O: What are the outcomes that you will study?

- PICO Question
- Example: In people with chronic back pain, is spinal manipulation more effective than light massage in reducing pain and functional disability
- P: Population: Chronic Back Pain
- I: Intervention: Spinal Manipulation
- C: Comparison: Light massage
- O: Reducing pain and functional disability
