## Slide 3

- Management principles (all respiratory patients)
- Tue 3 Feb · 12:00–13:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Core goals
- Optimise gas exchange (positioning, pacing, oxygen liaison) and reduce work of breathing.
- Maintain or restore mobility and function; prevent deconditioning.
- Support effective cough and secretion management when needed.
- Educate for self-management, pacing, energy conservation, and safe escalation.
- Safety & monitoring
- Baseline + continuous/interval monitoring: SpO2 trends, HR, RR, BP, symptoms (Borg).
- Stop/modify if: severe desaturation, chest pain, syncope/presyncope, significant arrhythmia, severe distress.
- Infection control: PPE, equipment cleaning, cough etiquette; consider isolation for TB/viral infections.
- Work within scope + local oxygen/alert policies; escalate concerns early.

## Slide 4

- Restrictive lung disease (e.g., ILD/fibrosis): physio focus
- Tue 3 Feb · 12:00–13:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Breathlessness + ventilation strategies
- Breathing control and paced breathing during exertion; optimise posture and thoracic mobility.
- Energy conservation: break tasks down; rest breaks; prioritise function over “perfect” technique.
- If desaturation: titrate activity intensity, liaise for oxygen assessment per local protocol.
- Education: symptom monitoring, when to stop, and when to seek help.
- Exercise rehabilitation (evidence-based)
- Pulmonary rehabilitation improves exercise capacity and symptoms in chronic respiratory disease; evidence base expanding for ILD/IPF.
- Include aerobic + resistance training, individualised to symptoms and oxygenation response.
- Use interval training if severe breathlessness/desaturation limits continuous exercise.
- Measure outcomes (e.g., 6MWT/ISWT) and reassess regularly.

## Slide 5

- Infective disease (pneumonia / exacerbations): physio focus
- Tue 3 Feb · 12:00–13:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Acute phase priorities
- Early mobilisation (as tolerated) to improve ventilation, reduce complications, and support function.
- Breathing exercises / thoracic expansion exercises when appropriate; optimise positioning.
- Airway clearance only when secretion retention is a problem (productive cough, atelectasis risk, weak cough).
- Coordinate with MDT: analgesia for pleuritic pain, hydration, medication timing.
- Preventing deterioration
- Monitor response: RR/HR, SpO2 and effort during mobility; watch for increasing oxygen needs.
- Escalate if: worsening hypoxia, rising work of breathing, hypotension, new confusion, inability to mobilise.
- Consider frailty and baseline function when setting goals and discharge criteria.
- Education: sputum management, breathing pacing, hydration, and safety-netting.

## Slide 6

- Airway clearance techniques (ACTs): indications and selection
- Tue 3 Feb · 12:00–13:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Common ACT options
- ACBT (breathing control + thoracic expansion + huff) – widely used and adaptable.
- PEP / OPEP devices – can assist secretion clearance in bronchiectasis.
- Autogenic drainage – technique requiring coaching and practice.
- Adjuncts: positioning, manual techniques (where appropriate), exercise as an ACT.
- Choosing and dosing ACTs
- Match to patient: secretion load, airflow limitation, fatigue, cognition, and preference/adherence.
- Set measurable goals: sputum volume, ease of expectoration, breathlessness, auscultation changes.
- Review and adapt—ACTs should be taught, assessed and optimised over time.
- Precautions: haemoptysis, pneumothorax risk, unstable cardiovascular status, raised ICP (context dependent).
