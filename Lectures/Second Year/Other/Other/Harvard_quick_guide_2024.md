# Harvard quick guide 2024

Harvard Referencing Quick Guide August 2024
Harvard referencing can vary in format. This guide provides examples based on the Cite Them Right manual, also available online. Check with your tutor and/or module handbook for further guidance on the referencing requirements for your assignments.
Please see the last page of this document for important Harvard referencing exceptions.
IMPORTANT - Exceptions to Cite them Right Harvard online
Authors/editors, 4 or more, and et al.
•	in the in-text citation, if you have four or more authors, list the first author’s surname followed by et al. (this is Latin for ‘and others’). et al. must be in italics followed by a full stop , e.g. (Carrabine et al., 2020).
•	In the reference list, you must list all the authors’ names. Do not use et al.
•	Use, and, not & (ampersand) in your reference list, to link the last 2 authors.
Use Single quotation marks for directly quoted text
When you quote directly from a work, word-for-word put ‘single quotation’ marks around the directly quoted text. Do not use double quotation marks, or speech marks.
Using acronyms
Organisations, departments, international institutions’ acronyms are useful, as it helps ease your word count.
•	If you use the same organisational source multiple times in your work, it might be useful to abbreviate the organisations name. Check that the abbreviation is acceptable and in common use – do not make it up.
•	In your in-text citation, at the first mention of the source in your work write the organisation’s name out in full. Then, in round brackets, the initials or acronym e.g.: National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE), Joseph Rowntree Foundation (JRF), Department for Education (DfE), United Nations Human Rights Office of the Commissioner (OHCHR).
For the second and subsequent mentions, you can use the acronyms/ initials only in your in-text citation e.g.: (NICE, 2018), (JRF, 2024), (DfE, 2021), (OHCHR, 2024).
In the reference list, write out the full name and include the acronym in round brackets, regardless of how many mentions of that organisation.
Secondary Referencing, Secondary citing or one author citing another.
DO NOT put the authors cited in the article, book or source you are reading, into your work if you have not read the original text.
You must:
find the original to read yourself
or
find someone else who has made the same point and cite them
As a last resort, you could use cited in, if paraphrased, or quoted in, if it is a direct quote from the text. You must put a page number for a quote or paraphrase.
