## Page 9

What is an 
outcome 
measure? 
Outcome measures generate scores that are intended to quantify a 
patient’s performance or health status based on standardized 
evaluation protocols or close-ended questions (Jette et al. 2009)

## Page 10

What can be measured or quantified?
• Objective measures – measuring 
something that can be observed
• E.g., height, weight, range of 
movement (ROM), muscle 
strength, behaviour
• Subjective measures – 
measuring something that the 
patient/individual reports to you
• e.g. pain, pins & needles 
(paresthesia), mood, psychological 
state

## Page 11

Types of 
Outcome 
measures
• Performance based measures 
•
Patient must be observed performing tasks 
•
Measured quantitatively e.g., time taken 
•
Measured qualitatively e.g., performed independently, required some 
assistance, unable to perform 
•
Measures capacity not habitual performance (i.e. - what patient can do) right 
now (at a particular point in time)
• Observer reported measures
•
Measures completed by another person e.g., parent, carer, partner 
•
May indicate habitual performance better (i.e. - what patient does do)
• Self-report or patient reported outcomes (PROs)
•
Subjective experience is quantified 
•
Measures habitual performance (i.e. - what patient does do)
• Clinician reported measures
•
 Measures completed by a health care professional
• Disease specific or generic

## Page 12

Psychometric 
properties of 
Outcome 
measures
RELIABILITY – stability over time if nothing has 
changed
VALIDITY – a tool measures what it intends to 
measure
RESPONSIVENESS – can detect changes in conditions 
or differentiate between groups
STANDARDISED outcome measures – standardise the 
assessment/measurement process to enhance the 
validity, reliability and hence the responsiveness

## Page 20

Purpose of the 
measure
• Generally, OMs are used for one of 3 reasons:
• Discriminant
• to distinguish between individuals or groups based 
on a particular characteristic
• Predictive
• to forecast future status and can be used to 
identify risk, determine a prognosis, and plan 
discharge e.g., Berg Balance scale
• Evaluative
• Determine the effectiveness of an intervention by 
evaluating change over time. e.g., BBS, gait speed

## Page 21

Type of measure
• Outcome measures can also be classified according to focus (generic or disease-specific) and 
mode of administration
Generic vs Specific
• Generic OMs – used across all patient populations regardless of specific health condition 
e.g., Barthel Index, SF-36, Functional Independence measure (FIM)
• Specific e.g., Parkinson Disease Questionnaire -39 (PDQ -39)
Performance-based vs self-reported
• Performance – patient performance in a specific environment – Berg Balance Scale
• Self-report OMs – patients' opinions on impact of condition – Activities Specific Balance 
Confidence scale

• Later – measures of participation
• Patient’s capability – not too difficult – ceiling and flooring effects

e.g., FIM

Domain
Evaluation
Questions to ask
Aim of the outcome measure
•
Is there information about why and 
how the measure was developed
•
How is it scored
What does the instrument plan to measure and 
wa it originally designed for that purpose?
Content
Describe the Outcome measure
User centredness
•
Whose perspective does it capture?
•
What domains are covered?
•
What is the method of 
administration?
•
Does the measure capture outcomes that are 
required by various users?
•
Are the outcomes meaningful to the 
patient/client, the healthcare professional, a 
carer, the service provider or commissioner?
•
Is the measure an observation of 
performance, a measure of opinions/attitudes 
or a self-reported measure of impact or 
performance
•
How does the scoring relate to the raw 
opinion or performance? Are there any 
distortions or biases present in the way the 
information is captured?
