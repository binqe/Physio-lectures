# Problem Solving Essay 25

PT5000 Problem Solving Essay
BSc 2025-2026
Patient Information
You are a physiotherapy student on placement in a primary care MSK clinic.
Your supervisor asks you to assess Mr. Aldo Turner, a 47-year-old warehouse worker, referred by his GP for acute low back pain.
Patient Interview
Mr. Turner reports that he developed sudden onset lower back pain two weeks ago after lifting a heavy box at work. The pain was sharp initially and now feels like a constant dull ache, rated 6/10 at rest and 8/10 with movement. The pain is mainly across the lower lumbar area and sometimes radiates into both buttocks and thighs but not below the knees.
He denies any specific trauma or fall. He reports stiffness in the morning lasting about 30 minutes, which eases with gentle movement. Coughing and sneezing increase his pain. He describes difficulty sleeping and limited ability to work.
When asked about general health, he mentions he’s had occasional night sweats over the past few weeks and has unintentionally lost about 4 kg in the last two months, which he attributes to “working harder and not eating properly.” He denies fever or recent infections. He has a past history of prostate issues but cannot recall the exact diagnosis and hasn’t followed up recently. He takes over-the-counter ibuprofen for pain with limited effect.
Objective Assessment
Upon observation he showed a slight forward-flexed posture, guarded movements.
He confirmed tenderness over L4–S1 region, mild paraspinal spasm.
AROM Flexion limited to 40°, pain at end range. Extension limited to 10°, reproduces right lumbar pain. Side flexion: painful to the right. Rotation (sitting) 30° bilaterally, Tightness only, no reproduction of sharp pain.
PROM: Lumbar flexion and extension restricted due to muscle guarding.
Passive accessory intervertebral movements (PAIVMs): Hypomobile and painful at L4–L5, stiffness noted at L5–S1.
MMT: Gluteus Maximus, bilateral 4+/5, Prone trunk extension (global extensors): able to lift chest off the plinth and hold for 10 seconds before pain limits endurance. Rectus abdominis: completed 10 reps, mild discomfort at lower back from rep 6 onward.
Straight leg raise test: 70° on right, 80° on left — no reproduction of leg pain
Neurological screen (L2–S1): Normal myotomes, dermatomes and reflexes. -ve Babinski test and Hoffman sign.
Vital signs: BP 128/82, HR 78 bpm, afebrile
No saddle anaesthesia
Gait: mildly antalgic but independent
No obvious structural deformity
