I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• In HFrEF secondary to ischaemic heart disease , prior myocardial infarction has caused irreversible loss of contractile myocardium — replaced by
non-compliant fibrous scar tissue.
• This reduces the left ventricle's stroke volume and ejection fraction (typically <40%), meaning the heart pumps inadequate blood forward to meet the
body's metabolic demands despite normal or elevated venous return.
• While initially adaptive, chronic RAAS activation causes progressive fluid overload , increasing both preload and afterload on the already failing
ventricle — perpetuating the cycle of deterioration.
• The elevated left ventricular end-diastolic pressure is transmitted retrogradely through the left atrium to the pulmonary veins, raising pulmonary capillary
hydrostatic pressure .
• When this exceeds the plasma oncotic pressure (approximately 25 mmHg), fluid leaks from pulmonary capillaries into the interstitium and — in severe
decompensation — into the alveolar spaces.
• This pulmonary oedema impairs gas diffusion, causes V/Q mismatch, reduces lung compliance, and substantially increases the work of breathing.
