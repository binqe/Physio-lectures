CASE D — Mr. John Harris
72-year-old Male · HFrEF — | Decompensated · Secondary to Ischaemic Heart Disease
Target: 70%+ | Physiotherapy
I PROBLEM LIST
• Reduced cardiac output (impaired EF)
• Pulmonary oedema — impaired alveolar gas exchange
• Bilateral peripheral oedema — reduced ankle mobility
• Orthopnoea — positional dyspnoea
• Skeletal muscle deconditioning / cardiac cachexia
• Impaired peripheral oxygen delivery
• Severely reduced exercise tolerance
• Dyspnoea completing ADLs (showering, dressing)
• Sleep disruption from orthopnoea / PND
• Reduced walking distance / community mobility
• Social isolation and reduced quality of life
I RED FLAGS
• I Acute pulmonary oedema (sudden severe dyspnoea, SpOI
<88–90%, pink frothy sputum)
• Immediate life threat — fluid flooding the alveoli prevents effective
gas exchange. Patient may deteriorate rapidly and require NIV or
emergency intubation.
• Action: Cease all physiotherapy immediately. Sit patient fully upright.
Apply supplemental OI. Call medical emergency. Do not leave
patient alone.
• I Chest pain — new or changing (possible ACS)
• Given his ischaemic heart disease aetiology, any new chest pain —
particularly exertional — must be treated as potential ACS until ruled
out. Exercise during active ischaemia can cause fatal arrhythmia.
• Action: Stop all activity immediately. Obtain 12-lead ECG urgently.
Alert medical team. Do not resume exercise until cleared.
• I Significant arrhythmia (new AF, rapid ventricular response, VT,
HR >120 or <40 bpm at rest)
• Arrhythmias are common in HFrEF and can cause haemodynamic
compromise, especially with any physical exertion. Exercise is unsafe
until rhythm is controlled.
