I PATHOPHYSIOLOGY (KEY POINTS)
• Bronchiectasis is characterised by permanent, abnormal dilation and distortion of the bronchi , resulting from a self-perpetuating cycle of infection,
inflammation, and airway wall destruction.
• In non-CF bronchiectasis, repeated insults (infection, immune dysfunction) damage the structural components of the bronchial wall — the elastic tissue
and smooth muscle — leading to irreversible dilation.
• The dilated airways are structurally abnormal and cannot effectively propel mucus via the mucociliary escalator.
• Mucus stagnates within these pouches and pools, providing an ideal culture medium for bacterial colonisation — commonly Haemophilus influenzae ,
Pseudomonas aeruginosa , or Staphylococcus aureus .
• Progressive mucus accumulation causes segmental atelectasis and V/Q mismatch , reducing oxygenation and increasing the work of breathing.
• Physiotherapy targets the mucus retention limb of this cycle.

I OBJECTIVE ASSESSMENTS & HOW TO PERFORM
Sputum Assessment (Colour, Consistency, Volume)
What it measures: Sputum quality and bronchodilator therapy may improve secretion clearance by opening airways. It also gives me a baseline to track
improvement, which is important for discharge decisions.
Expected Auscultation:
Widespread coarse crackles — may be more prominent post-spirometry due to increased tidal volume dislodging secretions (a useful productive sign).
Possible expiratory wheeze. Diminished air entry at bases if significant atelectasis is present.
Outcome Measure:
FEVI (% predicted) — repeat at end of admission. Target return toward admission baseline or pre-exacerbation spirometry values if known.
1-Minute Sit-to-Stand Test (1MSTS)
What it measures: Functional exercise capacity and physiological response to exertion — a validated, safe alternative to the 6MWT during acute
exacerbation when a corridor walk may be too demanding.
