## Slide 4

- Safety briefing (everyone, every station)
- Tue 3 Feb · 14:00–16:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Before you start
- Confirm infection control status (droplet/airborne/contact) and wear appropriate PPE.
- Identify monitoring plan: baseline obs + triggers for stopping/escalation.
- Check lines/tubes (O2, IV, drains) and mobility aids; ensure safe environment.
- Agree communication: “stop” signal, and who will document.
- Stop / escalate if…
- SpO2 drops and does not recover with rest/positioning per local policy.
- New chest pain, syncope/presyncope, severe breathlessness, or new confusion.
- Haemodynamic instability (hypotension, significant arrhythmia) or rapidly rising RR/HR.
- Any clinician concern—use escalation pathways early.

- Presenting symptoms, onset, and current functional limitations.
- Past respiratory history, exposures, aspiration risks, meds, comorbidities.

- Use a prompt card (provided) and answer consistently.

- Mention any equipment: oxygen, inhalers, sputum pot, walking aid.
- Provide feedback on clarity and empathy of questioning.

## Slide 6

- Station 2: Objective exam (bedside)
- Tue 3 Feb · 14:00–16:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Core measures
- Vitals: RR, HR, BP, temperature; SpO2 (rest + activity).
- Breathing pattern: accessory muscles, paradoxical breathing, speech test.
- Chest movement: expansion (tape measure) and thoracic mobility.
- Auscultation: location + description (fine/coarse crackles, wheeze, bronchial).
- Interpretation
- Link findings to likely mechanisms (e.g., crackles + dry cough → consider ILD).
- Decide what additional test you would request (e.g., ABG, CXR, PFT).
- Identify if ACTs are indicated (productive cough + ineffective clearance).
- Set immediate priorities: oxygenation, mobilisation, education, escalation.

## Slide 7

- Station 3: Functional test set-up (6MWT or STS)
- Tue 3 Feb · 14:00–16:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- 6MWT (if space) – essentials
- Measure a flat 30 m (or local standard) track; place cones and lap counter.
- Standardised instructions; encouragement at set intervals only.
- Record: distance, SpO2, HR, Borg dyspnoea, symptoms; note rest stops.
- Safety: stop if severe symptoms; document reason for termination.
- Alternative: sit-to-stand
- 1-minute or 30-second STS; record reps, SpO2/HR response, symptoms.
- Standard chair height and instructions; arms position standardised.
- Use as quick inpatient screen and to set initial exercise dose.
- Compare to baseline over time rather than single “norm”.

## Slide 8

- Station 4: ACT coaching (ACBT + huff)
- Tue 3 Feb · 14:00–16:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Teach sequence
- Breathing control (relaxed diaphragmatic breathing).
- Thoracic expansion exercises (3–4 deep breaths with inspiratory hold).
- Forced expiration technique (huffs) – medium then low volume as needed.
- Repeat cycles; finish with cough if secretions are mobilised.
- Coaching tips
- Positioning for comfort and ventilation; hydrate; time around bronchodilators where applicable.
- Listen for changes: more audible secretions, easier expectoration, reduced crackles.
- Know precautions: haemoptysis, significant bronchospasm, fatigue, dizziness.
- Agree a home “minimum dose” and how to progress during exacerbation.

## Slide 9

- Station 5: Mobilisation + exercise prescription
- Tue 3 Feb · 14:00–16:00
- PT5000 · CRP 3 · BSc Physiotherapy (Year 2)
- Acute infective (ward)
- Start simple: sit out of bed, short walks, stair practice as tolerated.
- Dose with symptoms and SpO2 response; use interval walking if needed.
- Incorporate pacing, breathing control, and safe use of oxygen devices.
- Set short-term SMART goals linked to discharge needs.
- Restrictive (ILD) – rehab mindset
- Aerobic + resistance training; interval approach if symptom-limited.
- Monitor desaturation; teach pacing and recovery strategies.
- Use functional outcomes (6MWT/ISWT) to guide progression.
- Plan education: energy conservation + symptom self-monitoring.
