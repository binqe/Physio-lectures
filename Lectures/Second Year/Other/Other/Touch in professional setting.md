## Slide 4

- Introduction
- Touch is an integral part of being human and a part of human interaction; it is manifest socially, relationally, culturally and professionally.
- Health care makes considerable use of touch, and for most health professionals it is essential to their practice.

**Notes:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468781219306198

## Slide 5

- Neurophysiology of human touch
- The neurophysiological response to touch includes the release of specific chemicals and neurotransmitters that lead to neuroendocrine effects; vagal stimulation; reduction of stress, pain and depression; and enhancement of immunity.
- Affective touch also appears to lessen allostatic load (i.e. stress) in critically ill patients, due to the positive effects on pathophysiological processes aggravated by stress, such as immune and neuroendocrine derangements and inflammation.

**Notes:** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6382052/#:~:text=The%20neurophysiological%20response%20to%20touch,depression%3B%20and%20enhancement%20of%20immunity.

## Slide 6

- Types of Touch
- Generally, touch could be differentiated into three main types:
- Diagnostic/instrumental (i.e. Taking a peripheral pulse, examining the abdomen),
- Therapeutic (i.e. Applying a dressing, mobilising a joint) and
- Expressive (i.e. A demonstration of empathy, a signal of partnership).

**Notes:** Expressive touch described as unrequired spontaneous touch normally aimed at communicating compassion.

The main benefit is centred around communication of compassion and improvements in nurse-patient rapport.

**Notes:** A large amount of evidence has identified hands-on techniques as a pain 
modulator playing a role at multiple levels beyond biomechanics that 
represents only one of the possible explanations of action (e.g., change in

proposed to better clarify the mechanism of action of manual techniques 
(Bialosky et al., 2009; Testa and Rossettini, 2016). The elicitation of

additional interpretations to explain why somatosensory stimuli delivered during 
the application of a manual technique are capable to modulate the patient’s 
processing of pain condition (Bialosky et al., 2018; Rossettini et al., 2018).

## Slide 12

- Understanding Unprofessional Touch
- unprofessional touch includes any physical contact that is inappropriate, non-consensual, or could be misinterpreted as unprofessional, sexual, or invasive.
- Intent: Touch that is meant to comfort or reassure but is interpreted as invasive or inappropriate.
- Context: Touch that might be appropriate in one setting but not another (e.g., social settings vs. clinical settings).
- Duration and Area: Touch that lingers too long or involves sensitive areas of the body without clinical necessity

## Slide 16

- CSP Policy for Chaperoning
- ‘Close’ examinations defined as:
- Examinations and/or interventions that may involve close bodily contact between patient and therapist, especially where the patient may be partially undressed
- Examinations and/or interventions that may involve therapist handling of the patient close to intimate areas, the lumbo-sacral areas, and thoracic areas of female patients, especially where the patient may be partially undressed
- Examinations involving complete removal of a patient’s outer clothing down to underwear
- Examinations involving the partial undoing or total removal of a patient’s underwear e.g. bras
- Any examination where the patient has requested a chaperone to be present

**Notes:** ‘Intimate’ examinations defined as:
Any invasive examination that involves the pelvic floor complex (vaginal and/or ano-rectal areas)
Any examination involving the genitalia or breasts

- Activity 5: Practice boundary setting
- Work in pairs
- Practice activity No 2 where you applied Elbow Passive ROM (PROM).
- Establish boundaries with your colleague:
- 1- Introduce yourself and your role
- 2- Explain what you will do
- 3- Explain why you want to do it.
- 4- Explain what the patient is expected to feel
- 5- Ask for Consent
- 6- Apply the technique.
- 7- practice a hypothetical situation where the patient felt uncomfortable: eg a woman did not like that she needed to expose her arm to you, how will you deal with this situation?
