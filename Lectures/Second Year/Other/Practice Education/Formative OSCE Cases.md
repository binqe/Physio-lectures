# Formative OSCE Cases

Case 1, MSK:
Mr. Bennett is a 54-year-old construction foreman with a 9-month history of worsening low back pain that now radiates into his right buttock and posterior thigh. Pain is a constant dull ache (VAS 5/10) but becomes sharp and shooting (VAS 8/10) when he attempts to lift heavy equipment or coughs. He has noticed his right foot feels "heavy" when walking up stairs.
Medical History: Controlled hypertension; smoker (20/day). Denies any changes to bladder or bowel habits.
Examination: Visible shift to the left in standing. Significantly limited lumbar flexion due to pain. Diminished right Achilles tendon reflex; 4/5 strength in right plantarflexion; positive Straight Leg Raise (SLR) at 35° on the right.
OSCE Tasks:
Identify 3–5 red flags relevant to this lumbar presentation and explain their clinical significance.
State your most likely diagnosis and provide two differential diagnoses. Explain how you differentiate them.
Demonstrate two to three objective tests to confirm your diagnosis and explain the meaning of a positive result.
Discuss your proposed treatment and management plan focusing on education, manual therapy and therapeutic exercise with specific cues for core/pelvic stability.
Case 2, NEURO:
Mr. Ibrahim is a 68-year-old retired teacher who suffered a left-sided Ischemic Stroke (Middle Cerebral Artery territory) 10 months ago. He is currently in the community rehabilitation phase. He reports a "heavy and stiff" right leg and difficulty clearing his foot during the swing phase of gait, leading to several "near-miss" trips recently. He is highly motivated but frustrated by a perceived lack of progress in his hand function.
Medical History: Atrial fibrillation (on anticoagulants); Type 2 Diabetes; Hypertension.
Examination: Increased tone (spasticity) in the right gastrocnemius and tibialis posterior (Modified Ashworth Scale 2). Reduced selective motor control of the right ankle; 3/5 strength in hip flexors; positive Babinski sign on the right. He demonstrates a circumduction gait pattern to clear the foot.
OSCE Tasks:
Identify 3–5 red flags relevant to a post-stroke patient in the community.
Briefly describe the pathophysiology of the patient's primary diagnosis and the typical signs and symptoms.
Demonstrate two to three objective assessment of motor control or spasticity and explain the clinical meaning of your findings.
Discuss your proposed treatment and management plan focusing on gait re-education. Demonstrate an exercise with specific verbal/tactile cues to improve knee flexion or ankle dorsiflexion during swing phase.
Case 3, CRP:
Mrs. Fletcher is a 74-year-old woman with a long-standing diagnosis of COPD (Emphysema pattern) who presents with increased shortness of breath and reduced exercise tolerance over the last 6 months. She reports a chronic productive cough, worse in the mornings. Recently, she has needed to sleep propped up on three pillows and has noticed her ankles are "puffy" by the end of the day.
Medical History: 40-pack-year smoking history (quit 5 years ago); Heart Failure (EF 40%).
Examination: Barrel chest appearance; use of accessory muscles (sternocleidomastoid) at rest. Decreased breath sounds at the bases with expiratory wheeze; Oxygen saturation 91% on room air; Pitting edema in bilateral ankles.
OSCE Tasks:
Identify 3–5 red flags that would require immediate medical escalation.
Briefly describe the pathophysiology of the patient's primary diagnosis and the typical signs and symptoms.
Demonstrate two to three objective assessments and explain what the findings indicate.
Discuss your proposed treatment and management plan including airway clearance or pacing/breathing control. Demonstrate an intervention and use specific cues to ensure diaphragmatic activation or effective cough production.
