## Slide 3

- Hazard Vs  Risk ?
- Hazard Vs risk
- A hazard is something which has the potential to cause harm, for example : a chemical, working at a height or noise
- A risk is the likelihood, whether low, medium or high, of that hazard causing a person harm

## Slide 4

- To us ?

## Slide 7

- Manual Handling – CSP guidance
- "Principle 1.2 of the CSP Code(11) requires that a physiotherapist and associate perform only duties which they are safe and competent to deliver. The development of manual handling skills is based not only on knowledge and training but also on relevant and sometimes specific experience. When considering competence and safety in relation to the manual handling of patients, all physiotherapists must be as realistic and analytically self-critical as they would be in relation to any other aspect of their professional practice. All members are empowered by this rule to say ‘no’ since it provides a rational basis for not proceeding with any task, including manual handling, which they consider to be beyond their competence or ability to work safely."

## Slide 8

- Manual Handling – CSP guidance
- "Principles 3.2 and 3.3 of the code clearly state the responsibility of the physiotherapist in considering the safety of other personnel and their competence in manual handling, addressing the situation by carrying out an appropriate risk assessment and documenting the process, and explaining and engaging the patient in the risk and decision-making processes."
- Members of the CSP will be covered by Personal indemnity insurance (See CSP Information paper PD027 Insurance and physiotherapy practice. www.csp.org.uk/ publications/insurance-physiotherapy-practice)
- "The PLI scheme covers individual for all activities that are within the scope of physiotherapy practice and this will include manual handling activities."

## Slide 9

- Manual Handling – The CSP and the law
- The law as set out by : The health and safety at work act 1974 and The management of Health and Safety at work regulations 1999
- Your employer should provide you with the adequate training and equipment to safely complete the manual handling you must have to complete to do your job.
- If you haven't received the adequate training you should not be doing the  manual handling, if your employer is not fulfilling this it should be escalated to the CSP and a governing body.

## Slide 10

- CSP guidance on risk assessment
- Step 1: Avoidance:
- Any general risk assessment taking place under MHSWR 1999(15) will identify, or should already have identified, the range of hazardous manual handling tasks taking place or likely to take place in a particular department / specialist setting.
- Many of these tasks will not be related directly to patient rehabilitation and every reasonably practicable step must be taken to avoid them.

## Slide 11

- CSP guidance on risk assessment
- Step 2: Risk assessment:
- There is an absolute requirement to assess the risks arising from manual handling operations that cannot reasonably practicably be avoided.
- Such risk assessments may be:
- generic, i.e. pertaining to groups or classes of routinely undertaken or foreseeable but unavoidable tasks such as, for example: routine transfers from wheelchair to treatment table, or the transport of certain items of equipment
- patient-specific, where the generic protocol is not appropriate to a particular patient at a particular time.
- In arriving at Step 2 the physiotherapist has already taken the decision that it is not reasonably practicable to avoid the manual handling task and has moved on to consider the extent of the risk potentially associated with carrying out the task.
- To avoid any doubt:
- Hazard – something (e.g. a circumstance or behaviour) with the potential to cause harm
- Risk – a notional consideration of the likelihood that a hazard will result in harm (to the handler, the patient or anyone else associated with the task) and of the severity or extent of that harm.
- When the guidance was set out in 2004 an acronym was created : TILE

## Slide 12

- TILE
- Task
- Individual
- Load
- Environment
- Eg. Mobilising to and from toilet or sitting on the edge of the bed
- The space in which the task is taking place. Taking into account things like footwear, lighting, space to complete task, attachments and any equipment you may need.
- The patient: What is their function, how much help do they need , do they have any behavioural problems eg. Impulsivity or aggression or hypoactive delerium
- The person carrying out the task i.e : you. This should consider skill set, experience etc

## Slide 13

- Documenting TILE
- Should be included in your observation section of your SOAP notes as it part of your assessment
- It should also be included in your analysis as a comment on that patients level of function
- Eg :
- O/ A:E assessment
- TILE : Ao1 with Walking stick for mob
- Or
- TILE : Ao3 for t.f with sara steady
- A/ pt admitted to ICU with chest sepsis, in multi-organ failure. Currently off baseline for mobility secondary to prolonged bed rest, requiring heavy assistance of 2 to sit on the edge of the bed. Will require specialised seating to sit out safely.

## Slide 17

- Equipment: giving out walking
- Very common therapy intervention often overlooked
- Before giving to the patient :
- Check the ferule
- Check the metal shaft / shafts of the aid
- Measure the patient for height modification : the handle of the aid should line up with their ulna styloid process
- Documentation :
- The lot number of the walking aid should be included in your documentation as part of the HCPC and CSP standards
- This means that if the aids in that lot are recalled there is a record of who was given them

## Slide 28

- Recognising mental health changes
- Anxiety :
- feel tired, on edge, restless or irritable
- feel a sense of dread
- be unable to concentrate or make decisions
- have trouble sleeping
- feel sick, dizzy, sweaty or short of breath
- be shaky or trembly
- get headaches or tummy aches
- avoid situations or put off doing things you are worried about
- have difficulty falling or staying asleep
- experience a noticeably strong, fast or irregular heartbeat
- have pins and needles
- have a dry mouth
- sweat excessively
- repeatedly check things or seek assurance from others
- Depression :
- Feeling low may cause someone to stop doing the things they like, cut themselves off from loved ones or have difficulty sleeping. Other signs include feeling:
- sad
- worried, anxious or panicked
- tired
- less confident
- frustrated, irritated or angry
- Burnout :
- feel overwhelmed
- have racing thoughts or difficulty concentrating
- be irritable
- feel constantly worried, anxious or scared
- feel a lack of self-confidence
- have trouble sleeping or feel tired all the time
- avoid things or people you are having problems with
- be eating more or less than usual
- drink or smoke more than usual
