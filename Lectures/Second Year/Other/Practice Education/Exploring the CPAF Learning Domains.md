## Slide 4

- Introduction to Learning Domains in Physiotherapy Placements
- Overview:
- The Common Placement Assessment Form (CPAF) outlines 10 core learning domains assessing clinical and professional competencies during placements.
- Framework:
- These domains align with HCPC standards and CSP professional values, ensuring students are prepared for clinical practice (HCPC, 2023; CSP, 2011).
- Pedagogical Insight:
- Biggs' (1996) Constructive Alignment emphasizes the need to align learning objectives, teaching methods, and assessments in clinical placements.

## Slide 5

## Slide 6

- Personal Development – Independent Learning
- Competency:
- Developing self-directed learning skills by actively seeking out learning opportunities and reflecting on clinical experiences.
- HCPC Guidelines:
- HCPC (2023) emphasizes continual professional development and staying updated on physiotherapy advancements.
- Pedagogical Framework:
- Kolb's (1984) Experiential Learning Cycle highlights "active experimentation," encouraging students to learn by doing in clinical environments.

**Notes:** Identifying your own learning needs is really important. Why? Because 
when you have done this, you are much more likely to change your 
practice than if you were purely being ‘told what to do’. This process 
can be challenging; but as it is personal to you, your motivation to learn 
increases as a result.

## Slide 8

- Personal Development – Seeking, Reflecting, and Responding to Feedback
- Importance:
- Feedback fosters critical self-reflection, ensuring students improve their clinical practices (HCPC, 2014).
- Assessment:
- Evaluates the ability to respond constructively to feedback and use it for skill development.
- Pedagogical Insight:
- Gibbs' (1988) Reflective Cycle supports a structured approach to reflection, crucial in healthcare for refining clinical practices.

**Notes:** As a student, your practice educator is there to guide and develop 
you. They should provide you with regular feedback throughout 
your placement and are key to helping you become a competent 
physiotherapist. How you seek feedback, take it on board, reflect 
and change your practice as a result is crucial.

## Slide 10

- Personal Development – Organization and Prioritization
- Competency:
- Effective time management and the ability to prioritize tasks are essential for patient care.
- Assessment:
- Students are evaluated on how they organize patient care and balance clinical responsibilities.
- CSP (2011) emphasizes prioritizing patient safety and treatment efficiency, while Moon (1999) supports reflective logs to improve organizational skills.

**Notes:** Being organised means you are taking responsibility in planning your 
own workload by prioritising the tasks that need to be completed first. 
How can you do this? Think of it as a To-do list for the day; you may 
want to rank the tasks in order of priority and add a deadline.

## Slide 11

- Interpersonal Skills – Communication
- Key Competency:
- Effective communication with patients and healthcare teams is essential for successful outcomes.
- Assessment:
- Evaluates clarity, empathy, and adaptability in verbal and non-verbal communication.
- Theoretical Framework:
- Vygotsky’s (1978) Social Development Theory highlights communication’s role in collaborative learning and development.

**Notes:** Communication is the most important skill of a physiotherapist. 
It’s how we interact with people. When effective, it makes everybody’s 
experience more positive; empowering others and building strong 
relationships. When ineffective it does just the opposite.

## Slide 12

- Interpersonal Skills – Working with Others
- Competency:
- Collaboration in multidisciplinary teams is key to integrated patient care.
- Assessment:
- Evaluates teamwork, engagement in team meetings, and coordination with peers and other professionals.
- Pedagogical Insight:
- Bandura’s (1977) Social Learning Theory suggests that students learn by observing and collaborating with others, vital in healthcare.

**Notes:** Who is in your team? This may include your practice educator, other 
therapists, other medical professionals, support workers, reception and 
admin staff and so on. Take time to get to know their unique roles and 
what they bring to the team. This will enable you to better understand 
both their contributions as well as yours – no matter the setting.

## Slide 13

- Interpersonal Skills – Individuals, Communities, and Populations
- Competency:
- Understanding health disparities and providing inclusive care.
- Assessment:
- Students must demonstrate adaptability to diverse patient needs, ensuring equitable care.
- Pedagogical Reference:
- Mezirow’s (1991) Transformative Learning theory stresses the importance of critical awareness in addressing public health challenges.

**Notes:** Many factors affect the health of individuals and communities. ‘Health is 
complex - many factors affect the health of individuals and communities.’ 
• Genetics 
• Ethnicity 
• The physical environment 
• The social environment 
• Health related behaviours 
If we think about these determinants we can really begin to understand 
the effect they can have on an individual’s health, the health of a 
community and even the health of populations.

## Slide 15

- Decision Making and Implementation – Gathering and Analysing Information
- Competency:
- Ability to collect and assess patient data to guide interventions.
- Assessment:
- Evaluates students' ability to gather, analyse, and interpret patient 	information.
- Sackett et al. (1996) Evidence-Based Medicine underlines the importance of combining clinical data with research evidence for decision-making.

**Notes:** Think about what information you gather whilst on placement. This 
may include subjective and objective assessments, literature searches, 
polls, questionnaires and focus groups among many other examples. 
This means that there is a lot of information for you to then analyse.

## Slide 17

- Decision Making and Implementation – Evidence-Based Practice
- Competency:
- Using research evidence to inform clinical interventions.
- Assessment:
- Students must demonstrate integration of current research into their clinical decision-making.
- Pedagogical Framework:
- The Cochrane Collaboration (1999) promotes the use of systematic reviews to inform physiotherapy practices, ensuring evidence-based care.

## Slide 18


## Slide 19

- Decision Making and Implementation – Clinical Reasoning and Intervention
- Competency:
- Demonstrating sound clinical reasoning to form treatment plans.
- Assessment:
- Students are evaluated on their clinical decision-making and application of reasoning in patient interventions.
- Pedagogical Insight:
- Schon’s (1983) Reflective Practitioner emphasizes reflective practice in clinical reasoning and decision-making, enabling students to refine their interventions.

**Notes:** Reasoning is fundamental to physiotherapy practice and is a skill that we 
will continue to develop as we learn and gain more experience. To put it 
simply, this is your ability to analyse information, evaluate its meaning, 
and decide the best course of action or intervention.

## Slide 20

- Decision Making and Implementation – Recording Information
- Competency:
- Accurate documentation of patient care, ensuring clarity and legal compliance.
- Assessment:
- Evaluates the completeness and professionalism of student documentation.
- HCPC (2023) highlights that clear, accurate, and timely record-keeping is a fundamental responsibility of healthcare professionals.

**Notes:** In a clinical placement, in person or remote, this includes your record 
keeping – more commonly referred to as ‘patient notes’. Once qualified, 
you will have a professional and legal obligation to keep an accurate 
record of your interactions with patients. If no record is made, the law 
may consider the events not to have happened. 
This domain is also very relevant on a non-patient facing placement, 
although it may be a bit trickier for you to picture how. We will explore 
this together and discuss how we make sure our recorded information 
is organised, understandable and accurate below.

## Slide 21

- Professionalism and Learning Domains
- Competency:
- Demonstrating professionalism consistently across all domains.
- Assessment:
- Students must show respect, accountability, and adherence to ethical and legal standards in clinical practice (HCPC, 2023).
- CSP (2011) and HCPC (2023) stress professional behaviours such as confidentiality, punctuality, and adherence to ethical guidelines.

## Slide 22

- Continuous Feedback and Reflective Practice
- Competency:
- Engaging with feedback for continuous self-improvement and professional growth.
- Assessment:
- Continuous assessment is vital for developing self-reflection and integrating feedback to improve clinical practice (Kolb, 1984).
- Pedagogical Insight:
- Boud, Keogh, and Walker (1985) argue that reflection turns clinical experience into learning opportunities, ensuring growth in professional practice.

## Slide 23

- Conclusion
- The CPAF Learning Domains offer a structured framework for evaluating physiotherapy students during placements, focusing on skills such as:
- clinical reasoning,
- teamwork,
- communication, and
- professional conduct.
