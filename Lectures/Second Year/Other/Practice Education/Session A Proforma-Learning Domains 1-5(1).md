# Session A Proforma-Learning Domains 1

Workshop on Learning Domains 1 – 5

| Session 1: Independent Learning (25 minutes)

Learning Domain: Independent Learning

Objective:
To develop self-directed learning strategies by identifying learning needs and using resources effectively.
Tasks and Activities:
Introduction to Independent Learning
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: Provide an overview of the importance of independent learning, including identifying personalised learning needs and engaging in development activities.
What practical examples can you use to enhance your learning on placements?
Personal Development Planning Exercise
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: Each student creates a Personal Development Plan (PDP), outlining one specific learning goal and identifying resources to achieve it (use the PDP from your CPAF as a guide!).
Specific Instructions:
Write down a clear and measurable learning goal.
Identify resources that will support the goal.
Set a timeline to achieve the goal during placement.
Reflection and Group Discussion/Presentation
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: In your groups, students will discuss their PDP goals and resources with peers.
Group Discussion: What resources did they find useful? What challenges might they face in achieving their goals? |
| Session 2: Seeking, Reflecting on, and Responding to Feedback (25 minutes)

Learning Domain: Seeking, Reflecting on, and Responding to Feedback

Objective:
To improve students' ability to seek feedback, reflect on it, and modify their behaviour in response.
Tasks and Activities:
Introduction to the Feedback Loop
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: Discuss the importance of seeking feedback proactively and acting on it to improve clinical skills.
Explain how feedback should be interpreted and reflected upon in the context of practice improvement.
Provide examples of useful feedback requests 
Role-Play Feedback Session
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: Students pair up to practice a feedback scenario, where one plays the student asking for feedback and the other acts as the educator providing feedback.
Role-Play Scenario: Asking for Feedback from a Supervisor
You are a student physiotherapist on your first placement. After completing an assessment with a patient, you feel unsure about your performance and want feedback from your supervisor. The patient was cooperative, but you feel you might have missed some important assessments and want to improve.

Specific Instructions:
The “student” requests feedback on a recent clinical task.
The “educator” provides constructive feedback, and the “student” asks clarifying questions to understand the feedback.
Reflection Discussion/Presentation
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Groups discuss the challenges of receiving feedback and how to incorporate it into practice.
Group Reflection: How will students approach feedback in future placements? |
| Session 3: Organisation and Prioritisation (30 minutes)

Learning Domain: Organisation and Prioritisation

Objective:
To develop skills in managing workloads effectively and prioritising clinical tasks based on urgency.
Tasks and Activities:
Introduction to Prioritisation and Time Management
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: Discuss the importance of prioritising patient care and adapting plans when clinical situations change.
Provide examples of real-world time management and prioritisation in clinical practice.
Case Study Prioritisation Activity (Time: 15 minutes)
Prioritisation in a Multidisciplinary Setting Scenario:
You are a physiotherapist on an acute medical ward. During your morning handover, you are informed that three patients are awaiting assessment:
Patient A: A 75-year-old woman with pneumonia. She is experiencing significant weakness and difficulty breathing but is medically stable.
Patient B: A 50-year-old man recovering from hip replacement surgery. He has been cleared for physiotherapy and needs to begin mobility exercises to facilitate early discharge.
Patient C: A 60-year-old man who experienced a stroke two days ago. He has left-sided weakness and needs an urgent mobility assessment to prevent complications like pressure sores and contractures.

Instruction: In small groups, students will work on a case scenario where multiple patients need attention.
Specific Instructions:
Identify high-priority patients based on their clinical needs.
Prioritise the order in which you will see these patients.
Justify the decisions made and develop a plan for managing remaining tasks.
Reflection Discussion/Presentation
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Groups will share their prioritisation strategies and discuss factors that influenced their decisions. |
| Session 4: Communication (20 minutes)

Learning Domain: Communication

Objective:
To enhance verbal, non-verbal, and written communication skills, focusing on patient care and team collaboration.
Tasks and Activities:
Introduction to Communication in Healthcare
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Present key communication strategies, including active listening, adapting communication to meet patient needs, and effective written communication.
Discuss how to modify communication when interacting with different audiences (e.g., patients, families, healthcare team members).
Role-Play Communication Scenarios
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: In pairs, students will role-play scenarios where they practice adapting communication with a patient (e.g., an elderly person) and a healthcare team member.
Role-Play Scenario 1: Communicating with a Patient Who Has Limited English Proficiency:
You are treating a 60-year-old man, Mr. Ali, who has been admitted for a musculoskeletal injury. Mr. Ali speaks very little English and is having trouble understanding the exercises you are prescribing. He seems frustrated and is not performing the exercises correctly, which is impacting his progress.

Specific Instructions:
Adjust tone, language, and non-verbal cues based on the audience.
Reflect on how well the communication adjustments were understood by the audience.
Group Reflection
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Discuss the effectiveness of communication techniques and how they could be applied in clinical settings. |
| Session 5: Working with Others (20 minutes)

Learning Domain: Working with Others

Objective:
To develop teamwork and collaboration skills within a multidisciplinary healthcare team.
Tasks and Activities:
Introduction to Interprofessional Collaboration
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Discuss the roles of different healthcare professionals in a multidisciplinary team and how they collaborate to improve patient outcomes.
Emphasise the importance of respecting each team member’s contributions.
Collaborative Care Plan Development
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: In small groups, students will develop a collaborative care plan for a hypothetical patient, with each student assuming the role of a different healthcare professional (e.g., nurse, physiotherapist).
Specific Instructions:
Each member must explain their role and contribution to the care plan.
Work together to develop a holistic care plan.
Group Debrief
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Discuss challenges faced in working with others and how to improve collaboration in real-world clinical settings. |
