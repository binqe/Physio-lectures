# Session B Proforma-Learning Domains 6

Workshop on Learning Domains 6 – 10

| Session 6: Individuals, Communities, and Populations (30 minutes)

Learning Domain: Individuals, Communities, and Populations

Objective:
To understand the broader determinants of health and how they influence patient care, health inequalities, and the physiotherapist's role in addressing them.
Tasks and Activities:
Introduction to Population Health and Health Inequalities
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: Present the concept of population health, including social determinants like socioeconomic status, environment, and ethnicity.
Discuss how these factors influence health outcomes and access to care.
Provide examples of health inequalities that may affect specific communities or populations.
Case Study on Health Inequalities
(Time: 15 minutes)
Instruction: In small groups, students will analyse a case study involving a patient from a disadvantaged background.
Case Study:
Patient: Maria, a 45-year-old woman from a low-income urban area.
Background: Maria has Type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and is overweight. She lives in a highly polluted area and works in a factory with long, sedentary shifts. Maria struggles with access to healthy food options and lives in a neighbourhood with few green spaces. She visits the clinic complaining of joint pain and general fatigue. She has been prescribed medications but is inconsistent with taking them due to financial constraints.
Specific Instructions:
Identify the social determinants affecting the patient’s health.
Discuss how these factors may impact the patient’s access to care, treatment, and rehabilitation outcomes.
Propose strategies the physiotherapist could implement to address these barriers (e.g., advocating for services, modifying treatment plans).
Group Discussion
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Groups will share their analysis and strategies, with a focus on understanding the physiotherapist’s role in tackling health inequalities. |
| Session 7: Gathering and Analysing Information (20 minutes)

Learning Domain: Gathering and Analysing Information

Objective:
To develop students’ ability to gather relevant clinical information and analyse it to draw meaningful conclusions that guide patient care.
Tasks and Activities:
Introduction to Information Gathering
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Explain the importance of gathering relevant clinical information from various sources (e.g., subjective and objective assessments, medical history, social factors).
Highlight the significance of choosing appropriate methods (e.g., interviews, physical exams, literature review) to gather the right information for patient assessment.
Practical Exercise: Information Gathering and Analysis
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: Provide a brief case scenario of a patient with multiple conditions.
Case Study:
Patient: John, a 30-year-old male, presents to physiotherapy following a motorcycle accident that resulted in a right tibial fracture. He underwent surgery 6 weeks ago and is now experiencing difficulty with weight-bearing and walking. He reports intermittent pain, particularly when attempting to increase activity levels, and is concerned about his ability to return to work as a delivery driver.

Specific Instructions:
Students will work individually to identify key information to gather from the patient (subjective and objective).
Analyse the gathered information to identify the main health concerns and how these might influence physiotherapy interventions.
Group Debrief
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Share the gathered information and key findings with the group. Discuss the relevance of certain information and how it guides clinical decisions. |
| Session 3: Evidence-Based Practice (25 minutes)

Learning Domain: Evidence-Based Practice

Objective:
To develop skills in applying evidence-based practice (EBP) by using clinical reasoning and available evidence to make informed decisions in patient care.
Tasks and Activities:
Introduction to Evidence-Based Practice
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction:
Introduce the concept of evidence-based practice (EBP), emphasizing the integration of best available evidence, clinical expertise, and patient preferences.
Explain the importance of applying EBP in clinical decision-making and how it improves patient outcomes.
Discuss common sources of evidence in clinical practice, such as clinical guidelines, research findings, and expert opinion.
Clinical Case Study Analysis
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: Provide students with a brief clinical case study involving a patient with a specific condition (e.g., chronic lower back pain or post-stroke rehabilitation).
Specific Instructions:
Read the case and identify the main clinical problem.
Discuss potential physiotherapy interventions that could be used to manage the patient’s condition.
Apply EBP by considering the available evidence for different interventions (e.g., exercise therapy, manual therapy, education) and the patient's preferences and specific condition.
Develop a treatment plan based on the most appropriate intervention supported by evidence.
Case Study:
Patient: 55-year-old male with chronic lower back pain.
History: Onset 8 months ago after a heavy lifting incident at work. The patient has tried medications and minimal physiotherapy with little improvement. He prefers non-invasive treatments and is hesitant about surgery. He has a sedentary lifestyle and works a desk job.
Clinical Problem: The patient presents with ongoing lower back pain affecting daily activities. He seeks an effective management plan without surgical intervention.
Reflection and Group Discussion
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction:
In small groups, students will share the interventions they proposed and discuss the evidence supporting their choices.
Discuss how the chosen interventions align with the patient’s preferences and clinical presentation.
Reflect on the balance between research evidence, clinical judgment, and patient values in shaping the treatment plan. |
| Session 9: Reasoning and Intervention (25 minutes)

Learning Domain: Reasoning and Intervention

Objective:
To enhance students’ clinical reasoning skills and their ability to select and apply safe, effective interventions based on patient needs.
Tasks and Activities:
Introduction to Clinical Reasoning
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Explain the importance of clinical reasoning in physiotherapy. Discuss how reasoning links evidence-based practice with the choice of appropriate interventions.
Highlight how setting, patient needs, and safety concerns affect decision-making.
Case Study on Intervention Selection
(Time: 15 minutes)
Instruction: Present a patient scenario where multiple intervention options are available (e.g., exercise prescription, manual therapy).
Case Study:
Patient: Sarah, a 68-year-old woman with a history of stroke 4 months ago, is receiving outpatient physiotherapy. She presents with left-sided weakness, difficulty with balance, and reduced mobility. Sarah lives alone but receives support from her family. Her main goal is to regain her independence in daily activities, including walking without assistance and being able to go to the local shop.

Specific Instructions:
In small groups, students will reason through the best intervention for the patient, considering their condition, safety, and preferences.
Discuss any contraindications or risks associated with the interventions.
Reflection and Group Discussion
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Groups will share their selected interventions and reasoning. Discuss how the intervention was chosen based on the patient's needs and setting. |
| Session 10: Recording Information (20 minutes)

Learning Domain: Recording Information

Objective:
To improve students' ability to document patient care accurately and appropriately for legal, professional, and clinical purposes.
Tasks and Activities:
Introduction to Documentation
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Discuss the importance of accurate and clear record-keeping in physiotherapy, including patient notes, treatment plans, and communication with other healthcare professionals.
Explain how poor record-keeping can lead to clinical and legal issues.
Practical Exercise: SOAP Note Writing
(Time: 10 minutes)
Instruction: Provide a patient case scenario for which students must write a SOAP (Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan) note.
Specific Instructions:
Write clear, concise, and structured notes based on the information provided in any of the cases from the other domains.
Ensure that the reasoning behind assessments and interventions is documented.
Review and Feedback
(Time: 5 minutes)
Instruction: Provide group feedback on the SOAP notes, highlighting areas of strength and potential improvement. |
