## Slide 3

- Structure of Longitudinal Placements
- MSc
- 20 days spread across 1-day-per-week placement (Thursdays)
- Starting with clinical simulation
- Practical exposure to real-world healthcare settings
- Weekly debriefs on longitudinal placements (Mandatory)
- BSc
- 10 days spread across 1-day-per-week placement (Thursdays)
- Starting with clinical simulation
- Practical exposure to real-world healthcare settings
- Weekly debriefs on longitudinal placements (Mandatory)
- Key Purpose: These placements offer a unique opportunity to develop core clinical and professional skills over time through a consistent clinical setting, emphasizing practical experience alongside academic learning (HCPC, 2013; CSP, 2011).

## Slide 5

- Focus and Objectives:
- Focus: Placements focus on specialties like MSK, Neuro, respiratory care, and community services.
- Goal: Build clinical skills gradually while concurrently applying classroom learning to real-world healthcare settings (HCPC, 2014).
- Core Skills Development: Students are expected to develop core competencies such as patient assessment, clinical reasoning, and treatment planning.
- Interprofessional Learning: Exposure to various clinical specialities and multidisciplinary teams to foster teamwork and broaden clinical knowledge
- Practical Experience: Students gain real-life experience, contributing to their clinical learning and professional development.

## Slide 6

- Learning Outcomes
- Knowledge: Develop understanding of common patient problems and how they affect patient lifestyles and participation.
- Performance: Demonstrate ability to assess patients, plan treatment, and apply clinical reasoning effectively.
- Attitudes and Behaviours: Reflect professionalism in patient care, maintain respect for privacy and dignity, and seek continuous feedback for improvement.

## Slide 7

- Assessment
- CPAF Level 4(Bsc)/Level 5 (MSc):
- The Common Placement Assessment Form (CPAF) evaluates student performance across learning domains like communication, clinical reasoning, and professionalism.
- Pass/Fail Evaluation: A minimum pass threshold of 40% is required across all domains, with regular feedback from practice educators.
- Failure Procedure: If a student's performance is unsatisfactory, a structured danger-of-failure process is initiated, involving meetings, action plans, and reviews to support improvement.

## Slide 8

- Support and Resources:
- Placement preparation: Clinical simulations
- Pre-Placement Reading: Students have access to reading materials and preparatory resources to help them succeed during placements.
- Support from Practice Educators: Each student is assigned a dedicated practice educator to guide them through the placement experience.
- University Resources: Students are supported by the university through access to the library, clinical simulation labs, and online resources for continuous learning.
- Themed weekly debrief sessions (mandatory)
- Link Tutor support: including a link tutor visit at half-way assessment

## Slide 9

- Student Expectations:
- Professional Conduct: Students are expected to uphold professional standards, including confidentiality, maintaining fitness to practice, seeking informed consent, and showing respect for colleagues and patients (HCPC, 2013)..
- Learning Contract: Students must complete a learning agreement with their practice educator, aligning placement learning goals with module objectives.
- Active Engagement: Take initiative in learning, progressively moving from observation to hands-on patient care as placement progresses (Sorinola and Onuegbu, 2024).

## Slide 10

- Stengths of LONGITUDINAL PLACEMENTS model:
- Strengths
- Opportunity to apply knowledge and skills in clinical settings early on.
- Follow patients’ journey for a longer period
- Opened up under-utilised placement capacity
- Opportunities to shape students within a clinical setting
- Reduce overall placement requests for later on the course

## Slide 11

- What to be mindful of:
- Students are in over a longer period
- Could be challenging for some clinical settings – e.g. ward based
- Could offer a different placement experience
- Innovative Novel Placement Idea: Optimise Link tutor and Practice educator support
