## Slide 10

- Modifiable risk factors
- In more than 90% of cases, the risk of developing CHD is related to nine potentially modifiable risk factors (Yusuf et al. 2004):
- smoking/tobacco use
- poor diet
- high blood cholesterol
- high blood pressure
- physical inactivity
- overweight/obesity
- Diabetes
- Psychosocial stress (linked to people's ability to influence the potentially stressful environments in which they live)
- Excess alcohol  consumption
- Other factors, such as maternal nutrition and air pollution may also be linked to the disease (Allender et al. 2007)

## Slide 24

- NHS Health Checks
- The NHS Health Check programme is a national risk assessment, awareness and management programme
- Systematically targets the top seven causes of preventable deaths:
- high blood pressure,
- smoking,
- high cholesterol,
- obesity,
- poor diet,
- physical inactivity and alcohol consumption
- (DH,2014)

## Slide 25

- The main aim of the NHS Health Check is to prevent heart disease, stroke, diabetes and kidney disease, and raise awareness of dementia both across the population and within high risk and vulnerable groups (DH, 2014)
- DH set targets and vascular checks guidance, to identify those people at risk of cardiovascular disease but who have not yet developed symptoms.
- Once identified, appropriate advice and treatment should be offered
- (DH 2008, 2009)

## Slide 32

- NHS Health Checks- the Islingtonmodel
- Islington- one of the most ethnically diverse communities in London with high levels of deprivation (DH, 2009)
- Eighth most deprived borough in England and fourth in London
- Two thirds of Islington residents live in the 20% most deprived areas in England (DH, 2010), resulting in a disproportionately higher rate of premature CVD (Davis et al., 2009)
- Substantial health inequalities within the borough and CVD mortality is reported to be associated with increasing deprivation (DH, 2010).
- CVD accounted for 35% of all deaths and 29% of deaths under the age of 75 in 2006-08 (DH, 2010)
- Deaths from CVD are one of the biggest contributors to the inequalities gap in life expectancy in Islington

## Slide 33

- Fall in CVD mortality rates (number of deaths per 100,000 standardized for age) for Islington, London and England illustrating that whilst CVD mortality rates have been declining, the rate of decline is consistently slower for Islington compared to London and England hence furthering the gap in health inequalities

## Slide 34

- Islington NHS Health Checks (INHSHC)
- The research team at London Metropolitan University were commissioned by Islington NHS to conduct an evaluation of the effectiveness and acceptability of  the opportunistic health checks

## Slide 35

- Evaluation
- Key function to improve performance of any aspect of health care delivery
- Demonstrates whether a program is accomplishing its intended goals
- Identifies weaknesses and strengths

## Slide 36

- The INHS Health Checks program targets all adults aged 35 (in Islington) to 74 for cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease
- Islington  primary care trust (PCT ) delivered  this program in  2009 through GP surgeries and pharmacies and extended the outreach in 2010 by using the community setting

## Slide 37

- Health Checks protocol for thecommunity setting
- NHS Islington commissioned Health Smart (service provider) to conduct the community based cardiovascular risk assessment service
- Opportunistic cardiovascular health checks were conducted for Islington residents aged between 35-74 years with no existing cardiovascular diseases
- Posters and leaflets were used to market the Health Checks
- The service was available to both men and women resident in the borough of Islington
- All participants indicated their agreement to a Health Check by signed consent

## Slide 38

- Venues for Islington NHSHC
- During the period of July to December 2010 health checks were conducted on random days of the week at the following venues:
- Islington Town Hall
- Nags Head Shopping Centre
- Waitrose, Holloway Road
- Muslim Welfare House, Finsbury Park mosque
- Archway Post Office
- Arsenal Red Learning Centre
- Andover Community Centre
- Hanley Community Centre

## Slide 39

- NHSHC Methodology
- Participants were recruited opportunistically by frontline staff and invited to undertake a health check
- Participants were weighed and their height measured
- Information about family and medical history, including current   medications and level of physical activity, were obtained
- All major CVD risk factors including total cholesterol and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) plasma levels, systolic blood pressure and diastolic blood pressure, random blood glucose level (BGL), smoking habit, and body mass index (BMI; weight (kg)/[height (m)]2) were assessed
- Total and HDL cholesterol levels and BGL were measured from finger prick blood samples using a portable desktop dry-chemistry analyzer

## Slide 40

- Data obtained by INHSHC
- Participants’ 10-year risk of developing cardiovascular events, using cardiovascular disease risk algorithm (QRISK2) was calculated
- Individuals whose 10-year absolute risk exceeded 20%
- Detection of one or more abnormal test results (i.e., SBP greater than or equal to 140 mmHg; DBP greater than or equal to 90 mmHg
- TC greater than or equal to 5.5 mmol/l; HDL-C below 1.0 mmol/l
- Or random BGL greater than or equal to 8 mmol/l), were asked to see their general practitioner for further assessment or management.

## Slide 41

- Information given to participants
- Referrals to smoking services, weight management services and exercise services were recommended where appropriate
- a printed copy of their health check results and educational material published by the Food Standard Agency and Department of Health
- verbal counseling about smoking, diet and exercise was also provided

## Slide 42

- Community Setting Evaluation
- Two questionnaires were devised to evaluate the pilot program in Islington
- Post-check questionnaire for face-to-face interviews after the health checks were completed to evaluate the delivery
- Follow-up telephone questionnaire to evaluate the outcome of the health checks and assess change in risk factors

## Slide 43

- Post health-check questionnaires
- Interviewing participants immediately after they had used the service would enable the researcher to capture initial thoughts about the health check
- This method of interview facilitated recall of the details of their experience and was thought to prevent the loss of data that may otherwise occur
