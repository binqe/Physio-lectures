## Page 3

Why it matters to public health 
• 1 in 4 adults globally do not meet recommended physical activity 
levels (WHO, 2023).
• Physical inactivity contributes to ~5 million deaths annually.
• Rising chronic disease burden (CVD, diabetes, MSK pain)
• Shift from treatment to prevention ( NHS long term plan)

## Page 5

What is exercise prescription ? 
• Definition: “Systematic, individualized guidance on physical 
activity to achieve specific health or fitness outcomes.”
• Components: Type, intensity, frequency, duration, progression.
• Aim: Promote safe, effective, and sustainable activity.

## Page 21

The FITTVP framework 
Frequency: how often.
Intensity: how hard.
Time: how long.
Type: what kind of activity.
Volume: total amount.
Progression: how it changes over time.

## Page 22

Applying FITT-VP in practice
• Start with what the person can realistically do.
• Use public health guidelines to inform targets, but tailor the 
starting point.
• Progress gradually to improve adherence and reduce dropout
• Use SMART goals

## Page 24

Why tailor exercise prescription 
• Public health guidance applies across the lifespan, but needs 
adapting.
• Different groups face different barriers, risks, and priorities.
• Good prescription balances safety, relevance, equity, and 
adherence.

## Page 25

Children and young adults
• Children and adolescents should average 60 minutes per day of 
moderate-to-vigorous physical activity across the week.
• Most activity should be aerobic, with vigorous and muscle- and 
bone-strengthening activities included at least 3 days per week.
• Active play, PE, sport, walking, and cycling all count.

## Page 26

Older adults 
• Older adults should aim for the same aerobic targets as other 
adults when able: 150–300 minutes moderate or 75–150 
minutes vigorous activity per week.
• They should also do strength work and include balance and 
functional activity, particularly if at risk of falls.
• Sedentary time should be reduced and broken up regularly.

## Page 27

Long term conditions and disability 
• People living with chronic conditions or disability should still be 
as active as their abilities and conditions allow.
• WHO recommends the same broad adult targets where possible, 
adapted to symptoms, function, and risk.
• For disabled adults, UK evidence reviews support 150 minutes of 
moderate-to-vigorous activity, plus 2 sessions per week of 
challenging strength and balance exercise, where appropriate.
