## Slide 4

- Regulations and Professionalism:
- The Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) is the regulator for Physiotherapy  profession.
- Their Standards of Conduct, Performance and Ethics set out the expectations of how we should behave and what the public should expect from us.
- They are an essential read, alongside their guidance document aimed specifically for students, helping you to understand the importance of your responsibilities and role (HCPC, 2023) .

## Slide 5

- Guidance and Professionalism:
- The CSP is the professional body who offer valuable guidance to the physiotherapy profession.
- Their Code of Members Professional Values and Behaviour document sets out their expected standards of behaviour (CSP, 2011).

## Slide 6

- Professional Behaviours and Responsibilities
- Professional behaviour is integral to a student physiotherapist’s development and future practice.
- Aligns with the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) standards and Chartered Society of Physiotherapy (CSP) guidelines (HCPC, 2023; CSP, 2011).
- A key aspect of demonstrating competence in clinical placements.

## Slide 7

- Key Professional Standards

## Slide 8

- Practice Safely and Effectively:
- Stay within your knowledge and scope of practice (HCPC, 2014).
- Seek help or guidance when needed to ensure patient safety.
- Example:
- Speak up if unsure about a treatment or request clarification from your educator.
- Contacting your PE when unsure about anything.

## Slide 9

- Promote and Protect the Interests of Others
- Respect patient dignity and involve them in decision-making.
- Practice inclusivity and avoid discrimination (CSP, 2011).
- Example:
- Adapt assessments to accommodate individual patient needs, such as cultural preferences or mobility restrictions.

## Slide 10

- Risk Assessment and Reporting
- Assess risks and take steps to limit harm to yourself, patients, and colleagues.
- Report any safety concerns promptly to your practice educator (HCPC, 2014).
- Example:
- Identify trip hazards before mobilizing a patient.
- Regular breaks form computer, ensure computer set-up is ergonomically friendly

## Slide 11

- Confidentiality and Professional Boundaries
- Maintain patient confidentiality in all settings.
- Avoid discussing patient details in public or on social media (HCPC, 2023).
- Example:
- Be mindful of what is shared during discussions outside of the clinical setting.
- Discussing concerns with PE.

## Slide 12

- Dress Code and Punctuality
- Follow organizational standards for dress, appearance, and hygiene.
- Ensure you are punctual and prepared for placement activities (CSP, 2011).
- Example:
- Arrive on time with necessary materials and be dressed according to placement site protocols.
- Making sure that hair is tied back and does not cover your face

## Slide 13

- Continuous Self-Reflection
- Use feedback and self-reflection to improve professional behaviour.
- Engage in reflective practice to identify areas for growth (Gibbs, 1988).
- Example:
- Reflect on feedback received during clinical sessions and apply it in future practices.

## Slide 14

- How Professionalism is Assessed in Physiotherapy Placements
- Assessment Tool: The Common Placement Assessment Form (CPAF) is used to evaluate student professionalism.
- Key Domains: Professionalism is assessed under various domains such as communication, ethical practice, patient confidentiality, and teamwork (HCPC, 2023).
- Feedback from Educators: Practice educators provide ongoing feedback on professional behaviour throughout the placement.
- Midway and Final Assessments: Professionalism is formally evaluated at the midway and final points of placement to ensure students meet required standards.
- Pass/Fail Criteria: Students must demonstrate professional behaviour to meet the minimum threshold for passing the placement.
- Example:
- Maintaining patient confidentiality, showing respect, and adhering to dress codes are essential behaviours that educators assess during placement.
