## Slide 3

- What is Treatment Adherence?
- - Definition: extent behaviour matches agreed recommendations
- - Adherence vs. compliance
- - Importance: outcomes, complications, costs
- WHO: Only ~50% of patients with chronic illness adhere

## Slide 4

- Factors Influencing Adherence (Overview)
- - Biological/clinical
- - Psychological
- - Social/environmental
- - Provider/system

## Slide 5

- Biological & Clinical Factors
- - Disease: chronic vs. acute
- - Treatment regimen: complexity, side effects
- - Patient’s physical ability
- Example: HIV treatment

## Slide 6

- Psychological Factors
- - Beliefs about illness & treatment
- - Motivation & attitudes
- - Mental health: depression, anxiety
- - Self-efficacy & control

## Slide 7

- Social & Environmental Factors
- - Family & peer support
- - Cultural norms
- - Barriers: cost, transport
- - Living conditions

## Slide 8

- Healthcare Provider & System Factors
- - Communication clarity
- - Trust & empathy
- - System barriers: wait times, continuity
- - Reminders & support tools (apps, SMS)
- Reflection: impact of poor healthcare experience

## Slide 9

- Motivation & Health Behaviors
- - Adherence depends on motivation, not just instructions
- - Motivation = energy + direction
- - Theories explain why behaviors start/continue/stop
- Think: Why continue smoking despite risks?

## Slide 10

- Health Belief Model (HBM)
- - Susceptibility
- - Severity
- - Benefits
- - Barriers
- - Cues to action
- - Self-efficacy
- Case: Hypertension patient feels fine → missing HBM factors

## Slide 11

- Self-Determination Theory (SDT)
- - Extrinsic vs. Intrinsic motivation
- - Needs: Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness
- - Meeting needs → stronger adherence

## Slide 16

- The 4 principles of MI
- Empathy
- Reflective listening.
- A communication strategy that involves actively listening to the speaker and then reflecting what has been said.
- Avoid judgmental statements.
- Judgmental language can create barriers between therapists and patients, leading to resistance or disengagement from treatment.

- Activity:
- Guided Role-Play of the OARS Framework
- Divide yourself into groups of three
- Role 1: the therapist practicing MI.
- Role 2: Patient based on a provided scenario.
- Role 3: Observer providing feedback on MI technique usage.

- Scenario Assignments: Each group gets a unique case from the scenarios shared earlier.

## Slide 33

- Debrief Questions for Each Pair
- Which motivational interviewing (MI) techniques were effectively applied by the physiotherapist?
- How did the patient react to these techniques?
- What alternative approaches could the physiotherapist adopt to enhance the interaction?
- Assign a score from 1 to 10 for each question, providing a rationale for the rating, including what was successful, what challenges arose, and suggestions for improvement.
