- What is Health?
- The WHO definition of health, formulated in 1948, described health as:
- “a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity”
- Much criticism over this definition as the requirement for complete health “would leave most of us unhealthy most of the time”

## Slide 9

- Health Inequalities defined
- “Differences in health status or in the distribution of health determinants between different population groups.”
- WHO believes:
- “avoidable health inequalities arise because of inequalities in society and in the conditions in which we are born, grow, live work and age, and the systems put in place to deal with illness. The conditions in which people live and die are, in turn, shaped by political, social and economic forces.”

## Slide 10

- Social determinants of health – the social and economic conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work and age, including the health system.
- Health inequalities – differences in the health (and increasingly wellbeing) experienced by different groups in a community which are avoidable and therefore held to be unacceptable.

## Slide 11

- Life Course
- An approach to health emphasising the overall effects of an individual’s experience across their life span:
- maintenance of health & prevention of disease,
- poor economic & social conditions in the very early years can adversely impact individuals’ growth and development
- increase risk of disease & ill health in later life & life expectancy (e.g. Gracner et al, 2024 https://www.science.org/doi/epdf/10.1126/science.adn5421)

## Slide 13

- The Black Report - did it deserve attention?
- Published by Dept. of Health & Social Security (1980)
- Written by the expert committee into health inequality
- Chaired by Sir Douglas Black (President of RCP)
- Described unequal distribution of ill-health and death
- Inequalities have been widening since establishment of NHS(1948)
- Inequalities attributable to social inequalities influencing health
- Include: income, education, housing, diet, employment, work conditions
- Recommended wide strategy of social policy measures
- Report heavily criticised by government Secretary of State for Social Services
- Very few copies printed

## Slide 14

- Acheson report (1998)
- Independent inquiry into health inequalities
- Purpose of inquiry was to inform the government's public health strategy and their policies within ‘‘Our Healthier Nation’’ paper
- The Report concluded that the ‘weight of scientific evidence supports a socio-economic explanation of health inequalities’
- https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a759e7c40f0b67b3d5c7e6f/ih.pdf
- ‘Poverty, low wages and occupational stress, unemployment, poor housing, environmental pollution, poor education, limited access to transport and shops, crime and disorder, and a lack of recreational facilities have had an impact on people’s health’
- (Acheson 1998)

## Slide 17

- 'Fair Society Healthy Lives’ (2010)
- Action on six policy objectives needed:
- Every child the best start in life
- Enable all children, young people and adults to maximise their capabilities & have control over their lives
- Create fair employment & good work for all
- Ensure healthy standard of living for all
- Create and develop healthy & sustainable places and communities
- Strengthen the role & impact of ill-health prevention
- Government's 2011 public health white paper welcomed the review
- Put reduction of health inequalities at  centre of public health aims
- based strategy on the Marmot review's recommendations
- PHOF greatly influenced by the Marmot review

## Slide 18

- Proportionate Universalism
- Sole focus on most disadvantaged will not reduce health inequalities sufficiently.
- To reduce the steepness of social gradient in health, actions must be universal, at scale & intensity proportionate to level of disadvantage

## Slide 24

- What is Public Health Outcomes Framework (PHOF)?
- Integrated and professional PH system:
- Increase accountability for the improvement and protection of the public’s health
- Local responsibility and localism to tackle specific health needs of their populations
- Central government responsibility of protecting population’s health and safeguarding against health threats
- The outcome framework will set out how success in public health will be measured both nationally and locally

## Slide 25

- Public Health Outcomes Framework (PHOF)
- Established 2013; reviewed every 3 yrs; vision for public health
- Focus on two high level outcomes to achieve across & beyond the public health system:
- 1.  Increased healthy life expectancy2.  Reduced differences in life expectancy and healthy life expectancy between communities
- Outcomes cover length of life and how well people live at all stages of life

**Notes:** Set out the Government’s goals for improving & protecting the nations’ health

 Narrow health inequalities through improving the health of the poorest, fastest

Have a mechanism for transparency & accountability nationally & locally for health improvement & protection & inequality reduction

## Slide 26

- Domains
- Wider determinants of health: Tackling factors which affect health, wellbeing & health inequalities
- Health Improvement: Helping people to live healthy lifestyles, make healthy choices and reduce health inequalities.
- Health Protection: Population’s health is protected from major incidents & other threats, whilst reducing health inequalities
- Healthy life expectancy and preventable mortality: Preventing people from dying prematurely & reduce health inequalities

**Notes:** Overarching indicators reported at birth and at age 65 years

## Slide 27

- PHOF Indicators
- Set of supporting indicators within each domain to help understand the progress being made
- Indicators cover the full spectrum of public health & what can be realistically measured
- Every 3 years consultations take place on changes to the indicators; revised framework based on the findings is published
- Wide consultation on health & social care statistical outputs (including PHOF) published 2024 (https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/health-and-social-care-statistical-outputs/outcome/health-and-social-care-statistical-outputs-consultation-response#overarching-health-and-social-care-outputs)
- Last PHOF update report November 2025

**Notes:** To ensure they remain fit for purpose

## Slide 28

- What is an indicator?
- Specific, observable & measurable characteristic
- Used to show changes/ progress  made towards achieving specific outcome
- Needs precise, unambiguous definition to describe clearly & exactly what is being measured
- Selected via consultations with wide group of stakeholders & experts
- Robust criteria for inclusion  agreed via consultations & expert input
- Life course approach an integral part of design of each domain; indicators within that domain required to cover all stages of life
