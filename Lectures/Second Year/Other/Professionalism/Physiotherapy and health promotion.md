## Slide 6

- Why talk about Public health in Physio
- Modifiable risk factors and their influence on long term conditions
- Physiotherapy setting and opportunity
- Ageing population
- Physiotherapy skills

**Notes:** Major causes of illness/disability (low back pain, OA, COPD, stroke) are strongly influenced by modifiable risk factors: physical inactivity (28% of UK adults inactive), smoking, obesity, alcohol.​

Physiotherapists routinely work with these conditions across multiple contacts/settings, giving repeated opportunities to address risk factors as part of normal care.​

Health services face rising multimorbidity/ageing population pressure – all clinicians, including physios, expected to contribute to prevention/health promotion.​

Your skills (exercise prescription, education, behaviour change, self-management) are central to tackling these public-health challenges

## Slide 7

- Examples from practice: MSK Practice

**Notes:** MSK outpatient: Recurrent low back pain patient avoiding movement/off work long-term → beyond manual therapy/exercises, address pain beliefs, promote staying active, explore return-to-work support, signpost community PA options.​

Pulmonary rehab: COPD group → combine exercise training with smoking cessation support, breathing/pacing education, vaccines → lowers exacerbation risk.​

Neuro/older adults: Post-stroke or frequent falls → strength/balance training, environmental modifications, confidence building → reduces hospital admissions.

## Slide 8

- Patient Examples: Resp

**Notes:** Pulmonary rehab: COPD group → combine exercise training with smoking cessation support, breathing/pacing education, vaccines → lowers exacerbation risk.​

## Slide 9

- Primary care
- Stop before it starts
- Secondary care
- Early intervention
- Tertiary care
- Reduce impact
- Prevention across levels

**Notes:** Primary (stop before starts): workplace ergonomics reducing back injuries; community exercise for diabetes/CVD risk; walking/cycling campaigns.​
Secondary (early intervention): early MSK access prevents chronicity; breathlessness → pulmonary rehab referral; falls risk screening.​
Tertiary (reduce impact): stroke rehab maximising independence; COPD/heart failure management; chronic pain programmes reducing disability.

## Slide 10

- Should be a routine part of EVERY consultation
- Ignoring major risk factors can lead to poor outcomes
- HCPC SOP 15.1-15.3
- “not an add-on”

**Notes:** "Public health" happens in routine consultations – asking about activity/smoking/falls risk, helping set goals, signposting local support = legitimate physiotherapy.​
Ignoring major risk factors (smoking in COPD, inactivity in diabetes+OA) compromises rehab outcomes, hard to justify professionally.​
HCPC SoP 15.1-15.3 + CSP health inequalities emphasis = regulatory/professional mandate.

## Slide 13

- Short consultations on smoking or physical activity increase:
- Quit attempts and successful quitting
- The proportion of adults reaching physical‑activity recommendations
- The key: advice is specific, structured and behaviour‑focused, not just “you should be healthier”
- Brief advice CAN make a difference

## Slide 14

- Physios regularly see people with pain, long‑term conditions and pre/post surgery.
- A brief but well‑timed conversation about smoking, activity, weight or alcohol can:
- Trigger meaningful behaviour change
- Enhance the impact of the physical treatment you provide
- Why it matters

## Slide 15

- In low back pain, a single physio session with assessment and clear advice to stay active plus a written leaflet can:
- Produce similar long‑term outcomes (pain and disability) to multiple routine physiotherapy sessions
- Brief advice and LBP outcomes

**Notes:** A well‑delivered brief advice session can be as powerful as a longer course of treatment for many patients

## Slide 16

- When patients understand why and how to exercise, they are more likely to adhere.
- Better adherence is linked to:
- Reduced pain and stiffness
- Improved strength and daily function
- So your public‑health role (education, coaching, problem‑solving barriers) is central to clinical outcomes
- Adherence, self‑management and outcomes

## Slide 17

- Short pre‑operative programmes (often a few physio sessions) that include:
- Targeted exercise
- Education and goal‑setting
- Behaviour‑change support
- These programmes improve early post‑operative function after hip, knee and spine surgery compared with usual care
- Prehabilitation: small dose, big impact

## Slide 18

- Your role is not just exercises after surgery:
- Before surgery: screen for smoking/inactivity, give brief advice, signpost support
- After surgery: reinforce activity guidelines, encourage safe progression and adherence
- Each brief conversation is a chance to improve recovery and reduce complications
- “Public health” in the surgical pathway

## Slide 19

- Brief physical‑activity counselling during or after PR focuses on:
- Setting realistic walking/step goals
- Problem‑solving barriers (fatigue, fear, environment)
- Even when changes in daily step count are modest, counselling can help people sustain gains in confidence and exercise tolerance achieved in rehab
- In COPD, patients who stop smoking and complete PR tend to:
- Walk further
- Have fewer exacerbations
- Report better quality of life
- Brief smoking‑cessation conversations during physio contacts (Ask–Advise–Offer support) increase the chance that patients will attempt and maintain quitting
- Activity and smoking cessation counselling in COPD

## Slide 20

- Brief, structured smoking‑cessation advice:
- More quit attempts and higher quit rates → better surgical and COPD outcomes
- Brief, structured exercise advice aligned with guidelines:
- Better pain relief, function and recovery in MSK and post‑operative patients
- These conversations are short, repeatable and fit naturally into physio contacts
- Key messages for practice

## Slide 21

- Breaking down the HCPC jargon
- "15.1 understand the role of their profession in health promotion, health education and preventing ill health".
- Understand physio prevents problems occurring/worsening (exercise prevents falls, CVD risk).​
- Health education = helping people understand condition + behaviour links (activity, smoking impact rehab).​
- Routine opportunity-seeking = core competence

## Slide 22

- Breaking down the HCPC jargon
- "15.2 understand how social, economic and environmental factors (wider determinants of health) can influence health/wellbeing".​
- Social: caring responsibilities, isolation, cultural pain beliefs.​
- Economic: low income, travel costs, insecure work.​
- Environmental: unsafe neighbourhoods, poor housing, no green space.​
- Same exercise programme won't work for everyone – adapt to real context.

## Slide 23

- Breaking down the HCPC Jargon
- "15.3 empower and enable individuals (service users/colleagues) to play part in managing own health".​
- Collaborative goal-setting vs imposing agenda.​
- Self-management strategies (pacing, graded activity, flare-up plans).​
- Community resource use (walking groups, online programmes) for sustained change.​
- Small individual changes × many people = population impact

## Slide 25

- MECC is about using routine health and care interactions to support people to make healthier choices.
- It focuses on brief, opportunistic conversations about topics like smoking, physical activity, diet, alcohol and mental wellbeing.
- Fits naturally into physio assessments and reviews
- Making every contact count (MECC)

## Slide 26

- Person‑centred and non‑judgemental.
- Brief (often 30 seconds to a few minutes).
- Uses open questions to explore readiness and priorities.
- Offers simple, realistic next steps and/or signposting.
- Key features of MECC

## Slide 27

- Demonstrates HCPC standards on health promotion, wider determinants and empowering individuals.
- Aligns with CSP’s emphasis on health‑promoting practice and behaviour change skills.
- A practical way to show, on placement, that you understand your public‑health role.
- MECC and HCPC/CSP

## Slide 28

- MECC role play
- “Designing a brief health‑promoting conversation”
- Work in groups of 3–4.
- Each group receives a short case scenario.
- Your task: choose one behaviour‑change opportunity and plan a 3–4 line MECC‑style conversation.
- You will then link your conversation back to specific HCPC standards.

## Slide 29

- Task breakdown
- Step 1: Identify one main public‑health focus (e.g. smoking, physical activity, falls risk, weight management).
- Step 2: Draft a 3–4 line conversation that:
- Asks permission to talk about it.
- Uses at least one open question.
- Offers one realistic next step or signpost (e.g. local service, leaflet, website).
- Step 3: Under your script, list the HCPC standards you think you are meeting (e.g. 15.1, 15.2, 15.3; communication; partnership with service users).

## Slide 34

- Physiotherapists are part of the public health workforce, not just “treatment providers”.
- HCPC and CSP expect you to promote health, understand wider determinants and empower patients.
- MECC‑style brief conversations are a practical way to live out these expectations in day‑to‑day practice.
- Key take-home messages

## Slide 35

- Asking about activity levels and collaboratively setting one extra‑walk goal.
- Briefly raising smoking or alcohol if relevant to rehab and offering signposting.
- Adapting advice to the person’s social and environmental context.
