## Slide 35

- Comparison: Healthy Weight, Healthy Lives (2008) vs. Current Policies (2025–26)1. Strategic Focus:
- Healthy Weight, Healthy Lives (2008)
- Emphasis on reversing national obesity trends, focusing strongly on children.
- Multi‑sector: schools, food industry, workplaces, NHS.
- Prevention was important, but treatment/support also included.
- Current Policies (2025–26)
- Focus on preventing childhood obesity, reducing inequalities, and improving healthy environments (schools, advertising regulation).
- Policies are more structural and regulatory than the 2008 strategy.
- Government rhetoric emphasises not “looking away” as children become unhealthier.

## Slide 36

- 2. Primary Targets
- HWHL (2008)
- Reduce childhood obesity to 2000 levels by 2020.
- Address adult obesity through activity, food environment and workplace health.
- 2025–26 Policies
- Reverse increasing obesity rates post‑pandemic, especially in early‑years and primary school children.
- Close widening deprivation gaps in childhood obesity.[gov.uk]

## Slide 37

- 3. Methods of Intervention
- HWHL (2008)
- Behaviour change messaging.
- Investment in early‑years programmes.
- Encouraging physical activity through infrastructure (e.g., cycle lanes). [thensmc.com]
- Current Strategies (2025–26)
- Regulatory focus: limits on advertising of junk food, restrictions on high‑caffeine energy drinks.
- School‑based nutrition interventions: expanded free school meals, universal breakfast clubs.
- Tackling structural inequality. [gov.uk]

## Slide 38

- 4. Governance & Implementation
- HWHL (2008)
- Strong cross‑government collaboration, with a formal strategy document.
- Nationally coordinated and centrally funded. [data.parliament.uk], [navigator....lth.org.uk]
- 2025–26 Policies
- Currently implemented through Department of Health and Social Care, NHS England, and Department for Education.
- No single unified national obesity strategy branded like HWHL — more modular policy measures.

## Slide 39

- 5. Status & Legacy
- HWHL
- No longer an active national strategy.
- Ended after the 2008–2011 funding window.
- Lives on in local frameworks and influenced later campaigns like Change4Life.[navigator....lth.org.uk], [thensmc.com]
- 2025–26 Policies
- Represent the current national response to obesity.
- Stronger regulatory and inequality‑focused approaches. [gov.uk]

| Focus | Childhood obesity, behaviour change, multisector action | Childhood obesity, inequality reduction, structural regulation |
