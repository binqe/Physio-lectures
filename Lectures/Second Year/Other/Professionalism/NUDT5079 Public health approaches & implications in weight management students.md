## Slide 15

- Partnership: Responsibility Deal
- Launched in 2011 (Coalition Government)
- Aimed to get businesses working in partnership towards health & wellbeing targets
- Pledges: alcohol, food, health @ work, physical activity & behaviour change
- Food pledges:
- Action to reduce salt (e.g. reformulation, chef training, salt availability)
- Action to reduce calories (e.g. reformulation, portion sizes, education, marketing)
- Action to encourage fruit & vegetable consumption (positive environment)
- Calorie information provision in out-of-home setting
- Exclusion of artificial trans fats
- https://webarchive.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ukgwa/20130104160351/http://responsibilitydeal.dh.gov.uk/2011/12/20/food-pledges/

## Slide 16

- Success?
- Government claimed success
- Review found limited evidence: business as usual & pledges made were not those which would be most effective in improving diets (Knai et al, 2015)
- (Knai et al, 2018)

## Slide 17

- Regulation
- Calorie labelling (out of home):
- Commitment July 2020; Legislation July 2021
- Introduced April 2022
- Small reduction in average energy content of menu items (replacement of higher calorie with lower calorie items) (Essman et al, 2025)
- Promotion (volume price) & placement of HFSS:
- Commitment July 2020, planned implementation April 2022
- Delayed until October 2022; Legislation December 2021
- May 2022 delay to volume price promotions until October 2023
- June 2023, delay until October 2025
- Location promotions came into effect October 2022
- Junk food ads on TV and online:
- Scope June 2025; from 5 Jan 2026

## Slide 18

- Regulation
- Location promotions:
- Impact of location regulation: reduction in HFSS in-store sales by 0.63% (Jenneson et al, 2025)
- Lack of awareness of shoppers; supportive but importance of affordability emphasised (Kininmonth et al, 2025a)
- Stakeholders supportive but concerns about complexity of interpretation & implementation and practical issues (Kininmonth et al, 2025b)
- Potential impact on reformulation (Dhuria et al, 2025)
- Banning sales of high-caffeine energy drinks to <16 years:
- Commitment July 2024; Consultation closed November 2025

## Slide 19

- Reformulation
- SACN (2015) recommended sugar reduction to ≤5% daily EI
- PHE (2015) recommended: sugar reduction programme, restrictions on advertising & promotions, tax on high sugar food & drink
- Sugar reduction programme (2016): challenged industry to reduce sugar in foods that contribute most to children’s intakes (<18 years) by 20% by 2020.
- Guidelines published 2017.
- 2018: juice & milk-based drinks added. Challenge: reduce sugar by 5% & 20% respectively from baseline by mid-2021.
- Guidelines published 2018.

## Slide 23

- Soft Drinks Industry Levy (SDIL)
- Announced 2016; effective 2018
- Three categories: drinks <5g sugar/100mls, 5-8g sugar/100 mls & drinks ≥8g/100mls sugar/100mls
- Decision to extend scope & reduce cut-off to 4.5g/100mls from Jan 2028 made Nov 2025
- Impact:
- 2015-2019: 65% of drinks >5g/100mls reformulated to <5g/100mls
- 47% reduction in sugar in soft drinks in scope of levy 2015-2024
- 12% reduction hospital admissions for dental caries tooth extractions children 0-18years; 0-4yrs: 28.6% reduction & 5-9yrs: 5.5% reduction (Rogers et al, 2023)

## Slide 27

- Conclusions
- Multiple different approaches being taken
- Traditionally most rely on individual behavioural change
- Previously action to change obesogenic environment voluntary (limited impact)
- Regulatory effects shown to have more impact
- New treatments for excess weight  have issues - nutritional impacts, ST availability, accessibility & cost
- No single approach will be enough
