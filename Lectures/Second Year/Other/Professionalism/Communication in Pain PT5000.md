Chronic Pain
●Chronic pain → pain that persist beyond normal tissue healing time,

●Chronic pain has a large ﬁnancial impact, estimated at £12 billion in the 
UK in 2010, and disproportionately aﬀects older people and those 
living in deprivation or isolation.

Biopsychosocial Intervention
●A biopsychosocial intervention is deﬁned as an intervention that includes 
a physical component (for example, exercise), and a psychosocial 
component (for example, awareness of cognitive inﬂuence on pain, 
self-management).
●Recommended by both national and international practice guidelines 
and systematic reviews.

## Page 33

Fear-avoidance 
model
Fear-Avoidance model (Vlaeyen, 2000), 
avoidance of feared movements as a 
consequence of catastrophic thinking.
Discuss and expose patients to feared 
movements, thus increasing physical function 
and reducing catastrophic thinking, directly 
measured through increase in previously feared 
movements and reduction in scores on the Pain 
Catastrophizing Scale. 
Generalised improvement on disability and 
function scales, but not necessarily in pain 
levels.

## Page 37

Acceptance and Commitment 
Therapy ACT
1. Developing acceptance of unwanted private experiences which are out of 
personal control
2. Commitment and action towards living a valued life
3. Type of psychotherapy that helps move beyond negative thoughts and feeling is 
a meaningful way
4. Encourages people to embrace challenging thoughts, feeling and memories 
rather than trying to change them
5. Focus on developing a more positive relationship with these experiences 
6. ACT is eﬀective for depression, anxiety, stress and chronic pain

## Page 38

Key Principles:
1. Acceptance: opening up and making room for 
painful feelings, urges and sensations. 
2. Cognitive Defusion: Detach from unhelpful 
thoughts, beliefs and memories 
3. Being in the Present Moment: Engaging fully with 
your here-and-now experience with an attitude of 
openness, curiosity, interest and receptivity. 
4. The observing self/self as context: being aware 
and can attend and detach from thoughts, feelings 
and actions. 
5. Values: Understanding your core values
6. Committed Action: Creating goals with clear 
actionable steps that are aligned with your values.
