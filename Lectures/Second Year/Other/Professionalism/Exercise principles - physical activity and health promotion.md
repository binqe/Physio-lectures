–
Sarcopenia
–
Loss of muscle strength and power

–
Worsening balance
–
Decline in basal metabolic rate
–
Increase in fatt mass
–
Decrease in bone mineral density
■
Muscle mass decreases by 3-8% per decade after age 30 with a greater decline after age 60
–
In the absence of resistance training
■
Accelerated the process of fat infiltration into skeletal muscles
■
Physical inactivity is found to be associated with narrower lumbar intervertebral discs

## Page 19

Current guidelines
■
Age 5 – 17 
■
In terms of physical activity, it is recommended that: 
–
At least an average of 60min/day of moderate-to-vigorous 
intensity, mostly aerobic, physical activity, across the week; 
–
Vigorous-intensity aerobic activities, as well as those that 
strengthen muscle and bone should be incorporated at least 3 
days a week. 
■
In terms of sedentary behaviour, It is recommended that: 
–
Limit the amount of time spent being sedentary, particularly the 
amount of recreational screen time.

## Page 20

Current guidelines
■
Age 18 -64
■
In terms of physical activity, It is recommended that:
–
All adults should undertake regular physical activity; 
–
Adults should do at least 150–300 min of moderate-intensity aerobic 
physical activity, or at least 75–150min of vigorous-intensity aerobic 
physical activity, or an equivalent combination of moderate-intensity and 
vigorous-intensity activity throughout the week for substantial health 
benefits; 
–
Adults should also do muscle-strengthening activities at moderate or 
greater intensity that involve all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a 
week, as these provide additional health benefits. 
–
Adults may increase moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity to 
>300min, or do >150min of vigorous-intensity aerobic physical activity, or an 
equivalent combination of moderate-intensity and vigorous-intensity activity 
throughout the week for additional health benefits

## Page 21

Current guidelines
■
Age 18– 64
■
In terms of sedentary behaviours, It is recommended that: 
–
Adults should limit the amount of time spent being sedentary. 
–
Replacing sedentary time with physical activity of any intensity 
(including light intensity) provides health benefits; 
–
To help reduce the detrimental effects of high levels of sedentary 
behaviour on health, adults should aim to do more than the 
recommended levels of moderate-to vigorous physical activity.

## Page 23

Current guidelines
■
Pregnant and postpartum women
■
In terms of physical activity, It is recommended that all pregnant and postpartum women without 
contraindication should: 
–
Undertake regular physical activity throughout pregnancy and postpartum;  
–
Do at least 150min of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity throughout the week for 
substantial health benefits; 
–
Incorporate a variety of aerobic and muscle-strengthening activities. Adding gentle 
stretching may also be beneficial. 
–
Women who, before pregnancy, habitually engaged in vigorous-intensity aerobic activity or 
who were physically active can continue these activities during pregnancy and the 
postpartum period.
■
In terms of sedentary behaviours, 
–
Pregnant and postpartum women should limit the amount of time spent being sedentary. 
Replacing sedentary time with physical activity of any intensity (including light intensity) 
provides health benefits.

Evidence of current guidelines
■
Physical activity in any bouts of length is associated with improved health outcomes, 
including all-cause mortality
■
150 min of moderate-intensity exercise per week may improve physical and mental quality 
of life, and cognitive function in those aged 45–50 years and older
■
Certain ranges of physical activity accumulated duration captures the maximal risk 
reduction of health outcomes, with conclusions that going beyond this range does not 
further reduce the risk of major outcomes
■
Cross-sectional studies and exercise interventions suggest that these doses of activity are 
associated with favourable changes in blood pressure lipid and lipoprotein profiles, markers 
of inflammation, insulin sensitivity and other risk factors for chronic diseases
■
Recommended doses of activity are associated with 20–30% reductions in the risks of 
cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, depression, cognitive impairment in older adults, 
post-menopausal breast cancer,  and all-cause. Higher doses of activity may be necessary 
to reduce the risks of other common cancers, such as colon cancer and, possibly, high-
grade or advanced prostate cancer
