Presenting Condition:
Patient has been on the ward for 1 month having inpatient stroke rehabilitation, now he wants to go back home.  He can now turn in bed, sit on the side of bed and stand up from sitting independently. He walks with a tripod in the left hand but can only walk for 5 meters before requiring rest. He uses wheelchair for longer distances. His upper limb is still weak, and he cannot do any functional activity with the arm. His 75-year-old wife has agreed to look after him but has concerns about how they will cope in their flat because of her health conditions - chronic bilateral knee osteoarthritis and rheumatoid arthritis of the hands.
Objective Assessment
General observation:
Symmetrical sitting posture in his wheelchair by the side of his ward bed
Upper limb rests on a pillow placed on his lap
Evidence of ankle clonus occasionally seen in the right foot but well positioned on the footplate of the wheelchair.
Patient appears well and able to understand instructions.
His speech has improved but still have challenges pronouncing some words.
Functional Assessment
Bed mobility: Can be achieved independently
Lying to sitting at edge of bed: Can be achieved independently
Sit to stand: can be achieved with the left upper limb pulling on the side of the chair, limited weight transfer to the right lower limb on standing
Walking: Patient walks with a typical hemiplegic gait and very limited ankle dorsiflexion and hip flexion. There is also an associated hyper-extension of the knee on attempts to transfer weight.
Upper limb function: No functional activity possible in the upper limb.

Physical Assessment

| Treatment Delivery and SOAP Note Writing
Neurology Case Progression (Left Hemisphere Stroke with Right Hemiparesis)

Short-Term Treatment Goals (2-4 weeks):
Focus: Prioritize improving mobility, walking endurance, and functional independence in activities of daily living (ADLs).

Treatment:
Strengthening: Implement lower limb strengthening exercises, focusing on the patient's right leg (e.g., resisted exercises for the hip and knee).
Gait Training: Provide gait training to address the hemiplegic gait pattern, with particular focus on improving ankle dorsiflexion and hip flexion.
Functional Task Practice: Facilitate sit-to-stand transfers and bed mobility, ensuring safety and appropriate use of upper limbs for assistance.
Use of Equipment: Continue mobility aids such as the tripod, but work on gradually reducing reliance through strength and balance training.
Patient Education: Teach compensatory techniques for upper limb deficits and energy conservation strategies for the patient and caregiver.

Long-Term Goal Progress (6-12 weeks):
Focus: Maximize the patient's independence at home and reduce reliance on assistive devices.

Treatment:
Advanced Gait Training: Introduce longer walking distances, focusing on minimizing the hyperextension of the knee and improving symmetry.
Balance and Stability Exercises: Implement exercises to improve balance during functional movements like standing and walking.
Upper Limb Function: Begin passive and active-assisted movement exercises to increase upper limb mobility and reduce tone in the elbow flexors.
Home Modifications: Assess for potential modifications in the patient's home, such as grab bars or ramps, to support mobility and safety.




SOAP Note for ongoing Treatment
Provide standardized documentation of the patient’s ongoing progress and treatment in a clinical setting. Below is a breakdown of what to include in each section based on the patient’s case.

S – Subjective:
Include any new information or changes reported by the patient or family regarding their condition or progress.


O – Objective:
Document the measurable data gathered during the session, such as strength tests, range of motion, or observations about the patient’s functional performance.


A – Assessment:
Provide a professional assessment of the patient's progress, functional status, and any changes in their condition.


P – Plan:
Outline the next steps in treatment based on the assessment and any changes in goals. |
