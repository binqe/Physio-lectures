## Page 7

Aetiology of neurological conditions
Sudden onset conditions – acute onset, followed by partial recovery e.g., acquired 
brain injury such as stroke or spinal cord injury  
Intermittent and unpredictable conditions - where relapses and remissions lead to 
marked variation in the care needed. e.g., epilepsy, certain types of headache or early multiple 
sclerosis 
Progressive conditions - where progressive deterioration in neurological function leads 
to increasing dependence on help and care from others. e.g., Motor Neurone Disease 
(MND), Parkinson’s Disease (PD).
Stable neurological conditions - but with changing needs due to development or 
ageing.  E.g., post-polio syndrome or cerebral palsy in adults.

## Page 25

Definition of Terms in the ICF
FUNCTIONING - POSITIVE
•
Body function refers to the physiological 
functioning of the body system
•
Body structures refers to anatomical parts 
of the body such as organs limbs etc.
•
Activity is the execution of a task or action 
by an individual e.g., Sit to stand, walking, 
bed mobility, self-care activities etc.
•
Participation in a life situation for example 
employment, leisure activities etc
DISABILITY – NEGATIVE CONSEQUENCES
•
Impairments or problems in body function 
or structure such as a significant deficit or 
loss e.g., reduced muscle strength, bulk etc
•
Activity limitations are difficulties an 
individual may have experience in executing 
activities e.g., Loss of independence in 
feeding, grooming etc
•
Participation restrictions are problems an 
individual may experience in involvement in 
life situations e.g., Difficulties encountered 
accessing employment/university education 
etc.

## Page 45

Chronic Case study
MT is a 55year old teacher who lives in a terraced house which has one flight of stairs with one 
of her two sons. She got divorced 10 years ago. She has a history of hypertension; she smokes 
and is overweight. Her hobbies are gardening, and twice a week she plays bridge at her local 
club. 
She suffered a stroke, six months ago, when she woke up and was unable to get out of bed or 
talk properly. She was taken to hospital and on admission she was paralysed on the right side of 
her body with low tone and reduced reflexes and could not speak. Patient was treated in the 
hospital and discharged after 3 months.
At 6 months,  MT is able to get up and downstairs with help from her son. She can also walk 
outside her house. However, MT has fallen three times in the past 3 months, twice while walking 
outside the house and once while getting out of bed at night. She continues to receive 
community physiotherapy.
