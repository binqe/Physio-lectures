Muscle disorders 
(Muscle 
dystrophy)
• Group of inherited disorders 
characterized by degeneration of skeletal 
muscle
• Rare
• Progressive
• Physical disability
• Reduced life expectancy
• Prevalence: 1: 1000
• Same age presentation
• Improvement in management
• Longer survival – older patients with MD

Types and classification of Muscle Diseases
• Advances in genetics and molecular biology 
• Genetic testing has broadened ability to detect 
different types
• BMD
• LGMD
• Muscular dystrophy (MD) – heterogenous 
group of genetically determined disorders 
associated with progressive degeneration of 
skeletal muscles 
• Classified according to age of onset, inheritance 
pattern, and molecular and genetic defect
• Dystrophin protein defect
• X-chromosome inheritance
• Males are affected but females are carriers
• Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD)
• Becker muscular dystrophy (BMD)
• Congenital muscular dystrophy (CMD)
• Emery-Dreifuss muscular dystrophy (EDMD) 
• Facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy 
(FSHD)
• Limb girdle muscular dystrophy (LGMD)
• Myotonic dystrophy (DM)
• Oculopharyngeal muscular dystrophy 
(OPMD)
• Acquired muscle disorders:
• Polymyositis
• Dermatomyositis
• Myastenia gravis

## Page 8

Duchenne Muscle 
Dystrophy (DMD)
• First described by Meryon in 1852, later by 
Duchenne
• Rapidly progressive
• Most severe form of MDs
• 1: 3500 male births
• Prevalence of 1.9 -4.8 per 100,000

## Page 9

Clinical and diagnostic 
features
• Delayed motor milestones
• Leaning difficulties (1/3)
• Late walker – 18 months
• Diagnosis often missed until weakness starts
• Hip and knee extensor weakness – Gower ‘s 
maneuver
• Weak abductors – waddling gait
• Wide base of support
• Hip abduction contractures (Iliotibial tract tightness)

DMD – clinical and diagnostic features
• Toes walking (7-8 years old)
• Equinus foot posture →
• Contracture of Achilles tendon 
• Inability to ambulate by 13th birthday
• Flexion contractures elbow, hips and 
knees
• Bilateral muscle involvement
• Proximal >> distal 
• Tendon reflex→ depressed → lost
• In ambulatory phase
• Pelvic >> shoulder girdle
• Extensors >> flexors
• Contractures
• Muscle imbalance and postural 
maladjustment
• Fixed
• Scoliosis  → ↑ respiratory muscle 
weakness → bed bound

Pathology of DMD
• Weakness  → gradual loss of functional muscle fibres with 
fat and connective tissue due to lack of dystrophin
• Dystrophin  - a protein encoded by Xp21 gene is the primary 
biochemical defect
• Dystrophin -  is integral within a complex of proteins which 
stabilises the integrity of the sarcolemma membrane
• Absence results in a reduction in permeability of the muscle 
cell membrane
• This leads to excessive quantities of calcium to accumulate 
within the muscle causing breakdown of myofibrils 
breakdown and muscle fibre degeneration

## Page 14

Becker Muscular 
Dystrophy (BMD)
• First described in 1956
• Allelic to DMD but milder
• Only affects males
• Prevalence of 1 in 30000
• Caused by partial deficiency of 
protein dystrophin
• Symptoms
• Muscle weakness
• Muscle atrophy
• Disability
• Cardiomyopathy
• Respiratory muscle weakness

## Page 15

Clinical & diagnostic features of BMD
• Wide spectrum
• Severe: lost ambulation by 16 years of age
• Mild: non-progressive cramps and 
myoglobulinuria
• Muscle weakness – distribution like DMD
• Slower progression
• Less severe contractures
• Calf pseudo-hypertrophy
• Ambulation lost – 40%
• Malignant hyperthermia from general 
anaethesia
• Cardiomyopathy – ECG & echocardiogram 
abnormalities  - 50%
•
Diagnosis is confirmed by:
•
Genetic mutation in the dystrophin gene
•
Abnormal dystrophin staining on a muscle biopsy

General management
• Longer term management
• Specialist MDT – community medical, educational, social worker
• At diagnosis
• Support worker to maintain contact
• MDT developmental assessment
• Screen for learning difficulty
• Social services to assess state financial support – home adaptation etc
• Physical management
• Tightness  - daily passive stretches of TA with night splints; iliotibial band tightness, hip flexors
• Prolong ambulation  - to prevent rapid development of scoliosis and joint contractures
