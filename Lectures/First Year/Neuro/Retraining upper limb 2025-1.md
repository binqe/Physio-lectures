GRASPING & MANIPULATION
Hand is the principal means to interact with people & objects in the 
external environment
Anatomical structure & specific cortico-motoneuronal connections to hand 
muscles allow many combinations of joint rotations and movement 
possibilities
Approach to grasp and manipulation is determined by:
Intention
Object characteristics
Task constraints
Studies have found that when the fingers do manipulative tasks, the 
pattern is one of:
Fractionating of muscle activity
During gripping tasks
A pattern of co-contraction emerges  with the number of muscles involved depending on the 
force exerted

## Page 14

BIMANUAL 
REACHING 
TASKS
Upper limbs co-operate together to perform the 
task
E.g. anticipatory postural adjustments during ball drop 
into a cup held by the other hand
Speed of hand movement is affected when both hands 
are used (if one hand performs a more complex task 
requiring longer time, the other hand slows down)
Given the complexity of bi-manual tasks, it is necessary 
to provide opportunities for the patient to practice such 
tasks otherwise regaining this will not occur
Upper limb in weight bearing
Tasks such as pushing a drawer shut, push the door, or 
push oneself up the floor (Closed-chain activities)
Movement occurs principally at the shoulder and elbow
Shoulder and elbow extensor muscles (Triceps brachii)
Flexible wrist to allow necessary extension so that 
pushing can occur through palm of hand

RETRAINING REACHING AND MANIPULATION
Effective functional use of the hand  -  dependent on functional hand grasp and 
release
Movement of the upper limb  - driven by the hand and the placement of the hand 
in a person’s close environment or workspace
Consequently, reaching practice should always involve:
Use of a target or an object in hand
Intensive & Repetitive practice of finger and wrist exercises
Early initiation of practice
With severe weakness, emphasis is on eliciting activities in key muscles plus repetitive practice of simple 
movements
Reaching and finger movements involved in grasp and release are emphasised
Focus  - to promote activity for different tasks and prevent contractures in at-risks muscles
Apply motor learning principles: instruction, feedback, external focus etc
Task modification: modify what is to be practiced, taking into account the 
individual’s motor impairments
Constraint of body segment

BIMANUAL PRACTICE
Important because the Upper 
Limb work co-operatively in most 
everyday functions e.g.
Unscrewing a jar
Folding clothes
Making tea
Crying a tea cup etc
How:
Early – as soon as patient have ability 
with simple movements
Practice simple tasks in which both 
hands work co-operatively
Do a task where both hands are performing 
different action – e,g, opening a can
Do a task where both hands are doing same 
action e.g. folding a towel
Working in opposite directions  using a gardening 
shears
Doing rhythmical sequential actions like arm 
cycling
