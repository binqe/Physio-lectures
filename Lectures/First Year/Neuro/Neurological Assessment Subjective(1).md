# Neurological Assessment Subjective

Neurological Assessment: Subjective
Practical guide
At the end of this practical, students should be able to:
Perform a subjective assessment of a neurological patient
PART A: Subjective Assessment (30 minutes)
Case study:
Referral:  You have been sent a referral which reads:
MT is a 55-year-old patient with stroke who lives in the community has been referred for continuing therapy after 6 months of cerebrovascular accident.
Conduct a subjective assessment of MT.
Task
In groups of three: one person acting as the physiotherapy student, another as the patient and the third an observer, conduct a subjective assessment for this patient.
Patient (reads background information)
Observer (reads background information)
Physiotherapists (do not read background information)
Note: The associated background information gives more details about this patient
Use the guide below for your questioning.
Things to think of:
What questions are you going to use to gather the information that you need to collect without using medical terminologies?
Areas to cover
Personal information (Demographics)
Name
Address
Age
Present complaint
The diagnosis
Main problems at the present time
Patient’s perceptions of their strength and weaknesses
Patient’s goals and expectations of physiotherapy
History of present complaint
Note: Some of these might have been collected by other professionals, so to avoid asking the same questions, look at other available notes. If the MDT uses electronic records, these should be readily available
Date of onset of the signs and symptoms
Progression of the condition
Any investigations and results
Medical management
Medical observations: in acute condition, or exacerbation of a chronic condition, the therapists should check the patient is medically stable before approaching to carry out subjective assessment
Other management: be aware of other treatments that is ongoing
Previous therapy
Pain
Past Medical History (PMH)
Co-morbidities e.g. mental health, learning disability, asthma, diabetes, osteoarthritis, hypertension, cardiac arrythmias
Previous surgery
Technology use: pacemaker, gastric tube etc
Special equipment e.g. walking aids
Risk factors: hypertension, obesity, raised cholesterol, diabetes mellitus, atrial fibrillation, other cardiac conditions etc
Previous neurological conditions: stroke, transient ischaemic attacks etc
Drug History (DH):
some drugs have side effects such as drowsiness etc
Social History (SH). Questions depends on
The setting: in-patient vs outpatient/community
The level of function of the patient
Activities of daily living (ADL)
Personal ADL (PADL) e.g. washing, dressing, feeding/drinking, toileting
Domestic ADL e.g. cooking, cleaning, shopping
Mobility
General Mobility e.g. transfers
Walking indoors e.g. how far can they walk indoors
Walking outdoors e.g. how far can they walk outdoors
Stairs and steps e.g. independence
Falls e.g. falls, and what circumstances
Other e.g. any other forms of exercises
A good way to get through this is to ask a patient to talk you through their normal day
Environment e.g. where patient live, access from outside, general layout, is it rural or urban.
Psychosocial
Personal factors e.g. lack of confidence, low self-esteem, depression, anxiety, stress, challenging behaviour ad lack of motivation
Lifestyle including occupation – job types, what job entails, hobbies, education
Support e.g. personal support – who can help patient – family, friends, colleagues or dependants. Personal finances – is patient financially independent
Family situation e.g. does patient live alone, married or have children
Discussion with Lecturer (15 minutes)
Did you find any aspect difficult? What do you think could help?
Group Discussion: What would you assess objectively in this patient and why?
