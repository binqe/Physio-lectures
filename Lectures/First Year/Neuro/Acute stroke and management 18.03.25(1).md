## Slide 9

- Management of Ischaemic Stroke
- preservation of penumbral tissue surrounding ischaemic core
- thrombolysis within 4.5 hours of symptom onset (alteplase or tenecteplase)
- mechanical thrombectomy within 6 hours of symptom onset for large artery occlusion within anterior circulation
- decompressive hemicraniectomy for large MCA territory strokes with specific criteria

## Slide 10

- Haemorrhagic Stroke

**Notes:** haemorrhagic stroke comprises ~ 12.5% of cases

## Slide 11

- Management of Haemorrhagic Stroke
- reversal of anticoagulation agents
- blood pressure management- aiming for systolic BP between 130-139 mmHg for the first 7 days
- neurosurgery in some instances e.g. external ventricular drain to manage hydrocephalus, clipping of burst aneurysm

## Slide 12

- Maintain Physiological Homeostasis
- patients should have their clinical status monitored closely, including:
- level of consciousness
- blood glucose (5 – 15 mmol/L)
- blood pressure
- oxygen saturation (O2 only if saturation <95%)
- hydration and nutrition, including safe swallowing
- temperature
- cardiac rhythm and rate

## Slide 13

- Post-Stroke Complications
- post-stroke are common, with a frequency between 30 – 95%
- post-stroke complications are associated with poor outcome, including death and disability
- factors associated with post-stroke complications include stroke severity, older age, pre-existing disability, diabetes mellitus, and length of hospital stay beyond 30 days
- many post-stroke complications are potentially preventable and treatable

## Slide 14

- Post-Stroke Complications
| Body System | Complication |
| Neurological | recurrent stroke, haemorrhagic transformation, epileptic seizures |
| Cardiovascular | cardiac arrythmias, deep vein thrombosis, pulmonary embolism |
| Respiratory | chest infection |
| Skin | pressure sores |
| Musculoskeletal | contractures, hemiplegic shoulder pain, falls |
| Gastrointestinal | dysphagia, malnutrition |
| Genitourinary | urinary tract infection, urinary and faecal incontinence |
| Cognitive | pain, fatigue |

## Slide 15

- Prevention of Common Complications
| Complication | Prevention and Management Strategies |
| Recurrent stroke | antiplatelet or anticoagulant medication |
| Deep vein thrombosis | intermittent pneumatic compression |
| Pressure sores | positioning, pressure relieving mattresses, adequate nutrition |
| Contractures | regular assessment, positioning, passive ranging |
| Pain | specialist assessment, strategies based on assessment findings |
| Chest infection | swallowing/respiratory assessment, strategies based on assessment |
| Urinary tract infection | regular personal care, adequate hydration, avoid catheter use |

## Slide 17

- Respiratory Management
- Respiratory problems post stroke can be divided into:
- neurological e.g. apnoea
- infective e.g. aspiration pneumonia
- cardiovascular e.g. pulmonary embolus
- Physiotherapy management involves:
- positioning
- sputum clearance techniques
- Treatment of atelectasis
- Mobilisation
- patient/staff education

## Slide 18

- Positioning
- correct positioning is postulated to:
- promote functional recovery
- prevent complications
- improve physiological functioning
- patients should have an initial specialist assessment for positioning as soon as possible and within 4 hours of arrival at hospital

## Slide 19

- Positioning
- patients should be allowed to adopt either a sitting-up or lying-flat head position in the first 24 hours, according to comfort
- patients should be positioned to minimise the risk of aspiration and other respiratory complications, shoulder pain and subluxation,  contractures and skin pressure ulceration

## Slide 20

- Early Mobilisation
- early mobilisation includes sitting on the edge of the bed, transferring out of bed, standing and walking
- patients with difficulty moving after stroke should be assessed as soon as possible within the first 24 hours to determine the most appropriate and safe methods of transfer and mobilisation

## Slide 21

- Early Mobilisation
- patients should be offered frequent, short daily mobilisations with access to appropriate equipment, typically beginning between 24 and 48 hours of stroke onset
- mobilisation within 24 hours of onset should only be for patients who require little or no assistance to mobilise

## Slide 22

- Upper Limb Management
- upper limb management is guided by the degree of upper limb impairment
- positioning and correct handling should be considered for patients with no or limited upper limb movement

## Slide 23

- Upper Limb Management
- patients with some upper limb movement should be offered repetitive task practice as the principal rehabilitation approach
- patients who have at least 20 o active wrist extension and 10 o active finger extension should be considered for constraint-induced movement therapy
- adjunctive therapies include functional electrical stimulation, mental practice and mirror therapy
