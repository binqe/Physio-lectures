## Page 10

Retraining sensory strategies
•
Exercises for over-reliance on proprioceptive information: 
•
Perform tasks while sitting or standing on surfaces with decreased somatosensory cues 
for balance: carpet, foam, pillows, moving surfaces-wobble boards. 
•
Exercises for over-reliance on visual cues for balance: 
•
Perform tasks where visual cues are absent (eyes closed, blindfolded) or reduced 
(blinders, diminished lighting).
•
Visual cues can also be made inaccurate through the use of glasses smeared with 
petroleum jelly, prism glasses, moving curtains with stripes, moving visual scenes.
•
Exercises for enhanced ability to use vestibular information: 
•
Exercises where one must maintain balance while both visual and somatosensory 
inputs are simultaneously reduced, such as standing on foam or a wobble board with 
eyes closed or moving visual scenes

## Page 11

Progressing 
balance 
exercises
• Practice with sitting and standing
• Increased time in static posture with eyes open and closed
• With reduced base of support (eyes open and closed)
• While changing the orientation of the head and trunk (e.g. 
looking over one’s shoulder, leaning over)
• While performing a variety of upper extremity tasks
beyond base of support
• As patient improves, more difficult and demanding tasks 
can be introduced.
• Incorporating cognitive tasks: e.g. maintaining 
standing balance with a small base of support while 
counting backwards in threes.
• Dual attention: e.g. walking while holding a glass of 
water; sitting on wobble board and trying to maintain 
balance while holding a glass of water with eyes open and 
eyes closed.
