# Polyneuropathy case study

Gullain Barre Syndrome Case study
Background to the case study
Tom Green is a 56-year-old male, who first presented to accident and emergency with a severe gastro-intestinal infection approximately 5 months ago. After this infection was dealt with medically, he was discharged home with no change in baseline or complications. A few weeks later he felt numbness and tingling in his feet and hands along with pain that got worse with movement. During the following week he proceeded to feel weaker, and the tingling progressively worsened, eventually leading to admission to accident and emergency.
Tom could hardly move for 2 weeks and required intensive care unit support. The healthcare team did an excellent job of ensuring he at lest attempted to mobilize routinely and was positioned properly to avoid contractures or other complications – using postural drainage techniques, splinting, active assisted range of movement, etc all performed as necessary. It has been 4 months since his last admission to the hospital. No other medical conditions/co-morbidities have since arisen and his medical status remained stable.
Tom is has been referred to you for sub-acute rehabilitation in the community.
Objective Assessment
Observation
Tom sits in a slouched sitting posture in the chair posture
Passive Range of movement
Bilateral rigidity and spasticity in his upper and lower limbs.
Passive movements are limited by mild cog-wheel type rigidity and muscle spasm. Movements at almost all joints are limited to some degree, however, the below movements are very limited and impose the most restriction to function
Shoulder abduction: 69 degrees out of 175 degrees
Hip flexion: 56 degrees out of 11degrees
Ankle dorsiflexion: 5 degrees out of 20 degrees
Manual muscle test
Able to perform all motions tested against gravity, however, he has general weakness present in all muscle groups. The greatest limitations are due to the following muscles
Ankle plantar flexors: 2
Hip flexors: 2
Knee flexors: 2
Shoulder abduction: 2
Neurological assessment
Reflexes: All reduced except Knee jerk which was absent
Sensation: No differences between sides but hypersensitivity found.
Balance
Able to stand on two feet independently with eyes open
Transfers & ADLs
Has difficulties transferring from sitting to standing and requires assistance of one person.
Once standing, he can walk with his four wheeled walker
Requires assistance to complete most of his ADLs such as dressing, toileting,
Walking
Able to ambulate 200m with a four-wheeled walker.
He walked at a slow pace and had to stop twice during the test.
He walks with a narrow and short stride.
He has a slight drop foot and relies predominantly on hip flexion and abduction to progress feet forward due to weak plantar flexors.

| Present condition | Tom reports that he has regained some motor control of his upper and lower extremities, but he stills feels very weak and uncoordinated. Weakness and impaired co-ordination lead to fumbling while completing daily chores. He also has lost some mobility, in particular with raising his arms and flexing his hip. This has led to difficulty dressing and getting into shower on his own. He gained enough motor control to eat independently; however, this is still a challenging task.

He can walk short distances, about 10 minutes at a time, with a four -wheeled walker, but must stop due to shortness of breath, fatigue, and pain in his feet. His sensitivity to pain, in general has increased notably. He has noticed that once non-painful things such as friction on his skin or muscle contractions elicit a painful response, he experiences particularly high level of pain in his back and thighs.

Tom reports feelings of embarrassment from all the help |
| Social History | Tom lives with his wife and teenage son, 
Before Tom had GBS, he worked on the assembly line at a car manufacturing plant. 
Tom’s work has been understanding, however, he has not been able to return to work yet and worries that the pain in his feet will keep him from ever returning to his regular duties. 
On weekends Tom would get together with his friends at the local pub to play billiards. Since the GBS he has not returned to the pub and misses the enjoyment this brought him. |
| Goals and expectations | Be able to get dressed and get into the shower on his own and have less dependence on his family for basic tasks. 
Return to work. Although he is close to retiring, he would like to keep working to support his son through college. 
Return to playing pool with his friends. He is worried that if he is not able to play pool he will lose touch with his friends. 
Hoping physiotherapy can help him increase his upper limb’s coordination, range of motion and strength. 
Hoping to receive some exercises to improve his walking so he can be less dependent on his walker. 
Lastly, Tom has heard that physiotherapy may also help with his pain but is a little sceptical that physiotherapy has much to offer him in terms of pain reduction. |
