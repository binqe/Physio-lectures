C. Neurological Terminologies 
1. Paresis –Paralysis/weakness 
2. Hemiparesis – Paralysis/weakness affecting half of the body 
3. Tetraparesis – Paralysis/weakness affecting 4 limbs 
4. Paraparesis – Weakness affecting the lower limbs 
5. Athetosis-slow writhing involuntary movements 
6. Proprioception – awareness of the location, movement and action of a body part in space 
7. Sensation – the conscious perception of feeling when the body is touched. Can refer to 
touch, pressure, pain and temperature. 
8. Paraesthesia- spontaneous burning, pins and needles sensation not associated with any 
physical findings 
9. Aphasia/dysphasia – an acquired loss or impairment of language - can affect spoken word, 
understanding, reading and writing. 
10. Aphonia-inability to speak or make sound due to damage to the laryngeal nerve 
11. Atrophy – wasting of muscles due to lack of use or a neurological disease 
12. Hypertrophy – increase size/volume of an organ or tissue. Often used to describe skeletal or 
cardiac muscle. 
13. Athetosis – involuntary movement disorder characterised by slow, purposeless, writhing 
movements, often more evident in the distal part of a limb. 
14. Dysarthria – Slurred speech 
15. Muscle Tone – the tension in the relaxed muscle/the resistance felt during passive 
movement when the muscles are at rest (Ganguly et al., 2021). 
16. Hypertonic-increased muscle tone 
17. Hypotonic-low muscle tone 
18. Flaccid- Lack of muscle tone and strength in a limb 
19. Subluxed - a partial dislocation of a joint. Often used to describe the shoulder joint after 
brain injury/stroke. 
20. Tremor – involuntary movement. Can be at rest or during movement. 
21. Clonus – regular, repetitive, rhythmic contraction of a muscle subjected to a sudden or 
maintained stretch (Barrett et al., 2016 cited Ganguly et al., 2021) 
22. Spasticity - disordered sensory-motor control, resulting from an UMN lesion, presenting as 
intermittent or sustained involuntary action (SPASM definition) 
23. Contracture- A permanent shortening of a muscle causing a loss of joint range of movement 
and relaxation of extensor muscles. Abrupt withdrawal of a body part in response to a 
painful or perceived noxious stimuli

24. Rigidity – Increased stiffness or resistance to movement. Can be lead pipe – constant 
resistance to movement through range or cogwheel – resistance that stops and starts as the 
limb is moved through range. 
25. Dystonia – A movement disorder that leads to abnormal muscle tone, uncontrolled 
movements and spasms. 
26. Dysmetria- Disturbance of co-ordination due to damage to the cerebellar where a person 
over or underestimates the movement needed to complete a task 
27. Dysphagia – Difficulty with swallowing 
28. Allodynia- Hypersensitivity to touch that causes pain or discomfort 
29. Apraxia – a disorder of movement characterised by the inability/difficulty to perform a 
voluntary movement despite an intact motor system. Can affect speech and movement. Can 
be described as ideomotor (difficulty in the ability to plan or complete a previously learned 
movement or mimic movements shown by someone else, despite adequate motor or 
sensory abilities) or ideational (Difficulties in executing a sequence of actions when 
performing a complex multi-task e.g. making a coffee 
30. Akinesia- Inability to start a movement 
31. Amnesia – Memory Loss 
32. Ataxia – a lack of voluntary coordination of muscle movements, impairing their smooth 
performance. Also termed for a group of disorders that affect coordination, balance and 
speech. 
33. Coordination – the ability to execute movement, which is accurate, smooth and controlled. 
This includes the appropriate speed, distance, direction, timing and muscular tension. 
34. Bradykinesia- Slowness of movement 
35. Festinating gait -commonly used to describe gait in PD. Characterised by a short stride 
length, increased speed of movement with tendency to lean forward. 
36. Freezing – Abrupt termination of a movement/difficult initiating movement. 
37. Nystagmus- Rhythmical oscillations of the eyes 
38. Hemianopia- loss of half vision field 
39. Diplopia- Double vision 
40. VOR – Vestibulo-ocular Reflex (Reflex that controls eye gaze)
