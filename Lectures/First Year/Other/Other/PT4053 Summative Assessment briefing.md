# Summative Assessment briefing

Evidence-based practice 1: Study skills and personal development, digital literacy and skills
Module Assessment Briefing
2024/25 Academic Year
Module learning outcomes:
At the end of this module, students will be able to:
Self-reflect on own knowledge and identify learning needs for continuing academic development as an important requirement for professional practice.
Develop effective academic study and digital skills required for life-long learning and professional development.
Demonstrate a range of skills to search and analyse academic and clinical literature.
Demonstrate ability to write academic essays and use appropriate referencing styles for different types of writing.
Identify the nature of measurements and consider factors that affects their utility for gathering information about patients’ health in research and clinical practice.
Identify common outcome measurements used to evaluate the outcomes of physiotherapy interventions and service improvements.
Summative assessment task
This module's assessment consists of a 1500-word academic portfolio designed to evaluate your achievement of the module's learning outcomes. The portfolio is composed of three key tasks, each contributing to the overall assessment where reflecting on academic skills  contributes 20% to your final assessment; critical writing is 30%, and outcome measures at 20%. These tasks will allow you to demonstrate your understanding and application of essential academic and clinical skills.
Assessment Tasks:
Reflection on Academic Skills and Self-Development Plan (20%)
In this task, you are required to critically reflect on your current academic skills. You should identify areas of strength as well as areas for improvement. A model of reflection could be used to guide your reflection. Based on this reflection, you will create a self-development plan, outlining specific strategies to enhance your academic abilities. This reflection should be supported with evidence based on reading in the literature and demonstrate a clear understanding of how you plan to achieve your academic goals. This part of the portfolio is weighted 20%
Words Count: 500 words
Critical Piece of Writing (30%)
This task requires you to submit a critical analysis on a relevant that will be provided to you ahead of time, demonstrating your ability to construct a well-reasoned argument. You must use appropriate academic referencing throughout supporting your reasoning and argument, ensuring your sources are credible and cited according to academic standards. This piece should display your critical thinking and engagement with academic literature. This exercise aims to evaluate your capacity to engage with scholarly sources and present well-founded, articulate insights on the given subject matter. This part of the portfolio is weighted 30%
Words Count: 500 words
Review of a Clinical Outcome Measure (20%)
In this task, you will critically evaluate a clinical outcome measure in Musculoskeletal, neurological or cardiorespiratory physiotherapy of your selection, demonstrating your understanding of its relevance, application, and limitations in practice. Your review should reflect evidence-based practice and your ability to assess clinical tools used in your field. This part of the portfolio is weighted 20%.
•	Words Count: 500 words
Other weighing of the final assessment include structure and clarity of writing (10%), and Use of Evidence and referencing (10%), are elaborated in detail in the marking rubric.
Total Word Count:
Each submission must be 500 words, a total of 1500 words across the portfolio. Any word count beyond the above numbers will not be marked.
The word count excludes the reference list.
Pass Mark:
The minimum pass mark for this module is 40%. You must achieve this to pass the module. The marking Rubric could be found in the assessment part of the module on Weblearn.
Submission Guidelines:
The portfolio must be submitted by 15:00 of the 25th of January 2025. It is your responsibility to ensure that you submit your coursework by the deadline.
Each task will be marked according to the criteria provided, and the portfolio will contribute 100% of the final module grade.
The academic tutor or the module lead could be asked for help relating to the writing process any time before 17:00 on 18/01/2025, allowing for 5 business days to respond.
Your submission must have a cover page with your student number and not your name, and the word count for your submission.
Referencing
You are expected to use a reference manager (e.g. Mendeley) in your submission as taught in your module.
All tasks must adhere to appropriate academic referencing standards.
You are expected to use Harvard referencing style  for in-text citations and reference lists.
Ensure that all sources are credible and peer-reviewed, including journal articles, books, and other academic publications.
The reference list is required for each task provided collectively at the end of your submission but will not count toward the word limit. Failure to include correct and consistent referencing will impact your marks.
Academic Integrity and Plagiarism
Academic integrity requires honesty in your studies. You should not present another person’s sentences or ideas as your own work. You should clearly identify quotations through the use of quotation marks and references to the sources. Failure to adhere to these academic standards may lead to allegations of academic misconduct, which will be investigated by the Casework Office.
Once you uploaded your file, you will see a Turnitin report, informing you of the similarity level. This should not exceed 20% and it excludes the references.
Academic misconduct covers a variety of practices, such as:
Plagiarism: copying another person’s ideas or words and presenting them as your own work, without the use of quotation marks and/or references.
Self-plagiarism: reproducing parts of one of your assignments in another piece of work.
Inventing, altering, or falsifying the results of experiments or research.
Commissioning another person to complete an assessment.
Collaborating with others in the production of a piece of assessed work which is presented as entirely your own work.
For full details of academic misconduct and how allegations are investigated, see the relevant section of the University’s academic regulations: https://student.londonmet.ac.uk/your-studies/student-administration/rules-and-regulations/academic-misconduct/.
