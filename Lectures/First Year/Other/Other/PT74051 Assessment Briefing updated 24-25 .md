# PT74051 Assessment Briefing 24-25

Foundations of Professional Practice
PT4051
Assessment Briefing: Reflective Portfolio
2024/25 Academic Year
Module learning outcomes:
Demonstrate an understanding of the nature of contemporary physiotherapy practice and its legal and professional expectations such as from the HCPC (standards of proficiency and standards of conduct, performance, and ethics) and CSP (knowledge, skills, behaviours, and values), including requirements for equality, diversity and inclusion, and fitness to practice etc.
Appraise their own learning needs and develop a personal development plan to support these needs for evidence-based physiotherapy practice.
Demonstrate ability to reflect on personal practice to identify own limits of competence, and the importance of developing and following a continuing professional development plan.
Demonstrate ability to use appropriate verbal and non-verbal communication skills when working with different service users, considering equality, diversity and inclusion, need for informed and valid consents, intersectional, cultural differences and confidentiality.
Formative reflective practice:
Using the template given for reflecting on practice and learning, write a brief reflective piece (250 words) on any one of the following:
Previous healthcare/workplace experience that made an impression on you about professionalism.
Reflect on one of the HCPC standards of proficiency (SOPS) on professionalism.
Deadline for submission is 25th October at 4:15pm
Summative Reflective Portfolio
Reflective Essay
Students will be required to produce a 1500-word reflective essay to demonstrate their ability to meet the expectations of the HCPC in these four areas:
1.	Promote and protect the interest of service users and carers.
2.	Communicate appropriately and effectively.
3.	Working within the limits of your knowledge and skills
4.	Being honest and trustworthy.
Your writing on each area should not be more than 300 words.  You will also be required to write a 300-word personalised development plan for continuing professional development in HCPC standards of proficiency and standard of conduct, performance, and ethics.
Attendance
Students are expected to attend at least 80% of scheduled sessions for the module. This attendance requirement account for 0% of the module mark.
Guidelines
Use the template provided in your reflective practice sessions to prepare your reflection. You are required to utilise at least one research paper to support your reflection in each of the reflective pieces you present.
You must include a list of references for your submission, and this should be presented in Harvard referencing style. See example below for your information and guidance.
Word count: 1500 words excluding references. Any more words will not be marked
Your submission must have a cover page with your student number and not your name, and the word count for your submission.
You are expected to use Harvard referencing style

Fleming, M.K., Sorinola, I.O., Roberts-Lewis, S.F., Wolfe, C.D., Wellwood, I., Newham, D.J. (2015). Academic Integrity and Plagiarism
Academic integrity requires honesty in your studies. You should not present another person’s sentences or ideas as your own work. You should clearly identify quotations through the use of quotation marks and references to the sources. Failure to adhere to these academic standards may lead to allegations of academic misconduct, which will be investigated by the Casework Office.
Once you uploaded your file, you will see a Turnitin report, informing you of the similarity level. This should not exceed 20% and it excludes the references.
Academic misconduct covers a variety of practices, such as:
Plagiarism: copying another person’s ideas or words and presenting them as your own work, without the use of quotation marks and/or references;
Self-plagiarism: reproducing parts of one of your assignments in another piece of work;
Inventing, altering, or falsifying the results of experiments or research;
Commissioning another person to complete an assessment;
Collaborating with others in the production of a piece of assessed work which is presented as entirely your own work;
For full details of academic misconduct and how allegations are investigated, see the relevant section of the University’s academic regulations: https://student.londonmet.ac.uk/your-studies/student-administration/rules-and-regulations/academic-misconduct/.
