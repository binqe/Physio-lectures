## Slide 39

- Capillary blood flow is slow and intermittent reflecting the need for an erythrocyte transit time of >1 second for complete O2 diffusion from the capillary to surrounding tissue
- The erythrocyte has a diameter of 7.2μm – larger than a capillary
- Types: nutritional capillaries & thermoregulatory capillaries
- Some capillaries are unresponsive to thermal stress and do not increase blood flow in response to heat
- Hyperaemia allows a 100 fold increase in capillary blood flow at maximal perfusion
- The number of capillaries per unit area is considered a determinant of tissue oxygenation:

**Notes:** 0.8 ml of blood/min/100 ml adequate for resting perfusion of skin

## Slide 42

- Lymphatic system
- Thin walled, highly permeable capillaries with one way valves and muscles cells
- Lymph fluid containing a significant number of lymphocytes (wbc)
- Nodes (glands)
- 1. Initial lymphatics: Receive interstitial fluid
- 2. Collecting lymphatics:

**Notes:** Drain protein rich lymph from the extracellular space of most organs.
Net outward movement of fluid from capillaries, this is requisitioned by the Lymphatic system (nutrients and hydration of tissues. But greater capillary oncotic pressure than in interstitial fluid due to protein concentration, prevents fluid movement back into the capillary. 

Interstitial hydrostatic pressure = −8 to +6 mmHg but Venous entry pressures = 15mmHg
