# Retraining Mobility Practical 2024

## RETRAINING MOBILITY

### OBJECTIVES
After this session the student should be able to:
Carry out an objective assessment of lower limb function including gait
Conduct training strategies to rehabilitate lower limb function including gait

### ASSESSMENT
Using the task-oriented approach, based on the ICF categories (International categories of functioning, disabilities and health) mobility dysfunction should be assessed at three levels
Functional (e.g. mobility disability in home and community environment)
Strategies (including those required to achieve mobility)
Underlying impairments (including sensory, motor and cognitive)

### FUNCTIONAL ASSESSMENTS - OUTCOME MEASURES
These tests measure a person’s ability in his/her natural environment. The assessment can be observed or reported.
Examples (see Appendix) of functional measures for walking ability include:
Gait velocity (6-meter walk test)
Walking Endurance (6-minute walk test)
Walking assessments
Timed up and go (TUG)
Dynamic Gait Index (DGI)
Students Activity 1
Practice conducting gait velocity assessment 6 – meter walk test and 6-minute walk tests.

### GAIT ANALYSIS RECAP
Stance: weight acceptance (loading), mid stance, terminal stance and pre-swing (push off)
Swing: Initial swing (lift off), mid-swing, terminal swing (reach)
Students’ activity 2
Students recap on gait analysis:
Kinematics (joint angles)
Muscle activities
Spatiotemporal characteristics
Position of the R leg in the sagittal plane at 40ms intervals during a single gait cycle.
Sagittal plane joint angles during a single gait cycle.
Key: IC= initial contact; OT=opposite toe off; HR=heel rise; OI=opposite initial contact; TO=toe off; FA=feet adjacent; TV= tibial vertical

### IMPAIRMENT ASSESSMENT
Musculoskeletal and Neuromuscular factors
Tests for lower limb passive range of motion
Strength
Tests to measure lower limb muscle strength including hip, knee and ankle
Spasticity (demonstrate spasticity tests)
Assess spasticity in the knee extensors and ankle plantar flexors using the Modified Ashworth Scale.
Sensation (demonstrate sensation tests)
Test light touch, proprioception, two-point discrimination, stereognosis and pain or temperature.

### TRAINING GAIT
Research findings
↓ self-directed and maximum speed
↓ magnitude of flexion/extension joint angle range in both lower limbs
↓ hip extension at the end of stance
↑ double support phase
↓ vertical reaction force at push off
↓ power generation at the ankle for push off and at the hip for pull off
↓ endurance
↓ adaptability to changing environmental conditions.
Contributors to walking dysfunction
Contents of gait training
Treadmill and overground walking
Exercise to increase strength and control of lower limb muscles
Activities to preserve muscle extensibility.
Training to maximise skill, speed, endurance and fitness
Key focus in training gait
10-150 hip extension of the paretic limb in late stance (provides mechanical advantage to hip flexion for power generation for pull off
Plantarflexion at the ankle at the end of stance for push-off
Flexion at the hip for pull off.

### Possible treatments
Muscle strengthening strategies
Ankle plantarflexors & dorsiflexors (heel raise and lower from a step)
Hip, knee and ankle extensors (step up and down; sit to stand; squatting)
Hip abductors
Training Parameters:
Grade the amount of resistance and number of reps to individual ability
Body weight, free weights, elastic bands
Vary body weight resistance by changing step height, chair height, weight of object lifted
General rule, three sets of 10 repetitions.
For weak muscles, use methods which facilitate muscle activation and force generation e.g. simple exercises, force biofeedback, mental practice and functional electrical stimulation.
