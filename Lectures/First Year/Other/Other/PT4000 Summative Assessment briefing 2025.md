# Summative Assessment briefing 2025

Physiotherapy Theory and Practice 1: Management of Long-Term Conditions (PT4000)
Module Assessment Briefing
2024/25 Academic Year
Module learning outcomes:
At the end of this module, students will be able to:
Apply sound knowledge and understanding of human functioning to formulate appropriate assessment, problem list and person-centred treatment goals for individuals with long term and related conditions seen by physiotherapists in a variety of clinical contexts using a biopsychosocial framework.
Deliver and evaluate safe and effective physiotherapeutic treatment and management including face to face, digital interventions, risk management interventions appropriate to individuals with long term and related conditions, their changing problems, environment, psychosocial needs, family/carer and other service providers.
Demonstrate the ability to work safely and effectively within own scope of practice in collaboration with others and refer appropriately when necessary.
Systematically analyse and justify the role for physiotherapy within a multidisciplinary approach to recovery, rehabilitation and reablement services for individuals with long term and related conditions seen by physiotherapists in different contexts.
Problem Solving Essay
Summative Problem-Solving essay
A case study involving an individual with a long-term condition will be published to module weblearn pages on 13 December 2024. Using the biopsychosocial model, you are required to submit a detailed essay on the multi-disciplinary management of the individual in the case.
Your essay should include the following:
An introduction to the case, highlighting a problem list across the domains of health.
Justify the aims of long-term management, roles of the physiotherapist, appropriate interventions and progressions, and outcome measures to evaluate impact of your management.
Discuss the roles of appropriate multi-disciplinary team members for wholistic management of the patient to ensure an optimised outcome.
Consideration of relevant health promotion approaches including self-management, physical activities etc, that would be necessary to achieve best outcome for the patient.
Support your discussions, recommendations, and management approaches with appropriate critically appraised research evidence, where available, and/or any relevant clinical guidance.
You must reference your sources and add these to a reference list.  References must be presented in Harvard referencing style. See example below for your information and guidance.
Refer to the marking rubric for further guidelines on how your essays will be marked.

Academic Integrity and Plagiarism
Academic integrity requires honesty in your studies. You should not present another person’s sentences or ideas as your own work. You should clearly identify quotations through the use of quotation marks and references to the sources. Failure to adhere to these academic standards may lead to allegations of academic misconduct, which will be investigated by the Casework Office.
Once you uploaded your file, you will see a Turnitin report, informing you of the similarity level. This should not exceed 20% and it excludes the references.
Academic misconduct covers a variety of practices, such as:
Plagiarism: copying another person’s ideas or words and presenting them as your own work, without the use of quotation marks and/or references.
Self-plagiarism: reproducing parts of one of your assignments in another piece of work.
Inventing, altering, or falsifying the results of experiments or research.
Commissioning another person to complete an assessment.
Collaborating with others in the production of a piece of assessed work which is presented as entirely your own work.
For full details of academic misconduct and how allegations are investigated, see the relevant section of the University’s academic regulations: https://student.londonmet.ac.uk/your-studies/student-administration/rules-and-regulations/academic-misconduct/.

Objective Structured Clinical Examination
Summative Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE)
The summative OSCE will consist of 30-minute preparation time and 45-minute examination time.  During the preparation time, you will be given three case studies of long-term conditions - one for each core speciality (musculoskeletal, neurological and cardiorespiratory) - prior to the OSCE assessment. During the preparation time, you will be permitted to write notes to bring into the examination but will not have access to other reference materials. After the preparation, you will be taken to the OSCE examination hall.
During the examination time, you will be examined on the three case studies. The examination will include questions on assessment and treatment techniques and overall management of the cases.  You will be asked to demonstrate any of the clinical skills by your examiner.  Examiners will ask you several standardised questions for each case study.
Questions will focus on:
Demonstrating relevant clinical skills  for the assessment and treatment of the patient presented in the case study.
Discussing your understanding and clinical reasoning relevant to the management of the case study.
Discussing relevant research evidence base for the assessment and management selected for the patient in the case study.
A student colleague will be acting as your patient/model for the OSCE, and you will be expected to communicate with them as if with a patient.
Date of OSCE: May exam period (WC 19 May 2025)

Formative Objective Structured Clinical Examination (OSCE)
The formative OSCE will offer you an opportunity to practice the format of your summative OSCE in May. While it does not contribute to your overall module grade, attendance is mandatory.
You will be assigned to a group of three students for your formative OSCE examination.  On the day of formative assessment, students in each group will randomly select one of three OSCE questions focusing on Musculoskeletal, Neurological, or Cardiopulmonary physiotherapy management of long-term conditions.
After selecting your question, you will have a 15-minute preparation period of the OSCE question you select, and this will be followed by a 15-minute examination.  You may take notes to use during the preparation for your OSCE and use this during the OSCE but no other reference materials will be allowed. Your OSCE group will rotate through three roles: examiner, model (patient), and student during the assessment.
You will be required to provide feedback to your peers using OSCE proformas, and lecturers will offer verbal feedback as well.
During the examination, you will be required to:
Demonstrate relevant clinical assessment skills for the patient presented in the case study.
Demonstrate relevant clinical treatment skills for the patient presented in the case study.
Discuss your understanding and related clinical reasoning for the case study.
