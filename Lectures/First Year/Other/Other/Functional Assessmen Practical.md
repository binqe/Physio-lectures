Functional Assessments
Basic movement functions include walking, balance, movement transfers, and upper limb function. In this practical we will focus on bed mobility, lying to sitting and sit to stand.
The aim of functional assessment is to observe how the patient moves. This can be done while the patient carries out a functional transfer such as turning from side to side in bed (rolling), sitting to lying, lying to sitting, sitting to standing, standing to sitting, walking indoors/outdoors, stairs, running. It is also useful to assess the patient’s movement during functional activities, for example, dressing/undressing, reaching and grasp, writing their name etc.
Principles
Chose the function to observe based on patient’s ability.
Observe patient performing the function and note:
The success of the performance and what assistance they require to complete the movement
How the patient performs the function: deviation from normal limit of efficient movement
Postural alignment – look for asymmetries
Quality of movement – smoothness, co-ordination, timing and effort required.
Compensatory movements – do they use any trick movements during functional activities?
Patterns of movement – do they use mass movement patterns?
Involuntary movement – are there abnormal movements? E.g. dystonia, athetoid, choreiform, clonus, associated reactions.
Active range of movement – are their obvious limitation in range of movement?
Balance – do they lose their balance?
Is there a lack of confidence – look for signs of anxiety
Exercise tolerance – do they fatigue very quickly on activity?
Analysis
Collate all the deviations and reason what the potential cause of the dysfunction could be.
