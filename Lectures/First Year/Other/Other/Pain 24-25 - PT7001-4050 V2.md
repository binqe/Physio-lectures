## Slide 8

- DO PAIN AND TISSUE DAMAGE CORRELATE?
- Fisher et al., 1995

**Notes:** A builder aged 29 went to the accident and emergency department having jumped down on to a 15 cm nail. 
As the smallest movement of the nail was painful he was sedated. 
The nail was then pulled out from below. 
When his boot was removed, despite entering proximal to the steel toecap the nail had penetrated between the toes: the foot was entirely uninjured.
People can "hallucinate" pain based on perception alone. Strongly suggest that pure psychosomatic pain is also possible.

## Slide 12

- HOW IS PAIN DETECTED?
- Noxious stimuli (chemicals, especially from inflammatory processes) are released (Potassium ions, Bradykinin, Serotonin, Prostaglandins, Substance P, etc.)
- When electrical energy reaches a threshold value, an action potential is induced and driven towards the central nervous system. This leads to the train of events that allows for the conscious awareness of pain
- This threshold value is subject to change i.e. bruise, DOMS or those in chronic pain

## Slide 23

- PAIN GATE THEORY
- Melzack and Wall (1965)
- For sensation of pain to be experienced, pain signals must pass to the brain unopposed
- Stimulation of skin mechanoreceptors at same spinal level creates additional sensory signals which stimulate the A-BETA fibres
- Pre-synaptic inhibition stops pain signals arriving at the brain and blocks the pain i.e. ‘closes the gate’

## Slide 24

- ASCENDING INHIBITION
- Noxious afferent signals blocked at spinal cord level by touch, heat, ice (sensory afferent impulses)
- If occurring at same spinal level i.e. same area then inhibited at Substantia Gelatinosa (a grey matter structure of the dorsal spinal cord) which is the part of spinal cord that receives sensory info and where C fibres usually terminate

- PAIN AS A PERCEPTION
- The ‘Pain Gate Theory’, although seminal, is outdated and based on the assumption that we have unimodal, as opposed to multimodal, nerve fibres

## Slide 27

- DESCENDING INHIBITION
- Whilst we need pain to protect us from further damage, we can turn off pain when appropriate, via our internal modulation mechanism i.e. fight or flight
- The brain can request the release of specific hormones (noradrenaline) that can have analgesic effects which can reduce or inhibit pain sensation
- The area of the brain that stimulates the release of these hormones is the hypothalamus

## Slide 35

- CHRONIC PAIN IDENTIFICATION
- Screen / identify them by looking for YELLOW flags:
- Attitude – Fear avoidance behaviour and an expectation that passive, rather than active Rx, will be best
- Beliefs – ‘Something must be seriously wrong’
- Compensation
- Diagnosis – ‘Its bone on bone’, ‘My spine is crumbling’, ‘I have the knee of a 75 year old’, etc.
- Emotions – Look out for signs of low mood
- Family & work – Either overbearing or unsupportive & a poor relationship with a job that they do not enjoy

## Slide 36

- CHRONIC PAIN MANAGEMENT
- Manage them using PINK flags:
- Show empathy
- Use clear communication with careful language and make sure that is understood
- Explain pain
- Do not send for further investigation if you do not believe the results will change the management
- Exercise rehabilitation
