## Slide 13

- 25 Years Later: Principles, Practice and Scientific Ebquiry
- Holistic Approach: George Engel introduced the biopsychosocial model as a holistic alternative to the biomedical model, emphasizing the need to consider biological, psychological, and social dimensions simultaneously to truly understand and address a patient’s suffering.
- Critique of Biomedical Model: Engel critiqued the reductionist and specialized approach of the biomedical model, which tends to dehumanize patients by treating them as objects and neglecting the subjective experience of illness.
- New Medical Paradigm: The biopsychosocial model was proposed as a new medical paradigm that includes the patient's subjective experience with objective biomedical data, promoting a more comprehensive and naturalistic model of causation, and transforming the patient-clinician relationship to empower patients.
- Integration with Evidence-Based Medicine: The article explores how the biopsychosocial model interfaces with evidence-based medicine, emphasizing the importance of integrating both approaches to improve patient care.
- Scientific and Ideological Shifts: Engel’s model responded to shifts in science from exclusively analytic approaches to more contextual and cross-disciplinary perspectives. His approach not only proposed a scientific change but also advocated for a fundamental ideological shift towards re-humanizing medical practice.
- Engel (1977); Borrell-Carrió (2004)
