## Slide 16

- Basic training principles: OVERLOAD
- A progressive heightening of the stressor to stimulate adaptation at a higher level
- Adaptation occurs in many systems including the muscular and skeletal systems, the cardiovascular system, and the nervous system
- You are aiming to create a higher potential for “activity” within cells/ tissues, so that the body can cope with higher exercise demands

## Slide 17

- OVERLOAD
- Training load of a given exercise session can be objectively evaluated by considering several quantifiable within-session variables e.g., RPE
- The intensity or volume of the work must be more than the current level in order to achieve overload and therefore physiological adaptations
- Any of the FIVT principle factors can be modified to produce overload
- Frequency Intensity Volume Type

## Slide 20

- oVERTRAINING
- Generally, training will induce fatigue that may take 24-48 hours (similar to DOMS)
- In a balanced training programme, it is typical to plan for a progressive overload - recovery may take 1-2 weeks
- However if overload is excessive alongside a poor programme, recovery is prolonged from weeks to months
- As a result, the constant build up of stress from overload, combined with a poor programme (poor rest timings and lack of recovery) leads to non-functional overreaching (NFOR) OR overtraining syndrome (OTS)
- Exercise addiction – athletes sometimes suffer from the euphoric feeling from training. Less focused on adaptations and more focused on the release of endorphins and dopamine

## Slide 28

- TRAINING INTERVENTION
- Equal total work done during training between the groups
- Muscle damage group indicated by 5 x higher levels of plasma levels of the enzyme creatine kinase (CK) and perceived muscle soreness
- RESULTS
- Muscle size and strength gains were not different for the two groups. Similar increases in levels of the growth factors
- Significant increases in CSA - equal in both groups
- Strength increases identical (NON DOMS=25% and DOMS=26% improvement)
- CONCLUSION: Muscle rebuilding leading to hypertrophy and strength development can be achieved without evidence of muscle damage
