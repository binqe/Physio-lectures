## Slide 15

- Code of professional values
- “The CSP's Code of Members' Professional Values and Behaviour is centred  around the needs of patients and clients. It sets out the high standards of  behaviour required of you as a CSP member; following the Code forms part  of your contract as a member of the CSP.”
- Built around 4 main principles
- Underlines the obligations of the therapist to follow relevant laws and  regulations as well as guidance from your employer/educator
- “enables you to take responsibility for your decision-making and your  actions, and to fulfil your duty of care to the people you serve”

## Slide 21

- Clinical reasoning

**Notes:** The clinical reasoning cycle is a tool that helps HP make decisions by considering many factors and stimulating critical thinking.

Look: Consider the current context and what is most important for the patient 
Collect: Gather information 
Process: Consider the information in the context of the patient's situation 
Identify: Identify the problem or issue 
Establish goals: Determine what is important in the short-term and potentially mid- to long-term 
Take action: Decide on the best course of action 
Evaluate outcomes: Consider what has changed after taking action 
Reflect: Consider what has been learned from the process

## Slide 34

- Case Scenario 1
- Sarah, a newly qualified physiotherapist, is treating a high-profile client with a knee injury. One day, while talking to her colleagues in the staff room, she shares details of the client’s treatment plan and progress. One of her colleagues reminds her that this might be a breach of patient confidentiality.

## Slide 35

- Case Scenario 2
- Tom, a senior physiotherapist, is working with an elderly patient, Mr. Johnson, who has difficulty understanding the treatment being proposed due to hearing loss and mild cognitive impairment. Tom explains the procedure quickly and begins treatment without ensuring that Mr. Johnson fully understands or agrees to it.

## Slide 36

- Case Scenario 3
- Emily, a physiotherapist, is treating a regular patient, Mark, who has been seeing her for six months for a chronic back condition. Mark has expressed his appreciation by giving her small gifts, and recently, he asked Emily to have dinner with him outside of work. Emily feels conflicted because she enjoys working with Mark but is unsure whether accepting the offer is appropriate.
