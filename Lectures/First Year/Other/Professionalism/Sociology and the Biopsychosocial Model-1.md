## Slide 3

- What is Sociology?
- Sociology
- Is the scientific study of human behavior and social structures.
- examines patterns of social relationships and how they shape human behavior.
- studies social structures and institutions, such as religion, education, government, and the economy.
- helps us to understand the ways in which culture, socialization, and social structures impact our lives.

## Slide 4

- Sociology An Overview
- Social Structures
- The organized patterns of relationships and social institutions that shape our lives, such as families, schools, and the workplace.
- Institutions
- Established and organized patterns of behaviour that govern social life. They include religion, education, government, and the economy.
- Culture
- Refers to the shared beliefs, values, customs, behaviours, and artifacts that characterize a society or group. It includes language, art, music, and religion, among other things.
- Socialization
- Process by which individuals learn the norms, values, and beliefs of their culture. It includes the ways in which we learn to think, feel, and behave as members of society.

## Slide 6

- Understanding Social Structure
- Social Hierarchies
- Social structure includes social hierarchies, such as class, race, and gender, which shape our opportunities and experiences in society.
- Patterns of Relationships
- Social structure includes the patterns of relationships and institutions in society, which affect how we interact and communicate with one another.

## Slide 7

- Examining Culture and Norms
- Culture and Identity
- Culture and norms shape our beliefs and behaviours, defining our identity and guiding our interactions with others.
- Social Norms
- Social norms are unwritten rules and customs that guide accepted behaviours and interactions in a society.

## Slide 8

- Roles and Institutions in Society
- Family
- A fundamental institution in society that provides emotional support and helps shape our values and beliefs. It plays a key role in the development of children and in shaping their experiences and opportunities.
- Education
- An important institution in society that plays a key role in shaping our experiences and opportunities. It provides knowledge and skills that are essential for personal and professional development.
- Healthcare
- An essential institution in society that provides medical care and support to individuals and communities. It plays a key role in promoting health and well-being and preventing illnesses.

## Slide 9

- Understanding Socialization
- ifelong process of learning and internalizing the norms, values, and beliefs of a society.
- Important because it helps individuals to develop their sense of self and their place in society.
- Types of socialization include primary, secondary, anticipatory, and resocialization.
- Agents of socialization include family, peers, school, the media, and religion.

## Slide 10

- Brief History of Sociology
- Auguste Comte
- Auguste Comte is regarded as the father of sociology. He developed the concept of positivism, which is a scientific approach to understanding society and social behavior.
- Emile Durkheim
- Emile Durkheim is considered as one of the founding fathers of sociology. He created the first sociology department in France and his work on social solidarity and suicide is still widely studied today.
- Max Weber
- Max Weber is widely considered as one of the most important figures in the development of sociology. He is known for his work on bureaucracy, social stratification, and the relationship between religion and capitalism.

## Slide 11

- Key Sociological Theories
- Functionalism
- A sociological theory that views society as a complex system of interdependent parts that work together to maintain social order and stability.
- Conflict Theory
- A sociological perspective that views society as a struggle for power between different groups, with resources and wealth being unequally distributed.
- Symbolic Interactionism
- A sociological perspective that focuses on the ways in which people create and maintain social meaning through language, gestures, and symbols.

## Slide 13

- Overview of the Biopsychosocial Model
- Definition of Biopsychosocial Model
- The biopsychosocial model recognizes that health and illness are shaped by biological, psychological, and social factors.
- Whole Person Approach
- The biopsychosocial model considers the whole person

## Slide 14

- Relevance to Health and Human Behavior
- The biopsychosocial model recognizes the complex interplay of factors that influence our well-being, including social determinants of health, psychological and biological factors.

## Slide 15

- Interaction of Biological, Psychological, and Social Factors
- The biopsychosocial model recognizes the interaction of biological, psychological, and social factors in health and behaviour. This holistic approach considers the whole person and the complex interplay of factors that influence well-being.

## Slide 16

- Social Factors in Health

## Slide 17

- Impact of Socioeconomic Status and Culture
- Socioeconomic Status Impact
- Socioeconomic status has a significant impact on health outcomes, with individuals from lower socioeconomic backgrounds experiencing poorer health outcomes compared to those from higher socioeconomic backgrounds.
- Culture Impact
- Culture can influence beliefs about health and healthcare, affecting the way individuals seek healthcare and engage with healthcare providers.

## Slide 18

- Influence of Family Dynamics on Health Outcomes
- Positive Family Relationships
- Positive family relationships and support can have a protective effect on health, improving overall physical and mental wellbeing.
- Negative Family Dynamics
- Negative family dynamics, such as conflict, abuse, and neglect, can have a negative impact on health, increasing the risk of mental health issues, chronic diseases, and mortality.

## Slide 19

- Role of Social Support Networks and Community
- Social Support Networks
- Social support networks can provide important emotional and practical support, which can contribute to improved health outcomes.
- Community Engagement
- Community engagement can promote social connectedness and well-being, leading to improved health outcomes.

## Slide 20

- Sociology And Biopsychosocial Approach

## Slide 21

- Synthesis of Sociological Concepts and the Biopsychosocial Model
- Biopsychosocial Model
- The biopsychosocial model is a holistic approach to health that considers the interaction of biological, psychological, and social factors in shaping health outcomes.
- Sociological Concepts
- Sociological concepts, such as social determinants of health and social support, can deepen our understanding of the social factors that influence health outcomes.

## Slide 22

- Strengths and Limitations of Both Approaches
- Sociology Approach
- This approach has strengths in highlighting the importance of social determinants of health but also limitations in ignoring biological and psychological factors.
- Biopsychosocial Model
- This approach has strengths in recognizing the complexity of health but also limitations in being difficult to operationalize and measure.

## Slide 23

- A Holistic Perspective

## Slide 24

- Importance of a Holistic Approach
- Whole Person Approach
- A holistic approach considers the whole person, including their social, psychological, and biological context, to develop more effective interventions and promote better health outcomes.
- Complex Interplay of Factors
- A holistic approach recognizes the complex interplay of factors that influence health and well-being, including social determinants of health, psychological factors, and biological factors.

## Slide 25

- Interdisciplinary Thinking in Healthcare and Social Sciences
- Interdisciplinary thinking is critical to a holistic approach to health, as it brings together different perspectives and expertise to gain a more comprehensive understanding of health and develop more effective interventions.
