## Slide 4

- What is biomechanics?
- The science that involves the study of biological systems in a mechanical sense (Hatze, 1974)
- Prefix for life or living organism
- Study of motion, forces, response of materials
- How trees grow
- Cardiovascular system
- Musculoskeletal system & its relationship to  movement
- Motion = movement
- Forces = the factors that cause movement
- Response = bending, compression, stretching, etc.
- Any living thing

## Slide 30

- Poor scapulohumeral rhythm (dyskinesis) alters the mechanics of overhead motion, reduces the subacromial space and increasing compression on the supraspinatus tendon and subacromial bursae
- Either…
- Weakness or fatigue in the lower trapezius, serratus anterior, rhomboids, and/or,
- Imbalance between deltoid and rotator cuff muscles
- …causes excessive superior migration of the humeral head during arm elevation, increasing compression and shear forces on the rotator cuff
- Biomechanics and shoulder injuries

## Slide 31

- Epicondylitis (medial or lateral)
- Repetitive wrist extension / flexion and gripping activities lead to eccentric overload of the common extensor / flexor tendon origin
- Poor kinetic chain mechanics (e.g., weak stabilization at the shoulder) increase reliance on forearm muscles
- Improper grip mechanics during sports or work tasks increases strain on the tendons
- Ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) sprain
- Excessive valgus stress during throwing motion causes progressive attenuation of the UCL
- Poor trunk and hip rotation mechanics in throwing leads to over-reliance on the arm
- Biomechanics and elbow injuries

## Slide 32

- Carpal tunnel syndrome
- Poor grip ergonomics or excessive finger flexor activity leads to hypertrophy of the flexor tendons, reducing available space in the tunnel
- Weak proximal shoulder stabilizers cause excessive reliance on wrist flexors for gripping tasks, contributing to repetitive strain, swelling and therefore space
- Biomechanics and wrist and hand injuries

## Slide 35

- Biomechanics is the study of biological systems in a mechanical sense
- The shoulder joint is highly reliant on active support to optimally function due to its inherent instability. This is offset by the elbow which is highly stable but with limited mobility
- To be performed effectively overhead movements need kinetic chain contribution
- Kinematic chain deficiencies, muscle imbalances and motor control deficits can cause injury due to the body following the path of least resistance
