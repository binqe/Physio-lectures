## Slide 5

- INFERIOR RADIO-ULNAR JOINT
- The interosseous membrane is a thin fibrous sheet that connects the radius and ulnar and aids in transfer of forces between the two bones
- A synovial pivot joint formed by the head of the ulnar and the ulnar notch of the radius
- Assists the superior radio-ulnar joint with pronation and supination

## Slide 6

- INFERIOR RADIO-ULNAR JOINT
- Supported anteriorly and posteriorly by the volar (or palmar) and dorsal radio-ulnar ligaments

## Slide 7

- TFCC
- Triangular fibrocartilage complex (TFCC) is a load-bearing structure between the lunate, triquetrum, and ulnar head
- The function of the TFCC is to act as a stabilizer for the ulnar aspect of the wrist

## Slide 8

- RADIOCARPAL JOINT
- The wrist is an ellipsoidal (condyloid) type synovial joint, allowing for movement along two axes
- Compromised of the distal end of the radius (and ulnar) and the proximal row of carpals
- Produces flexion, extension, ulnar deviation and radial deviation

## Slide 9

- RADIOCARPAL JOINT
- The dorsal aspect of the wrist includes the dorsal radiocarpal ligament

## Slide 10

- CARPAL TUNNEL
- The flexor retinaculum is a thick connective tissue ligament that bridges the space between the medial and lateral sides of the base of the carpal arch (Scaphoid to Pisiform and Trapezium to Hamate)
- Converts the carpal arch into the carpal tunnel
- It also holds the flexor tendons close to radio-carpal joint

## Slide 11

- CARPAL TUNNEL
- The four tendons of the flexor digitorum profundus, the four tendons of the flexor digitorum superficialis, and the tendon of the flexor pollicis longus pass through the carpal tunnel, as does the median nerve

## Slide 20

- CARPAL TUNNEL SYNDROME
- Carpal tunnel syndrome is an entrapment syndrome caused by pressure on the median nerve within the carpal tunnel
- Usually, a direct effect of increased pressure on the median nerve caused by overuse, swelling of the tendons and tendon sheaths and cysts arising from the carpal joints

## Slide 21

- CARPAL TUNNEL SYNDROME
- Patients typically report pain and pins-and-needles sensations in the distribution of the median nerve
- Weakness and loss of muscle bulk of the thenar muscles (those at the base of the thumb) may also occur
- Gently tapping over the median nerve readily produces these symptoms
- Initial treatment is aimed at reducing the inflammation and removing any repetitive actions that produce the symptoms
- If this does not lead to improvement, nerve conduction studies will be necessary to confirm nerve entrapment, which may require surgical decompression of the flexor retinaculum

## Slide 22

- COLLES’ FRACTURE
- Alternative name for a distal radius fracture (when there is displacement), which are common following a fall onto an outstretched hand (FOOSH)
- Characterised by posterior displacement of the distal end of the radius

## Slide 23

- SCAPHOID FRACTURE
- Common following a fall onto an outstretched hand (FOOSH)
- Occurs at the ‘waist’ of the scaphoid
- Pain usually present on palpation of the ‘snuffbox’
- In 10% of the population, the scaphoid has one vessel of blood supply (scaphoid branch of the radial artery) which can cause avascular necrosis, if the scaphoid fractures and is not recognised and managed
- Other issues include onset of wrist arthritis later
- FOOSH can sometimes lead to a scapholunate ligament rupture which causes instability symptoms

## Slide 24

- DE QUERVAINS SYNDROME
- De Quervain's syndrome is an inflammatory disorder that involves the extensor pollicis brevis tendon and abductor pollicis longus tendon
- Patients typically present with significant wrist pain preventing flexion/extension and abduction of the thumb
- The cause of this disorder is often overuse and is common in new mothers who are constantly lifting young children
- Identified using Finklestein’s test

## Slide 26

- SUMMARY
- The wrist complex is made up of the radius, ulnar and proximal row of carpals
- Supported by articular disc, TFCC and ligaments
- Many structures are present in a small space and some of these (flexor tendons and median nerve) run through the carpal tunnel
- Common traumatic injuries include distal radius and scaphoid fractures
- Common insidious injuries include carpal tunnel and De Quervains syndromes
