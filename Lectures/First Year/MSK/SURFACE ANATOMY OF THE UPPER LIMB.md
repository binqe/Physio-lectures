Deltoid Region
• The lateral aspect of the shoulder, covering the deltoid muscle. It 
forms the rounded contour of the shoulder.
Key Anatomical Features:
•
Boundaries:
• Superiorly: Clavicle, acromion of the scapula
• Inferiorly: Deltoid tuberosity of the humerus
• Anteriorly: Overlaps with the clavipectoral and pectoral regions
• Posteriorly: Blends with the scapular region
• In your groups, trace and identify these regions on your models

## Page 5

Clavipectoral Region of the Upper Limb: 
Surface Anatomy
• The clavipectoral region is a part of the anterior 
shoulder and upper chest area, located deep to the 
pectoralis major muscle and extending from the 
clavicle to the upper part of the axilla (armpit). 
Boundaries:
• Superiorly: Clavicle
• Inferiorly: Upper border of pectoralis minor
• Medially: First rib
• Laterally: Coracoid process of the scapula
In your groups, trace and identify these regions on your 
models

## Page 6

Pectoral Region
• The pectoral region refers to the anterior chest wall, 
particularly the area covering the upper thorax and 
shoulder.
Key Anatomical Features:
•
 Boundaries:
• Superiorly: Clavicle
• Inferiorly: Sixth or seventh rib
• Medially: Sternum
• Laterally: Transition into the axillary and deltoid regions
In your groups, trace and identify these regions on your 
models
13-6

## Page 7

Scapular Region
• The scapular region refers to the posterior aspect of the 
shoulder girdle, centred around the scapula (shoulder 
blade).
Key Anatomical Features:
•
 Boundaries:
•Superiorly: Level of the second rib and spine of the scapula
•Inferiorly: Inferior angle of the scapula (around the 7th rib level)
•Medially: Vertebral column
•Laterally: Shoulder joint and deltoid region
In your groups, trace and identify these regions on your 
models

Axillary Folds
•     The anterior axillary fold is formed by the lower
margin of the pectoralis major, and can be
palpated by the finger.
•     This can be made by asking the patient to press
his or her hand against the ipsilateral hip.
•     The posterior axillary fold is formed by the tendons
of latissimus dorsi  and teres major muscles
•
Axilla
•     The axilla should be examined with the forearm
supported and the pectoral muscles relaxed.
•     When the arm by the side, the inferior part of the       
head of the humerus can be easily palpated
through the floor of the axilla.
•     The pulsations of the axillary artery can be felt
high up in the axilla, and around the artery the
cords of the brachial plexus.
•     The medial wall of the axilla is formed by the upper
ribs covered by the serratus anterior.
•     The lateral wall is formed by the coracobrachialis
and biceps brachii and the bicipital groove.

anterior and posterior surfaces of the arm.
•    The biceps tendon can be palpated in the cubital
fossa, immediately lateral to the midline.
•    The triceps tendon can be palpated where it is
attached to the olecranon process.
•    There are 2 grooves: Medial and lateral grooves
separate the bulges formed by the biceps and
triceps.
•    The cephalic vein ascends superiorly in the lateral
groove and
•    The basilicvein ascends in the medial groove.
