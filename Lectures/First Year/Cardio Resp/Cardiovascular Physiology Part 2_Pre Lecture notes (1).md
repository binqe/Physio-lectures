## Slide 5

- Affects of parasympathetic stimulation
- Mediated by release of acetylcholine (ACh), causing reduction in heart rate via the Sinus node
- Cardiac muscarinic (acetylcholine) M2 receptors located in the heart reduce atrial force but little impact on the ventricular force
- The local signalling compound nitric oxide (NO), increases the amount of Ach released in response to each vagal action potential

**Notes:** Vagus nerve interfaces with the parasympathetic control of the heart.

## Slide 6

- Affects of sympathetic stimulation
- Mediated by release of noradrenaline, causing:
- Increased heart rate
- Increased AV node conduction velocity
- Shortening of myocyte action potential
- Increased contractile force
- Quicker relaxation
- Thus sympathetic stimulation not only increases heart rate, but also shortens components of the cardiac cycle

## Slide 10

- Gravity/posture
- Compared with lying down, standing redistributes about 500 ml of blood from the thorax to the lower limbs, due to the effect of gravity and the distensibility of the leg veins
- This lowers CVP and reduces SV from about 100 to 70 ml.
- Factors influencing Central Venous Pressure

**Notes:** Vena cava carries deoxygenated blood to the right side of the heart.

## Slide 15

- Stroke volume and its control
- Stroke volume at rest is 70-80 ml
- This will increase to about 100ml in heavy exercise, or 120 ml in trained subjects
- Olympic athletes may have stroke volumes of 160 ml
- Cardiac output is determined by heart rate and stroke volume, both of which contribute to increased cardiac output during exercise:
- CO  = HR  x SV
- Training effects are primarily confined to stroke volume

## Slide 16

- Stroke volume
- Stroke volume is equal to the amount of blood pumped from the left ventricle in a single cardiac contraction
- It is equal to the end-diastolic volume (EDV) minus the end-systolic volume (ESV)
- Typical resting values for these two variables are 120 and 50 ml respectively
- Note that the ESV in the heart is not zero: there is a substantial volume of blood left in the ventricle at the end of systole

## Slide 27

- What does it all mean?
- The rhythm represents the depolarisation and repolarisation stages of a heart beat.

## Slide 28

- P wave
- The P wave represents atrial depolarisation.
- SA node has started an action potential, causing the atriums to contract.

## Slide 29

- QRS Complex
- The QRS complex phase represents ventricular depolarisation and atrial repolarisation.
- Ventricular contractions supply the body with the amount of blood required to sufficiently perfuse the bodies cells.

## Slide 30

- T Wave
- The T wave represents the repolarisation of the ventricles.

- Cardiomyopathies- Heart walls can become thickened, dilated or have genetic abnormalities

## Slide 39

- Baroreceptor reflex
- Associated wit homeostasis of blood pressure through a negative feedback loop:
- Increased blood pressure = decreased heart rate
- Baroreceptors sense arterial wall tension and can promote a response within one cardiac cycle (such as in regulating postural hypotension).
