## Page 29

Summary
• Many factors affect respiratory centre activity to ensure:
- Adequate ventilation 
- ABG profile maintained within an acceptable range
• Every cell must have O2
• Every cell produces CO2 to be excreted
• CO2 is the most profound stimulus to breath in health
• CO2 works primarily on central chemoreceptors via H+ and secondly via peripheral chemoreceptors
• In health, pO2 only influences ventilation via enhancing sensitivity of the central chemoreceptors to 
pCO2
• In disease, a pO2 of < 8kPa becomes the prime stimulus to breathe

## Page 32

Chronic Obstructive 
Pulmonary Disease (COPD):
The GOLD initiative defines COPD as :
“A heterogenous lung condition characterized by chronic respiratory
symptoms (dyspnoea, cough, sputum production) due to abnormalities
of the airways (bronchitis, bronchiolitis) &/or alveoli (emphysema)
Which cause persistent, often progressive, airflow obstruction.”
A disease state characterised by airflow obstruction 
resulting from chronic bronchitis or emphysema 
Airway obstruction is progressive 
Airway obstruction is only partially reversible and 
does not change markedly over several months

## Page 34

Epidemiology of COPD:
COPD is laden with gloomy statistics : 
• Globally around 3 million deaths annually due to COPD 
• Expected to increase to 5.4 million by 2060
• Asthma + Lung UK (previously known as BLF UK) estimated 1.2 million people (2% of the 
population) are living with COPD (2019)
• Affects roughly 10% of the global population (Mulhall 2016)
• Half of elderly smokers have the disease (Lindberg et al 2016)
• Huge financial burden on the NHS
• Respiratory disease accounts for approximately 6% of the total healthcare budget

## Page 39

Causes of 
Chronic Bronchitis 
• Cigarette smoke. People who smoke or who live with a smoker are 
at higher risk of both acute bronchitis and chronic bronchitis.
• Low immune resistance. This may result from another acute illness, 
such as a cold, or from a chronic condition that compromises your 
immune system. Older adults, infants and young children have 
greater vulnerability to infection.
• Exposure to irritants on the job. Your risk of developing bronchitis is 
greater if you work around certain lung irritants, such as grains or 
textiles, or are exposed to chemical fumes.
• Gastric reflux. Repeated bouts of severe heartburn can irritate your 
throat and make you more prone to developing bronchitis.

## Page 44

Emphysema:
• Emphysema is characterized by abnormal permanent 
enlargement of alveoli with the destruction of their 
walls (without any fibrosis) and destruction of lung 
parenchyma with loss of elasticity. 
• There are secondary effects on larger airways.

## Page 45

Emphysema 
• SMOKING is the major cause 
• Alpha 1 antitrypsin deficiency
• Genetic cause 
• Alpha 1 anti-trypsin inhibits the 
action of neutrophil elastase 
which destroys elastin 
• Develops before the age of 40
• Equal distribution between sexes

## Page 46

Pathophysiology 
•
Proteolytic degradation of the alveolar walls and destruction 
of elastic fibres 
•
In the alveoli, erosion of alveolar septum breaking down into 
bullae
•
Distally, bronchioles are destabilizing by loss of the elastic 
recoil which normally splints them open. Therefore, airways 
become floppy, can collapse and cause gas trapping. 
•
Hyperinflation

Positioning to Relieve Breathlessness
• Leaning forward in standing or sitting 
moves abdominal contents so that the 
anterior part of the diaphragm is raised
• Similar effect achieved in-side 
lying/high side lying
• Increased curvature of diaphragm 
• Optimises length tension relationship 
of the diaphragm 
• Facilitates diaphragmatic contraction 
and reduces work of breathing

## Page 81

Breathing Techniques
Breathing Control/Diaphragmatic Breathing:
• Reduces WOB
• Reduces RR
• Reduced energy expenditure 
• Decreased oxygen demand
Pursed Lip Breathing: 
• Creates positive expiratory pressure
• Prevents airway collapse
• Improved lung compliance 
• Ensure neck and shoulder muscles are relaxed
• Inhale through the nose and gently out through 
pursed lips
Blow-as-you-go: 
• Breathing out during the effortful part of any task 
e.g. ascending stairs 
• Avoids breath holding/inhaling with effort – valsalva 
maneuver 
• Use pursed lips
• Can be applied to any activities/exercise

## Page 82

Principles of 
Exercise in 
Cardiorespiratory 
Disease: 
High prevalence of comorbidity in people with respiratory disease that is 
influenceable with exercise 
Offsetting of complications associated with respiratory medications
Modulates elements of the disease itself 
• Evidences support that exercise is able to modulate inflammatory pathways in 
asthma and COPD, including cytokines and leukotriene pathways 
• Reversal/slow down cachexia in interstitial lung disease 
• Strengthens immune response therefore reduce infection risks 
Pacing progression
• Want to avoid boom and bust 
• Avoid discouraging- Too much too soon
• Be wary of the patient group- Not all will have exercised before 
• We want exercise to be a habit for life not a quick fix
• Avoidance of post exertional malaise
